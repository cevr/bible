# Design language: the studio

The studio's tokens, shell, kit and pages. Written in pass 1 of the architecture loop (HEAD `f812e9ca`, a read-only sweep of `righteousness-by-faith` driven with `agent-browser` at 390×844 and 1440×900) and brought up to the code in pass 2, when the kit was built (§5 _The kit as built_). §1 is the pass-1 inventory and stays as the record of where the studio started; §3 onward is the language the code follows.

Mocks are in `mocks/`, one HTML page each, at 390 and 1440 by the same file (`.ph` shows below 900 px, `.lap` from 900 px); `index.html` shows every mock at both widths on one page. The mocks show real data from the film. Their stills (`mocks/stills/`: one tape still every 5 s, one poster a scene, three large frames) are frames from the lab's `/api/review/frame` route; they are the film's media, so git keeps them out (`.gitignore`) and they travel with the published page.

Serves **Studio**, **Mobile-first** (and "Mobile-first beats density"), **Progressive disclosure** (and its tiebreak), **Comb at a glance**, **Addressable**. Every ui-reduction verdict (UR-1..120, AA-1..18) holds: what is at rest stays at rest, what is disclosed stays one affordance away. This sweep decides how things look and where the shell puts them, not what is shown.

Terms used below (owner rule: industry names, each explained):

- **Page bar**: the row of the studio's parts (Resolve's Media · Cut · Edit … row). Here: Films · Scenes · Lab · Choices · Project · Play.
- **Panel / panel header**: a fixed area of a page with a thin title row holding its name, its tabs and its `⋯` (Resolve, Ableton).
- **Inspector**: the panel of the selected thing's properties (Resolve's Inspector, Blender's N-panel).
- **Dock**: a panel area along one edge, here the timeline along the foot of the Lab.
- **Transport**: play, frame step, timecode, rate and loop.
- **Timecode**: `HH:MM:SS:FF`, hours, minutes, seconds and frames at 30 fps.
- **Tape**: the whole film laid end to end as stills (Resolve's Source Tape); the **tape bar** is the one-line overview of it with the acts ruler.
- **Sheet**: a panel that slides up over the page on a phone (iOS sheet, Base UI Drawer).
- **Receipt**: the toast after a write, with what moved and Undo.
- **State band**: a thin coloured line saying a scene's state.

**Picked: JetBrains Mono, self-hosted from `@fontsource-variable/jetbrains-mono` (latin, latin-ext and greek subsets, one variable woff2 each).** The UI lives at 10-13 px on a dark ground, and JetBrains Mono is the one face of the three drawn for exactly that: its tall x-height and flat-sided shapes keep 10-11 px labels (tape names, ruler, field units) legible where Plex's humanist curves blur. One variable file carries every weight the hierarchy needs (400 body, 500 labels, 650 titles), the package is healthy (a July 2026 release, ~2 M downloads a week), and the licence lets the lab serve the file from the box. Ligatures are turned off (`"calt" 0`) so `->`, `!=` and `>=` in notes and findings read as typed. It is also the only one of the three whose package ships Greek, which notes and findings on a Bible film can quote (Strong's words); serve the greek subset too. Berkeley Mono stays out (paid; an owner purchase).

The film's own faces (`assets/fonts/fonts.css`: Fraunces, EB Garamond, Frank Ruhl Libre, Gaegu) are the picture's, not the UI's, and stay exactly as they are. Inter leaves the UI.

## 6. The scenes as one continuous flow (p1-scenes)

**Decision: the wrapped tape.** The whole film lies end to end as stills at a regular time step, wrapped into rows like lines of text: on the laptop 12 stills a row at 5 s each, **one row a minute** (the 8:46 film is 9 rows and fits one 900 px screen under the header); on the phone 6 stills a row, **one row per 30 s** (18 rows, about one and a half screens), with a timecode at the start of each row like a line number. Scene boundaries are thin 1 px rules at their exact time inside the row, each with the scene's name above it (anchored right of the rule past 78 % of the row so it never overflows; a scene too short to hold its name before the next boundary, such as the 3 s title, shows only its state dot, the name on hover and in the focus panel); a 2 px state band under each row says each scene's state in its colour. Above the rows sits the **tape bar**: the acts ruler (cold open · message · faith · forgiveness · power · landing, from `acts.ts`), the scene bands in their hues, the playhead, and **Follow** at its right (the view keeps the playhead's row in sight while playing; off when the owner scrubs away).

Why wrapped rows, against the sources:

- **Comb at a glance** wants the whole flow seen at once. A horizontal tape at 1440 shows about 12 stills (one minute) and needs an overview to see the rest; that is Resolve's dual timeline and Ableton's overview strip, which solve it with a second, coarser band. The wrapped tape is itself the overview: every frame step is on screen, and the tape bar above it is only the act-level map.
- **Mobile-first**: the same layout works at 390 (six columns instead of twelve), so the phone gets the whole film as a vertical read with no second layout to build or maintain. A horizontal tape on a phone is the narrowest possible window on the film.
- Storyboard Pro's thumbnails view, Descript's storyboard view and a contact sheet already read a film as rows; the wrap keeps Resolve's Source Tape continuity (no gaps at scene changes: a scene can start mid-row, so the flow never breaks to fit a grid).
- Rejected: one card per scene in a grid (the look-book and PA-3's first sketch): scenes of 3 s and 43 s get the same size, so pace disappears; the cards become the focus panel instead.

Gestures: drag along a row scrubs the time under the finger (the still under it updates, the header timecode follows); a vertical drag scrolls (`touch-action: pan-y` on rows, as the track does at `player.css:100`); tap a still selects its scene at that time; long-press (right-click) opens the scene's context menu: Open in Lab at this frame (`E`), Approve, Comment, Versions, Copy link (AA-15). `⌘+`/`⌘−` change the time step (2.5 s, 5 s, 10 s), so a long film still fits; "Fit film" is the default on the laptop.

The **focus panel** is the inspector for the selected scene: a large still that scrubs the scene, the scene's name, act, `01:53:28–02:35:27 · 42.0 s`, its badges (Out of date, findings count, notes count, versions ≥ 2), the primary verb **Open in Lab** (`E`), and below, collapsed groups: Findings (the scene's own), Notes, Versions, Choices in this scene, Info. On the phone it is a sheet that peeks one line (`word · 19.44 s · Out of date · 3 findings  [Open in Lab]`) and opens on tap. The selection is the path `/films/<f>/scenes/<scene>` with the time in the hash (PA-1).

Live drawing stays as Settled: stills are rendered frames (the review's `frame` route or cue-edge stills), and only the scrubbed row draws live on one shared canvas.

5. **Where a Set sits**: under Project for the film's own folder, under Films for a folder that is not a film's. 6. **Picked moves off gold** (a filled check, not a colour): the owner has seen gold as "picked" on Choices. A pixel change of chrome only, not of the film (the art-direction rule covers frames, not chrome), so no owner OK is required, but the owner sees it in the mocks.
7. **Pixels of the film are not touched**: the compare divider, note marks and handles drawn over the picture keep their geometry; only their colours map to tokens (`--accent`, `--state-note`, white divider kept).
8. **Owner**: has not seen the mocks yet (done-when). Nothing here needs a purchase; Berkeley Mono only if the owner wants it.
9. **Mocks use only real data.** Where real data was thin, the mocks show it as it is: the Set has one version (the only one the folder holds), Project shows no comment dots (no scene has comments), Project's inspector selects word (its three DurOnWord findings, on cues grow, lean and wide, are known exactly; roof's four are only a count).
