# North star

The film lab is becoming the one studio where every film is tweaked and
reviewed: always on, served live from the source, addressable down to the
scene, the frame and the note, and designed for the phone first and the laptop second.

Scope: the lab and the review it holds — `packages/film/src/lab/**`,
`packages/film/src/player/**`, the page's commands and host adapters
(`packages/film/src/command/**`, `packages/film/src/browser/**`), the lab's
server (`packages/film/src/tools/` `lab*.ts`, `api-server.ts`,
`page-render*.ts`, `review*.ts`, `choices*.ts`, `project-http.ts`,
`studio*.ts`, `steps-http.ts`, `notes-*.ts`, `narration-route.ts`,
`source-writer.ts`, `scene-*.ts`, `film-services.ts`), `packages/film/src/core/api.ts`, and the
app's entries (`apps/animations/*.html`, `apps/animations/src/*.ts`,
`apps/animations/cli.ts`, `apps/animations/server.ts`). The framework's own
north stars (`.claude/skills/film-architecture-loop/north-stars.md`: **Pure
frames**, **Narration is the clock**, **Declared once**, **Effect-native
tooling**, **Explicit over implicit**, **Lab-first**, …) hold underneath every
row here; the film loop sweeps the engine, these sweep the studio.

## Tiebreaks

- **Agent-led harness** beats **Studio** density and power-user reach (owner, 2026-10-14: "not prioritizing power usage, in the traditional sense, more of clarity of what's happening"). This supersedes, in part, 2026-10-02's "build primitives that power users can really thrive off of" and "make certain affordances dense": a view is judged by how fast the owner understands where the film is and what he is asked, not by how much he can do from it. Capability moves to the agents (MCP and CLI) rather than hiding behind affordances. The Studio's look (monospace, one sheet, the professional calm) stays; its density does not.
- **Progressive disclosure** beats **one-tap reach** for anything used in under half the visits to a view (owner, 2026-10-02): it moves behind one affordance (a context menu by right-click or long-press, a key binding, `⌘K`/`?`, a sheet, the selection's panel), never two deep, and every affordance has a touch path. **Capability** is never the price (owner, 2026-10-02: "we don't want to reduce capability but only presentation"): a hidden action stays complete and precise, and a dense open affordance beats a sparse one that drops options. **Mobile-first** still holds: disclosed is not removed. Rejected candidates look like a toolbar row of rarely used toggles, a status line of engine counts, a panel open by default "so it is discoverable", or a feature cut to make a view cleaner.

- **Framework north stars** beat every row here (owner, 2026-10-02): a studio feature never trades a pure frame, a declared-once cue or a literal in the scene file for a UI shortcut. Rejected candidates look like a sidecar of values the lab keeps, or a cache carried between frames to scrub faster.
- **Addressable** beats **local convenience** (owner, 2026-10-02): a state a reviewer would cite goes in the URL even when it costs a history entry. Per-viewer settings stay local: playback speed, onion, the mic, quality (`film-lab-view:<film>` sessionStorage, localStorage); Show only (`?only=`) is the link's. Rejected candidates look like a selection or a view mode kept only in memory "to keep the URL short".
- **Path** beats **query** for what a view is about; **query** for how it is shown; **hash** for the frame (owner, 2026-10-02). Rejected: a resource named by a param (`?film=`, `?project=`), a time in the path.
- **Mobile-first** beats **density** (owner, 2026-10-02): every page is designed at phone width first; a laptop layout may show more, but never holds an action the phone lacks.
- **Live from source** beats **cold-start speed** (owner, 2026-10-02): an on-demand build of about a tenth of a second per change is accepted over a prebuilt bundle that can go stale.

## Owner rules

Propose nothing against these.

- Rive is the main film engine; 3D is deferred (2026-10-12). The paper-cut look is refined in Rive: hands are plain circles with no thumb, faces are a few marks, expressive but minimal (2026-10-12).
- Shade with colour, never black: a shadow shifts toward the light's complement and keeps or raises its saturation, a highlight leans to the light's hue; no black, grey or alpha-black shading (film skill CRAFT rule 12; 2026-10-12).
- The lab's chrome draws one focus ring, `--focus-ring`: no control falls back to the browser's own ring (the keys dialog's buttons, the composer, the command query; 2026-10-12).
- Every pass ends with a retro of the pass: what went wrong, and the tooling to add, change or remove so the next pass costs less work and fewer tokens (architecture-loop step 11; 2026-10-12).
- The lab is the one place files are shown; hand out its links, never box paths. Two links, always both: local `http://localhost:8229/…` and the phone's `http://100.92.183.50:8230/…` (Tailscale); no tunnel, no auth (2026-10-14, superseding the 2026-10-02 HTTPS address: the run moved to the laptop).
- The owner's asks offer choices first, with a recommendation; a text field is optional unless the answer cannot be a choice (2026-10-14).
- An ask is a guided review, not a pointer (owner, 2026-10-15: "with the ask … this one here is asking about the script, but where is the script? I'd have to go look for it. So instead, it should be … step one, read the script, then you have the questions … step two, here's the storyboard, step three, here's some renders, step four"). It replaces clicking around the lab to find what to review: each step shows the material itself in place (the script to read, the Board, the renders, the takes), then that step's questions, in the flow's order. An ask never sends the owner elsewhere to find what it asks about. An ask is **one page** (owner, 2026-10-15: "lets always make it one page no need for multiple pages"): the material and its questions in the flow's order down one scrolling page, never steps behind Send. A script in an ask is the Script reader itself, its sound playable and its delivery marks editable in place (owner, 2026-10-15: "this doesnt seem like the script editing component, where can we play the sound?").
- The lab's UI is Solid 2 with effect-machine machines; `@bible/ui` is a simple port of Base UI's parts, used, not grown into a library (2026-10-13).
- Every workspace's tests, and the root `bun run test`, finish in under 10 s wall; speed comes from concurrency, shared setup and `TestClock`, never from skips or longer timeouts (2026-10-14).
- Review, verify, render and critique agents run at effort max; a taste or art review goes to a fresh Opus at max, code review to counsel (Codex) (2026-10-13).
- The owner relies on the loop for naming and industry context: name views, tools and states by the conventions of film and animation review tools (`PRIOR_ARTS.md`), and say in the README what each term means (2026-10-02).
- Films are not checked, rendered, mixed or reviewed by the loop until the owner asks for scene review (2026-10-01).
- A recorded take is never overwritten; ElevenLabs stages only (owner voiceover).
- Art direction beats performance: a change that moves pixels needs the owner's OK and stills identical to the eye (2026-09-27).
- Paid runs (ElevenLabs voice, score, sounds) only with the owner's go-ahead; budgets are generous once approved.
- Commit to `main` locally; publish and deploy only on the owner's ask, handed to him as a `!` command. The owner's lab on 8229 may be restarted to pick up merged server code (the Live check's Stop line).
- Push: `main` to `origin` after each commit, fast-forward only; never a rift branch, never force; the orchestrator pushes, batch agents do not (owner, 2026-10-03).
- No persisted-format change unless additive and optional: notes files, `catalogue.json`, timings, `library.lock.json`, `review.json`.
- On a Sabbath pause (Friday sundown to Saturday sundown) stop every background agent, workflow and loop.
