# Sabbath School — Q3 W2 (2026)

## The Message of the Cross

**Memory Verse:** "For the preaching of the cross is to them that perish foolishness; but unto us which are saved it is the power of God." (1 Corinthians 1:18)

_Week of July 4–10 · Read: 1 Cor. 1:17–31; Col. 1:20; 1 Pet. 2:24; Acts 13:16–47; 1 Cor. 2:1–5_

---

## Sabbath Afternoon — Introduction

- **Theme**: The cross Rome would not even name is the power of God unto salvation. Paul answers every Corinthian problem — division first (1 Cor 1:10–17) — by planting the cross in the middle of it.
- [DYK🔎] Cicero told the Romans not even to _think_ about the cross, so abhorrent was it as a means of death. Against that backdrop Paul glories in it.
- what the cross does:
  - reconciles: "having made peace through the blood of his cross, by him to reconcile all things unto himself" (Col 1:20)
  - cancels the debt: "Blotting out the handwriting of ordinances that was against us ... nailing it to his cross" (Col 2:14)
- "Christ died on the cross to save the world from perishing in sin. He asks your co-operation in this work. You are to be His helping hand." (Mar 100)
- [→] this week: the message (Sunday), its offense (Monday), its power (Tuesday), its Person (Wednesday), its wisdom (Thursday)

---

## Sunday — The Gospel of the Cross

- sent to preach, not to polish
  - "For Christ sent me not to baptize, but to preach the gospel: not with wisdom of words, lest the cross of Christ should be made of none effect." (1 Cor 1:17)
- human eloquence can _empty_ the cross of its power — the danger is not ignorance but polish
  - "He determined to avoid elaborate arguments and discussions, and 'not to know anything' among the Corinthians 'save Jesus Christ, and Him crucified.'" (AA 244.1)
- the cross is the center from which everything else radiates:
  - "The cross of Calvary challenges, and will finally vanquish every earthly and hellish power. In the cross all influence centers, and from it all influence goes forth." (LHU 230)
  - "Christ on the cross was the medium whereby mercy and truth met together, and righteousness and peace kissed each other. This is the means that is to move the world." (LHU 230)

[Q] **Is Paul condemning learning and eloquence as such?**
→ No — he rejects the wisdom that competes with God. "That your faith should not stand in the wisdom of men, but in the power of God." (1 Cor 2:5)

---

## Monday — Foolishness to Those Who Are Perishing

- the world's verdict on the gospel — folly, six times over in 1 Cor 1:18–31
  - "Where [is] the wise? where [is] the scribe? where [is] the disputer of this world? hath not God made foolish the wisdom of this world?" (1 Cor 1:20)
  - "But we preach Christ crucified, unto the Jews a stumblingblock, and unto the Greeks foolishness" (1 Cor 1:23)
- [DYK🔎] the word rendered "foolishness" (_mōria_) appears in the New Testament only in 1 Corinthians — auxiliary confirmation of what the text says plainly: "the natural man receiveth not the things of the Spirit of God: for they are foolishness unto him" (1 Cor 2:14)
- the Jews wanted a conquering Messiah, the Greeks a philosopher's system — a crucified Lord offended both (1 Cor 1:22)
- yet the "foolish" message is God's chosen instrument:
  - "it pleased God by the foolishness of preaching to save them that believe" (1 Cor 1:21)

[Q] **If the educated reject it, why keep preaching to them?**
→ "Many of the greatest scholars and statesmen, the world's most eminent men, will in these last days turn from the light because the world by wisdom knows not God. Yet God's servants are to improve every opportunity to communicate the truth to these men. Some will acknowledge their ignorance of the things of God and will take their place as humble learners at the feet of Jesus, the Master Teacher." (AA 241.3)

## Tuesday — Power to Those Who Are Being Saved

- what the cross accomplished for us:
  - "Who his own self bare our sins in his own body on the tree, that we, being dead to sins, should live unto righteousness: by whose stripes ye were healed." (1 Pet 2:24)
- destruction is self-caused; salvation is a granted gift
  - "For the wages of sin [is] death; but the gift of God [is] eternal life through Jesus Christ our Lord." (Rom 6:23)
- Paul proves his point from the prophets — Scripture its own expositor (Miller's Rule 5): 1 Cor 1:19 quotes Isaiah
  - "the wisdom of their wise [men] shall perish, and the understanding of their prudent [men] shall be hid." (Isa 29:14)
  - God will destroy that which is already destroying itself; He is never the author of the perishing
- the experience of "us which are saved":
  - "The power of the cross will move in you the mysterious springs of hope and fear, adoration, and love. ... You are only too glad to impart to others the gift you have received." (LHU 252)

[Q] **"Being saved" — present tense. Why does that matter?**
→ Salvation is not self-help; we _are being_ saved by a power outside ourselves, daily. The same cross that pardons also keeps (1 Cor 1:30).

---

## Wednesday — A Messiah Crucified

- everyone's expectations broke on the cross
  - "For the Jews require a sign, and the Greeks seek after wisdom" (1 Cor 1:22)
  - even the disciples recoiled from a crucified Messiah (Mark 8:31, 32; 9:30–32; 10:32–34)
- in Pisidian Antioch Paul preached the same cross (Acts 13):
  - "through this man is preached unto you the forgiveness of sins: And by him all that believe are justified from all things, from which ye could not be justified by the law of Moses." (Acts 13:38, 39)
  - "I have set thee to be a light of the Gentiles, that thou shouldest be for salvation unto the ends of the earth." (Acts 13:47)
- Paul knew by experience that the Crucified One lives — the Damascus road:
  - "Christ here identifies Himself with His people. In persecuting the followers of Jesus, Saul had struck directly against the Lord of heaven." (AA 116, 117)
- so his settled resolve at Corinth:
  - "For I determined not to know any thing among you, save Jesus Christ, and him crucified." (1 Cor 2:2)

[Q] **What does the cross say about God acting contrary to our expectations?**
→ Faith is governed by the evidence of the word, not by how we expected God to move (Miller's Rule 14). The cross is standing proof that God's unexpected way _is_ salvation — remember that when His providence surprises you.

## Thursday — Christ, the Power and Wisdom of God

- two wisdoms, mutually exclusive
  - "Because the foolishness of God is wiser than men; and the weakness of God is stronger than men." (1 Cor 1:25)
  - "But unto them which are called, both Jews and Greeks, Christ the power of God, and the wisdom of God." (1 Cor 1:24)
- God's choice of the weak silences all boasting
  - "not many wise men after the flesh, not many mighty, not many noble" (1 Cor 1:26)
  - "That no flesh should glory in his presence." (1 Cor 1:29)
- Christ is made everything to the believer
  - "But of him are ye in Christ Jesus, who of God is made unto us wisdom, and righteousness, and sanctification, and redemption" (1 Cor 1:30)
  - the Scriptures "are able to make thee wise unto salvation through faith which is in Christ Jesus." (2 Tim 3:15)
- the historical proof of that power:
  - "By the cooperation of the divine Spirit, the apostles did a work that shook the world. To every nation was the gospel carried in a single generation." (GAG 275)
  - "As Christ sent forth His disciples, so today He sends forth the members of His church. ... If they will make God their strength, He will work with them, and they shall not labor in vain." (GAG 275)

## Friday — Further Thought

- Read: Ellen G. White, "Calvary," pp. 741–757, in _The Desire of Ages_
- "But in Paul's day the cross was regarded with feelings of repulsion and horror. To uphold as the Saviour of mankind one who had met death on the cross, would naturally call forth ridicule and opposition." (AA 245.1)
- "But to Paul the cross was the one object of supreme interest. ... He knew by personal experience that when a sinner once beholds the love of the Father, as seen in the sacrifice of His Son, and yields to the divine influence, a change of heart takes place, and henceforth Christ is all and in all." (AA 245.3)
- [TANGENT] the preacher's charge:
  - "Let not your lips utter a sentence of doubt. ... Lift Him up, the Man of Calvary, higher and still higher. There is power in the exaltation of the cross of Christ." (TUL 58)

## Discussion Questions

1. In Gethsemane Jesus prayed, "O my Father, if it be possible, let this cup pass from me" (Matt 26:39). What does this prayer say about the immense price Jesus paid on the cross?
2. "The foolishness of God is wiser than men" (1 Cor 1:25). In what ways is the wisdom of God different from human wisdom?
3. The message of a crucified Christ was a stumbling block for the Jews and foolishness for the Greeks. What biblical truths that we preach today produce the same effect in modern audiences, and why?
4. "The natural man receiveth not the things of the Spirit of God" (1 Cor 2:14). How can we speak about Jesus in a way that touches such hearts — and when might our actions alone have to reach them?

---

## Appeal

- The cross is not one theme among many — it _is_ the power of God. Determine, this quarter, to know nothing else among your neighbors save Jesus Christ, and Him crucified.
- Let every doubt die where the Saviour died; lift Him up, higher and still higher.
- _"That, according as it is written, He that glorieth, let him glory in the Lord." (1 Corinthians 1:31)_
