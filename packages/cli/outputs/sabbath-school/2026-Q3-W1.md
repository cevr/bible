# Sabbath School — Q3 W1 (2026)

## Paul's Ministry in Corinth

**Memory Verse:** "Then spake the Lord to Paul in the night by a vision, Be not afraid, but speak, and hold not thy peace: For I am with thee, and no man shall set on thee to hurt thee: for I have much people in this city." (Acts 18:9, 10)

_Week of June 27 – July 3 · Read: 1 Cor. 1:1; Gal. 1:1; Acts 17:16–34; 1 Cor. 5:9–11; Acts 18:4–10; 2 Cor. 2:4_

---

## Sabbath Afternoon — Introduction

- **Theme**: God planted a church in the most corrupt city of Greece — and kept His apostle there by a promise. The answer to Corinth's problems (and ours) is not better arguments but the message of the Cross.
- "And because he was of the same craft, he abode with them, and wrought: for by their occupation they were tentmakers." (Acts 18:3)
  - Paul's trade paid the expenses; his real business was winning souls
  - "While they preached the gospel, Paul and his companion wrought at their trade as tentmakers, and in doing this they could give a more thorough knowledge of Christ to those who heard them." (TDG 203)
- [DYK🔎] Corinth commanded the isthmus between two seas — a harbor east (Cenchreae) and west (Lechaeum), crossroads of north–south and east–west trade, capital of Achaia
  - "An opportunity was thus presented for the spread of the gospel. Once established at Corinth, it would be readily communicated to all parts of the world." (LP 98.3)
- [→] this week: the man (Sunday), the move (Monday), the city (Tuesday), the promise (Wednesday), the letters (Thursday)

---

## Sunday — Paul, a God-Called Apostle of Jesus

- **identity precedes mission** — Paul opens almost every letter with who called him
  - "Paul, called [to be] an apostle of Jesus Christ through the will of God" (1 Cor 1:1)
  - "Paul, an apostle, (not of men, neither by man, but by Jesus Christ, and God the Father, who raised him from the dead;)" (Gal 1:1)
- **called before he was born**, like Jeremiah
  - "Before I formed thee in the belly I knew thee; and before thou camest forth out of the womb I sanctified thee, [and] I ordained thee a prophet unto the nations." (Jer 1:5)
- the calling flowed from a real encounter with the risen Christ (1 Cor 15:8–11) — evidence, not feeling
- the greatness of the work drove him deeper into the word:
  - "The greatness of the work before him led him to give much study to the Holy Scriptures, in order that he might preach the gospel 'not with wisdom of words, lest the cross of Christ should be made of none effect.'" (YRP 279)
- [→] "apostle" = one _sent_; not all are sent as apostles, but all are called to be faithful followers in whatever work the Lord appoints

[Q] **"I'm no apostle — does calling apply to me?"**
→ "Brethren, Christ calls you. Will you heed His voice? Will you become His messengers? Will you seek the sheep that are lost?" (TUL 91) — the call to be Christ's witness is as personal as Paul's, if not as public.

---

## Monday — From Athens to Corinth

- **Athens**: eloquence met with eloquence — little fruit
  - "his spirit was stirred in him, when he saw the city wholly given to idolatry" (Acts 17:16)
  - "And when they heard of the resurrection of the dead, some mocked" (Acts 17:32) — yet some believed (Acts 17:34)
- **Corinth**: a deliberate change of method
  - "In preaching the gospel in Corinth, the apostle followed a course different from that which had marked his labors at Athens. ... He determined to avoid elaborate arguments and discussions, and 'not to know anything' among the Corinthians 'save Jesus Christ, and Him crucified.'" (AA 244.1)
- **synagogue first** — "he reasoned in the synagogue every sabbath, and persuaded the Jews and the Greeks" (Acts 18:4)
  - to the Jew first: "I am not ashamed of the gospel of Christ: for it is the power of God unto salvation to every one that believeth; to the Jew first, and also to the Greek." (Rom 1:16)
- and he stayed: "he continued [there] a year and six months, teaching the word of God among them." (Acts 18:11)
- [ILL] watch Paul let Scripture be its own expositor (Miller's Rule 5):
  - "Behold the apostle preaching in the synagogue at Corinth, reasoning from the writings of Moses and the prophets, and bringing his hearers down to the advent of the promised Messiah. Listen as he makes plain the work of the Redeemer as the great high priest of mankind—the One who through the sacrifice of His own life was to make atonement for sin once for all, and was then to take up His ministry in the heavenly sanctuary." (AA 246.2)

[Q] **Why did plain preaching succeed where philosophy failed?**
→ "And my speech and my preaching [was] not with enticing words of man's wisdom, but in demonstration of the Spirit and of power" (1 Cor 2:4) — the power is in the word preached, not the orator.

## Tuesday — The City of Corinth

- prosperous, strategic, young, pluralistic — and rotten
  - "The city was almost wholly given up to idolatry. Venus was the favorite goddess; and a great number of dissolute women were employed in connection with the worship of this reigning deity. ... The Corinthians had become conspicuous, even among the heathen, for their gross immorality." (LP 99.1)
- [DYK🔎] Rome razed Corinth in 146 B.C.; Julius Caesar refounded it as a Roman colony in 44 B.C. — the New Testament Corinth was barely a century old, open to every new idea and every old vice
- tentmaking kept the apostle independent and credible (Acts 18:2, 3)
  - "The history of the apostle Paul is a constant testimony that manual labor cannot be degrading, that it is not inconsistent with true greatness and elevation of human or Christian character." (CC 342)
- the church must live _in_ the world without going _out_ of it
  - "Yet not altogether with the fornicators of this world, or with the covetous, or extortioners, or with idolaters; for then must ye needs go out of the world." (1 Cor 5:10)
- [→] God deliberately chose the hard, strategic field: where sin abounded, grace was to abound — and travel there was gospel infrastructure

[Q] **Is our city really worse than Corinth?**
→ Hardly. Idolatry and immorality were "part of daily life" there — yet God said, "I have much people in this city." No field is too dark for the seed.

## Wednesday — "Many in This City"

- mixed results: opposition and blasphemy — then breakthrough
  - "And when they opposed themselves, and blasphemed, he shook [his] raiment ... from henceforth I will go unto the Gentiles." (Acts 18:6)
  - "And Crispus, the chief ruler of the synagogue, believed on the Lord with all his house; and many of the Corinthians hearing believed, and were baptized." (Acts 18:8)
  - God's word never returns void: "it shall accomplish that which I please, and it shall prosper [in the thing] whereto I sent it." (Isa 55:11)
- Paul at his lowest — "And I was with you in weakness, and in fear, and in much trembling." (1 Cor 2:3)
  - "He doubted the wisdom of building up a church from the material he found there. He considered Corinth a very questionable field of labor, and determined to leave it." (LP 106.3)
- the night vision — the memory verse (Acts 18:9, 10)
  - "Paul understood this to be a command to remain in Corinth, and a guarantee that the Lord would give increase to the seed sown. Strengthened and encouraged, he continued to labor there with great zeal and perseverance for one year and six months. A large church was enrolled under the banner of Jesus Christ." (LP 107.1)
  - the same voice that said, "Fear thou not; for I [am] with thee: be not dismayed; for I [am] thy God: I will strengthen thee; yea, I will help thee" (Isa 41:10)
- [→] "I have much people in this city" — God counts His people before they are converted; faith reads the promise as evidence (Miller's Rule 14)

[Q] **When do we push through, and when do we move on?**
→ Paul left Athens and stayed in Corinth. The difference was not the difficulty of the field but the word of the Lord. Direction comes from God's word and providence — never from discouragement.

## Thursday — Paul's Letters to the Corinthians

- Chloe's report (1 Cor 1:11) and the church's own letter (1 Cor 7:1) drew out 1 Corinthians
  - "there are contentions among you. ... every one of you saith, I am of Paul; and I of Apollos; and I of Cephas; and I of Christ." (1 Cor 1:11, 12)
  - "Is Christ divided? was Paul crucified for you? or were ye baptized in the name of Paul?" (1 Cor 1:13)
- written with tears, received as the word of God
  - "For out of much affliction and anguish of heart I wrote unto you with many tears; not that ye should be grieved, but that ye might know the love which I have more abundantly unto you." (2 Cor 2:4)
  - "the apostle wrote to the church at Corinth one of the richest, most instructive, most powerful of all his letters." (AA 300.4)
  - "The absence of Paul from the churches of his care was partially supplied by communications weighty and powerful, which were received generally as the word of God." (LP 109.1)
- the letters' burden: Christ's power to keep His people from evil
  - "In this letter to the Corinthians Paul endeavored to show them Christ's power to keep them from evil. ... 'Ye are Christ's,' he declared. 'Ye are not your own. . . . Ye are bought with a price: therefore glorify God in your body, and in your spirit, which are God's.'" (AA 306, 307)
- [TANGENT] Paul likely wrote three or four letters to Corinth: an earlier lost letter (1 Cor 5:9), 1 Corinthians, the "severe letter" (2 Cor 2:3, 4, 9; 7:8), then 2 Corinthians — a shepherd who would not let go

## Friday — Further Thought

- Read: Ellen G. White, "Corinth," pp. 243–254, in _The Acts of the Apostles_
- "Faithfully he warned them of their dangers and reproved them for their sins. He pointed them again to Christ and sought to kindle anew the fervor of their early devotion." (AA 301.1)
- "The Corinthian believers needed a deeper experience in the things of God. ... Paul's desire for them was that they might be filled with all the fullness of God, following on to know Him whose going forth is prepared as the morning." (AA 306, 307)
- [TANGENT] the lay-missionary pattern of Corinth is ours:
  - "Let little companies of workers go out as the Lord's missionaries, and do as Christ commissioned the first disciples to do. ... Tell the people the story of Creation, and how at the close of His work the Lord rested upon and blessed the Sabbath day, setting it apart as a memorial of His work." (TUL 91)

## Discussion Questions

1. Paul was convinced that he was an apostle of Jesus and that his calling was from God. Why is it so important to know who we are and what our call is?
2. For a moment, Paul felt like giving up his work in Corinth and leaving the city. What caused him to change his mind? How can this help us when we feel like giving up on a missionary project — and might there be a time we should?
3. The church members in Corinth were significantly influenced by the surrounding culture. How can we be in the world (John 17:11, 15) and not be influenced by "the desires of the flesh and the desires of the eyes and pride of life" (1 John 2:16)? In what other ways is the surrounding culture negatively influencing the church?

## Appeal

- The Lord still says of every hard place: "I have much people in this city" (Acts 18:10). He does not ask you to guarantee the harvest — He asks you to speak, and hold not your peace.
- Stay at your post until the Word releases you.
- _"Fear thou not; for I [am] with thee: be not dismayed; for I [am] thy God: I will strengthen thee; yea, I will help thee; yea, I will uphold thee with the right hand of my righteousness." (Isaiah 41:10)_
