# What is Truth? — Series Review: Executive Summary

## The series, in one read

The three-night arc holds together as a genuinely skeptic-credible case: Night 1 builds a falsifiable rubric for truth, Night 2 stress-tests Scripture against that rubric using self-naming prophecy, and Night 3 turns the rubric on the problem of evil — a clean criteria→wager→verdict spine. The series is strongest where it concedes ground and lets the text self-identify (Night 1's neutral anchors and steelman of relativism, Night 2's Daniel 8/9 self-naming chain, Night 3's courtroom framing that leads with Epicurus). It is weakest at its persuasive peaks, where load-bearing claims overreach what the on-slide text earns and the honest concessions hide in speaker notes — most acutely across Night 2's Revelation 9 / Ottoman block (96–107), the deck's single riskiest stretch. The recurring failure mode is uniform across all three nights: maximal claim on screen, honesty in the notes, handing a phone-armed skeptic a clean kill shot at exactly the moment trust matters most.

## Top must-fix items across all three nights

1. **[N2] Slide 100** — Litch date self-contradicts (slide says _Signs of the Times_ Mar 20, 1840; notes say Aug 1) on the one citation that makes the prophecy non-fakeable; cite a verifiable pre-event print date or pull the exact-day claim. Series-ending fact-check.
2. **[N2] Slides 96–107** — the entire Revelation 9 / Ottoman block is the weakest stretch by unanimous verdict (contested 1299 start, "the same key" method overreach, exact-date fulfillment, uncorroborated "1,000 skeptics"); de-risk to its defensible core or cut.
3. **[N1] Slide 14 (+25, 15)** — the relativism refutation attacks a strawman ("assumes humans have no capacity for evil"); reframe to "relativism gives no _objective_ ground to call anything evil" — this is the conceptual spine of Night 1.
4. **[N1] Slides 46, 51–54** — absolute Glueck archaeology quote plus four slides staking credibility on a contested Mount Sinai site, right before the "reliable track record" payoff; soften to "archaeology keeps confirming named people and places," compress, never assert a disputed site.
5. **[N3] Slide 37** — Romans 3:4 as "THE CENTRAL VERSE" rests on a KJV rendering most modern translations reverse; in a "verify everything" series this thesis verse is the top fact-check exposure — demote or openly own the translation point.
6. **[N1] Slide 67** — Mark 16:15 is textually disputed (absent from earliest manuscripts) yet Night 1 stakes credibility on manuscript fidelity; swap to Matthew 28:19.
7. **[N2] Surface the notes-only concessions** (Isaiah dating /11, Daniel composition /48, 457-vs-458 /34, Tiberius reckoning /39, modern Tyre /12) onto the slides; every hidden concession reads as the presenter unaware or hiding it.
8. **[N3] Slides 84–85** — the closing scorecard's five ✓ and "You said yes" present the case as settled verdict and put words in skeptics' mouths at the moment of the ask; reframe as "here's the case, you judge."

## Cross-night coherence

The criteria→wager→verdict thread does pay off structurally: Night 1 sets five tests and frames a wager, Night 2 supplies the "test no human author can fake" (dated prophecy), and Night 3 delivers the benevolence verdict — each night explicitly cues the next. The arc's integrity, though, depends on threads that are currently _claimed but not delivered at the altitude promised_. Night 1's Slide 58 promises "exactly one test no human author can fake… with dates"; Night 2 cashes this with strong Daniel 9 material but stakes its boldest payoff on the self-contradicting Litch date (N2/100) — so the series' signature "non-fakeable" claim lands on its weakest evidence. Two bookend/verdict threads are set up but not cleanly landed: Night 3's Psalm 34:8 "bookend" (slide 88) misremembers its own opener (Jeremiah 29:11, slide 5), and the closing scorecard (N3/84) stamps Night-2 prophecy and tonight's case as settled ✓ when Night 2's own weakest block undercuts that finality. The fix is consistent across the seam: let the strong, fact-check-proof cases (Daniel 8 self-naming, Daniel 9, the scroll physically predating the cross) carry the verdict, and never let a disputed claim sit at a load-bearing payoff.

## What's working (keep)

- **Honesty discipline as a trust engine** — all three nights repeatedly distinguish "reliable" from "divine," name surface referents (Ezekiel/Tyre, Isaiah/Babylon), and steelman the opponent (relativism, Epicurus) before answering; this is the series' strongest persuasive asset.
- **Self-naming prophecy as the skeptic-proof backbone** — Daniel 8:20-21 (the text names its own symbols), the Daniel 2/7 empire chain, and the Daniel 9 / AD 27 timeline read as genuinely falsifiable evidence and survive fact-checking.
- **Neutral, verifiable anchors** — Merriam-Webster, the SI kilogram redefinition, manuscript counts, and the Great Isaiah Scroll's physical dating ground the case in evidence a skeptic can independently confirm.
- **The falsifiable, hypothesis-testing posture** — framing claims as "testable predictions" and binding the case to a stated rubric lowers defenses and keeps the series evidential rather than devotional.
- **Leading with the hardest punch** — opening Night 3 on Epicurus and converting the emotional problem of evil into an evidentiary courtroom case is exactly the move that earns a skeptic's attention.
