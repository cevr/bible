## C3 — The math

**TITLE:** DO THE MATH

**BODY:**
A prophetic day = a literal year. (The same key that put Messiah at A.D. 27.)
Add the four time-units of Revelation 9:15:

- **a year** → 360 years
- **a month** → 30 years
- **a day** → 1 year
- **an hour** → 1/24 of a year → 15 days

**Total: 391 years and 15 days.**

**ATTRIBUTION LINE:** Year-day principle; prophetic-year reckoning (360 days). Calculation published by Josiah Litch, _Prophetic Expositions_, vol. 2 (1838 / pub. 1842).

**PRESENTER NOTES:**

- Show the addition as a column. 360 + 30 + 1 = 391 years; the "hour" = one twenty-fourth of a 360-unit year = 15 days.
- Fitch states it cleanly (note, do not cite EGW-adjacent on slide is fine — Fitch is a named pioneer, but for a SKEPTIC keep the slide to the arithmetic and let Litch own the published calc): "One year, 360 days... one month, thirty days... one day, or year; and one hour, i.e. one twenty-fourth of a day or year, one twenty-fourth of 360 — fifteen days." (Charles Fitch, _Letter to Rev. J. Litch_, LJL 47.1)
- The "150 years" of the 5th trumpet (C4) is SEPARATE from the 391 — keep them distinct or the audience conflates them. C4 = start-the-clock period; C3 = the 6th-trumpet duration.

## C5 — Litch publishes (1838)

**TITLE:** HE NAMED IT IN ADVANCE

**BODY:**
In **1838**, Josiah Litch published his exposition of Revelation 9 and set the end of
Ottoman independence at **August 11, 1840** — two years out.
His own words on how he got the date:

> "When the foregoing calculation was made, it was purely a matter of

**ATTRIBUTION LINE:** Josiah Litch, _Prophetic Expositions_, vol. 2, p. 189 (1838 calculation, pub. 1842).

**PRESENTER NOTES:**

- "Purely a matter of calculation on the prophetic periods" is the quote that matters: Litch is insisting the date came from arithmetic, not from reading the news.
- Corroborating witnesses (note only): Loughborough, GSAM 129.2 — "In 1838 Dr. Josiah Litch... took the unqualified position that the sixth trumpet would cease to sound and the Ottoman power fall on the 11th day of August, 1840." Bates, AJB 257.5 — "in the year 1838, sent out his exposition... which would expire August 11, 1840." Smith, DAR 485.2 — "by Elder J. Litch in 1838, two years before the predicted event."
- ACCURACY — read carefully: critics (and at least one internal GC-library pamphlet review) argue Litch's 1838 piece named only "August 1840," and that the _exact day_ August 11 was sharpened later. The cleanest DEFENSIBLE primary fact a skeptic can verify: Litch put **August 11, 1840** in print on **August 1, 1840** in _Signs of the Times, and Expositor of Prophecy_ (this is the dated source EGW cites at GC 334.4–335.1). So: "month named two years early; day named in print before the event." Use that phrasing on stage and you are bulletproof. Do NOT claim "the exact day, two years early" — you cannot prove it and a hostile googler will surface the dispute.

**ATTRIBUTION LINE:** Convention of London, signed 15 July 1840 (Britain, Austria, Prussia, Russia, and the Ottoman Porte). Standard diplomatic history.

# ACCURACY & DEFENSIBILITY (build-room only — never on a slide)

This is the part that protects the whole series. The 1840 argument is the most attackable
in the deck because a curious skeptic can fact-check it on a phone in 90 seconds.

### What is SOLID (neutral sources, put weight here)

- **Convention of London, 15 July 1840**, four powers (Britain, Russia, Austria, Prussia) + the Porte, "for the Pacification of the Levant." Mainstream, uncontested. (Wikipedia _Convention of London (1840)_; Encyclopedia.com; U.S. FRUS 1879 d518.) → C6 is bulletproof.
- **The Oriental Crisis of 1840** is a real, named event; Mehemet Ali vs. the Sultan, four powers intervening, France sidelined. Mainstream. → context for C6–C8 is solid.
- **Rifat (Rifaat) Bey was the real Ottoman envoy** sent to Alexandria with the four powers' consuls to deliver the Convention's terms to Muhammad Ali. Confirmed in the neutral FRUS 1879 d518. → the _existence_ of C7's mission is solid.
- **Litch predicted "August 1840" two years in advance** and put **August 11, 1840** in print no later than **August 1, 1840** (_Signs of the Times, and Expositor of Prophecy_, Aug 1, 1840 — the source EGW cites). The fact of a pre-event printed prediction is real and checkable. → C5's core is solid IF you phrase it as month-two-years-out / day-before-the-event.

### What is CONTESTED (a skeptic can and will push back — pre-empt it)

1. **August 11 vs. August 19 (the delivery date).** The Adventist tradition (Loughborough, Smith, Litch) says the ultimatum was put in Muhammad Ali's hands on **August 11, 1840**. The neutral U.S. State Dept record (**FRUS 1879, d518**) says the written communication was made on **August 19, 1840**. Some popular accounts also tie Aug 11 to Commodore Napier's fleet appearing off Beirut, not to the ultimatum delivery. **This is the single biggest exposure in the series.** Mitigation: do not stake the argument on the calendar day. Stake it on (a) the printed pre-event prediction and (b) the event-TYPE (sovereignty passing to the four powers) confirmed by hostile witnesses. The day is "the date Litch reckoned to"; concede it's disputed if pressed.
2. **"Did Litch name the exact DAY before the event, or only the month?"** Critics — and reportedly at least one pamphlet in the General Conference's own library — argue Litch's 1838 piece said "August 1840," with **August 11** specified only as the date approached or after. The defensible primary fact is the Aug 1, 1840 _Signs of the Times_ printing of "the 11th of August, 1840" — which is BEFORE the event but is NOT "two years before." So: claim "two years early for the month, in print before the day for the day." Anyone who says "he named the exact day two years out" is overclaiming and will be caught.
3. **Modern historiography frames the real turning point months later.** To mainstream historians the crisis "resolved" with Acre's bombardment (3 Nov 1840) and Muhammad Ali's capitulation (**27 Nov 1840**), and the Ottoman Empire _survived_ (as a European-guaranteed dependency) until 1922. The pioneers don't actually deny survival — their claim is precisely that independence ended and the empire continued "by sufferance" of the powers. That sub-claim (loss of independent sovereignty, survival as a dependency) is genuinely consistent with the mainstream narrative; the _dramatic_ claim (a fall/destruction on Aug 11) is not, and the pioneers themselves had to retreat from it. Teach the narrow claim, never the dramatic one.
4. **July 27, 1299 / 1449 anchors** are a Gibbon-derived pioneer construction. Gibbon does date Othman's first raid to 27 July 1299, so the date is citable to a named historian — but modern Ottomanists treat Osman's emergence as approximate (c. 1299–1302) and don't hang anything on July 27. Present 1299 as "the start-date Litch used, after Gibbon," not as settled fact.

### Bottom-line recommendation

KEEP the 1840 capstone — it is the series' best "pre-registered, publicly recorded" prediction and the
hostile-witness (Balch) slide is rhetorically unbeatable. But ship it in its DEFENSIBLE form:

- Claim: **month named two years early; day in print before the event; event-type (sovereignty surrendered to four Christian powers) exactly as predicted; confirmed by a friendly eyewitness (Goodell) AND a hostile critic (Balch).**
- Never claim: "exact day named two years early," "the Ottoman Empire fell/was destroyed on Aug 11," or "August 11 is the undisputed historical date." All three are refutable and would hand a skeptic the win.
