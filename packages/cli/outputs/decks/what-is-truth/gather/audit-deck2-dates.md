## 1. Decree dates (Cyrus 538/537, Darius ~520, Artaxerxes 457)

**VERDICT: REPLACE deck's "537" with 538 BC for Cyrus; KEEP 457 BC (strong).**

- **Cyrus decree.** Scholarly consensus dates Cyrus's capture of Babylon to 12 October
  539 BC, and his decree releasing the exiles (Ezra 1:1-4) to his "first year" as ruler
  of Babylon = **538 BC**. The deck says 537; that is defensible only on a fall-reckoning
  technicality and looks like an error to a skeptic checking a reference book. Use **538 BC**.
  - On-slide: _"Cyrus's decree, 538 BC (Ezra 1:1-4) — fulfilling Isaiah 44:28."_
- **Darius reaffirmation ~520 BC.** Darius I (not Darius II) reaffirmed the temple-rebuilding
  decree in his second year = **520 BC** (Ezra 6:1-12; Haggai/Zechariah date the resumed
  work to 520). Note: the deck/task said "Darius II" — that is wrong; the reaffirming king
  is **Darius I (the Great)**. Drop the "II."
- **Artaxerxes I 7th year = 457 BC (the key date).** This is the strongest of the three.
  - Ezra 7:7-9: Ezra went up "in the seventh year of Artaxerxes the king," departing Babylon
    Nisan 1 and arriving Jerusalem Ab 1.
  - Artaxerxes I acceded 465 BC (after Xerxes' murder). By Jewish fall-to-fall reckoning his
    7th year ran fall 458 – fall 457 BC; the spring Nisan departure therefore lands in spring
    **457 BC**. Standard secular reference works often print 458 (spring-to-spring Persian
    reckoning); the 457 case rests on the Jewish autumn calendar.
  - **Support to cite:** the **Elephantine papyri** (Aramaic legal documents of a Jewish
    military colony at Elephantine, Egypt, double-dated by the Egyptian and Jewish calendars,
    establishing Jewish fall-to-fall regnal reckoning in the Persian period); **Nehemiah's own
    double reference** to "the twentieth year" across Kislev and the following Nisan (Neh 1:1;
    2:1) confirms a non-Persian (Tishri-to-Tishri) count; and the chronological work of
    **S. H. Horn & L. H. Wood** building on **Ptolemy's Canon** (astronomically anchored,
    error-free where checkable). Historian **A. T. Olmstead** (_History of the Persian Empire_,
    Univ. of Chicago, 1948) is the standard secular anchor for the Artaxerxes accession.
- **Sources:** Ministry Magazine, "Research: The Seventh Year of Artaxerxes I" (1953);
  Siegfried H. Horn, "The Basic Date of the 2300-Year Period…" (1953); Associates for Biblical
  Research, "Did Ezra Come to Jerusalem in 457 BC?"; Olmstead, _History of the Persian Empire_.
- **Speaker note:** Be honest that mainstream reference books frequently say 458; the 457 date
  is a _defended_ date resting on the Jewish calendar + Elephantine, not a bare consensus. Frame
  it as "the year the decree actually went into effect on the ground."

## 2. Tiberius co-regency → AD 27 baptism

**VERDICT: KEEP, but reframe honestly. AD 27 is defensible, not the only reading.**

- Deck claim: Tiberius "began his reign in AD 12 as co-regent," so 15th year (Luke 3:1) = AD 27.
- **Primary-source basis:** **Velleius Paterculus 2.121** (a contemporary, writing c. AD 30)
  records Tiberius's increased powers around the time of his Pannonian/Dalmatian triumph; the
  Fasti Praenestini inscription fixes that triumph to **23 October AD 12**. **Suetonius,
  _Tiberius_ 20-21** records that after the triumph a law was passed that Tiberius "should
  govern the provinces jointly with Augustus and hold the census with him" — a provincial
  co-imperium. Counting that co-regency (AD 12/13) by inclusive reckoning yields the **15th
  year = AD 27**, which is where Tertullian and the early church placed the start of the
  ministry, and it dovetails with an AD 30 crucifixion.
- **Honest alternative:** the standard/critical count begins from Tiberius's _sole_ accession
  on Augustus's death, **19 August AD 14**, giving a 15th year of **AD 28/29**.
- **Strongest defensible framing for AD 27:** "Luke wrote for the provinces, and in the
  provinces Tiberius already exercised co-imperium from AD 12-13 (Velleius Paterculus 2.121;
  Suetonius, _Tiberius_ 21). Counted from there, Luke's 'fifteenth year' is AD 27 — and notably,
  every reckoning (AD 26, 27, or 29) still places Jesus's baptism in the same general window
  that the broader chronology requires."
- **Sources:** Tyndale Bulletin, "Reckoning Tiberius's Reign and Jesus's Baptism…" (Luke 3:1);
  Associates for Biblical Research, "What was the 'Fifteenth Year of Tiberius'?" and "The First
  Regnal Year of Tiberius as Indicated on Roman Coins."
- **Speaker note (red-flag honesty):** Do NOT present AD 27 as undisputed. Present it as the
  _best-supported_ date once Luke's provincial vantage point is granted, and name the AD 14
  alternative out loud — a skeptic who knows it will pounce if you hide it.

## 3. Earliest Daniel manuscripts predate the fulfillments

**VERDICT: KEEP. "2nd century BC" is correct and conservative.**

- **4QDan-c (4Q114):** Dead Sea Scrolls copy preserving Daniel 10:5–11:12 (the final vision),
  paleographically dated by Frank M. Cross (Harvard, 1961) to the **late 2nd century BC (~125 BC)**.
  Radiocarbon gives a calibrated range with weight in 230–160 BCE — i.e., even on the most
  generous late reading the physical copy is 2nd c. BC.
- **Greek (LXX/OG) Daniel:** translated in the **2nd century BC**, attesting the book's
  circulation in Greek before the Common Era.
- **Why it still predates the fulfillments:** AD 27 (baptism), AD 31 (cross), AD 70 (temple)
  are all 1st century AD. A manuscript physically copied ~125 BC predates every one of those by
  ~150-200 years — so whatever Daniel says about those events was demonstrably written _before_
  they happened, regardless of the authorship debate. (The honest limit: this defends Daniel's
  _Messianic/70-weeks_ and _Rome_ horizon, NOT the Maccabean-era events of Dan 11, which the
  copy postdates.)
- **On-slide:** _"Oldest surviving Daniel manuscript (4QDan-c, Dead Sea Scrolls): ~125 BC.
  Greek Daniel: 2nd century BC. Both predate the AD 27 / 31 / 70 events by ~150+ years."_
- **Sources:** Associates for Biblical Research, "New Light on the Book of Daniel from the
  Dead Sea Scrolls"; Bible Interp (Univ. of Arizona), "Redating the Dead Sea Scrolls and the
  Book of Daniel"; Frank M. Cross paleographic dating (1961).

- **Age:** sources split between **32 and 33**. Born 356 BC, died June 323 BC → age **32**

- **The objection (state it honestly):** mainstream critical scholarship assigns Isaiah 40-66 to

- **The trap to AVOID:** do NOT lean on "the Great Isaiah Scroll proves it." The Great Isaiah

  2. **Manuscript-bounded framing:** "Whatever the composition date, the Cyrus naming was fixed

     one of only a handful of cases where a deliverer is named generations ahead in the _claimed_

- **On-slide (recommended):** _"Isaiah names Cyrus — 'my shepherd… my anointed' (Isa 44:28-45:1)
  — and Cyrus's 538 BC decree freed the exiles to rebuild Jerusalem (Ezra 1:1-4). The text itself

## 10. The 70-weeks arithmetic (457 BC − 483 yrs, no year zero → AD 27)

**VERDICT: KEEP — the no-year-zero handling is correctly stated.**

- 69 weeks (7 + 62) of Daniel 9:25 = 69 × 7 = **483 prophetic years** to "Messiah the Prince."
- Start: 457 BC (Artaxerxes' decree, item 1). 457 BC + 483 years would be AD 27 — **and the
  no-year-zero adjustment is what makes it land exactly on AD 27, not AD 26.**
- **The arithmetic, stated correctly:** there is no year 0 between 1 BC and AD 1. So crossing the
  BC/AD boundary you lose a year. From 457 BC: 456 years brings you to 1 BC; the next year is
  AD 1 (not "year 0"); so 457 BC + 483 = **AD 27** (because 483 − 456 = 27, with the BC/AD jump
  consuming the missing zero). If you naively did 483 − 457 = 26 you'd get AD 26 — the
  no-year-zero correction _adds one_, giving **AD 27**. The deck's statement ("457 BC + 483 = AD 27
  because no year 0") is **correct**.
- Result: AD 27 = the baptism/anointing of Jesus ("Messiah" = anointed), tying item 10 back to
  item 2 (AD 27 baptism). Internally consistent.
- **On-slide:** _"457 BC + 483 years = AD 27 (no year 0 between 1 BC and AD 1). 'Unto Messiah
  the Prince… 69 weeks' (Daniel 9:25) → the anointing of Jesus, AD 27."_
- **Speaker note:** This is clean math; the only soft joints are upstream (457 itself, item 1;
  AD 27 vs AD 28/29, item 2). The no-year-zero step itself is airtight.
