# Two Falls — Defensibility Check

**Argument:** Satan was cast from heaven TWICE, proven from two separate text sets.

- **First fall** (pre-human rebellion): Ezek 28:15–16, Isa 14:12, Jude 6, Luke 10:18.
- **Second casting-down** (at/after the cross): Rev 12:7–12, anchored to "the blood of the Lamb" (12:11); John 12:31.

**Key claim under test:** Rev 12's "war in heaven" (vv. 7–9) is the SECOND event (post-cross), NOT the original pre-creation fall.

The two-fall _framework_ is safe — the first-fall texts are independent and uncontroversial as "Satan fell." All the weight is on dating Rev 12 to the cross. Defend THAT carefully.

---

## 1. The case FOR reading Rev 12 as post-cross

Lead with **the sequence of the chapter**, then **the explicit time-markers**. Don't argue from Rev 12 in isolation — argue from its internal order.

**A. The narrative order places the war AFTER the incarnation.**

- v.5 — the woman "brought forth a man child, who was to rule all nations with a rod of iron: and her child was caught up unto God, and to his throne." A male ruler born and then caught up to God's throne = Christ's birth and ascension (cf. Ps 2:9, "rod of iron"; the throne language = ascension/enthronement).
- v.6 — the woman "fled into the wilderness... a thousand two hundred and threescore days" = a post-ascension church era.
- **THEN** v.7 — "And there was war in heaven." The war is narrated _after_ the man-child is enthroned. A pre-creation reading has to make the chapter run backwards.

**B. The victory hymn dates itself to the cross.**

- v.10 — "Now is come salvation... and the power of his Christ: for the accuser of our brethren is cast down." The casting-down coincides with salvation arriving through Christ — not with creation.
- v.11 — "And they overcame him **by the blood of the Lamb**." A shed-blood victory presupposes Calvary. There was no slain Lamb's blood before the cross. This is the single hardest line for a primordial reading to absorb.

**C. The Gospel supplies the same "now" at the same moment.**

- John 12:31 (spoken days before the cross) — "**Now** is the judgment of this world: **now** shall the prince of this world be cast out." Present/imminent, tied to the passion — same verb-idea (cast out) and same timing as Rev 12.
- This independent witness keeps the claim from resting only on Revelation's symbolism.

**The one-sentence spine:** _Born → caught up to the throne → war → "blood of the Lamb" → "the devil is come down" — the chapter sequences the eviction after Calvary, and John 12:31 says the same thing in plain prose._

---

## 2. The main objections (be honest — these are real)

**(a) Recapitulation / telescoping.** Revelation works in symbolic cycles, not a single timeline. Rev 12 may sweep the WHOLE conflict (origin → cross → end) into one panel, so "the war" isn't a datable calendar event at all. _This is the strongest objection._ The dragon's tail casting "the third part of the stars" (v.4) sounds like the original angelic fall, sitting in the same vision as the man-child.

**(b) Majority of commentators read v.7–9 as primordial** or as the whole arc, precisely because v.9 names "that old serpent... the Devil, and Satan" — the same primeval enemy of Genesis 3. Many take the war as Satan's original loss, retold here.

**(c) "Cast down" language overlaps with the first-fall texts.** "Cast out into the earth" (v.9) echoes Isa 14:12 "fallen from heaven" and Luke 10:18 "fall from heaven." A skeptic says: you can't use the same falling-imagery to prove two _different_ events — you may be reading one event twice.

**(d) Does "blood of the Lamb" date the war, or just describe HOW believers win?** v.11's subject is "they" (the brethren), not the war itself. Grammatically it explains the _means of the saints' victory_, not necessarily the _timestamp of the heavenly battle_. A critic will say you've smuggled a date out of a sentence about Christian perseverance.

**(e) Luke 10:18 cuts against a strict pre-creation first fall.** Jesus says "I beheld Satan as lightning fall" in the present, watching the disciples cast out demons — so even your "first fall" proof-text is arguably about the _ongoing_ defeat of Satan, not the primeval one. (Don't lean on Luke 10:18 as your strongest first-fall text.)

---

## 3. How to hold the claim defensibly

**Reframe the second event from a geographic eviction to a loss of legal standing.** This survives every objection above:

- Don't claim Satan was physically re-located from heaven a second time. Claim that **at the cross he lost his STANDING as "the accuser of our brethren... which accused them before our God day and night"** (v.10). The cross stripped his courtroom access — he can no longer hold sin against the redeemed because the Lamb's blood answered the charge. That is what "cast down" means in v.10's own gloss: _the accuser is cast down_ — defined relationally, not geographically.
- This makes "blood of the Lamb" the natural cause: the accuser falls _because_ the price was paid. Objection (d) dissolves — the blood both arms the saints (v.11) AND is why the accuser has no case (v.10).
- It also disarms objection (c): the first fall was a fall of _holiness and place_; the second is a fall of _legal claim_. Same imagery, genuinely different events — not a doubled reading.

**Concede that Rev 12 is symbolic — then insist the symbols are SEQUENCED.** Grant objection (a)/(b): yes, the chapter is a compressed symbolic panel and yes it touches the whole conflict (v.4 may glance at the original fall). But the panel is _ordered_: man-child enthroned (v.5) before the accuser's fall (v.10). Recapitulation explains why both falls appear together; it does NOT erase the sequence the text itself lays down.

**Anchor the date OUTSIDE Revelation.** John 12:31 is your insurance. Plain narrative, no apocalyptic symbolism, spoken by Jesus, tied to the cross: "now shall the prince of this world be cast out." Whatever Rev 12's genre, the Gospel independently places a casting-out of "the prince of this world" at Calvary. Make this the load-bearing proof and let Rev 12 confirm it.

**Order of presentation that survives scrutiny:** (1) John 12:31 — Jesus dates a casting-out to the cross. (2) Rev 12:10–11 — the same event, named: the accuser cast down by the blood of the Lamb. (3) The sequence v.5→v.10 confirms it's post-ascension. (4) Only then mention the first-fall texts as the _other_, earlier event.

**What NOT to claim:** don't say "the original fall is NOT in Rev 12" (v.4 plausibly is). Say the _casting-down of v.10_ is the post-cross event. Don't over-rest on Luke 10:18.

---

## 4. One-line answers to likely challenges

- **"Rev 12 is just symbolic, not a dated event."** — Agreed it's symbolic; but it's _sequenced_ — the man-child reaches the throne (v.5) before the accuser is cast down (v.10). Symbols can still be in order.
- **"Most scholars read the war as the original fall."** — Then explain v.10: how is "salvation... and the power of his Christ" _now come_ at a pre-creation battle? The hymn dates itself to redemption.
- **"'Cast down' is the same language as Isaiah 14 — it's one fall, not two."** — Same falling-imagery, different fall: Isaiah loses his _place_; Rev 12:10 loses his _court case_ as accuser. The text glosses its own verb.
- **"'Blood of the Lamb' just describes how Christians overcome."** — It does — and that's the point: the accuser is cast down (v.10) _because_ the blood the saints plead has already answered every charge. The cause and the date are the same.
- **"There was no slain Lamb before creation, so how could a primordial war mention His blood?"** — Exactly — which is why this war can't be primordial. (Turn the objection into the proof.)
- **"John 12:31 is hyperbole / refers to the end, not the cross."** — Jesus says "_now_," days before His death, and ties it to "the judgment of this world." The nearest event is the cross.
- **"You're reading one fall twice to invent a second one."** — Two separate text sets, two separate functions: pre-cross texts describe a fall of _standing in heaven_; Rev 12:10 / John 12:31 describe loss of _accusing access_ at Calvary. Neither needs the other to stand.

---

**Bottom line for the speaker:** The two-fall _framework_ is solid; the first fall is uncontested. The whole defense rides on dating Rev 12:10's casting-down to the cross — defensible if you (1) lead with John 12:31, (2) read v.10 as loss of the accuser's legal standing rather than a second eviction, and (3) concede symbolism while holding the sequence. Avoid claiming the original fall is absent from Rev 12, and don't over-lean on Luke 10:18.
