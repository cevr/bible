## §1 — Recap / Criterion-5 (does it bear good fruit?)

**Jeremiah 29:11** — "For I know the thoughts that I think toward you, saith the LORD, thoughts of peace, and not of evil, to give you an expected end."
_Role:_ Establishes God's stated intent toward humanity as "peace, not evil" — the benevolence claim the rest of the deck must defend against the problem of suffering.

**Psalm 34:8** — "O taste and see that the LORD [is] good: blessed [is] the man [that] trusteth in him."
_Role:_ Criterion-5 framing — an empirical invitation ("taste and see"), not a demand for blind belief; sets the "test it yourself" tone that recurs in the §11 appeal.

## §2 — The Question (the Book itself asks it)

**Job 30:26** — "When I looked for good, then evil came [unto me]: and when I waited for light, there came darkness."
_Role:_ A righteous sufferer voices the raw expectation-vs-reality of undeserved pain — the skeptic's complaint placed in Scripture's own mouth.

**Ecclesiastes 4:1** — "So I returned, and considered all the oppressions that are done under the sun: and behold the tears of [such as were] oppressed, and they had no comforter; and on the side of their oppressors [there was] power; but they had no comforter."
_Role:_ Names systemic injustice and the absence of a comforter — the Bible naming the problem of evil without flinching.

**Habakkuk 1:2-3** — "(2) O LORD, how long shall I cry, and thou wilt not hear! [even] cry out unto thee [of] violence, and thou wilt not save! (3) Why dost thou shew me iniquity, and cause [me] to behold grievance? for spoiling and violence [are] before me: and there are [that] raise up strife and contention."
_Role:_ The prophet directly interrogates God ("why?", "how long?") — proof the Book doesn't dodge the question; it asks it first. Key reassurance for skeptics.

## §3 — God Is Love / Freedom

**1 John 4:8** — "He that loveth not knoweth not God; for God is love."
_Role:_ The definitional axiom — God's character IS love; everything downstream (freedom, the cross, the delay) must be consistent with this.

**1 John 4:16** — "And we have known and believed the love that God hath to us. God is love; and he that dwelleth in love dwelleth in God, and God in him."
_Role:_ Repeats and personalizes the axiom — love is not just God's attribute but the relationship He offers.

**Genesis 1:26-27** — "(26) And God said, Let us make man in our image, after our likeness: and let them have dominion over the fish of the sea, and over the fowl of the air, and over the cattle, and over all the earth, and over every creeping thing that creepeth upon the earth. (27) So God created man in his [own] image, in the image of God created he him; male and female created he them."
_Role:_ Humans made "in the image of God" — moral agents, not automatons; grounds the capacity for genuine choice that love requires.

**Deuteronomy 30:19** — "I call heaven and earth to record this day against you, [that] I have set before you life and death, blessing and cursing: therefore choose life, that both thou and thy seed may live:"
_Role:_ God explicitly sets a real choice before humans — love requires freedom, and freedom requires the genuine option to refuse.

**Joshua 24:15** — "And if it seem evil unto you to serve the LORD, choose you this day whom ye will serve... but as for me and my house, we will serve the LORD."
_Role:_ Models freedom in practice — "choose you" — God invites allegiance, never coerces it.

**Isaiah 1:18** — "Come now, and let us reason together, saith the LORD: though your sins be as scarlet, they shall be as white as snow; though they be red like crimson, they shall be as wool."
_Role:_ "Let us reason together" — God appeals to the mind, not to fear; the deck's invitation to a thinking, skeptical audience. (Reused in §11.)

## §4 — Lucifer / The Accusation

**Ezekiel 28:12-17** — "(12) Son of man, take up a lamentation upon the king of Tyrus... Thou sealest up the sum, full of wisdom, and perfect in beauty. (13) Thou hast been in Eden the garden of God; every precious stone [was] thy covering... (14) Thou [art] the anointed cherub that covereth; and I have set thee [so]: thou wast upon the holy mountain of God... (15) Thou [wast] perfect in thy ways from the day that thou wast created, till iniquity was found in thee. (16) By the multitude of thy merchandise they have filled the midst of thee with violence, and thou hast sinned: therefore I will cast thee as profane out of the mountain of God... (17) Thine heart was lifted up because of thy beauty, thou hast corrupted thy wisdom by reason of thy brightness: I will cast thee to the ground..."
_Role:_ The "perfect till iniquity was found in thee" portrait — evil originates in a created, perfect being who self-corrupts; introduces the origin of the rebellion. **⚠ FLAG (address in speaker notes):** the surface text addresses the "king of Tyrus"; the application to Lucifer is the traditional dual-fulfillment reading, NOT explicit in the verse. Frame as interpretation, not as the verse "saying" Lucifer.

**Isaiah 14:12-14** — "(12) How art thou fallen from heaven, O Lucifer, son of the morning!... (13) For thou hast said in thine heart, I will ascend into heaven, I will exalt my throne above the stars of God... (14) I will ascend above the heights of the clouds; I will be like the most High."
_Role:_ The five "I will"s — the self-exalting motive behind the rebellion. **⚠ FLAG (address in speaker notes):** context is a taunt against the "king of Babylon" (Isa 14:4); "Lucifer" = KJV rendering of Hebrew _helel_ (morning star). The Satan application is traditional dual-fulfillment, not explicit.

**Revelation 12:7-9** — "(7) And there was war in heaven: Michael and his angels fought against the dragon; and the dragon fought and his angels, (8) And prevailed not; neither was their place found any more in heaven. (9) And the great dragon was cast out, that old serpent, called the Devil, and Satan, which deceiveth the whole world: he was cast out into the earth, and his angels were cast out with him."
_Role:_ The clearest, explicit NT statement of the war in heaven and the casting-out — solid ground (no dual-fulfillment caveat needed); this verse names "the Devil, and Satan" directly.

**Revelation 12:10** — "And I heard a loud voice saying in heaven, Now is come salvation, and strength, and the kingdom of our God, and the power of his Christ: for the accuser of our brethren is cast down, which accused them before our God day and night."
_Role:_ Names the core charge: Satan is "the accuser" — the controversy is a courtroom/accusation, not merely a power struggle. Pivots the deck toward the "accusation" theme.

**Job 1:6-12** — "(6) Now there was a day when the sons of God came to present themselves before the LORD, and Satan came also among them. (7)... From going to and fro in the earth... (8) ...Hast thou considered my servant Job... a perfect and an upright man... (9) ...Doth Job fear God for nought? (10) Hast not thou made an hedge about him...? (11) But put forth thine hand now, and touch all that he hath, and he will curse thee to thy face. (12) ...Behold, all that he hath [is] in thy power; only upon himself put not forth thine hand..."
_Role:_ Shows the accusation in action — Satan charges that loyalty to God is bought ("for nought?"); God's character is on trial before a watching universe.

**Job 2:1-7** — "(1) Again there was a day when the sons of God came to present themselves before the LORD, and Satan came also among them... (3) ...still he holdeth fast his integrity, although thou movedst me against him, to destroy him without cause. (4)... Skin for skin... (5) But put forth thine hand now, and touch his bone and his flesh... (6) ...Behold, he [is] in thine hand; but save his life. (7) So went Satan forth... and smote Job with sore boils..."
_Role:_ Escalates the accusation and shows WHO does the smiting — Satan, not God ("Satan... smote Job"). Critical for the §7 theodicy: God permits, the accuser afflicts.

**John 8:44** — "Ye are of [your] father the devil... He was a murderer from the beginning, and abode not in the truth, because there is no truth in him. When he speaketh a lie, he speaketh of his own: for he is a liar, and the father of it."
_Role:_ Jesus' own verdict — the devil is "a liar from the beginning"; the rebellion's weapon is deception, not force. Anchors the accusation theme in Christ's words.

**Zechariah 3:1-2** — "(1) And he shewed me Joshua the high priest standing before the angel of the LORD, and Satan standing at his right hand to resist him. (2) And the LORD said unto Satan, The LORD rebuke thee, O Satan... [is] not this a brand plucked out of the fire?"
_Role:_ A second courtroom scene — Satan "to resist" / accuse a believer; God answers the accusation with rebuke and mercy. Reinforces the accuser motif from a different book.

## §5 — Why Not Just Destroy the Rebel?

**1 Corinthians 4:9** — "For I think that God hath set forth us the apostles last, as it were appointed to death: for we are made a spectacle unto the world, and to angels, and to men."
_Role:_ The "spectacle... unto angels" — establishes that the universe (angels included) is a watching audience; the controversy is public, which is why a summary execution wouldn't settle it.

**Ephesians 3:10** — "To the intent that now unto the principalities and powers in heavenly [places] might be known by the church the manifold wisdom of God,"
_Role:_ God's wisdom is being demonstrated TO the heavenly "principalities and powers" through events on earth — the cosmic-audience rationale for the long delay rather than instant destruction.

**Job 1:8-12** — "(8) ...Hast thou considered my servant Job... (9) ...Doth Job fear God for nought? (10) Hast not thou made an hedge about him...? (11) But put forth thine hand... and he will curse thee to thy face. (12) ...Behold, all that he hath [is] in thy power; only upon himself put not forth thine hand..."
_Role:_ The "wager/permission pattern" — God lets the accusation be tested rather than silenced by force; the only way to disprove "they only serve you because it pays" is to let the hedge down. (Overlaps §4; here it carries the "why not destroy" logic.)

**Romans 3:4** — "God forbid: yea, let God be true, but every man a liar; as it is written, That thou mightest be justified in thy sayings, and mightest overcome when thou art judged."
_Role:_ The thesis verse for the whole deck — God consents to BE JUDGED ("overcome when thou art judged") and to be vindicated by evidence, not by fiat. This is why He permits the trial instead of ending it.

## §6 — Earth Falls

**Genesis 2:16-17** — "(16) And the LORD God commanded the man, saying, Of every tree of the garden thou mayest freely eat: (17) But of the tree of the knowledge of good and evil, thou shalt not eat of it: for in the day that thou eatest thereof thou shalt surely die."
_Role:_ The single prohibition amid total freedom ("freely eat... but") — one real test, with the stakes stated plainly. Freedom from §3 made concrete.

**Genesis 3:1-6** — "(1) Now the serpent was more subtil than any beast... Yea, hath God said...? (2-3)... (4) And the serpent said unto the woman, Ye shall not surely die: (5) For God doth know that in the day ye eat thereof... ye shall be as gods, knowing good and evil. (6) And when the woman saw that the tree [was] good for food... she took of the fruit thereof, and did eat, and gave also unto her husband with her; and he did eat."
_Role:_ The mechanism of the fall — the lie ("ye shall not surely die") contradicts God directly, ties the §4 deceiver into earth's story; humanity chooses to believe the accuser.

**Genesis 3:17-19** — "(17) ...cursed [is] the ground for thy sake; in sorrow shalt thou eat [of] it all the days of thy life; (18) Thorns also and thistles shall it bring forth to thee... (19) In the sweat of thy face shalt thou eat bread, till thou return unto the ground... for dust thou [art], and unto dust shalt thou return."
_Role:_ The consequence — a cursed creation, toil, and death enter; explains the broken world the skeptic actually lives in.

**Romans 5:12** — "Wherefore, as by one man sin entered into the world, and death by sin; and so death passed upon all men, for that all have sinned:"
_Role:_ The NT diagnosis — sin and death entered through one man and spread to all; connects Eden to the universal human condition.

**Romans 6:23** — "For the wages of sin [is] death; but the gift of God [is] eternal life through Jesus Christ our Lord."
_Role:_ States the law of the system (sin earns death) AND previews the rescue ("but the gift") — bridges the fall to the cross.

**Luke 4:5-6** — "(5) And the devil, taking him up into an high mountain, shewed unto him all the kingdoms of the world in a moment of time. (6) And the devil said unto him, All this power will I give thee, and the glory of them: for that is delivered unto me; and to whomsoever I will I give it."
_Role:_ Establishes Satan's claim to rulership of the world ("delivered unto me") — and Jesus does not dispute the claim — explaining why the world is under hostile dominion.

**John 12:31** — "Now is the judgment of this world: now shall the prince of this world be cast out."
_Role:_ Jesus names Satan "the prince of this world" — confirming the usurped dominion of Luke 4; sets up the cross as the moment of his casting-out (§8).

**2 Corinthians 4:4** — "In whom the god of this world hath blinded the minds of them which believe not, lest the light of the glorious gospel of Christ... should shine unto them."
_Role:_ "The god of this world" blinds minds — explains why the truth is resisted; reframes skepticism itself as partly the product of an active deceiver, gently.

## §7 — Suffering Explained

**Galatians 6:7-8** — "(7) Be not deceived; God is not mocked: for whatsoever a man soweth, that shall he also reap. (8) For he that soweth to his flesh shall of the flesh reap corruption; but he that soweth to the Spirit shall of the Spirit reap life everlasting."
_Role:_ The sowing/reaping principle — much suffering is consequence, built into a moral universe, not arbitrary cruelty from God.

**James 1:13-15** — "(13) Let no man say when he is tempted, I am tempted of God: for God cannot be tempted with evil, neither tempteth he any man: (14) But every man is tempted, when he is drawn away of his own lust, and enticed. (15) Then when lust hath conceived, it bringeth forth sin: and sin, when it is finished, bringeth forth death."
_Role:_ Explicitly exonerates God from being the source of evil/temptation — the chain runs lust → sin → death, internal to the human, not authored by God.

**Hosea 8:7** — "For they have sown the wind, and they shall reap the whirlwind: it hath no stalk: the bud shall yield no meal..."
_Role:_ Vivid restatement of reaping — choices compound; small wind, large whirlwind. Illustrates escalating consequence.

**Matthew 13:24-30** (esp. 28-29) — "(24)... The kingdom of heaven is likened unto a man which sowed good seed in his field: (25) But while men slept, his enemy came and sowed tares among the wheat... (27)... didst not thou sow good seed...? from whence then hath it tares? (28) He said unto them, An enemy hath done this. The servants said... Wilt thou then that we go and gather them up? (29) But he said, Nay; lest while ye gather up the tares, ye root up also the wheat with them. (30) Let both grow together until the harvest..."
_Role:_ The deck's central theodicy parable — "an enemy hath done this" (suffering has a hostile author, not God) AND "lest ye root up also the wheat" (why God delays judgment — premature destruction would harm the innocent). Directly answers "why doesn't God stop it now?"

**Job 1:21-22** — "(21) ...Naked came I out of my mother's womb, and naked shall I return thither: the LORD gave, and the LORD hath taken away; blessed be the name of the LORD. (22) In all this Job sinned not, nor charged God foolishly."
_Role:_ The sufferer's faith response — and a careful note: Job "blessed" God, but the narrative (Job 2:7, §4) showed Satan did the smiting. Use to model trust without misattributing the harm to God. **⚠ FLAG (handling note):** Job 1:21 attributes the taking to "the LORD" in Job's mouth; pair with Job 2:7 so the deck doesn't accidentally teach that God caused the calamity. The deck's theodicy depends on the §4 distinction.

**Isaiah 63:9** — "In all their affliction he was afflicted, and the angel of his presence saved them: in his love and in his pity he redeemed them; and he bare them, and carried them all the days of old."
_Role:_ God is not a distant spectator — "in all their affliction he was afflicted"; He suffers WITH His people. Counters the "absent/uncaring God" charge.

**John 11:35** — "Jesus wept."
_Role:_ The shortest, sharpest proof — God incarnate weeps at a graveside; He does not stand aloof from human grief.

**Hebrews 4:15** — "For we have not an high priest which cannot be touched with the feeling of our infirmities; but was in all points tempted like as [we are, yet] without sin."
_Role:_ God has FELT human suffering from the inside — qualifying Him to judge and to sympathize; the suffering God, not the armchair deity.

**Lamentations 3:33** — "For he doth not afflict willingly nor grieve the children of men."
_Role:_ The explicit divine disposition — affliction is not God's will or delight ("not... willingly"); seals the §7 case that God is not the eager author of pain.

## §8 — The Cross

**Romans 5:8** — "But God commendeth his love toward us, in that, while we were yet sinners, Christ died for us."
_Role:_ The love-axiom (§3) proven by action — love demonstrated "while we were yet sinners," before any merit; the cross as evidence, not sentiment.

**John 3:16-17** — "(16) For God so loved the world, that he gave his only begotten Son, that whosoever believeth in him should not perish, but have everlasting life. (17) For God sent not his Son into the world to condemn the world; but that the world through him might be saved."
_Role:_ The cost and the motive — "so loved... he gave"; and v.17 corrects the caricature of a condemning God: the mission is rescue, not condemnation.

**John 15:13** — "Greater love hath no man than this, that a man lay down his life for his friends."
_Role:_ Defines the ceiling of love — self-sacrifice — which the cross then exceeds (Christ died for enemies, per Rom 5:8). The qualitative measure of the cross.

**Colossians 2:14-15** — "(14) Blotting out the handwriting of ordinances that was against us... nailing it to his cross; (15) [And] having spoiled principalities and powers, he made a shew of them openly, triumphing over them in it."
_Role:_ The cross as cosmic-courtroom verdict — it "made a shew openly" of the accuser before the watching universe (ties back to §5's audience); the legal charge against humanity is cancelled.

**John 12:31-32** — "(31) Now is the judgment of this world: now shall the prince of this world be cast out. (32) And I, if I be lifted up from the earth, will draw all [men] unto me."
_Role:_ The cross is the turning point of the controversy — the accuser is "cast out" and Christ "draws all" — the verdict of §4-5 rendered at Calvary.

**1 John 3:8** — "He that committeth sin is of the devil; for the devil sinneth from the beginning. For this purpose the Son of God was manifested, that he might destroy the works of the devil."
_Role:_ States the mission explicitly — Christ came "to destroy the works of the devil"; the cross is the decisive blow against the §4 rebellion.

**Hebrews 2:14** — "Forasmuch then as the children are partakers of flesh and blood, he also himself likewise took part of the same; that through death he might destroy him that had the power of death, that is, the devil;"
_Role:_ Explains HOW the cross defeats the enemy — by Christ's own death, He disarms "him that had the power of death." The mechanism of victory.

**Isaiah 53:4-6** — "(4) Surely he hath borne our griefs, and carried our sorrows... (5) But he [was] wounded for our transgressions, [he was] bruised for our iniquities: the chastisement of our peace [was] upon him; and with his stripes we are healed. (6) All we like sheep have gone astray... and the LORD hath laid on him the iniquity of us all."
_Role:_ The substitution stated centuries in advance — He bore what we earned (links to Rom 6:23 wages); the OT prophecy that interprets the cross. Strong with a skeptic because it predates the event.

## §9 — Why So Long?

**2 Peter 3:9** — "The Lord is not slack concerning his promise, as some men count slackness; but is longsuffering to us-ward, not willing that any should perish, but that all should come to repentance."
_Role:_ The delay reframed as mercy, not absence or impotence — "not willing that any should perish"; every extra day is a rescue window.

**Matthew 24:14** — "And this gospel of the kingdom shall be preached in all the world for a witness unto all nations; and then shall the end come."
_Role:_ A stated purpose for the delay — the message must reach "all nations" first; the timeline is mission-driven, with a defined endpoint ("then shall the end come").

**Habakkuk 2:3** — "For the vision [is] yet for an appointed time, but at the end it shall speak, and not lie: though it tarry, wait for it; because it will surely come, it will not tarry."
_Role:_ Answers the §2 "how long?" — the delay is "for an appointed time," not indefinite; the promise "will surely come." Closes the loop opened by Habakkuk in §2.

**Revelation 6:10-11** — "(10) And they cried with a loud voice... How long, O Lord, holy and true, dost thou not judge and avenge our blood...? (11) And white robes were given unto every one of them; and it was said unto them, that they should rest yet for a little season, until their fellowservants also and their brethren... should be fulfilled."
_Role:_ Even the martyrs ask "how long?" — Scripture validates the impatience AND gives the reason for the wait (a number yet to be completed). The Book again owns the question (echoes §2).

## §10 — What's Coming

**John 14:1-3** — "(1) Let not your heart be troubled... (2) In my Father's house are many mansions... I go to prepare a place for you. (3) And if I go and prepare a place for you, I will come again, and receive you unto myself; that where I am, [there] ye may be also."
_Role:_ The promise of return in Christ's own words — personal ("I will come again... receive you"); the hope the deck has been building toward.

**Acts 1:9-11** — "(9)... he was taken up; and a cloud received him out of their sight. (10)... two men stood by them in white apparel; (11)... this same Jesus, which is taken up from you into heaven, shall so come in like manner as ye have seen him go into heaven."
_Role:_ The return will be literal and visible — "so come in like manner" — guarding against a merely spiritual/metaphorical reading; eyewitness-anchored.

**1 Thessalonians 4:16-17** — "(16) For the Lord himself shall descend from heaven with a shout... and the dead in Christ shall rise first: (17) Then we which are alive [and] remain shall be caught up together with them in the clouds, to meet the Lord in the air: and so shall we ever be with the Lord."
_Role:_ The mechanics of the return and resurrection — answers "what happens to those who already died?"; reunion, not loss.

**Revelation 20:11-12** — "(11) And I saw a great white throne... (12) And I saw the dead, small and great, stand before God; and the books were opened... and the dead were judged out of those things which were written in the books, according to their works."
_Role:_ The transparent judgment — "the books were opened"; evidence-based, public, "according to their works." Vindicates the §5 "let God be judged" thesis — the verdict is open to inspection.

**Nahum 1:9** — "What do ye imagine against the LORD? he will make an utter end: affliction shall not rise up the second time."
_Role:_ The permanence of the resolution — "affliction shall not rise up the second time"; evil is ended once, not on an eternal-recurrence loop. Answers "won't this just happen again?"

**Revelation 21:1-4** — "(1) And I saw a new heaven and a new earth... (2)... the holy city, new Jerusalem... (3)... the tabernacle of God [is] with men, and he will dwell with them... (4) And God shall wipe away all tears from their eyes; and there shall be no more death, neither sorrow, nor crying, neither shall there be any more pain..."
_Role:_ The resolution of §2's problem of evil — "no more death... sorrow... pain"; the tears of Ecclesiastes 4:1 personally wiped away. The payoff verse.

**Revelation 22:1-5** — "(1)... a pure river of water of life, clear as crystal... (2)... the tree of life... the leaves of the tree [were] for the healing of the nations. (3) And there shall be no more curse... (4) And they shall see his face... (5) And there shall be no night there... and they shall reign for ever and ever."
_Role:_ The reversal of Eden's curse (§6) — "no more curse," the tree of life restored, face-to-face access regained. Bookends the fall.

**Isaiah 65:17, 21-25** — "(17) For, behold, I create new heavens and a new earth: and the former shall not be remembered... (21) And they shall build houses, and inhabit [them]; and they shall plant vineyards, and eat the fruit of them. (22) They shall not build, and another inhabit... (23) They shall not labour in vain, nor bring forth for trouble... (24)... before they call, I will answer... (25) The wolf and the lamb shall feed together... They shall not hurt nor destroy in all my holy mountain, saith the LORD."
_Role:_ Concrete, earthy future — houses, vineyards, meaningful labor, no predation — reverses the §6 curse of futile toil (Gen 3:17-19). Tangible, not vaporous, hope for a skeptic.

**1 Corinthians 2:9** — "But as it is written, Eye hath not seen, nor ear heard, neither have entered into the heart of man, the things which God hath prepared for them that love him."
_Role:_ Caps the section with humility — the restoration exceeds description; what's pictured is the floor, not the ceiling.

## §11 — The Appeal

**Revelation 3:20** — "Behold, I stand at the door, and knock: if any man hear my voice, and open the door, I will come in to him, and will sup with him, and he with me."
_Role:_ The non-coercive invitation — Christ "knocks," waits for the door to be opened from inside; freedom (§3) honored to the very end. The ask, not a demand.

**Isaiah 1:18** — "Come now, and let us reason together, saith the LORD: though your sins be as scarlet, they shall be as white as snow..."
_Role:_ Reprise of §3 — the appeal is to reason, with the offer of cleansing; closes the deck the way it opened, inviting the mind.

**John 5:39** — "Search the scriptures; for in them ye think ye have eternal life: and they are they which testify of me."
_Role:_ Direct command to investigate the evidence ("search") — licenses and demands the skeptic's scrutiny rather than fearing it.

**Acts 17:11** — "These were more noble than those in Thessalonica, in that they received the word with all readiness of mind, and searched the scriptures daily, whether those things were so."
_Role:_ The flagship verse for a skeptical audience — the Bereans are commended ("more noble") for fact-checking the preacher against Scripture "whether those things were so." Models exactly the verify-don't-trust posture the deck wants. **Strongest closer for skeptics.**

**Psalm 34:8** — "O taste and see that the LORD [is] good: blessed [is] the man [that] trusteth in him."
_Role:_ Final note (reprise of §1) — an empirical challenge: test it yourself. Closes the circle on criterion-5.

## Red Flags (claims that need user attention)

1. **Ezekiel 28:12-17 — "king of Tyrus," not "Lucifer."** The verse's surface addressee is the king of Tyrus. The Lucifer/Satan application is a traditional dual-fulfillment interpretation, NOT stated in the text. For a skeptical audience, presenting it as "the Bible says Lucifer was a perfect cherub" invites an easy debunk. **Recommendation:** on-slide, lead the §4 origin-of-evil case with **Revelation 12:7-9** (which explicitly names "the Devil, and Satan") and treat Ezekiel 28 / Isaiah 14 as supporting/illustrative with an honest "many read this as also describing the being behind the king" framing in speaker notes.

2. **Isaiah 14:12-14 — "king of Babylon," and "Lucifer" is a translation artifact.** Context (Isa 14:4) makes this a taunt-song against Babylon's king; "Lucifer" is the KJV's Latin-derived rendering of Hebrew _helel_ ("morning star/shining one"). Modern translations read "morning star" / "day star," and a prepared skeptic may raise this. Same handling as #1 — use as illustration, anchor the explicit claim in Revelation 12.

3. **Job 1:21 vs. Job 2:7 — attribution of the harm.** Job says "the LORD... hath taken away," but the narrative shows Satan did the smiting (Job 2:7). The §7 theodicy depends on this distinction. If Job 1:21 is shown without that framing, the deck risks teaching the opposite of its own thesis (that God authors calamity). **Recommendation:** always pair Job 1:21-22 with the Job 1-2 permission frame; consider a speaker-note line: "Job, in his grief, credits God — Scripture's narration tells us who actually struck."
