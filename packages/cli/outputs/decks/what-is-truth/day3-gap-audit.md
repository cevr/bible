# Audit: "Why Suffering?" (105 slides) vs. the full arc of _Story of Redemption_

## 1. Overall assessment

The deck is an unusually faithful and complete traversal of the _Story of Redemption_ arc — arguably tighter than SR's own pacing. It hits every macro-beat: Lucifer's fall (SR ch. 1), creation and the pre-fall witness stand (ch. 2), the temptation and fall (ch. 4), the plan of salvation and God's declaration of war (ch. 5), Cain/Abel and the two seeds (ch. 6), Seth/Enoch, the Flood, Babel, the patriarchs and Moriah, the Exodus/law/sanctuary, the apostasy of Israel and the captivity, Daniel's timetable, the first advent, the wilderness temptation, betrayal/crucifixion/resurrection/ascension, Pentecost, the persecuted church, the chained Word and the Reformation (Wycliffe/Hus/Tyndale/Luther), the last-day deceptions (spiritualism as SR's "Witchcraft in Modern Form," ch. 60), the second advent, the final panorama where **every knee bows and the accuser himself concedes** (SR 425.3, 427.2-3 — landed precisely on slide 100), the cleansing fire (ch. 65-66), and the new earth (ch. 67). Crucially, the deck _deliberately_ compresses the OT monarchy — and so does SR, which itself skips David-and-Goliath, Solomon's reign, and the individual prophets, jumping from the ark/Samuel (ch. 24) straight to Solomon's temple and the baptism of Jesus. So the deck is not "missing" what SR also elides. The genuine gaps are a small number of _character-of-God_ beats that SR dwells on and the deck skips — moments that would sharpen the "is God love or a tyrant?" thesis for a skeptic. None are structural holes; all are enrichment opportunities.

## 2. Ranked list of missing / underserved moments

### 1. Gethsemane — God sweating blood, abandoned, choosing to go through with it (HIGHEST value)

- **SR coverage:** ch. 28 "The Betrayal of Christ," SR 210.1 ("In the Garden") and SR 210.2 — "The Son of God prayed in agony. Great drops of blood gathered upon His face and fell to the ground… only one [angel] was commissioned to go and strengthen [Him]… In that dreadful hour He had not the sympathy and prayers of even His disciples."
- **Deck status:** OMITTED. The deck goes straight from the triumphal entry (slide 67) to the courtroom logic of the cross (slide 68). Gethsemane — the single most human, most agonized scene in the whole story — is absent.
- **Why it matters for THIS thesis:** The deck's most distinctive answer to Epicurus is slide 64: "the God of this Book feels the suffering from the inside." Gethsemane is the empirical proof of that claim. To a suffering non-Christian, a God who _chose_ to enter dread, isolation, and a longing to escape ("remove this cup") — and stayed — is far more disarming than the legal transaction of slide 69. It also mirrors slide 31's Adam ("a man walking into death for love of his bride") — Gethsemane is God answering it with "the true version," which slide 31's own note promises but never pays off before the cross.
- **Suggested placement:** New slide **after slide 67** (triumphal entry / weeping over the city), before the justice-mercy logic of 68. It bridges the emotional register between the King who weeps and the cross that reasons.

### 2. Why the delay costs GOD, not just us — "it was even a struggle with the God of heaven" / God bears the reproach while sin runs

- **SR coverage:** SR 45.1 ("Think ye that the Father yielded up His dearly beloved Son without a struggle? No, no. It was even a struggle with the God of heaven…"); SR 47.1 (the grief felt in heaven); reinforced by SR 42.1 (Christ three times "shut in" with the Father over whether to pay the cost).
- **Deck status:** UNDERSERVED. Slides 19-21 explain _why_ God lets sin run its course (force can't answer a charge; the case must mature) — a rational, almost clinical argument. What's missing is that this delay is agony _for God_, not a detached experiment. Isaiah 63:9 ("in all their affliction He was afflicted") is quoted at slide 64, but only about the incarnation, not about the whole long wait.
- **Why it matters:** The skeptic's real objection to slides 19-21 is "so God is running a multi-millennia demonstration project while I bleed?" The unspoken counter — that every day of the delay is a day God chooses to _keep suffering alongside us_ rather than end it prematurely and lose the verdict — turns cold cosmic logic into shared pain. It converts the "why doesn't He just stop it" argument from _legal_ to _relational_.
- **Suggested placement:** Fold into or add a slide **after slide 21** (Nahum 1:9 / "dig out the root"). One line: the delay is not God's patience with evil at our expense — it is God bearing the reproach _with_ us so the cure is permanent.

### 3. The heavenly sanctuary / Christ our High-Priestly mediator (the "where is Jesus now?" beat)

- **SR coverage:** ch. 53 "The Sanctuary" (SR 375.1, 377.1 — "The sanctuary in heaven, in which Jesus ministers in our behalf, is the great original…") and ch. 53's "The Cleansing of the Sanctuary" (SR 378.1); tied to Dan 8:14 and the investigative judgment (SR 375, 385).
- **Deck status:** OMITTED (deliberately). The only sanctuary in the deck is the wilderness tent (slide 55, "the sanctuary with its Lamb"). Between the ascension (slide 78) and the final judgment (slide 99), there is no picture of what Christ is _doing_ right now.
- **Why it matters:** The deck's own logic sets this up and then skips it. Slide 78 makes the load-bearing point that "our Brother, with our nature and His scars" is earth's representative before the throne, and "every charge against you now has to pass through nail-pierced hands." That IS the high-priestly ministry — but the deck never names that Christ is _presently interceding_. For a newcomer asking "so where is God between the resurrection and the end?", the answer "He is standing in for you, right now" is pastorally powerful and completes the courtroom metaphor.
- **Suggested placement:** New slide **after slide 78** (the accuser cast out / Christ enters as second Adam). This is the natural home: the accuser is out, the Advocate is in and _at work_. Note: the deck explicitly defers "the judgment" to the follow-up Daniel & Revelation studies (slide 105), so a _full_ investigative-judgment treatment would over-reach the night — but one slide naming Christ as present mediator would close a felt gap without opening the 1844 machinery.

### 4. Enoch/Elijah translated _without dying_ — the resurrection hope shown, not just asserted

- **SR coverage:** Enoch is in SR ch. 3 ("Seth and Enoch"); the deck actually HAS this well (slide 42). But the _second death / resurrection payoff_ — the righteous dead raised and the living translated at the advent, "the pious slave rises in victory" (SR 410.1) — is thin.
- **Deck status:** UNDERSERVED. The empty tomb (slide 76) answers "death is beaten," and the new earth (slide 101) shows "no more death," but the moment _between_ — the actual resurrection/translation of God's people at the second coming — is never staged. Slide 42's own note promises "a whole generation… who will meet Him without seeing death," a thread left dangling.
- **Why it matters:** For a grieving skeptic (the audience slide 3 names — "most doubt is born of pain"), the concrete promise "your dead will be raised, you may never die at all" is the emotional resolution of the whole suffering question. SR gives it a vivid scene (SR 410); the deck states the outcome but skips the reunion.
- **Suggested placement:** New slide **after slide 96** (the second-advent pivot) or **between 100 and 101** — the resurrection/reunion as the personal payoff just before "no more death."

### 5. Christ _volunteers_ — the moment the Son steps forward (the cost decided in heaven)

- **SR coverage:** SR 42.1, 45.1, 48.1 — an angel's life could not pay the debt; only the Son could; and He chose it in council with the Father before a single human sinned.
- **Deck status:** UNDERSERVED. Slide 35 says a plan was "conceived in love, at infinite cost," and slide 75 says the cross "vindicated AND rebuilt the bridge," but the deck never dramatizes the _decision_ — that redemption was not automatic, that it cost a struggle in heaven, and that the Son freely chose to pay what no one else could.
- **Why it matters:** It reframes the cross from "God's damage-control mechanism" to "God's costliest free choice, made before we existed." That directly rebuts the tyrant charge: a tyrant demands sacrifice; this God _becomes_ it, by choice, in advance.
- **Suggested placement:** Enrich **slide 35** (the plan conceived before the fall) with the "it was even a struggle with the God of heaven" note (SR 45.1). Lowest-effort, high-yield: it's a one-line deepening of an existing slide rather than a new one.
