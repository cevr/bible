## 1. GETHSEMANE — new slide, insert AFTER slide 67 (triumphal entry), before 68 (justice-mercy charge)

**Why:** The deck's sharpest answer to Epicurus is "God feels the suffering from the inside" (slide 64). Gethsemane is the proof. It also pays off slide 31's promise — Adam "walked into death for love of his bride"; here God gives the true version. Source: SR 210.1-2 (ch. 28 "In the Garden").

**Presenter note:**

> Before the courtroom, before a single nail, there is a garden — and this is the scene the whole night has been reaching toward. He goes in among the olive trees to pray, and what comes out of Him is not calm resolve. It is agony. "Father, if Thou be willing, remove this cup from Me." He does not want to do this. Story of Redemption says the drops of blood gathered on His face and fell to the ground, and all heaven leaned in — the angels cast down their crowns and harps, and only one was allowed to go and strengthen Him. They ached to surround Him and carry Him out of it. They were not permitted, because the plan had been laid and it had to be fulfilled.
>
> And here is the part that should stop you. In that dreadful hour He did not even have His friends. He came back three times and found them asleep — "could ye not watch with Me one hour?" So when you ask whether God understands what it is to suffer alone, to dread what is coming, to beg for another way and get silence — this is your answer. He has been in that garden. He chose to stay in it. Remember Adam, back at the beginning, walking into death for love of his bride? This is the true version of that story — and here the Bridegroom does not fall with us. He goes down under it _for_ us, on purpose, eyes open.

## 2. THE DELAY COSTS GOD — new slide, insert AFTER slide 21 (case must mature), before 22 (charge needs witnesses)

**Why:** Slides 19-21 argue _rationally_ why God lets sin run (force can't answer a charge). The skeptic's real objection: "so God runs a multi-millennia demonstration while I bleed?" The counter — the delay is agony _for God_, who chooses to keep suffering alongside us rather than end it early and lose the verdict. Converts the argument from legal to relational. Source: SR 42.1, 45.1, 47.1.

**Presenter note:**

> Now, if you're honest, there's a hard edge to what I just said. If God is letting this whole terrible case run its course so the cure is permanent — fine. But what does that cost _me_, waiting down here in the wreckage? It's a fair question. So let me show you who is paying the higher price for the delay.
>
> When it became clear that man was lost, Story of Redemption says sorrow filled heaven. And this is not God watching from a safe distance. Three times the Son withdrew into the light around the Father, in what the account calls "close converse" — and the angels waited in unbearable suspense. When He finally stepped out, His face was calm; a way had been made. But hear what it cost to reach that calm: "Think ye that the Father yielded up His dearly beloved Son without a struggle? No, no — it was even a struggle with the God of heaven."
>
> So understand what the delay really is. It is not God's patience with evil _at your expense_. Every day this long trial runs, God is not standing off to the side waiting for it to finish — He is down inside it, bearing the reproach _with_ us, holding the wound open so that when the cure comes it is total and it is forever. He is not asking us to endure something He is unwilling to endure Himself.

## 3. PRESENT MEDIATOR — new slide, insert AFTER slide 78 (Christ enters heaven as second Adam), before 79 (Pentecost)

**Why:** Slide 78 makes the point that "every charge against you now has to pass through nail-pierced hands" — that IS the high-priestly ministry, but the deck never names that Christ is _presently_ interceding. Answers the newcomer's "so where is Jesus now, between the resurrection and the end?" — "standing in for you, right now." Completes the courtroom metaphor without opening the 1844 machinery (deferred to the follow-up studies, slide 105). Source: SR 377.1 (ch. 53 "The Sanctuary").

**Presenter note:**

> So the accuser has been thrown out of the court, and our Brother — with our nature and His scars — has taken His place before the throne. But don't picture Him sitting down, finished. Picture Him _working_. Because the question a lot of people quietly carry is: that was two thousand years ago — so where is Jesus _now_?
>
> Here's the Bible's answer. Long ago God had Moses build a tent in the wilderness — the sanctuary we saw earlier, with its lamb and its altar — and it was never the real thing. It was a scale model. Story of Redemption puts it plainly: "The sanctuary in heaven, in which Jesus ministers in our behalf, is the great original, of which the sanctuary built by Moses was a copy." There is a real one, above, and right now there is a Man in it — our Man — serving as our priest.
>
> Think about what that means for the guilt you walked in here carrying tonight. You are not waiting for a distant verdict from a Judge who has never met you. Your case is already in the hands of the only Person who ever lived your life without failing it — and who has the marks in His hands to prove He'd rather die than lose you. He is not done with you. He is, at this very moment, standing in for you.

## 4. THE FIRST RESURRECTION / REUNION — new slide, insert BETWEEN slide 100 (trial's final scene) and 101 (Rev 21:4 no more death)

**Why:** The deck answers "death is beaten" (empty tomb, slide 76) and "no more death" (new earth, 101), but never _stages_ the moment in between — the righteous dead raised, the living translated, the reunion. For the grieving skeptic the deck names in slide 3 ("most doubt is born of pain"), this is the personal, emotional resolution of the whole suffering question. Source: SR 410.1, 411.1 (ch. "The Second Advent" / "The First Resurrection").

> _Placement note:_ 101 currently opens "Watch what the fire actually does." This new slide comes just before the final scene resolves into the new earth — OR, if you'd rather keep the panorama→fire→new-earth flow tight, place it right after slide 96 (the pivot to "unturned pages") instead. I've written it to sit before 101.

**Presenter note:**

> Before we step into that new world, there is one scene I refuse to rush past — because it is the one the person who came here grieving has been waiting all night to hear.
>
> When He comes, the graves open. Story of Redemption describes it like a jubilee morning: the faithful who died — the ones lowered into the ground with a promise that seemed too good to believe — rise in victory and shake off the chains that held them. And it's not silent. The account says they lift their voices together in one triumphant strain: "Lo, this is our God; we have waited for Him, and He will save us." The very people who, a moment before, the world had cornered and mocked — "where is your God?" — now stand in the glory that has come for them.
>
> Do you hear what that means? For everyone in this room who has stood at a graveside, who has prayed into the dark and gotten silence — the story does not end at the grave. There is a reunion written into the last pages. The God who felt every one of those losses from the inside is coming back, and He is bringing your dead with Him. Some of us, He says, will meet Him without ever seeing death at all.

## 5. CHRIST VOLUNTEERS — ENRICH existing slide 35 (no new slide)

**Why:** Slide 35 says the plan was "conceived in love, at infinite cost," but never dramatizes the _decision_ — that redemption wasn't automatic, that an angel's life couldn't pay it, that the Son _freely chose_ what no one else could. Reframes the cross from damage-control to God's costliest free choice, made before we existed — directly rebutting the tyrant charge. Source: SR 45.1, 48.1. Lowest-effort, highest-yield: a paragraph added to the existing note, no image change.

**Revised slide 35 note (adds the volunteering beat to the existing note — new material in the final paragraph):**

> He never reckoned with this. While one being in the universe was calculating how to win his case, Someone else was already answering the charge — not with an argument, but with a life laid down.
>
> Sorrow filled heaven when it was understood that man was lost, that the whole family of Adam must die. But this was no afterthought, no scramble for a fix after the fall. The plan was already in place, laid in eternity before the ground ever went wrong. Three times the Son withdrew into the light around the Father to plead man's case — and each time the anxious angels waited. When He came back, His face was calm. A way of escape had been made.
>
> And don't miss _who_ made it, or how freely. This was not automatic. When the debt came due, angels stepped forward — there were those in heaven willing to give their own glory, their own lives, for perishing man. And they were told: it would avail nothing. "An angel's life would not pay the debt." The law was as sacred as God Himself; only One who had created man could redeem him. So the Son — with everything to lose and nothing to gain — volunteered. Not a tyrant demanding a sacrifice, but a God who _becomes_ the sacrifice, by His own choice, before we had drawn a single breath. That is who was already moving while the accuser was still counting his votes. Conceived in love, settled at infinite cost — before the accuser ever opened his mouth.
