# Verse-Clouds — Trumpet Figure & First Four Trumpets (V1)

Every fragment below verified verbatim against the KJV in `~/.bible/bible.db`. Defining verses only: (a) the equation stated, (b) the symbol used with its meaning supplied in context, or (c) the canonical type-scene the symbol points back to. Strongest/definitional-first.

---

## SYMBOL: trumpet (the war-alarm)

- _Num. 10:9._ "if ye go to war in your land against the enemy... then ye shall blow an alarm with the trumpets" — God's own statute fixes the equation: the trumpet IS the war-signal.
- _Jer. 4:19._ "the sound of the trumpet, the alarm of war" — the prophet names it outright; trumpet = the alarm of war, no gloss needed.
- _Amos 3:6._ "Shall a trumpet be blown in the city, and the people not be afraid? shall there be evil in a city, and the LORD hath not done it?" — the trumpet warns of judgment, and the judgment (the "evil," the war) is God's own doing — the theology of every Revelation trumpet in one verse.
- _1 Cor. 14:8._ "if the trumpet give an uncertain sound, who shall prepare himself to the battle?" — Paul treats trumpet = battle-call as already-settled common knowledge.
- _Joel 2:1._ "Blow ye the trumpet in Zion, and sound an alarm... for the day of the LORD cometh" — the trumpet is the alarm that the day of the LORD / the invading army (vv. 2-11) is at hand.
- _Zeph. 1:16._ "A day of the trumpet and alarm against the fenced cities, and against the high towers" — the day of the LORD is itself styled "a day of the trumpet," a war on walled cities.
- _Hos. 8:1._ "Set the trumpet to thy mouth. He shall come as an eagle against the house of the LORD" — the trumpet announces the heathen power loosed as God's rod against apostate Israel — the very pattern of the woes.
- _Ezek. 33:3._ "if when he seeth the sword come... he blow the trumpet, and warn the people" — the watchman's trumpet is defined as the warning that the sword (war) is coming.
- _Ezek. 33:6._ "if the watchman... blow not the trumpet... his blood will I require at the watchman's hand" — the silent watchman is charged with the slaughter he failed to announce; the trumpet's whole office is to warn of the sword.
- _Jer. 6:1._ "blow the trumpet in Tekoa... for evil appeareth out of the north, and great destruction" — the trumpet sounds because the invader (out of the north) is coming; warning precedes the war.
- _Isa. 58:1._ "lift up thy voice like a trumpet, and shew my people their transgression" — the trumpet-voice = the plain warning cry, set against sin before the stroke.
- VERDICT: The trumpet is the Bible's standing alarm of war and of coming judgment — the watchman's warning that the sword is at the door (Num. 10:9; Amos 3:6; Jer. 4:19; Joel 2:1; Zeph. 1:16; Hos. 8:1; Ezek. 33:3, 6); so the seven trumpets of Revelation are seven successive wars sent of God.

## SYMBOL: hail

- _Isa. 28:17._ "the hail shall sweep away the refuge of lies, and the waters shall overflow the hiding place" — hail = the sweeping, overwhelming destruction that carries off every false shelter.
- _Isa. 28:2._ "as a tempest of hail and a destroying storm, as a flood of mighty waters... shall cast down to the earth" — the LORD's "mighty and strong one" comes AS a hailstorm; hail = an irresistible destroying invader.
- _Ezek. 13:11, 13._ "great hailstones, shall fall... I will even rend it with a stormy wind in my fury... and great hailstones in my fury to consume it" — hail is God's fury executed in judgment, breaking down the wall daubed with lies.
- _Isa. 30:30._ "the flame of a devouring fire, with scattering, and tempest, and hailstones" — hail paired with devouring fire as the instrument of the LORD's indignation against His enemy.
- _Exod. 9:23-25._ "the LORD sent thunder and hail, and the fire ran along upon the ground... the hail smote every herb of the field, and brake every tree of the field" — the canonical type-scene: the Egypt plague of hail-and-fire that smites tree and herb, the exact picture re-sounded in the first trumpet (Rev. 8:7).
- VERDICT: Hail is sweeping, irresistible destruction sent in God's fury (Isa. 28:2, 17; Ezek. 13:13) — the Egypt-plague hail-and-fire that breaks every tree (Exod. 9:24-25), here the Goths' northern storm breaking on Rome.

## SYMBOL: fire (devouring war)

- _Joel 2:3._ "A fire devoureth before them; and behind them a flame burneth... behind them a desolate wilderness; yea, and nothing shall escape them" — fire = the devouring advance of the invading army, leaving a wilderness.
- _Amos 1:4._ "I will send a fire into the house of Hazael, which shall devour the palaces of Benhadad" — God's "fire" sent on a kingdom = the war that devours its strongholds (so also Amos 1:7, "a fire on the wall of Gaza").
- _Jer. 21:14._ "I will kindle a fire in the forest thereof, and it shall devour all things round about it" — God-kindled fire = the consuming judgment-war on Judah.
- _Deut. 32:22._ "a fire is kindled in mine anger... and set on fire the foundations of the mountains" — fire = the burning of God's anger that consumes the land and its kingdoms.
- _Joel 1:19-20._ "the fire hath devoured the pastures of the wilderness, and the flame hath burned all the trees of the field" — fire devouring trees and pastures = the desolating stroke on a land.
- _Ezek. 30:8._ "when I have set a fire in Egypt, and when all her helpers shall be destroyed" — God "sets a fire" in a nation = He brings the destroying war.
- _Nah. 3:15._ "There shall the fire devour thee; the sword shall cut thee off" — fire and sword stand in apposition: fire = the war that eats up the city.
- VERDICT: Fire is devouring war — the consuming advance of the invading army God sends and kindles in His anger (Joel 2:3; Amos 1:4; Jer. 21:14; Deut. 32:22; Nah. 3:15), leaving a wilderness behind.

## SYMBOL: blood

- _Ezek. 14:19._ "I send a pestilence into that land, and pour out my fury upon it in blood, to cut off from it man and beast" — blood = the slaughter in which God pours out His fury.
- _Ezek. 14:21._ "my four sore judgments... the sword, and the famine, and the noisome beast, and the pestilence" — the same context names the bloodshed among God's standing judgment-strokes.
- _Jer. 46:10._ "the sword shall devour, and it shall be satiate and made drunk with their blood: for the Lord GOD of hosts hath a sacrifice in the north country by the river Euphrates" — blood = the day-of-the-LORD slaughter; the sword drunk with it.
- _Isa. 34:3._ "their slain also shall be cast out... and the mountains shall be melted with their blood" — blood = the carnage of the slain in the day of vengeance.
- _Isa. 34:6-7._ "The sword of the LORD is filled with blood... for the LORD hath a sacrifice in Bozrah, and a great slaughter" — blood explicitly = the great slaughter, a sacrifice of the nations.
- _Ezek. 38:22._ "I will plead against him with pestilence and with blood; and I will rain upon him... great hailstones, fire, and brimstone" — blood ranged with hail/fire/brimstone as the kit of God's war-judgment.
- VERDICT: Blood is slaughter — the carnage of the war God pours out in fury (Ezek. 14:19; Jer. 46:10; Isa. 34:3, 6-7), filling the rivers and seas of the trumpets.

## SYMBOL: trees (great men, rulers)

- _Dan. 4:20-22._ "The tree that thou sawest... It is thou, O king" — the equation stated outright: a great tree = a great ruler.
- _Ezek. 31:3._ "the Assyrian was a cedar in Lebanon with fair branches... of an high stature; and his top was among the thick boughs" — the towering cedar IS the Assyrian monarch; the whole chapter cuts that tree down = the fall of the power.
- _Isa. 2:12-13._ "the day of the LORD of hosts shall be upon every one that is proud and lofty... and upon all the cedars of Lebanon, that are high and lifted up, and upon all the oaks of Bashan" — the high cedars and oaks brought low in the day of the LORD = the proud and lofty men brought low; the verse pairs tree and proud man as one.
- _Isa. 10:18-19._ "shall consume the glory of his forest... and the rest of the trees of his forest shall be few" — the forest = the host/glory of the Assyrian power, consumed in war.
- _Zech. 11:1-2._ "let the fire may devour thy cedars. Howl, fir tree; for the cedar is fallen; because the mighty are spoiled" — "the cedar is fallen" is glossed in the same breath "the mighty are spoiled": trees = the mighty.
- VERDICT: Trees are the great men — kings, rulers, the lofty and proud (Dan. 4:22; Ezek. 31:3; Isa. 2:12-13; Zech. 11:2) — burnt up when the war falls.

## SYMBOL: green grass (the common people)

- _Isa. 40:6-7._ "All flesh is grass... The grass withereth, the flower fadeth... surely the people is grass" — the equation stated outright: the people = grass.
- _1 Pet. 1:24._ "For all flesh is as grass, and all the glory of man as the flower of grass. The grass withereth" — the apostle re-states it: all flesh, all mankind = grass.
- _Ps. 103:15-16._ "man, his days are as grass... For the wind passeth over it, and it is gone" — man / the common multitude = grass, soon cut down.
- _Ps. 37:2._ "they shall soon be cut down like the grass, and wither as the green herb" — the mass of the wicked = grass, mown down in judgment.
- _Isa. 51:12._ "afraid of a man that shall die, and of the son of man which shall be made as grass" — mortal man = grass; the perishable common people.
- VERDICT: Green grass is the common people — the perishable mass of mankind (Isa. 40:6-7; 1 Pet. 1:24; Ps. 103:15) — burnt up with the trees (the great) when the first trumpet's war falls.

## SYMBOL: mountain (a kingdom / seat of power)

- _Dan. 2:35, 44._ "the stone that smote the image became a great mountain, and filled the whole earth"; "shall the God of heaven set up a kingdom" — the great mountain is interpreted = the kingdom; the equation stated.
- _Jer. 51:25._ "Behold, I am against thee, O destroying mountain... which destroyest all the earth... and will make thee a burnt mountain" — Babylon the world-empire is addressed as a "mountain," a "burnt mountain" — a great mountain burning, the exact phrase of the second trumpet (Rev. 8:8).
- _Isa. 2:2._ "the mountain of the LORD'S house shall be established in the top of the mountains" — God's kingdom = a mountain set above other "mountains" (kingdoms).
- _Zech. 4:7._ "Who art thou, O great mountain? before Zerubbabel thou shalt become a plain" — the opposing "great mountain" = the hostile power flattened before God's work.
- VERDICT: A mountain is a kingdom or seat of power (Dan. 2:35, 44; Jer. 51:25; Isa. 2:2); "a great mountain burning with fire cast into the sea" (Rev. 8:8) = a kingdom in flames hurled upon the nations.

## SYMBOL: sea / waters (peoples, nations)

- _Rev. 17:15._ "The waters which thou sawest, where the whore sitteth, are peoples, and multitudes, and nations, and tongues" — the equation stated outright: waters = peoples and nations.
- _Isa. 17:12-13._ "the multitude of many people, which make a noise like the noise of the seas... The nations shall rush like the rushing of many waters" — peoples and nations explicitly likened to the roaring seas/waters.
- _Isa. 8:7-8._ "the Lord bringeth up upon them the waters of the river, strong and many, even the king of Assyria... he shall overflow and go over" — the flooding "waters of the river" are glossed in the verse = the king of Assyria and his host overflowing the land.
- _Dan. 7:2-3._ "the four winds of the heaven strove upon the great sea. And four great beasts came up from the sea" — the kingdoms (beasts) rise out of the sea = out of the strife of peoples (cf. v. 17).
- _Rev. 13:1._ "saw a beast rise up out of the sea" — the kingdom-beast emerges from the sea = from the populous, settled nations.
- VERDICT: Sea / waters are peoples, multitudes, nations (Rev. 17:15; Isa. 17:12-13; 8:7-8; Dan. 7:2-3) — the maritime peoples on which the second trumpet's burning mountain falls.

## SYMBOL: star (a leader / teacher; fallen = apostate or meteor-conqueror)

- _Rev. 1:20._ "The seven stars are the angels of the seven churches" — the equation stated: a star = the messenger/minister set over a church.
- _Dan. 8:10._ "it cast down some of the host and of the stars to the ground, and stamped upon them" — the "stars" = the leaders/holy people the little horn casts down (cf. v. 24, "the mighty and the holy people").
- _Mal. 2:7._ "the priest's lips should keep knowledge... he is the messenger of the LORD of hosts" — supplies the star-as-teacher idea: the ordained teacher is God's "messenger" (the word rendered "angel"/star in Rev. 1:20).
- _Dan. 12:3._ "they that turn many to righteousness as the stars for ever and ever" — the faithful teachers shine = stars; the symbol's positive pole.
- _Isa. 14:12._ "How art thou fallen from heaven, O Lucifer, son of the morning! how art thou cut down to the ground" — the FALLEN star = the exalted leader/power cast down, the apostate pole (re-sounded in Rev. 9:1).
- _Num. 24:17._ "there shall come a Star out of Jacob, and a Sceptre shall rise out of Israel" — a star paired with a sceptre = a ruler/conqueror arising; star = a rising chief.
- _Jude 1:13._ "wandering stars, to whom is reserved the blackness of darkness for ever" — false teachers branded "wandering stars": apostate stars.
- VERDICT: A star is a leader or teacher — a church's messenger or a ruler (Rev. 1:20; Mal. 2:7; Num. 24:17; Dan. 12:3); fallen, it is an apostate power or a meteor-conqueror cast down (Isa. 14:12; Jude 13), as the third trumpet's burning star = Attila.

## SYMBOL: wormwood / bitter waters

- _Rev. 8:11._ "the name of the star is called Wormwood: and the third part of the waters became wormwood; and many men died of the waters, because they were made bitter" — the symbol named in the trumpet itself: wormwood = poisoned, bitter, death-dealing waters.
- _Jer. 9:15._ "I will feed them... with wormwood, and give them water of gall to drink" — God-given wormwood = the bitter cup of judgment poured out on a guilty people.
- _Jer. 23:15._ "I will feed them with wormwood, and make them drink the water of gall: for from the prophets of Jerusalem is profaneness gone forth" — wormwood/gall = the bitter judgment on corrupt leaders whose poison spread through the land.
- _Lam. 3:15, 19._ "He hath filled me with bitterness, he hath made me drunken with wormwood... the wormwood and the gall" — wormwood = the affliction and misery of the chastening stroke.
- _Deut. 29:18._ "a root that beareth gall and wormwood" — apostasy itself is the bitter root that yields wormwood; the source of the poison.
- _Exod. 15:23._ "they could not drink of the waters of Marah, for they were bitter" — the canonical type-scene of "bitter waters" that men cannot drink (Marah = "bitterness").
- VERDICT: Wormwood / bitter waters are the bitter, poisoning cup of judgment — affliction and ruin made undrinkable (Rev. 8:11; Jer. 9:15; 23:15; Lam. 3:19; Exod. 15:23), the desolation Attila the "Scourge of God" left bitter.

## SYMBOL: sun / moon / stars (a government's luminaries)

- _Gen. 37:9-10._ "the sun and the moon and the eleven stars made obeisance to me... Shall I and thy mother and thy brethren indeed come to bow down" — the equation stated by Jacob himself: sun = father, moon = mother, stars = the brethren; the ruling household's heads = the heavenly lights.
- _Ezek. 32:7-8._ "when I shall put thee out, I will cover the heaven, and make the stars thereof dark; I will cover the sun with a cloud... All the bright lights of heaven will I make dark over thee" — God extinguishing Pharaoh (a government) is figured as putting out its sun, moon, stars, and "bright lights" — the precise fourth-trumpet picture: a kingdom's rulers blotted out.
- _Isa. 13:10._ "the stars of heaven... shall not give their light: the sun shall be darkened... and the moon shall not cause her light to shine" — the fall of Babylon's day = its luminaries darkened; a government's lights put out.
- _Joel 2:10._ "the sun and the moon shall be dark, and the stars shall withdraw their shining" — the invading army's day = the darkening of the heavenly lights over the doomed power.
- VERDICT: Sun, moon, and stars are a government's luminaries — its ruling heads, the great offices that give it light (Gen. 37:9-10; Ezek. 32:7-8; Isa. 13:10) — smitten when the fourth trumpet puts out Western Rome's emperors, senators, and consuls.

## SYMBOL: "the third part"

- _Ezek. 5:1-2._ "divide the hair... Thou shalt burn with fire a third part... a third part thou shalt smite about it with a knife: and a third part thou shalt scatter in the wind" — the canonical type-scene: God's judgment portioned out in thirds, one third at a time.
- _Ezek. 5:12._ "A third part of thee shall die with the pestilence... and a third part shall fall by the sword... and I will scatter a third part into all the winds" — the thirds interpreted: each third a distinct judgment-stroke; not the whole, but one division under the rod.
- _Zech. 13:8-9._ "two parts therein shall be cut off and die; but the third shall be left... And I will bring the third part through the fire" — "the third part" used as a fixed measure of those struck / those spared in judgment.
- _Rev. 8:7-12._ "the third part of trees... the third part of the sea... the third part of the sun" — the repeated "third part" of the first four trumpets: one division of the empire under the scourge at a time, not the whole.
- VERDICT: "The third part" is one division struck at a time — not the whole, but a portion under the scourge (Ezek. 5:2, 12; Zech. 13:8) — the Roman empire split in three (after Constantine), the rod falling on one division per trumpet.
