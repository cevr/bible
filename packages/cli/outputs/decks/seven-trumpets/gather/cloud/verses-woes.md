# VERSE-CLOUDS — Symbols of the Three Woes

> Each symbol below is defined by Scripture as its own expositor. Verses verified verbatim against `~/.bible/bible.db` (KJV). Strongest/definitional-first. Bracketed `[ ]` KJV supplied-words are dropped from fragments for readability; the quoted words are exact.

---

## SYMBOL: woe

- _Rev. 8:13._ "Woe, woe, woe, to the inhabiters of the earth by reason of the other voices of the trumpet of the three angels, which are yet to sound" — the text's own definition: a woe IS the voice of a trumpet; three trumpets are re-named three woes.
- _Rev. 9:12._ "One woe is past; and, behold, there come two woes more hereafter" — woes are counted off in sequence, one kind of event, each a discrete trumpet-war.
- _Rev. 11:14._ "The second woe is past; and, behold, the third woe cometh quickly" — the sixth trumpet ends the second woe; the seventh brings the third, fixing woe = trumpet = the heaviest war-judgments.
- _Rev. 12:12._ "Woe to the inhabiters of the earth and of the sea! for the devil is come down unto you, having great wrath" — same phrase as 8:13; a woe is a season of concentrated wrath loosed on the earth-dwellers.
- _Isa. 3:11._ "Woe unto the wicked! it shall be ill with him: for the reward of his hands shall be given him" — a woe is a pronounced judgment that recompenses guilt.
- _Jer. 4:13._ "his horses are swifter than eagles. Woe unto us! for we are spoiled" — the cry "woe" raised at the approach of the invading war-host, the very setting of the trumpet-woes.
- VERDICT: a **woe** is the voice of a trumpet — a war-judgment of the heaviest kind, loosed of God on the apostate earth-dwellers (the last three trumpets, scourging not pagan Rome but idolatrous Christendom).

## SYMBOL: fallen star with a key

- _Rev. 9:1._ "I saw a star fall from heaven unto the earth: and to him was given the key of the bottomless pit" — the symbol itself: a star already fallen, now armed with a key to unlock a hidden power.
- _Isa. 14:12._ "How art thou fallen from heaven, O Lucifer, son of the morning! how art thou cut down to the ground, which didst weaken the nations" — Scripture's own picture of a fallen star: an apostate power cast down, yet able to weaken the nations.
- _Luke 10:18._ "I beheld Satan as lightning fall from heaven" — Christ defines the fall-from-heaven figure as a once-exalted power overthrown, the type behind every fallen star.
- _Rev. 1:18._ "have the keys of hell and of death" — a "key" in Revelation's grammar is delegated authority to open or shut a realm; the fallen star is given just such a key over the pit.
- VERDICT: a **fallen star with a key** is an apostate, cast-down power granted authority to unlock a dark realm and loose its hordes (the fall of Persia / Chosroes opening the way for Islam out of Arabia).

## SYMBOL: bottomless pit / the deep (waste region)

- _Rev. 9:2._ "he opened the bottomless pit; and there arose a smoke out of the pit, as the smoke of a great furnace" — the pit defined in use: a covered abyss that, opened, vents darkening smoke and a swarming army.
- _Gen. 1:2._ "the earth was without form, and void; and darkness was upon the face of the deep" — the Greek ἄβυσσος ("the deep") names the earth in chaos: a waste, dark, uncultivated region — exactly the desert wastes the pit pictures.
- _Jer. 4:23._ "I beheld the earth, and, lo, it was without form, and void; and the heavens, and they had no light" — the same "without form, and void" chaos-language reapplied to a land laid desolate, confirming the deep = a depopulated waste.
- VERDICT: the **bottomless pit / the deep** is a dark, waste, depopulated region (chaos-land) out of which a hidden power is loosed when its "key" is turned — the unknown wastes of the Arabian desert.

## SYMBOL: smoke (load-bearing — error + torment, Sodom's token)

- _Rev. 14:11._ "the smoke of their torment ascendeth up for ever and ever: and they have no rest day nor night" — the defining equation: smoke = the rising, perpetual token of torment under judgment.
- _Gen. 19:28._ "the smoke of the country went up as the smoke of a furnace" — Sodom's destruction supplies the type-scene; the same "smoke... of a furnace" phrase John borrows for the pit (Rev. 9:2), tying the pit-smoke to Sodom's overthrow.
- _Rev. 9:2._ "there arose a smoke out of the pit, as the smoke of a great furnace; and the sun and the air were darkened by reason of the smoke" — smoke that darkens sun and air = error eclipsing gospel light (Islam's "dense smoke," not a light from heaven).
- _Isa. 34:10._ "the smoke thereof shall go up for ever: from generation to generation it shall lie waste" — in the day-of-the-LORD's-vengeance text, ascending smoke marks a land given over to perpetual desolation (verbatim source of Rev. 14:11 / 19:3).
- _Rev. 19:3._ "And her smoke rose up for ever and ever" — Babylon's judgment-smoke, same formula, sealing smoke = the standing memorial of a power consumed in wrath.
- _Joel 2:30._ "I will shew wonders in the heavens and in the earth, blood, and fire, and pillars of smoke" — smoke listed among the day-of-the-LORD's war-tokens (blood, fire, smoke), the trumpet-vocabulary of judgment.
- _Rev. 18:9._ "when they shall see the smoke of her burning" — the kings see the smoke and know the war-judgment is finished; smoke = the visible sign that the stroke has fallen.
- VERDICT: **smoke** is the rising, abiding token of war-judgment and torment — and, ascending from a furnace, the token of darkening error (Sodom's overthrow-smoke is its type; Islam's doctrine the antitype darkening the gospel sun).

## SYMBOL: locusts (load-bearing — the innumerable eastern army, children of the east)

- _Joel 2:25._ "the locust... my great army which I sent among you" — God's own equation: the locust IS the great army He commissions and sends as judgment.
- _Joel 1:6._ "a nation is come up upon my land, strong, and without number, whose teeth are the teeth of a lion" — the locust read outright as an innumerable invading nation with a lion's teeth (cf. Rev. 9:8).
- _Judg. 7:12._ "the children of the east lay along in the valley like grasshoppers for multitude; and their camels were without number" — the only people-group Scripture lays "like grasshoppers for multitude": the children of the east, the desert Arab confederacy. (KJV "grasshoppers" = the same Hebrew word, H697 _'arbeh_, rendered "locust" in Joel and Exodus.)
- _Judg. 6:5._ "they came as grasshoppers for multitude; for both they and their camels were without number" — the Midian/Amalek/east coalition mustered as the locust-horde, fixing the address: the eastern desert peoples.
- _Nah. 3:17._ "Thy crowned are as the locusts, and thy captains as the great grasshoppers" — military leaders likened to locusts, the figure already settled as a vast armed host.
- _Exod. 10:14._ "the locusts went up over all the land of Egypt... before them there were no such locusts" — the plague-of-Egypt template: locusts as an overwhelming, land-covering judgment (the trumpet-as-plague pattern).
- _Prov. 30:27._ "The locusts have no king, yet go they forth all of them by bands" — the negative mark: natural locusts have NO king; so when Rev. 9:11's locusts HAVE a king, the symbol must be men, not insects.
- _Joel 1:4._ "that which the locust hath left hath the cankerworm eaten" — successive devouring swarms = waves of a consuming army, the locust as total devastation.
- VERDICT: **locusts** are an innumerable, God-commissioned invading army — and specifically the children of the east, the camel-riding desert confederacy of Arabia (the Saracens), men under a king, not insects.

## SYMBOL: scorpions (sting/chastise not slay)

- _1 Kgs. 12:11._ "my father hath chastised you with whips, but I will chastise you with scorpions" — Scripture's own definition: scorpions = a heavier instrument of chastisement, to afflict and torment, not to kill.
- _Rev. 9:5._ "they should not kill them, but that they should be tormented five months: and their torment was as the torment of a scorpion, when he striketh a man" — the locust-army's commission is the scorpion's: to sting and torment, expressly _not_ to slay.
- _Rev. 9:10._ "tails like unto scorpions, and there were stings in their tails: and their power was to hurt men five months" — the scorpion-sting commission repeated, sealing the figure: power to hurt, not to kill.
- _Luke 10:19._ "I give unto you power to tread on serpents and scorpions" — scorpions ranked among hostile, stinging powers under the enemy's hand, the standard biblical sense of painful affliction.
- _Deut. 8:15._ "that great and terrible wilderness, wherein were fiery serpents, and scorpions" — scorpions as the desert's stinging tormentors, fittingly the figure for a desert (Arabian) power.
- VERDICT: **scorpions** signify power to sting, chastise, and torment — but not yet to slay (the Saracen woe afflicting Christendom 150 years without conquering it).

## SYMBOL: torment (load-bearing)

- _Rev. 9:5._ "they should not kill them, but that they should be tormented five months: and their torment was as the torment of a scorpion" — torment defined against killing: it is sustained affliction short of death, the scorpion's sting prolonged.
- _Rev. 14:11._ "the smoke of their torment ascendeth up for ever and ever: and they have no rest day nor night" — torment = restless suffering under the unmixed wrath, its smoke the perpetual token.
- _Rev. 14:10._ "he shall be tormented with fire and brimstone in the presence of the holy angels" — the content of the final torment named: fire and brimstone (Sodom's stroke), the executing-wrath sense.
- _Rev. 11:10._ "because these two prophets tormented them that dwelt on the earth" — even truth/testimony can "torment" the guilty, i.e. press and distress them; torment = anguish inflicted.
- _Matt. 8:29._ "art thou come hither to torment us before the time?" — torment as dreaded punitive suffering awaiting a fixed time, the judgment sense.
- _Luke 16:28._ "lest they also come into this place of torment" — torment = the punitive suffering of the lost, anguish under judgment.
- VERDICT: **torment** is sustained punitive affliction short of (or culminating in) death — the scorpion-sting woe in history, the unmixed fire-and-brimstone wrath at the end.

## SYMBOL: five months / day-for-year

- _Rev. 9:5._ "tormented five months" — the prophetic period stated; five months = 150 days on the 30-day month, read as 150 years by the day-for-year rule.
- _Rev. 9:10._ "their power was to hurt men five months" — the same 150-day span repeated, confirming a fixed, measured commission (1299–1449).
- _Num. 14:34._ "After the number of the days... each day for a year, shall ye bear your iniquities" — God's own day-for-year key: a prophetic day equals a literal year.
- _Eze. 4:6._ "thou shalt bear the iniquity of the house of Judah forty days: I have appointed thee each day for a year" — the day-for-year rule restated and applied to a prophetic sign-act.
- VERDICT: **five months** = 150 prophetic days = 150 literal years by the day-for-year rule (Num. 14:34; Eze. 4:6) — the Saracen tormenting period, 1299–1449.

## SYMBOL: horses prepared unto battle

- _Rev. 9:7._ "the shapes of the locusts were like unto horses prepared unto battle" — the locust-army shown as cavalry, the mounted war-power.
- _Prov. 21:31._ "The horse is prepared against the day of battle: but safety is of the LORD" — Scripture's plain equation: the horse is the creature made ready for war.
- _Joel 2:4._ "The appearance of them is as the appearance of horses; and as horsemen, so shall they run" — the invading locust-army (Joel) explicitly likened to charging horsemen, the source-image for Rev. 9:7.
- _Jer. 4:13._ "his chariots shall be as a whirlwind: his horses are swifter than eagles. Woe unto us! for we are spoiled" — horses as the swift, irresistible war-power that brings the cry of "woe."
- _Job 39:19._ "Hast thou given the horse strength? hast thou clothed his neck with thunder?" — the horse defined by its battle-strength and terror, the emblem of armed might.
- VERDICT: **horses prepared unto battle** are the mounted, armed war-power made ready for the day of battle — the swift Arab cavalry charging out of the east.

## SYMBOL: crowns like gold

- _Rev. 9:7._ "on their heads were as it were crowns like gold" — note "as it were" / "like": not real crowns but something resembling them on the heads of the war-host (the Saracen turban).
- _Nah. 3:17._ "Thy crowned are as the locusts, and thy captains as the great grasshoppers" — the only other place Scripture joins "crowned" to the locust-figure: a locust-army's leaders wear what crowns them, confirming crown-like headgear on a multitude of warriors.
- VERDICT: **crowns like gold** are the turban-like, crown-resembling headgear of the locust-warriors — not literal royal diadems but the conqueror's headdress (the Saracens' yellow turbans).

## SYMBOL: breastplates

- _Rev. 9:9._ "they had breastplates, as it were breastplates of iron; and the sound of their wings was as the sound of chariots" — the war-host armored for battle, iron cuirasses on charging cavalry.
- _Rev. 9:17._ "having breastplates of fire, and of jacinth, and brimstone" — the sixth-trumpet horsemen likewise armored, the breastplate marking them as men of war.
- _Isa. 59:17._ "he put on righteousness as a breastplate, and an helmet of salvation upon his head" — the breastplate established as the warrior's defensive armor, the standard figure for being arrayed for battle.
- _1 Thess. 5:8._ "putting on the breastplate of faith and love; and for an helmet, the hope of salvation" — breastplate as the soldier's chest-armor, confirming the military sense.
- VERDICT: **breastplates** are the body-armor of a war-host — the iron cuirasses of the locust-cavalry, marking the symbol as armed men prepared for battle.

## SYMBOL: Euphrates (load-bearing — the eastern power's river)

- _Rev. 9:14._ "Loose the four angels which are bound in the great river Euphrates" — the sixth-trumpet ground: the river from which the eastern war-power (the Ottoman Turks) is loosed.
- _Rev. 16:12._ "the sixth angel poured out his vial upon the great river Euphrates... that the way of the kings of the east might be prepared" — the Euphrates defined by what it gates: the eastern kings; drying it up opens the road for the powers of the east.
- _Jer. 46:10._ "the day of the Lord GOD of hosts... hath a sacrifice in the north country by the river Euphrates" — the only OT verse joining "the day of the LORD" and "the river Euphrates": the Euphrates is the appointed theatre of God's war-sacrifice.
- _Isa. 8:7._ "the Lord bringeth up upon them the waters of the river, strong and many, even the king of Assyria" — the river named outright as the invading eastern empire; "waters of the river" = the overflowing eastern war-power, the symbol keyed to its political meaning.
- _Gen. 15:18._ "the great river, the river Euphrates" — the Euphrates fixed as the eastern boundary of the promised land, hence the frontier across which the eastern powers press.
- VERDICT: the **Euphrates** is the river of the eastern power — the frontier and theatre of the invading powers of the east (Assyria/Babylon in type, the Ottoman Turks in the sixth trumpet/sixth vial).

## SYMBOL: four angels bound / loosed

- _Rev. 9:14._ "Loose the four angels which are bound in the great river Euphrates" — four powers held in restraint until the command to loose them — the four Turkish sultanies of the Euphrates ground.
- _Rev. 9:15._ "the four angels were loosed, which were prepared for an hour, and a day, and a month, and a year, for to slay the third part of men" — once loosed, the four execute a dated, commissioned slaughter; binding/loosing = divine restraint and release of a war-power.
- _Rev. 7:1._ "I saw four angels standing on the four corners of the earth, holding the four winds of the earth, that the wind should not blow" — Revelation's own parallel: angels who _hold_ destroying powers until God releases them; "the four winds" = the strife let loose when the holding ends.
- VERDICT: **four angels bound / loosed** are war-powers held under divine restraint until God's appointed hour releases them to execute judgment (the four Ottoman sultanies, restrained then loosed in 1449).

## SYMBOL: hour-day-month-year

- _Rev. 9:15._ "prepared for an hour, and a day, and a month, and a year, for to slay the third part of men" — a compound prophetic clock: hour + day + month + year, each reckoned by the day-for-year rule, summing the Ottoman commission (391 years, 15 days).

- _Eze. 4:6._ "I have appointed thee each day for a year" — the same rule restated, the basis for reading the four time-units as a years-clock, not a literal year-and-change.
- VERDICT: **hour-day-month-year** is a compound prophetic period read by the day-for-year rule (Num. 14:34; Eze. 4:6) — 391 years and 15 days, the dated span of Ottoman supremacy (1449–Aug. 11, 1840).

## SYMBOL: fire + smoke + brimstone together (load-bearing)

**The triad in the trumpet:**

- _Rev. 9:17._ "out of their mouths issued fire and smoke and brimstone" — the three named together as the sixth-trumpet's slaying instruments issuing from the horsemen (gunpowder warfare).
- _Rev. 9:18._ "By these three was the third part of men killed, by the fire, and by the smoke, and by the brimstone" — the triad expressly counted as "these three," the executing agents of the war-judgment.

**Each member traced to the Sodom type-scene:**

- _Gen. 19:24._ "Then the LORD rained upon Sodom and upon Gomorrah brimstone and fire from the LORD out of heaven" — the canonical type: brimstone-and-fire is God's rained judgment of total overthrow.
- _Gen. 19:28._ "the smoke of the country went up as the smoke of a furnace" — the third member, smoke, completing the Sodom triad: fire + brimstone + ascending furnace-smoke.
- _Luke 17:29._ "the same day that Lot went out of Sodom it rained fire and brimstone from heaven, and destroyed them all" — Christ re-publishes Sodom as the standing type of sudden, total fire-and-brimstone judgment.
- _Deut. 29:23._ "the whole land thereof is brimstone, and salt, and burning... like the overthrow of Sodom, and Gomorrah" — "the overthrow of Sodom" fixed as the byword for a land consumed in fire-and-brimstone wrath.

**The triad as the day-of-the-LORD / final-wrath signature:**

- _Isa. 34:9-10._ "the dust thereof into brimstone, and the land thereof shall become burning pitch... the smoke thereof shall go up for ever" — in the day-of-the-LORD's-vengeance text, brimstone + burning + ascending smoke = the Sodom-stroke on the nations (verbatim root of Rev. 14:11).
- _Ps. 11:6._ "Upon the wicked he shall rain snares, fire and brimstone, and an horrible tempest: this shall be the portion of their cup" — fire-and-brimstone named the very "portion of their cup," binding the triad to the cup of wrath.
- _Eze. 38:22._ "I will rain upon him... great hailstones, fire, and brimstone" — Gog's overthrow by rained fire and brimstone, the eschatological war-judgment in the Sodom mold.
- _Rev. 14:10-11._ "tormented with fire and brimstone... the smoke of their torment ascendeth up for ever" — the triad lands in the final unmixed wrath: fire + brimstone + perpetual smoke, Sodom's stroke universalized.
- _Rev. 21:8._ "their part in the lake which burneth with fire and brimstone: which is the second death" — fire-and-brimstone as the ultimate executing judgment, the second death.
- VERDICT: **fire + smoke + brimstone together** is the Sodom-stroke of total, God-rained judgment (Gen. 19:24, 28; Luke 17:29) — its triad runs from Sodom through the day-of-the-LORD texts (Isa. 34; Ps. 11:6; Eze. 38:22) into the trumpet-war (Rev. 9:17-18) and the final unmixed wrath (Rev. 14:10-11), one and the same stroke each time.

## SYMBOL: plague (= stroke of war / judgment)

- _Rev. 9:20._ "the rest of the men which were not killed by these plagues yet repented not of the works of their hands" — the Holy Ghost names the sixth-trumpet _war-slaughter_ (Rev. 9:18) a "plague," fixing plague = war-stroke of judgment.
- _Rev. 9:18._ "By these three was the third part of men killed" — the killing of v. 18 is what v. 20 calls "these plagues": a plague is the lethal stroke of the trumpet-war.
- _Exod. 9:14._ "I will at this time send all my plagues upon thine heart, and upon thy servants, and upon thy people" — the Egypt template: a plague is a God-sent judicial stroke, the model the trumpets follow.
- _Rev. 16:9._ "God, which hath power over these plagues: and they repented not to give him glory" — the vials wear the identical word "plagues" and the identical "repented not" as the sixth trumpet, proving trumpet-stroke = vial-stroke = plague.
- _Rev. 15:1._ "seven angels having the seven last plagues; for in them is filled up the wrath of God" — the seven last plagues are the executing-wrath strokes, the same kind of judgment escalated to the finale.
- _Rev. 11:6._ "to smite the earth with all plagues, as often as they will" — a plague is a smiting judgment a commissioned agent inflicts on the earth.
- VERDICT: a **plague** is a God-sent stroke of judgment — in the trumpets a stroke of war (Rev. 9:18, 20), in the vials the unmixed executing wrath (Rev. 15:1; 16:9); trumpet = plague = vial, one kind of judgment.

## SYMBOL: cup / wine of wrath

- _Jer. 25:15-16._ "Take the wine cup of this fury at my hand, and cause all the nations... to drink it... they shall drink, and be moved, and be mad, because of the sword that I will send among them" — God's own definition: the cup IS the sword-judgment He sends among the nations.
- _Jer. 25:27._ "Drink ye, and be drunken... because of the sword which I will send among you" — the cup explicitly equated, a second time, with the sent sword; drinking the cup = receiving the war-judgment.
- _Isa. 51:17._ "which hast drunk at the hand of the LORD the cup of his fury; thou hast drunken the dregs of the cup of trembling" — the cup of fury administered from God's own hand, its dregs the full measure of judgment.
- _Jer. 51:7._ "Babylon hath been a golden cup in the LORD'S hand, that made all the earth drunken" — the conquering empire IS the cup in God's hand by which the nations drink the fury (root of Rev. 17:4's golden cup).
- _Ps. 75:8._ "in the hand of the LORD there is a cup, and the wine is red; it is full of mixture... the wicked of the earth shall... drink them" — the historical cup is "full of mixture" (judgment mingled with mercy), the deliberate foil to the unmixed cup of Rev. 14:10.
- _Rev. 14:10._ "drink of the wine of the wrath of God, which is poured out without mixture into the cup of his indignation" — the cup carried into the final wrath: now "without mixture," wrath with no mercy mingled in.
- _Rev. 16:19._ "to give unto her the cup of the wine of the fierceness of his wrath" — the seventh plague hands Babylon the cup, the lex-talionis turn (Hab. 2:16 fulfilled).
- _Hab. 2:16._ "the cup of the LORD'S right hand shall be turned unto thee" — the cup travels: she who made others drink must drink in turn, the transfer-principle behind Rev. 16:19; 18:6.
- _Obad. 1:16._ "so shall all the heathen drink continually... and they shall be as though they had not been" — the all-nations cup ends in non-existence (annihilation, not endless torment).
- VERDICT: the **cup / wine of wrath** is the sword-judgment God serves the nations from His own hand (Jer. 25:15-16, 27; Isa. 51:17) — administered through a conquering instrument-empire (Jer. 51:7), mingled with mercy in history (Ps. 75:8) but poured out "without mixture" in the final wrath (Rev. 14:10; 16:19).

## SYMBOL: rod of indignation

- _Isa. 10:5-6._ "O Assyrian, the rod of mine anger, and the staff in their hand is mine indignation. I will send him against an hypocritical nation... to take the spoil" — the explicit equation: a heathen power (Assyria) is the rod God raises to punish His apostate people.
- _Isa. 10:12._ "when the Lord hath performed his whole work upon mount Zion... I will punish the fruit of the stout heart of the king of Assyria" — the rod, once it has done God's chastening work, is itself judged: the woe-pattern of the trumpets exactly.
- _Jer. 50:17._ "the lions have driven him away: first the king of Assyria... and last this Nebuchadrezzar king of Babylon hath broken his bones" — successive heathen rods (Assyria, then Babylon) raised against erring Israel, the standing pattern.
- _Isa. 14:5-6._ "The LORD hath broken the staff of the wicked... He who smote the people in wrath with a continual stroke... that ruled the nations in anger" — the rod/staff named as the smiting power that ruled the nations in anger, then broken when its work is done.
- _Lam. 3:1._ "I am the man that hath seen affliction by the rod of his wrath" — the rod = the affliction-instrument of God's wrath, felt by the chastened people.
- _Isa. 30:27._ "the name of the LORD cometh from far, burning with his anger... his lips are full of indignation, and his tongue as a devouring fire" — indignation as the burning, devouring anger that the rod executes.
- VERDICT: a **rod of indignation** is a heathen war-power God raises up to chastise His apostate people, then judges in turn when its commission is done (Assyria/Babylon in type; the woe-powers in the trumpets).

## SYMBOL: day of the LORD (its trumpet-vocabulary signature)

- _Zeph. 1:16._ "A day of the trumpet and alarm against the fenced cities, and against the high towers" — the day of the LORD defined outright as "a day of the trumpet": its very signature is the war-alarm against walled cities.
- _Zeph. 1:14-15._ "The great day of the LORD is near... That day is a day of wrath, a day of trouble and distress, a day of wasteness and desolation, a day of darkness" — the full vocabulary cluster: wrath, desolation, darkness — the trumpet-day's character.
- _Joel 2:1._ "Blow ye the trumpet in Zion, and sound an alarm... for the day of the LORD cometh" — the duty of warning IS to blow the trumpet, because the day of the LORD is at hand: trumpet and the day are bound in one verse.
- _Isa. 13:6._ "Howl ye; for the day of the LORD is at hand; it shall come as a destruction from the Almighty" — the day announced with the alarm-cry "howl," coming as a destroying war.
- _Isa. 13:9._ "the day of the LORD cometh, cruel both with wrath and fierce anger, to lay the land desolate" — the day defined as wrath that lays the land desolate, the war-judgment in full.
- _Amos 5:18._ "Woe unto you that desire the day of the LORD!... the day of the LORD is darkness, and not light" — the day joined to "woe" and to "darkness," the same darkness the trumpet-smoke brings.
- _Joel 1:15._ "Alas for the day! for the day of the LORD is at hand, and as a destruction from the Almighty shall it come" — the alarm-cry "Alas" raised; the day is a destruction from the Almighty (twin of Isa. 13:6).
- _Acts 2:20._ "before that great and notable day of the Lord come" — Peter re-publishes the day-of-the-LORD signature (darkened sun, blood moon) at Pentecost, carrying it into the NT as a fixed, future term.
- VERDICT: the **day of the LORD** is the great war-judgment of God on the nations, and its standing signature is trumpet-vocabulary — "a day of the trumpet and alarm" (Zeph. 1:16), heralded by howl/alarm and marked by wrath, darkness, and desolation; the seven trumpets sound that day in installments, the seventh executing its finale.

## SYMBOL: days of Lot (addendum)

- _Luke 17:28._ "Likewise also as it was in the days of Lot; they did eat, they drank, they bought, they sold, they planted, they builded" — the days of Lot named the type of the last generation: ordinary life pursued to the very edge of the fire.
- _Luke 17:30._ "Even thus shall it be in the day when the Son of man is revealed" — Christ fixes the Sodom-escape as the express pattern of His revealing; the come-out / fly-the-fire appeal (v29 at the fire+smoke+brimstone cloud above) keyed to His return.

- Three keyword-coincidences were checked and **excluded** as non-defining: _Eze. 7:26_ (rumour/judgment but the word "woe" is absent), _Hab. 2:6_ (a human "taunting proverb," not the trumpet-woe), and _Rev. 16:11_ ("repented not" but the word "plague" is absent — use Rev. 16:9 for the vial-plague link).
- Sodom-token cross-checks (_2 Pet. 2:6_; _Jude 1:7_) confirm Sodom as the standing brimstone type-scene but were left out of the fire-smoke-brimstone cloud because neither verse names the triad's three members; the four verses that do (Gen. 19:24, 28; Luke 17:29; Deut. 29:23) carry it.
- Load-bearing clouds (smoke, locusts, torment, Euphrates, fire+smoke+brimstone, cup/wine of wrath, day of the LORD) run 6-9 verses, definitional-first; minor clouds (crowns, breastplates, five months, hour-day-month-year) run 2-5.
