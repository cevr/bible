# The Two Classes — A Bible Handbook Study

**Thesis.** From the gate of Eden God Himself divided mankind into two classes, and only two: "I will put enmity between thee and the woman, and between thy seed and her seed" (Gen. 3:15). The enmity is God-put; the classes are not races or ranks but worshipers — Abel, who brought the appointed lamb by faith, and Cain, who brought the fruit of his own hands and his own way. From that altar the two lines diverge and never merge: Cain went out from the presence of the LORD and builded a city; Seth's line began to call upon the name of the LORD, dwelt apart, and walked with God. The serpent's seed congregates — cities, plains, valleys, convenience, the arts of a civilization that forgets God — and attacks the institutions of Eden: marriage by polygamy, appetite by indulgence, and appointed worship by will-worship. The woman's seed are pilgrims and strangers — tents, altars, mountains, the wilderness — a peculiar people who worship in spirit and in truth, needing no city. And every time God's people have mingled with the world — the sons of God taking the daughters of men, Israel joined to Baal-peor and demanding a king "like all the nations," the church seeking the favor of the empire — the peculiar people lost their peculiarity, and the distinction had to be remade by judgment: the flood, the scattering, the wilderness. Christ fixed the type for the last age: "as it was in the days of Noah... as it was in the days of Lot." The two classes stand at last fully formed and fully visible — they that worship the beast and his image, and they that keep the commandments of God, and the faith of Jesus — and the final call of the everlasting gospel is the old call of the ark and of Sodom's gate: "Come out of her, my people."

**Method.** After Haskell's Bible Handbook: ref → gloss, scannable; every symbol and doctrine defined FIRST from Scripture (Miller's Rule — the Bible defines its own figures). The argument is built from the text — Genesis 3-6 (the two seeds, the two offerings, the two lineages, the mingling), Genesis 11-19 (Babel, Abraham the pilgrim, Lot in the plain), Numbers 22-25 and 1 Samuel 8 (Israel mingling), Hebrews 11 (the cloud of witnesses, pilgrims and strangers), Luke 17 and Matthew 24 (the days of Noah and of Lot), and Revelation 7, 12-14, 18 (the seal, the mark, the woman in the wilderness, the final call). # Part I — The Two Seeds

## 1. The Enmity — Two Seeds from One Fall

> At the gate of Eden God HIMSELF declares war — "I will put enmity" — and by one sentence divides mankind into exactly two seeds, the woman's and the serpent's. The enmity is grace, not curse: fallen man is naturally at peace with Satan, and the whole controversy from Abel's altar to the remnant is the history of that God-put antagonism — and of what happens when it is lost.

**The charter — God puts the enmity — Gen. 3:14-15:**

- _Gen. 3:14._ "And the LORD God said unto the serpent, Because thou hast done this, thou art cursed above all cattle, and above every beast of the field" — the verdict falls on the serpent first; the curse is his. What follows in v. 15 is spoken TO the serpent but given FOR the man: not more curse, but the first promise.
- _Gen. 3:15._ "And I will put enmity between thee and the woman, and between thy seed and her seed; it shall bruise thy head, and thou shalt bruise his heel" — the charter of the whole study. Mark the "I": God is the author of the enmity, not fallen man; and by one sentence He divides the race into exactly two seeds — the woman's and the serpent's — locked in a war that ends with a bruised heel and a crushed head.
  - **the enmity** = the God-PUT antagonism between the two seeds — grace, not curse. "I will put enmity" (_Gen. 3:15_): it is not native to fallen man, whose evil nature is at peace with the originator of sin; friendship with the world IS enmity with God (_Jas. 4:4_), and the world's hatred of the saints is this same enmity running outward, because Christ chose them out of the world (_John 15:19_). Defined in full at the close.
- _GC 505.2._ White: "God declares: 'I will put enmity.' This enmity is not naturally entertained. When man transgressed the divine law, his nature became evil, and he was in harmony, and not at variance, with Satan... Had not God specially interposed, Satan and man would have entered into an alliance against Heaven; and instead of cherishing enmity against Satan, the whole human family would have been united in opposition to God" — the exact grace-thesis, opening with the very words of _Gen. 3:15_: the fallen natural state is PEACE with the serpent; the enmity is God's special interposition, and without it the race is one seed only — Satan's.
- _GC 506.2._ White: "It is the grace that Christ implants in the soul which creates in man enmity against Satan. Without this converting grace and renewing power, man would continue the captive of Satan... But the new principle in the soul creates conflict where hitherto had been peace" — the enmity named outright as implanted grace: "conflict where hitherto had been peace." The antagonism is a gift, and the gift is Christ's; to feel no war with Satan is the mark of still belonging to him.

**The seed of the woman — Christ first, and His people in Him — Gal. 3:16, 29; Rom. 16:20; Matt. 13:37:**

- _Gal. 3:16._ "He saith not, And to seeds, as of many; but as of one, And to thy seed, which is Christ" — Scripture defines its own figure: the promised Seed is SINGULAR, and it is Christ. The seed of the woman is Christ first, before it is anyone else.
  - **the seed of the woman** = Christ first, and in Him His people. The singular "seed" is Christ (_Gal. 3:16_); those who are Christ's are then reckoned that same seed (_Gal. 3:29_); the Seed's head-bruising is shared with the church (_Rom. 16:20_); Christ Himself sows the class as "the good seed... the children of the kingdom" (_Matt. 13:37-38_); and the line runs to "the remnant of her seed" at the end (_Rev. 12:17_). Defined in full at the close.
- _Gal. 3:29._ "And if ye be Christ's, then are ye Abraham's seed, and heirs according to the promise" — in Him, His people become the seed. The class is defined by belonging to Christ, not by bloodline; membership is by union, not descent.
- _Rom. 16:20._ "And the God of peace shall bruise Satan under your feet shortly" — Gen. 3:15's head-bruising applied to the church: "under YOUR feet." The woman's seed includes the saints in Christ, who share the Seed's victory over the serpent.
- _Matt. 13:37._ "He that soweth the good seed is the Son of man" — the woman's seed is planted by the Son of man Himself; Christ both IS the Seed and SOWS the seed-class.

**The seed of the serpent — paternity by practice, not blood — John 8:44; Matt. 13:38-39; 1 John 3:12:**

- _John 8:44._ "Ye are of your father the devil, and the lusts of your father ye will do. He was a murderer from the beginning... When he speaketh a lie, he speaketh of his own: for he is a liar, and the father of it" — Christ defines the serpent's seed as SPIRITUAL paternity: religious men, sons of Abraham after the flesh, are "of your father the devil" because they DO his lusts. Fatherhood here is moral, not biological.
  - **the seed of the serpent** = not a bloodline but a spiritual paternity — those who belong to the devil by doing his works: "ye are of your father the devil" for "the lusts of your father ye will do" (_John 8:44_); the tares are "the children of the wicked one," sown by the devil (_Matt. 13:38-39_); Cain, a natural son of Eve, is "of that wicked one" (_1 John 3:12_); the standing test is practice (_1 John 3:8, 10_). Defined in full at the close.
- _Matt. 13:38-39._ "The field is the world; the good seed are the children of the kingdom; but the tares are the children of the wicked one; The enemy that sowed them is the devil; the harvest is the end of the world" — the two seeds are the two paternities: children of the kingdom and children of the wicked one, and no third planting. The classes grow together in one field until the harvest at the end of the world.
- _1 John 3:12._ "Not as Cain, who was of that wicked one, and slew his brother. And wherefore slew he him? Because his own works were evil, and his brother's righteous" — the proof-case: Cain, a physical son of Adam and Eve, is "of that wicked one." The first murderer belonged to the devil while sharing Abel's parents — the serpent's seed is by spirit, never by descent; and the murder itself is the enmity of the serpent's seed against the woman's.
- _COL 152.1._ White: "The Pharisee and the publican represent two great classes into which those who come to worship God are divided. Their first two representatives are found in the first two children that were born into the world" — the two classes traced to Cain and Abel at the altar: Cain "came to God with a thank offering only," but Abel "came with the blood that pointed to the Lamb of God" — the original heads of the serpent's line and the woman's.
- _PP 72.5._ White: "Cain and Abel represent two classes that will exist in the world till the close of time. One class avail themselves of the appointed sacrifice for sin; the other venture to depend upon their own merits" — exactly two classes, lasting "till the close of time," and the dividing question is the appointed sacrifice — the blood received or refused.
- _PP 74.2._ White: "Cain hated and killed his brother, not for any wrong that Abel had done, but 'because his own works were evil, and his brother's righteous.' 1 John 3:12. So in all ages the wicked have hated those who were better than themselves" — Ellen White cites John's verdict herself and generalizes it to "all ages": the serpent's seed hating the woman's is the standing pattern of history.

**The test of the two classes — doing, not descent — 1 John 3:8-10:**

- _1 John 3:8._ "He that committeth sin is of the devil; for the devil sinneth from the beginning. For this purpose the Son of God was manifested, that he might destroy the works of the devil" — the defining criterion of the serpent's seed: practice. And in the same breath the head-bruising of Gen. 3:15 restated: the Son of God manifested "that he might destroy the works of the devil" is the Seed come to crush the head.
- _1 John 3:9._ "Whosoever is born of God doth not commit sin; for his seed remaineth in him: and he cannot sin, because he is born of God" — the mirror truth for the woman's line: the divine seed "remaineth in him." The righteous class also stands by paternity — born of God, the seed abiding within — not by pedigree.
- _1 John 3:10._ "In this the children of God are manifest, and the children of the devil: whosoever doeth not righteousness is not of God, neither he that loveth not his brother" — the two classes named explicitly — "children of God" and "children of the devil" — and told apart by doing righteousness and loving the brethren. Scripture knows no third class.

**No neutral ground — to lose the enmity is to change sides — Jas. 4:4; John 15:19:**

- _Jas. 4:4._ "know ye not that the friendship of the world is enmity with God? whosoever therefore will be a friend of the world is the enemy of God" — there is no neutral ground. The enmity points one way or the other: to lose the God-put enmity toward the serpent's world is not to become neutral but to relocate the enmity — against God.
- _John 15:19._ "If ye were of the world, the world would love his own: but because ye are not of the world, but I have chosen you out of the world, therefore the world hateth you" — Christ traces the enmity forward: the world hates His people BECAUSE He has chosen them out of it. The hatred the woman's seed endures is the same God-put antagonism running outward — the hatred is the evidence of the grace.
- _SR 62.1._ White: "The descendants of Seth were called the sons of God; the descendants of Cain, the sons of men. As the sons of God mingled with the sons of men, they became corrupt and, by intermarriage with them, lost... their peculiar, holy character" — the historical anchor of the danger: MINGLING the two classes cost the woman's line its distinction. (The "sons of God" of Gen. 6:2 are here read as the covenant line of Seth, not angels: Adam was "the son of God" (_Luke 3:38_), and the covenant people are "the children of the LORD your God" (_Deut. 14:1_) — sonship by covenant standing, the reading the two-class frame requires; the mingling is traced in full in "The Mingling — How the Peculiar People Lost Their Peculiarity.")
- _PP 81.2._ White: "For some time the two classes remained separate... But in the lapse of time they ventured, little by little, to mingle with the inhabitants of the valleys. This association was productive of the worst results... they lost their peculiar, holy character" — the arc in detail: the Sethites withdrew to the mountains and were preserved; they mingled with the Cainite valleys "little by little" and were ruined. Separation preserved the enmity; incremental mingling dissolved it — the recurring ruin the enmity was put to prevent.

**The terminus — the dragon and the remnant of her seed — Rev. 12:9, 17:**

- _Rev. 12:9._ "And the great dragon was cast out, that old serpent, called the Devil, and Satan, which deceiveth the whole world" — Scripture's own identification, closing the chain: the dragon of Revelation 12 IS "that old serpent" of Eden. The war of Gen. 3:15 and the war of Rev. 12:17 are one war, with one enemy.
- _Rev. 12:17._ "And the dragon was wroth with the woman, and went to make war with the remnant of her seed, which keep the commandments of God, and have the testimony of Jesus Christ" — the end-time terminus of Gen. 3:15: the dragon (that old serpent — _Rev. 12:9_) wars on "the remnant of her seed" — the LAST of the woman's line, marked by commandment-keeping and the testimony of Jesus. The two-seed war opened at Eden runs unbroken to the close.
- _MWV2 213.1._ Miller: "it is evident by the expression 'remnant of her seed,' that it means the last of the church 'who keep the commandments of God, and have the testimony of Jesus Christ'" — the pioneer definition: the remnant of her seed = the commandment-keeping last of the church, the final generation of the woman's line under the serpent's final wrath.

**DEFINITION — THE ENMITY — TWO SEEDS FROM ONE FALL =** at the fall God Himself interposed an antagonism — "I will put enmity" (_Gen. 3:15_) — dividing mankind into exactly two seeds and only two. The seed of the woman is Christ first, the singular Seed of promise (_Gal. 3:16_), and in Him all who are His (_Gal. 3:29_), sown by the Son of man as "the children of the kingdom" (_Matt. 13:37-38_), sharing His bruising of the serpent's head — "under YOUR feet" (_Rom. 16:20_) — down to "the remnant of her seed" at the end (_Rev. 12:17_). The seed of the serpent is no bloodline but a paternity of practice: "ye are of your father the devil" because "the lusts of your father ye will do" (_John 8:44_); the tares are "the children of the wicked one," sown by the devil (_Matt. 13:38-39_); Cain, Eve's own son, was "of that wicked one" (_1 John 3:12_); "he that committeth sin is of the devil" (_1 John 3:8_), while the born of God carry the divine seed remaining in them (_1 John 3:9_) — "the children of God are manifest, and the children of the devil" (_1 John 3:10_), and Scripture admits no third class. The enmity between them is GRACE, not curse: fallen man's nature was "in harmony, and not at variance, with Satan," and without God's special interposition "the whole human family would have been united in opposition to God" (_GC 505.2_); it is "the grace that Christ implants in the soul" that "creates conflict where hitherto had been peace" (_GC 506.2_). Therefore no neutral ground exists — "the friendship of the world is enmity with God" (_Jas. 4:4_) — and the world's hatred of the saints is the same enmity running outward, for Christ has "chosen you out of the world" (_John 15:19_). History proves both edges: Cain and Abel head "two classes that will exist in the world till the close of time," divided over the appointed sacrifice (_PP 72.5_; _COL 152.1_; _PP 74.2_), and the antediluvian mingling of the Sethite and Cainite lines — "little by little" — dissolved the distinction and lost the holy character of the woman's seed (_PP 81.2_; _SR 62.1_). The standing danger this study traces is the losing of the enmity; the standing hope is that the war God declared at Eden He finishes — the dragon, "that old serpent" (_Rev. 12:9_), spends his last wrath on a remnant that still keeps the commandments of God and the testimony of Jesus (_Rev. 12:17_; _MWV2 213.1_). Two seeds, one war, no neutral ground: everything that follows in this handbook traces this single antagonism from Eden to the remnant.

**Symbols defined here:**

- **the enmity** = the God-put antagonism between the two seeds — grace, not curse: fallen man is naturally at peace with the serpent, so "I will put enmity" is a supernatural interposition of implanted grace, creating "conflict where hitherto had been peace"; to lose it by befriending the world is to become "the enemy of God," and the world's hatred of the saints is the same enmity running outward (_Gen. 3:15_; _Jas. 4:4_; _John 15:19_; _GC 505.2_; _GC 506.2_).
- **the seed of the woman** = Christ first, and in Him His people — the singular seed "which is Christ," reckoned to all who are Christ's, sown by the Son of man as the children of the kingdom, sharing the head-bruising victory, and terminating in "the remnant of her seed" who keep the commandments of God and have the testimony of Jesus (_Gen. 3:15_; _Gal. 3:16_; _Gal. 3:29_; _Matt. 13:37-38_; _Rom. 16:20_; _Rev. 12:17_).
- **the seed of the serpent** = a spiritual paternity, not a bloodline — those who belong to the devil by doing his works: "of your father the devil" by doing his lusts, "the children of the wicked one" sown as tares, Cain "of that wicked one" though Eve's own son; the test is practice — "he that committeth sin is of the devil," "whosoever doeth not righteousness is not of God" (_John 8:44_; _Matt. 13:38-39_; _1 John 3:12_; _1 John 3:8_; _1 John 3:10_).

**Symbols carried:** none — this is the opening section, establishing the charter (_Gen. 3:15_) on which the whole handbook stands: the two seeds, the paternity-by-practice that defines them, and the God-put enmity whose keeping or losing the following sections trace from Cain's altar to the dragon's war on the remnant.

What "little by little" minglings today most quietly dissolve the God-put enmity in a family or a church, and what would it cost this week to keep the line distinct?

## 2. Cain and Abel — the Two Worships

> The two classes first appear at an altar: two brothers, two offerings, two worships — the appointed lamb brought by faith, and the fruit of man's own way — and the enmity announced in Eden draws its first blood over worship.

**The enmity comes to the altar — two brothers, two callings — Gen. 3:15; 4:2:**

- _Gen. 3:15._ "I will put enmity between thee and the woman, and between thy seed and her seed" — the charter carried in from Eden (see "The Enmity — Two Seeds from One Fall"): the God-put enmity between the two seeds first draws blood at Cain's altar; the worship war is the seed war.
- _Gen. 4:2._ "And Abel was a keeper of sheep, but Cain was a tiller of the ground." — the two brothers, two callings: the shepherd who will bring a lamb and the tiller who will bring the ground's fruit. The two worships are seeded before the altar is built.

**The two offerings — the appointed lamb and the fruit of the ground — Gen. 4:3-5; John 1:29; Heb. 11:4; Col. 2:23:**

- _Gen. 4:3._ "And in process of time it came to pass, that Cain brought of the fruit of the ground an offering unto the LORD." — Cain's offering: the fruit of the ground — his own labor, his own way, bloodless. The Bible's first false worship, worship on man's terms.
  - **Cain's offering (the fruit of the ground)** = will-worship: the self-chosen, bloodless way substituted for God's appointed way, and refused of God (_Gen. 4:3_; _Gen. 4:5_; _Gen. 4:7_; _Col. 2:23_). Defined in full at the close.
- _Gen. 4:4._ "And Abel, he also brought of the firstlings of his flock and of the fat thereof. And the LORD had respect unto Abel and to his offering" — Abel's offering: the firstlings of the flock — the appointed lamb, brought by faith, pointing to the Lamb of God. God has respect to it; the appointed way is accepted.
  - **Abel's offering (the firstlings of his flock)** = the appointed lamb brought by faith — righteousness by faith enacted at the world's first altar (_Gen. 4:4_; _Heb. 11:4_; _John 1:29_; _Gen. 3:15_). Defined in full at the close.
- _John 1:29._ "Behold the Lamb of God, which taketh away the sin of the world." — defines what Abel's firstling meant: the appointed lamb was the shadow of THE Lamb of God. To bring the lamb by faith was to confess the coming Redeemer; to withhold it was to reject Him.
- _Heb. 11:4._ "By faith Abel offered unto God a more excellent sacrifice than Cain, by which he obtained witness that he was righteous, God testifying of his gifts" — heaven's verdict on the two altars: by FAITH Abel's was more excellent. The difference was faith in the appointed sacrifice, not the quality of produce — righteousness by faith at the first altar.
- _Gen. 4:5._ "But unto Cain and to his offering he had not respect. And Cain was very wroth, and his countenance fell." — the self-chosen way is refused, and the refused worshiper turns wroth. The refusal, not favoritism, brings the Eden-planted enmity of _Gen. 3:15_ to its first outbreak at the altar.
- _Col. 2:23._ "Which things have indeed a shew of wisdom in will worship" — Scripture's name for self-devised worship — the principle of Cain's offering: WILL worship — worship devised by the human will, wearing a shew of wisdom yet refused by God. Cain's fruit-offering is the archetype.
- _PP 71.2._ White: "they were to show their faith in the blood of Christ as the promised atonement by offering the firstlings of the flock in sacrifice. Besides this, the first fruits of the earth were to be presented before the Lord as a thank offering" — defines the two offerings from the appointment itself: the firstling was the God-ordained faith-offering pointing to Christ's blood; the fruit was only a thank offering, never a substitute for the lamb.
- _PP 71.3._ White: "Abel presented a sacrifice from the flock, in accordance with the Lord's directions... But Cain, disregarding the Lord's direct and explicit command, presented only an offering of fruit" — the contrast fixed: obedience to God's prescribed worship versus disregard of a direct command.
- _3SG 48.1._ White: "Abel brought of the firstlings of his flock, and of the fat as God had commanded; and in full faith of the Messiah to come... A light flashes from Heaven and consumes the offering of Abel. Cain sees no manifestation that his is accepted" — the early (1864) witness: the firstling brought "as God had commanded" in faith of the coming Messiah, heavenly fire on the appointed offering and none on Cain's.
- _PP 72.1._ White: "He chose the course of self-dependence. He would come in his own merits... Cain obeyed in building an altar, obeyed in bringing a sacrifice; but he rendered only a partial obedience. The essential part, the recognition of the need of a Redeemer, was left out" — names the will-worship exactly: self-dependence, his own merits, partial obedience omitting faith in the Redeemer (_Col. 2:23_).
- _PP 72.3._ White: "Through the shed blood he looked to the future sacrifice, Christ dying on the cross of Calvary; and trusting in the atonement that was there to be made, he had the witness that he was righteous" — confirms _Heb. 11:4_: Abel's offering was righteousness by faith, the shed blood looking forward to Calvary.

**The door stood open — the rejection was of the way, not the man — Gen. 4:7; Prov. 14:12; John 4:23:**

- _Gen. 4:7._ "If thou doest well, shalt thou not be accepted? and if thou doest not well, sin lieth at the door." — God's own testimony that Cain was not the victim of arbitrary rejection: the door to acceptance stood open; the issue was doing well — coming God's appointed way.
- _PP 72.4._ White: "Cain had the same opportunity of learning and accepting these truths as had Abel. He was not the victim of an arbitrary purpose... Abel chose faith and obedience; Cain, unbelief and rebellion. Here the whole matter rested" — guards _Gen. 4:7_: the rejection was of Cain's chosen way, not an arbitrary decree; two worships, two free choices.
- _Prov. 14:12._ "There is a way which seemeth right unto a man, but the end thereof are the ways of death." — the way of Cain in a proverb: a self-chosen worship that seems right to the man ends in death — first Abel's, then, in the war it opens, the worshiper's own.
- _John 4:23._ "the true worshippers shall worship the Father in spirit and in truth: for the Father seeketh such to worship him." — the permanent test surfaced at Cain's altar: God seeks worship in spirit and in TRUTH — His appointed way — not worship on the worshiper's own invented terms.
- _PP 73.1._ White: "The class of worshipers who follow the example of Cain includes by far the greater portion of the world; for nearly every false religion has been based on the same principle—that man can depend upon his own efforts for salvation" — the fountainhead named: Cain's altar heads the world's false worship, self-effort without the blood.

**False worship persecutes true — the first martyr dies over worship — Gen. 4:8; 1 John 3:12; Matt. 23:35:**

- _Gen. 4:8._ "Cain rose up against Abel his brother, and slew him." — false worship persecutes true worship: the first murder is a worship murder, the rejected worshiper killing the accepted one. The first martyr dies over worship.
- _1 John 3:12._ "Not as Cain, who was of that wicked one, and slew his brother. And wherefore slew he him? Because his own works were evil, and his brother's righteous." — the motive named: Cain was OF that wicked one — the serpent's seed by spiritual paternity (see "The Enmity — Two Seeds from One Fall") — and he slew because the righteous worship condemned his own.
- _Matt. 23:35._ "from the blood of righteous Abel unto the blood of Zacharias son of Barachias, whom ye slew between the temple and the altar." — Christ names "righteous Abel" the first of the martyrs' line: the persecuting stream of false worship runs unbroken from Abel's blood to blood shed at the temple altar itself.
- _PP 74.2._ White: "Cain hated and killed his brother, not for any wrong that Abel had done... So in all ages the wicked have hated those who were better than themselves. Abel's life of obedience and unswerving faith was to Cain a perpetual reproof" — the persecution principle of all ages: the righteous life reproves the false worshiper, and the false worshiper answers with the sword.

**Out from the presence — the way of Cain becomes a lineage — Gen. 4:16; Jude 1:11:**

- _Gen. 4:16._ "And Cain went out from the presence of the LORD, and dwelt in the land of Nod, on the east of Eden." — the self-appointed worshiper departs from God's presence and founds a rival line; the going-out that follows refused worship.
- _Jude 1:11._ "Woe unto them! for they have gone in the way of Cain" — "the way of Cain" becomes a named, transmissible apostasy, set beside Balaam's hire and Korah's rebellion against appointed worship. A woe rests on all who walk it.
  - **the way of Cain** = self-appointed worship that hardens into a lineage and ends in persecuting the true (_Jude 1:11_; _1 John 3:12_; _Gen. 4:8_; _Matt. 23:35_). Defined in full at the close.
- _PP 81.1._ White: "He had gone out from the presence of the Lord... thus standing at the head of that great class of men who worship the god of this world" — _Gen. 4:16_ as the founding of a rival worship-line: Cain heads "that great class of men who worship the god of this world."
- _COL 200.2._ White: "As Cain went out from the presence of the Lord to seek his home; as the prodigal wandered into the 'far country,' so do sinners seek happiness in forgetfulness of God" — Cain's going-out is the standing type of the sinner's self-chosen separation from God's presence.
- _PP 81.2._ White: "For some time the two classes remained separate... The children of Seth went 'in the way of Cain' (Jude 11); they fixed their minds upon worldly prosperity and enjoyment and neglected the commandments of the Lord" — names "the two classes" outright and shows the way of Cain is transmissible even to the covenant line: true worship kept pure by distinction, corrupted by mingling (traced in "The Mingling — How the Peculiar People Lost Their Peculiarity").

**DEFINITION — CAIN AND ABEL — THE TWO WORSHIPS =** the two seeds of _Gen. 3:15_ first stand visible at an altar, as two worships. One generation from Eden, the shepherd and the tiller bring their offerings (_Gen. 4:2-4_): Abel the firstlings of the flock — the appointed lamb, brought by faith, confessing the coming Lamb of God (_Heb. 11:4_; _John 1:29_) — and Cain the fruit of the ground, his own labor offered his own way, the Bible's first false worship (_Gen. 4:3_). God has respect to the appointed way and not to the self-chosen one (_Gen. 4:4-5_), yet the rejection is of the way, not the man: "If thou doest well, shalt thou not be accepted?" (_Gen. 4:7_; _PP 72.4_). Scripture names Cain's principle "will worship" — a shew of wisdom, refused by God (_Col. 2:23_) — the way that "seemeth right unto a man" but ends in death (_Prov. 14:12_), the standing opposite of worship "in spirit and in truth" (_John 4:23_); and Ellen White names its posterity: "the class of worshipers who follow the example of Cain includes by far the greater portion of the world" (_PP 73.1_). Refused worship turns persecutor: Cain, "of that wicked one," slays his brother "because his own works were evil, and his brother's righteous" (_1 John 3:12_; _Gen. 4:8_) — the serpent-seed's signature attack on God's appointed worship, and the opening of the martyr stream Christ dates "from the blood of righteous Abel" (_Matt. 23:35_). Cain then "went out from the presence of the LORD" (_Gen. 4:16_) to head "that great class of men who worship the god of this world" (_PP 81.1_), and his way becomes a named, walkable apostasy — "the way of Cain" (_Jude 1:11_) — self-appointed worship that persecutes the true. Here the whole controversy is cast in miniature: two seeds, two altars, two worships — the appointed way accepted, the self-chosen way refused, and the refused slaying the accepted — the pattern that runs in blood from Abel's field and terminates at the end of the age in the final contest over worship, the seal or the mark (see "The Seal or the Mark — the Distinction Made Visible"). The two worships become two civilizations in "The Two Lineages — the City and the Name."

**Symbols defined here:**

- **Abel's offering (the firstlings of his flock)** = the appointed lamb brought by faith — righteousness by faith enacted at the world's first altar, the firstling's blood confessing the coming Lamb of God who takes away sin; accepted because it comes God's appointed way, trusting wholly in the promised atonement (_Gen. 4:4_; _Heb. 11:4_; _John 1:29_; _Gen. 3:15_).
- **Cain's offering (the fruit of the ground)** = will-worship: the self-chosen, bloodless way substituted for God's appointed way — the first false worship; partial obedience (an altar, a gift) that omits the essential thing, faith in the promised Redeemer's blood, and comes in the worshiper's own merits, and to which God has not respect (_Gen. 4:3_; _Gen. 4:5_; _Gen. 4:7_; _Col. 2:23_).
- **the way of Cain** = self-appointed worship that hardens into a lineage and ends in persecuting the true — the false worshiper hating and slaying the true because his own works are evil and his brother's righteous; the pattern that opens the martyr stream from righteous Abel onward (_Jude 1:11_; _1 John 3:12_; _Gen. 4:8_; _Matt. 23:35_).

**Symbols carried:** the seed of the woman, the seed of the serpent, and the enmity — the God-put antagonism here drawing its first blood, Cain being "of that wicked one" (see "The Enmity — Two Seeds from One Fall"); Cain's going out from the presence of the LORD, which becomes the first city and the rival civilization (see "The Two Lineages — the City and the Name"); the corruption of the covenant line that later walks "in the way of Cain" (see "The Mingling — How the Peculiar People Lost Their Peculiarity"); and the worship war's terminus, where the two worships stand fully formed as they that worship the beast and they that keep the commandments of God and the faith of Jesus (see "The Seal or the Mark — the Distinction Made Visible").

**For discussion:**

1. Cain "obeyed in building an altar, obeyed in bringing a sacrifice; but he rendered only a partial obedience" (_PP 72.1_), and God told him the door stood open: "If thou doest well, shalt thou not be accepted?" (_Gen. 4:7_). Where in our own worship do we bring the altar and the gift but withhold "the essential part, the recognition of the need of a Redeemer" — and how would we know?
2. "By faith Abel offered unto God a more excellent sacrifice than Cain" (_Heb. 11:4_) — the difference was not the produce but faith in the appointed Lamb (_John 1:29_). What does it mean that righteousness by faith is enacted at the very first altar in Scripture, and how does that guard us from thinking acceptance with God was ever, in any age, by man's own efforts (_PP 73.1_)?
3. Cain slew Abel "because his own works were evil, and his brother's righteous" (_1 John 3:12_), and "Abel's life of obedience and unswerving faith was to Cain a perpetual reproof" (_PP 74.2_). If false worship still answers the reproof of the righteous with hostility, what should the accepted worshiper expect (_Matt. 23:35_) — and how does Abel's altar, not Cain's sword, remain his only weapon?

## 3. The Two Lineages — the City and the Name

> The two worships become two civilizations: Cain's line goes out from the Presence and builds a city; Seth's line stays and calls upon the name of the LORD — and from Genesis 4 to Revelation the war of the two classes runs on between the city and the Name.

**From the altar to the city — the worship-war becomes a civilization — Gen. 4:3, 5, 16-17:**

- _Gen. 4:3._ "Cain brought of the fruit of the ground an offering unto the LORD" — the self-appointed offering: worship on man's own terms, the fruit of the cursed ground and his own labor, refusing the blood (the first false worship, defined in "Cain and Abel — the Two Worships"). The city-line's religion begins at this altar.
- _Gen. 4:5._ "But unto Cain and to his offering he had not respect. And Cain was very wroth, and his countenance fell" — God's non-respect for the self-willed offering ignites the wrath that becomes murder (v. 8) and then a civilization (v. 17). The two lineages divide first at the altar, then spread into two peoples.
- _COL 152.1._ White: "The Pharisee and the publican represent two great classes... Their first two representatives are found in the first two children that were born into the world" — the perennial two classes of worshipers are rooted in the two offerings; what Genesis 4 now traces is those two worships hardening into two lineages.
- _Gen. 4:16._ "And Cain went out from the presence of the LORD, and dwelt in the land of Nod, on the east of Eden" — the city-line begins with a departure. Everything Cain builds afterward is built by a fugitive from the face of God; he settles away from the Presence, and the geography of the two classes is born here.
- _Gen. 4:17._ "he builded a city, and called the name of the city, after the name of his son, Enoch" — the first city in Scripture: built by the first murderer, self-named and self-perpetuating. Cain, cut off from the Name, makes a name.
  - **the city of Cain** = the serpent-seed's congregation — fortified convenience built by the fugitive from God's presence: man staying put in the cursed earth, distinguished in arts, industry, and violence-as-culture while God is forgotten; the civilization whose spirit prevails in the eating-drinking-marrying world Christ names as the type of the last age (_Gen. 4:16-17_; _Gen. 4:21-23_; _Matt. 24:38_; _PP 81.1_).
- _PP 81.1._ White: "He had gone out from the presence of the Lord, cast away the promise of the restored Eden, to seek his possessions and enjoyment in the earth under the curse of sin, thus standing at the head of that great class of men who worship the god of this world" — Cain heads a CLASS, not merely a family: the worshipers of the god of this world, seeking their portion in the earth under the curse.

**The city's culture — the home broken, the arts advancing, violence sung — Gen. 4:19-24; Gen. 2:24; Matt. 19:4-8:**

- _Gen. 4:19._ "And Lamech took unto him two wives: the name of the one was Adah, and the name of the other Zillah" — the first polygamist, fifth in Cain's line. The serpent-seed's assault on the FIRST institution of Eden — marriage — is now culture, not lapse.
- _Gen. 2:24._ "Therefore shall a man leave his father and his mother, and shall cleave unto his wife: and they shall be one flesh" — the Eden charter Lamech breaks: one man, one wife, one flesh. The war of the two lineages runs through the home, not merely the altar.
- _Matt. 19:4._ "he which made them at the beginning made them male and female" — Christ roots marriage in creation; the Cainite innovation is a departure from the order the Maker fixed.
- _Matt. 19:6._ "What therefore God hath joined together, let not man put asunder" — Lamech's two wives put asunder what God joined: the city-line remaking the home to suit itself.
- _Matt. 19:8._ "from the beginning it was not so" — Christ's rule against every corruption of marriage back to Eden; the Cainite polygamy is judged by the original, unbroken pattern.
- _Gen. 4:21._ "he was the father of all such as handle the harp and organ" — Jubal: music and culture flourish inside the city of Cain — refinement without repentance, arts advancing while God is forgotten.
- _Gen. 4:22._ "Tubalcain, an instructer of every artificer in brass and iron" — metallurgy and industry, the technological genius of the Cainite city. The line godless in worship is distinguished in earthly and material progress.
- _PP 81.1._ White: "In that which pertains to mere earthly and material progress, his descendants became distinguished... To the crime of murder, in which Cain had led the way, Lamech, the fifth in descent, added polygamy" — the two marks of the city named together: material brilliance, and the broken home.
- _Gen. 4:23._ "for I have slain a man to my wounding, and a young man to my hurt" — Lamech's sword-song: violence set to poetry, boasted openly to his two wives. Cain's murder has become the city's culture; bloodshed is now sung, not hidden.
- _Gen. 4:24._ "If Cain shall be avenged sevenfold, truly Lamech seventy and sevenfold" — God's protective word over Cain twisted into a boast of self-vengeance. The city-line's religion is self-serving even where it names God.

**The Name — the faithful seed re-established — Gen. 4:25-26; Luke 3:38:**

- _Gen. 4:25._ "For God, said she, hath appointed me another seed instead of Abel, whom Cain slew" — the faithful seed re-established in Seth: "another seed" in the room of the martyr. The two lineages are now formally set — the line of the murderer and the line of the appointed seed.
- _Luke 3:38._ "Which was the son of Enos, which was the son of Seth, which was the son of Adam, which was the son of God" — the genealogy of Christ runs through Enos and Seth to "the son of God": the Sethite stream is the covenant line, the lineage of the promised Seed of "The Enmity — Two Seeds from One Fall."
- _Gen. 4:26._ "then began men to call upon the name of the LORD" — the dividing act of the faithful seed. Cain named a city after his son; Seth's line calls on the Name of the LORD. Not a wall but a confessed allegiance marks off the faithful.
  - **calling on the name of the LORD** = the dividing act of the faithful seed — open, avowed worship: a public profession of loyalty to God that marks off the Sethite line from the city; its living crown is Enoch, who walked with God in the corrupt age and preached judgment to the very city of Cain (_Gen. 4:26_; _Gen. 5:24_; _Jude 1:14-15_).
- _PP 80.2._ White: "The faithful had worshiped God before; but as men increased, the distinction between the two classes became more marked. There was an open profession of loyalty to God on the part of one, as there was of contempt and disobedience on the part of the other" — the definition confirmed: what began at Enos was not worship itself but MARKED worship, the open profession that makes the two classes visible.

**The crown of the calling line — Enoch, the seventh from Adam — Gen. 5:22, 24; Jude 1:14-15:**

- _Gen. 5:22._ "And Enoch walked with God after he begat Methuselah three hundred years, and begat sons and daughters" — the walk was not a hermit's trance but the whole tenor of a working life: husband, father, citizen, three hundred years of communion in the heart of a corrupt age.
- _PP 85.1._ White: "Enoch's walk with God was not in a trance or vision, but in all the duties of his daily life... as a husband and father, a friend, a citizen, he was the steadfast, unwavering servant of the Lord" — calling on the Name embodied: open, avowed faithfulness lived out in the ordinary offices of life.
- _GW 52.1._ White: "In the midst of a life of active labor, Enoch steadfastly maintained his communion with God... he would withdraw, to spend a season in solitude" — Enoch's rhythm of withdrawal and return is the personal form of the Sethite separation: apart to commune, back to labor.
- _GW 51.2._ White: "The infinite, unfathomable love of God through Christ, became the subject of his meditations day and night. With all the fervor of his soul he sought to reveal that love to the people among whom he dwelt" — the inner spring of the walk: communion kept apart, then poured out toward the people — separation that exists for testimony, not escape.
- _Gen. 5:24._ "And Enoch walked with God: and he was not; for God took him" — the crown of the Sethite stream: the man who held the upward current while the world flowed the other way is translated. Proof that the calling-on-the-Name line can be kept pure in the midst of the city's age.
- _Jude 1:14._ "And Enoch also, the seventh from Adam, prophesied of these, saying, Behold, the Lord cometh with ten thousands of his saints" — the New Testament fixes Enoch as the seventh from Adam and the first advent preacher: the faithful line's witness is judgment-preaching from the beginning.
- _MWV2 17.3._ Miller: "Jude tells us that Enoch, the seventh from Adam, prophesied, saying, Behold the Lord cometh, with ten thousands of his saints" — the pioneer of the advent movement stands Enoch at the head of his own line of evidence: the second-advent hope is as old as the seventh from Adam.
- _Jude 1:15._ "To execute judgment upon all, and to convince all that are ungodly among them" — Enoch's message was a warning of judgment on the ungodly city-line: the faithful seed does not merely withdraw; it testifies against the civilization it separates from.
- _PP 86.1._ White: "His labors were not restricted to the Sethites. In the land where Cain had sought to flee from the divine Presence, the prophet of God made known the wonderful scenes that had passed before his vision" — separation is not silence: the mountain-line carried the warning down into the very land of Cain's flight.

**Mountains and plains — the geography of the two classes — Gen. 4:16; 6:1-2:**

- **mountains and plains** = the geography of the two classes: the Cainites, spreading from their first settlement away from the Presence, dispersed over the plains and valleys; the Sethites, to escape their contaminating influence, withdrew to the mountains — God's people dwelling apart, the serpent's seed congregating for convenience. This is the geography of SEPARATION, not of appointed worship — worship is "in spirit and in truth" wherever the faithful are (_John 4:21-24_); the mountain is where the peculiar character is kept, not where God is uniquely found. Scripture gives the pattern its own voice in the angel's word to Lot, "Escape to the mountain, lest thou be consumed" (_Gen. 19:17_; taken up in "Abraham and Lot — the Tent and the Plain") (_Gen. 4:16_; _Gen. 6:1_; _Gen. 6:2_; _Gen. 19:17_).
- _PP 81.2._ White: "For some time the two classes remained separate. The race of Cain... dispersed over the plains and valleys where the children of Seth had dwelt; and the latter, in order to escape from their contaminating influence, withdrew to the mountains, and there made their home. So long as this separation continued, they maintained the worship of God in its purity" — THE ANCHOR: "the two classes" named outright, the mountain/plain geography given exactly, and the doctrinal weight stated — separation preserved pure worship.
- _Gen. 6:1._ "when men began to multiply on the face of the earth, and daughters were born unto them" — the setting for the fatal breakdown of the separation the mountains had kept.
- _Gen. 6:2._ "the sons of God saw the daughters of men that they were fair; and they took them wives of all which they chose" — the sons of God = the covenant line of Seth (_Luke 3:38_), not angels; the descent from the mountains to marry the city dissolves the peculiar character of the faithful (walked in full in "The Mingling — How the Peculiar People Lost Their Peculiarity").
- _Gen. 6:5._ "every imagination of the thoughts of his heart was only evil continually" — the moral fruit of the merged lineages: when the two classes mingle, the Cainite spirit prevails, and the whole earth is condemned.
- _GC 65.3._ White: "Behind the lofty bulwarks of the mountains—in all ages the refuge of the persecuted and oppressed—the Waldenses found a hiding place. Here the light of truth was kept burning amid the darkness of the Middle Ages" — the geography recurs through history: the mountains keep the faith while the plains keep the world (traced in "The Church in the Wilderness — the Mountains Keep the Faith").

**The end-time antitype — the city civilization named for the last days — Matt. 24:38:**

- _Matt. 24:38._ "For as in the days that were before the flood they were eating and drinking, marrying and giving in marriage, until the day that Noe entered into the ark" — Christ names the world the Cainite city-spirit conquered as the type of the last age. Note what damns it: not open atheism but appetite and self-indulgent normalcy — eating, drinking, marrying — the city of Cain's spirit running at full culture until the door shuts.
- _DA 633.1._ White: "Christ does not here bring to view a temporal millennium... He tells us that as it was in Noah's day, so will it be when the Son of man comes again" — the two-lineages pattern is prophetically appointed, not merely historical: the last generation replays the city and the Name.
- _TTAM 6.1._ J. White: "In the third angel's message... but two classes are brought to view. One is oppressive, and persecutes the saints, and has the mark of the beast... The other class is oppressed and driven... and are sealed with the seal of the living God by keeping 'the commandments of God'" — the pioneer witness confirms independently that at the end only two classes exist: the marked and the sealed. That this final division is the antitype of Genesis 4's two lineages is this handbook's argument, drawn out in "The Seal or the Mark — the Distinction Made Visible."

**DEFINITION — THE TWO LINEAGES — THE CITY AND THE NAME =** the two worships of the first altar (_Gen. 4:3-5_) harden into two civilizations. Cain, having "gone out from the presence of the LORD" (_Gen. 4:16_), "builded a city" (_Gen. 4:17_) — the first in Scripture, built by a fugitive and named for his son — and his line fills it with the marks of the class: the home broken by Lamech's polygamy against the one-flesh charter of Eden (_Gen. 4:19_; _Gen. 2:24_; _Matt. 19:4-8_), the arts and industry of Jubal and Tubalcain advancing while God is forgotten (_Gen. 4:21-22_; _PP 81.1_), and violence sung as culture in Lamech's sword-song (_Gen. 4:23-24_). Over against the city stands the Name: the faithful seed re-established in Seth, "another seed instead of Abel" (_Gen. 4:25_), the covenant line of the genealogy of Christ (_Luke 3:38_), marked off not by a wall but by open, avowed worship — "then began men to call upon the name of the LORD" (_Gen. 4:26_; _PP 80.2_) — and crowned in Enoch, the seventh from Adam, who "walked with God" through all the duties of a working life (_Gen. 5:22, 24_; _PP 85.1_) and carried the first advent-and-judgment sermon into the very land of Cain's flight (_Jude 1:14-15_; _PP 86.1_; _MWV2 17.3_). The two classes take a geography: Cainites over the plains and valleys, Sethites withdrawn to the mountains, and "so long as this separation continued, they maintained the worship of God in its purity" (_PP 81.2_) — until the sons of God came down to marry the daughters of men and the merged earth grew "only evil continually" (_Gen. 6:1-2, 5_). Christ appoints this whole picture to the last days: "as in the days that were before the flood they were eating and drinking, marrying and giving in marriage" (_Matt. 24:38_; _DA 633.1_), and the pioneer witness confirms that at the end "but two classes are brought to view," the marked and the sealed (_TTAM 6.1_). Every soul stands in the city or calls on the Name; there is no third lineage.

# Part II — The Mingling and the Flood

## 4. The Mingling — How the Peculiar People Lost Their Peculiarity

> The peculiar people were not destroyed by persecution but dissolved by alliance: the sons of God saw, chose by sight, and took wives of the daughters of men — and when the two classes became one flesh, the distinction God had put between them was gone, "all flesh had corrupted his way," and the faithful stream shrank to eight souls. The mingling is the standing mechanism of apostasy: the world never rises to the church; the church always descends to the world.

**The two lines before the union — pilgrims and the world-worshiping class — Gen. 2:24; 4:19; Deut. 14:1-2:**

- _Gen. 2:24._ "Therefore shall a man leave his father and his mother, and shall cleave unto his wife: and they shall be one flesh" — Eden's marriage institution: one man, one wife, one flesh. This is the standard against which the ruin of this section is measured; the attack falls not on a human custom but on a gift from before sin.
- _Gen. 4:19._ "And Lamech took unto him two wives: the name of the one was Adah, and the name of the other Zillah" — polygamy begins in Cain's line, the fifth from the fugitive who "went out from the presence of the LORD" (_Gen. 4:16_; see "Cain and Abel — the Two Worships"). Before the mingling, the corruption of marriage is confined to the serpent's seed; the mingling is how it crosses the line.
- _Deut. 14:1-2._ "Ye are the children of the LORD your God... For thou art an holy people unto the LORD thy God, and the LORD hath chosen thee to be a peculiar people unto himself, above all the nations that are upon the earth" — Scripture's own idiom: "children of the LORD" names the professed covenant people, and their calling is PECULIARITY — set apart above the nations. This is the very thing the mingling dissolves.
- _PP 81.1._ White: "thus standing at the head of that great class of men who worship the god of this world.... To the crime of murder, in which Cain had led the way, Lamech, the fifth in descent, added polygamy.... the descendants of Seth... counting themselves 'strangers and pilgrims on the earth'" — the two lines drawn before the union: Cain's, the world-worshiping class with polygamy already in it; Seth's, the pilgrim people.
- _GSAM 40.1._ Loughborough: "Afterward Seth was counted in the line of descent from Adam.... Cain, the real first-born, is not counted in the pedigree.... after the birth of Seth, men began to call themselves by the name of the Lord" — the pioneer witness to the historical fact of two genealogical streams: Seth counted in the covenant pedigree, Cain excluded, the Sethites bearing the Lord's name (see "The Two Lineages — the City and the Name").

**Scripture defines its own phrase — who are the sons of God? — Gen. 6:1-2; Luke 3:38; Matt. 22:30:**

- _Gen. 6:1-2._ "And it came to pass, when men began to multiply on the face of the earth, and daughters were born unto them, That the sons of God saw the daughters of men that they were fair; and they took them wives of all which they chose" — the text of the ruin. Two classes, one act: the sons of God SAW, judged by the sight of the eyes ("fair"), and TOOK "wives of all which they chose" — choice unbounded by God's counsel, and in "all which they chose" the plural liberty of Lamech now practiced in the faithful line.
  - **the sons of God** = the covenant line of Seth, the professed people of God. Scripture defines the phrase in this very genealogy: Adam is "the son of God" (_Luke 3:38_), and the covenant people are "the children of the LORD your God," a "peculiar people" (_Deut. 14:1-2_) — read literally, per Miller's Rule, not angels, "for in the resurrection they neither marry, nor are given in marriage, but are as the angels of God in heaven" (_Matt. 22:30_) (_Gen. 6:2_; _Luke 3:38_; _Deut. 14:1_; _Matt. 22:30_).
  - **the daughters of men** = the Cainite line, the world — the daughters of the "men" who "began to multiply on the face of the earth" (_Gen. 6:1_), the class descended from the man who "went out from the presence of the LORD" (_Gen. 4:16_), into which polygamy had already entered (_Gen. 4:19_) (_Gen. 6:1-2_; _Gen. 4:16_; _Gen. 4:19_).
- _Luke 3:38._ "Which was the son of Enos, which was the son of Seth, which was the son of Adam, which was the son of God" — Scripture's own definition, planted in the antediluvian genealogy itself: "son of God" is covenant language for Adam and his faithful line. The Bible defines its own figures; no angel-mythology is needed or allowed.
- _Matt. 22:30._ "For in the resurrection they neither marry, nor are given in marriage, but are as the angels of God in heaven" — Christ's own testimony closes the door on the angel reading of _Gen. 6:2_: angels do not marry. The widespread "sons of God = angels" interpretation is excluded on Scripture's own terms — the sons of God are men of the covenant line, and the tragedy of Genesis 6 is a CHURCH tragedy, not a monster story.
- _CTr 39.2._ White: "The worshipers of God called themselves the sons of God," and "there were two parties in the world then, and there always will be" — the most direct inspired confirmation of the Sethite reading: the phrase of _Gen. 6:2_ is the self-designation of the worshipers of God, and the two-party division it records is universalized — "there always will be." The Genesis 6 division is the division of this whole study (see "The Enmity — Two Seeds from One Fall").

**The mechanism of ruin — saw, chose, took — Gen. 6:2; PP 81.2; AH 121.3:**

- _Gen. 6:2._ "the sons of God saw the daughters of men that they were fair; and they took them wives of all which they chose" — mark the verbs: SAW (attraction), CHOSE (alliance), TOOK (descent). Not one word of persecution, compulsion, or heresy — the peculiar people were not conquered; they married.
  - **the mingling** = the alliance of the two classes by which the peculiar people lose their peculiarity — the standing mechanism of apostasy. The sons of God chose by sight and took "wives of all which they chose" (_Gen. 6:2_), and the end was that "all flesh had corrupted his way" (_Gen. 6:12_); the abiding law is one-directional — the yoke of light with darkness is forbidden (_2 Cor. 6:14_), "evil communications corrupt good manners" (_1 Cor. 15:33_), "the friendship of the world is enmity with God" (_Jas. 4:4_) — the church descends, the world never rises (_Gen. 6:1-5_; _Gen. 6:12_; _2 Cor. 6:14_; _1 Cor. 15:33_; _Jas. 4:4_).
- _PP 81.2._ White: "For some time the two classes remained separate.... the latter, in order to escape from their contaminating influence, withdrew to the mountains, and there made their home.... But in the lapse of time they ventured, little by little, to mingle with the inhabitants of the valleys.... The children of Seth, attracted by the beauty of the daughters of Cain's descendants, displeased the Lord by intermarrying with them.... they lost their peculiar, holy character" — the load-bearing anchor, and the section's title in her own words: the separation held, the descent came "little by little," the attraction was by beauty, the act was intermarriage, and the loss was exactly "their peculiar, holy character." The same paragraph converts two inferences into cited fact: "the restrictions of the seventh commandment were disregarded, 'and they took them wives of all which they chose'" — Lamech's plural liberty (_Gen. 4:19_) now practiced in the faithful line — and the mingled Sethites went "in the way of Cain" (_Jude 11_; see "Cain and Abel — the Two Worships").
- _CTr 39.2._ White: "The descendants of Seth went up into the mountains and there made themselves homes separate from the sons of Cain.... But after the exhortations and the influence of Enoch were removed from them, they commenced to unite with the descendants of Cain" — the timing: the separation held only while Enoch's voice held; when the living witness was removed, the union began. Distinction is not self-sustaining; it must be kept.
- _AH 121.3._ White: "There is in itself no sin in eating and drinking, or in marrying and giving in marriage. It was lawful to marry in the time of Noah, and it is lawful to marry now.... But in the days of Noah men married without consulting God or seeking His guidance and counsel" — the sin located precisely: not marriage, which is Eden's own institution (_Gen. 2:24_), but marriage by the sight of the eyes without God — "wives of all which they chose." The lawful thing, taken out of God's hand, became the instrument of ruin.

**The result — the distinction dissolves, corruption is total — Gen. 6:5, 11-12; 1 Cor. 15:33:**

- _Gen. 6:5._ "And GOD saw that the wickedness of man was great in the earth, and that every imagination of the thoughts of his heart was only evil continually" — the sons of God "saw" and took; now GOD saw. Once the distinction dissolved, corruption ran without remainder — "every imagination... only evil continually" — thought itself apostate.
- _Gen. 6:11-12._ "The earth also was corrupt before God, and the earth was filled with violence. And God looked upon the earth, and, behold, it was corrupt; for all flesh had corrupted his way upon the earth" — "ALL flesh": the two classes have become one class. The union did not lift the Cainites; it erased the Sethites.
- _1 Cor. 15:33._ "Be not deceived: evil communications corrupt good manners" — the apostle states the one-directional law the antediluvian record demonstrates: corrupt association corrupts good character, never the reverse. "Be not deceived" — the deception is precisely the hope that the mingling will run uphill.
- _AH 460.1._ White: "It was by associating with idolaters and joining in their festivities that the Hebrews were led to transgress God's law.... So now it is by leading the followers of Christ to associate with the ungodly and unite in their amusements that Satan is most successful in alluring them into sin.... God requires of His people now as great a distinction from the world, in customs, habits, and principles, as He required of Israel anciently" — the law confirmed across every age: association allures the faithful downward, and the required distinction is undiminished now.

**The remnant of the unmingled — Gen. 6:8-9; 1 Pet. 3:20:**

- _Gen. 6:8-9._ "But Noah found grace in the eyes of the LORD.... Noah was a just man and perfect in his generations, and Noah walked with God" — against "all flesh," one "but." Noah is "perfect in his generations" — his family line unmingled, the distinction kept — and he "walked with God" as Enoch had. When the class dissolved, the enmity survived in one house.
- _1 Pet. 3:20._ "when once the longsuffering of God waited in the days of Noah, while the ark was a preparing, wherein few, that is, eight souls were saved by water" — the arithmetic of the mingling: the covenant line, once a class filling the mountains, is "few, that is, eight souls." When the church marries the world, it is the church that is lost — the world remains, eight souls come out of it.

**The lesson codified into law — no yoke with darkness — Deut. 7:3-4; 2 Cor. 6:14-17; Jas. 4:4:**

- _Deut. 7:3-4._ "Neither shalt thou make marriages with them; thy daughter thou shalt not give unto his son, nor his daughter shalt thou take unto thy son. For they will turn away thy son from following me... and destroy thee suddenly" — God writes the antediluvian lesson into covenant statute, and gives the reason: "for they will turn away thy son." The reason IS the mechanism of Genesis 6 — Infinite Wisdom legislating against the mingling because its direction never varies (the statute Israel broke; see "Israel Mingles — 'Like All the Nations'").
- _2 Cor. 6:14._ "Be ye not unequally yoked together with unbelievers: for what fellowship hath righteousness with unrighteousness? and what communion hath light with darkness?" — the New Testament form of the same law. The five questions of vv. 14-16 all expect the answer NONE: no fellowship, no communion, no concord, no part, no agreement — the two classes admit no yoke.
- _2 Cor. 6:16-17._ "ye are the temple of the living God... Wherefore come out from among them, and be ye separate, saith the Lord, and touch not the unclean thing; and I will receive you" — the remedy for the mingling is separation, and God's reception is conditioned on it. The "come out" that closes this study is already sounding here (see "Come Out, My People — the Pilgrim Call").
- _Jas. 4:4._ "Ye adulterers and adulteresses, know ye not that the friendship of the world is enmity with God? whosoever therefore will be a friend of the world is the enemy of God" — the Genesis 6 sin transposed to the church and named ADULTERY: friendship with the world is the marriage of the sons of God to the daughters of men in its spiritual form. There is no third class — to befriend the world is to change seeds.
- _CCh 120.3._ White: "The Lord commanded ancient Israel not to intermarry with the idolatrous nations around them: 'Neither shalt thou make marriages with them.'... Infinite Wisdom, foreseeing the result of such unions, declares: 'For they will turn away thy son from following Me.'... In the New Testament are similar prohibitions concerning the marriage of Christians with the ungodly" — Deuteronomy 7, the peculiar-people calling, and the unequal-yoke command bound into one continuous law flowing out of the antediluvian ruin.
- _8MR 108.1._ White: "All combinations with unbelievers that bind as a yoke must be broken. These words apply not only to making a marriage covenant with an unbeliever, but to the making of all unions where the worldly element can have a prevailing influence over believers" — the principle widened beyond marriage: EVERY alliance in which the worldly element can prevail is the Genesis 6 mechanism in another dress.
- _Ev 617.4._ White: "The Lord's injunction, 'Be ye not unequally yoked together with unbelievers,' refers not only to the marriage of Christians with the ungodly, but to all alliances in which the parties are brought into intimate association, and in which there is need of harmony in spirit and action" — the same confirmation: wherever the two classes are brought into intimate association, the unequal-yoke command stands guard.

**The type stamped on the last days — Matt. 24:37-39:**

- _Matt. 24:37-39._ "But as the days of Noe were, so shall also the coming of the Son of man be. For as in the days that were before the flood they were eating and drinking, marrying and giving in marriage, until the day that Noe entered into the ark, And knew not until the flood came, and took them all away" — Christ Himself seals this section as prophecy: "marrying and giving in marriage" — the very engine of the antediluvian apostasy — is named as the sign-condition of the generation that meets Him. The mingling is not ancient history; it is the last-day mechanism, walked in full at "As in the Days of Noah — the Last Mingling."

**DEFINITION — THE MINGLING — HOW THE PECULIAR PEOPLE LOST THEIR PECULIARITY =** the alliance of the two classes by which the covenant people ceased to be a distinct people, and the standing mechanism of apostasy in every age. The two lines stood defined — Cain's, the world-worshiping class with polygamy already in it (_Gen. 4:19_; _PP 81.1_), Seth's, the "children of the LORD," a "peculiar people" (_Deut. 14:1-2_) counted in the covenant pedigree (_GSAM 40.1_) — until "the sons of God saw the daughters of men that they were fair; and they took them wives of all which they chose" (_Gen. 6:2_). The sons of God are the Sethite covenant line, defined by Scripture's own genealogy — Adam "the son of God" (_Luke 3:38_) — confirmed by the testimony ("the worshipers of God called themselves the sons of God," _CTr 39.2_), and not angels, "for in the resurrection they neither marry, nor are given in marriage" (_Matt. 22:30_). The mechanism of ruin was not persecution but marriage: attraction, alliance, descent — the lawful institution of Eden (_Gen. 2:24_) taken up by sight "without consulting God" (_AH 121.3_), "little by little" after the two classes had "for some time remained separate," until "the restrictions of the seventh commandment were disregarded" and "they lost their peculiar, holy character" (_PP 81.2_; _CTr 39.2_). The result ran one direction only: "every imagination of the thoughts of his heart was only evil continually" (_Gen. 6:5_), "all flesh had corrupted his way" (_Gen. 6:12_) — the world did not rise, the church descended, for "evil communications corrupt good manners" (_1 Cor. 15:33_) — and the faithful stream shrank to one man "perfect in his generations" (_Gen. 6:8-9_) and "eight souls" (_1 Pet. 3:20_). God codified the lesson into law — "Neither shalt thou make marriages with them... for they will turn away thy son" (_Deut. 7:3-4_), "Be ye not unequally yoked... come out from among them, and be ye separate" (_2 Cor. 6:14-17_), extended to "all unions where the worldly element can have a prevailing influence" (_8MR 108.1_; _Ev 617.4_; _CCh 120.3_) — and named its church-form adultery: "the friendship of the world is enmity with God" (_Jas. 4:4_). And Christ stamped it on the end: "as the days of Noe were... marrying and giving in marriage, until the day that Noe entered into the ark" (_Matt. 24:37-39_). Between the translation of Enoch and the Flood, the peculiar people unmade themselves by alliance; the same mechanism runs to the coming of the Son of man, for "there always will be" two parties (_CTr 39.2_). When the church marries the world, the church is what is lost.

## 5. The Days of Noah — the Institutions of Eden Under Attack

> Eden gave three gifts before sin — marriage, the appointed diet, and the Sabbath — and by Noah's day the serpent's seed had struck the first two openly and corrupted worship at its root; the flood found a world wholly absorbed in the lawful, and one household that did all God commanded.

**The three gifts before sin — the institutions of Eden — Gen. 2:24; 1:29; 3:18; 2:1-3:**

- _Gen. 2:24._ "Therefore shall a man leave his father and his mother, and shall cleave unto his wife: and they shall be one flesh" — the FIRST institution of Eden: marriage, spoken before sin; one man, one woman, one flesh. This is the marriage law the serpent's seed will attack.
  - **the institutions of Eden** = the three gifts God established before sin, each rooted in Eden: MARRIAGE (_Gen. 2:24_, one man and one woman made one flesh), the APPOINTED DIET (_Gen. 1:29_, the seed-bearing herbs, fruits, and grains given for meat), and the SABBATH (_Gen. 2:1-3_, the seventh day blessed and sanctified). All three predate the fall; all three are assaulted by the serpent's seed running down through Cain's line — polygamy strikes marriage (_Gen. 4:19_; _Gen. 6:2_), indulged appetite strikes the diet (_Matt. 24:38_; _Luke 17:27_), and self-appointed worship strikes the appointed worship (_Gen. 4:3-5_; see "Cain and Abel — the Two Worships"), the last war meeting the Sabbath at the end (Rev. 13-14). Defined in full at the close.
- _Gen. 1:29._ "Behold, I have given you every herb bearing seed, which is upon the face of all the earth, and every tree, in the which is the fruit of a tree yielding seed; to you it shall be for meat" — the SECOND gift, touching appetite: the original diet. Fruits, grains, herbs, the seed-bearing bounty; flesh is conspicuously absent from the grant.
  - **the original diet** = the _Gen. 1:29_ grant — "every herb bearing seed... and every tree... yielding seed; to you it shall be for meat" — fruits, grains, and (after the fall) the herb of the field (_Gen. 3:18_). Flesh was NOT granted in Eden and was not granted until after the flood (_Gen. 9:3_); therefore through the whole antediluvian age the vegetable grant still stood, and the days-of-Noah eating and drinking was appetite indulged against, not within, God's appointed diet. Defined in full at the close.
- _Gen. 3:18._ "Thorns also and thistles shall it bring forth to thee; and thou shalt eat the herb of the field" — after the fall the diet is enlarged to "the herb of the field" (vegetables), but still no flesh; the vegetable grant of _Gen. 1:29_ continues even under the curse.
- _Gen. 2:1-3._ "And on the seventh day God ended his work which he had made; and he rested on the seventh day... And God blessed the seventh day, and sanctified it" — the THIRD institution: the Sabbath, blessed and sanctified before sin — God's own memorial of creation, the appointed rest and the appointed worship.
- _AH 340.4._ White: "He referred them to the blessed days of Eden when God pronounced all things 'very good.' Then marriage and the Sabbath had their origin, twin institutions for the glory of God in the benefit of humanity" — names two of the three outright: marriage and the Sabbath, "twin institutions," born in Eden before sin, corroborating _Gen. 2:24_ and _Gen. 2:1-3_.
- _Ed 250.2._ White: "The Sabbath and the family were alike instituted in Eden, and in God's purpose they are indissolubly linked together. On this day more than on any other, it is possible for us to live the life of Eden" — Sabbath and family "indissolubly linked," both Eden institutions; to keep them is to live the life of Eden.
- _CD 81.2._ White: "Grains, fruits, nuts, and vegetables constitute the diet chosen for us by our Creator" — the diet confirmed: the Creator's chosen table is the _Gen. 1:29_ grant, flesh absent — the last of the three gifts to receive its witness.

**The first blow — marriage struck in Cain's line, then in Seth's — Gen. 4:19; 6:2; Luke 3:38; Deut. 14:1:**

- _Gen. 4:19._ "And Lamech took unto him two wives: the name of the one was Adah, and the name of the other Zillah" — the serpent's seed strikes marriage first: Lamech of Cain's line is Scripture's first polygamist, breaking the one-flesh law of _Gen. 2:24_. The attack begins in the city-building line (see "The Two Lineages — the City and the Name").
- _Gen. 6:2._ "That the sons of God saw the daughters of men that they were fair; and they took them wives of all which they chose" — the attack crosses into the covenant line: marriage debased into appetite, choosing by the eye, "all which they chose." The two classes merge (the mingling itself is walked in "The Mingling — How the Peculiar People Lost Their Peculiarity").
- _Luke 3:38._ "Which was the son of Enos, which was the son of Seth, which was the son of Adam, which was the son of God" — Scripture's own key to _Gen. 6:2_: the "sons of God" are the line of Seth, traced through Enos to Adam who "was the son of God." The Bible defines its own figure — the covenant line, not angels.
- _Deut. 14:1._ "Ye are the children of the LORD your God" — the idiom confirmed by usage: God's covenant people are His sons; "sons of God" names the faithful line, sealing the Sethite reading of _Gen. 6:2_.
- _PP 81.2._ White: "For some time the two classes remained separate... So long as this separation continued, they maintained the worship of God in its purity. But in the lapse of time they ventured, little by little, to mingle... Mingling with the depraved, they became like them in spirit and in deeds; the restrictions of the seventh commandment were disregarded, 'and they took them wives of all which they chose'" — the confirming witness names "the two classes," quotes _Gen. 6:2_ as the covenant line's fall through intermarriage, and states outright that the seventh commandment was disregarded: Lamech's plurality now the practice of the professed people of God, the first institution debased and the distinction dissolved with it.

**The second blow — appetite made the horizon of life — Matt. 24:38-39; Luke 17:27; Gen. 9:3:**

- _Matt. 24:38._ "For as in the days that were before the flood they were eating and drinking, marrying and giving in marriage, until the day that Noe entered into the ark" — Christ names the mark of the heedless class: not scandalous crime but lawful things — eating, drinking, marrying — made the whole of life; appetite made the horizon.
  - **eating and drinking** = Christ's mark of the heedless class before the flood: lawful appetite made the whole of life, so that the ordinary swallowed the eternal and men "knew not" until judgment fell (_Matt. 24:38-39_; _Luke 17:27_) — the sign of a world that has erased the distinction between the holy and the common. Defined in full at the close.
- _Luke 17:27._ "They did eat, they drank, they married wives, they were given in marriage, until the day that Noe entered into the ark, and the flood came, and destroyed them all" — Luke piles the verbs: eat, drank, married — the tempo of a life wholly given to the permitted, right up to the shut door. The same class Christ warns will be present at His coming (see "As in the Days of Noah — the Last Mingling").
- _Gen. 9:3._ "Every moving thing that liveth shall be meat for you; even as the green herb have I given you all things" — the proof-text of the whole appetite argument: only AFTER the flood is flesh granted. Through the entire antediluvian age the _Gen. 1:29_ grant still stood; the world "did eat, they drank" in violation of, not permission for, the original diet.
- _3SM 289.1._ White: "The flesh of dead animals was not the original food for man. Man was permitted to eat it after the Flood because all vegetation had been destroyed" — confirms the grant-boundary exactly as _Gen. 1:29_ against _Gen. 9:3_: flesh was no part of the Eden table, and the permission is post-flood.
- _DA 636.2._ White: "The crisis is stealing gradually upon us... Men are still eating and drinking, planting and building, marrying, and giving in marriage... yet probation's hour is fast closing" — the heedless-class pattern of _Matt. 24:38_ carried to the end: the same absorption in the lawful while the door swings shut.

**The third blow — appointed worship: the way of Cain runs to the end:**

- The war on the third institution was already open when Cain brought his self-appointed offering and God refused it (_Gen. 4:3-5_ — the ground walked in "Cain and Abel — the Two Worships"). Scripture records no antediluvian Sabbath-breaking, and none is claimed here; what it records is the principle — worship on man's terms against worship on God's terms — and that war does not close with the flood. It runs down the whole controversy and meets the Sabbath of _Gen. 2:1-3_ at the end, where the appointed rest becomes the visible line between the two classes (Rev. 13-14; see "The Seal or the Mark — the Distinction Made Visible").

**The fruit of the threefold assault — one apostate mass — Gen. 6:5:**

- _Gen. 6:5._ "And GOD saw that the wickedness of man was great in the earth, and that every imagination of the thoughts of his heart was only evil continually" — the fruit of the threefold assault: with marriage debased, appetite enthroned, and worship self-appointed, the whole earth becomes one apostate mass — save one household.

**Noah — the distinction kept, and remade by God's own hand — 2 Pet. 2:5; Gen. 6:3; Heb. 11:7; Gen. 6:22; Matt. 24:39; Gen. 7:16:**

- _2 Pet. 2:5._ "And spared not the old world, but saved Noah the eighth person, a preacher of righteousness, bringing in the flood upon the world of the ungodly" — the one preserved witness: "a preacher of righteousness," the peculiar line that would not be absorbed. Eighth person: the remnant few against "the world of the ungodly."
- _Gen. 6:3._ "My spirit shall not always strive with man, for that he also is flesh: yet his days shall be an hundred and twenty years" — Scripture dates the window itself: 120 years of striving Spirit before the judgment, the measured probation of the mingled world.
- _4T 308.3._ White: "Noah preached to the people of his time that God would give them one hundred and twenty years in which to repent of their sins and find refuge in the ark, but they refused the gracious invitation" — the _Gen. 6:3_ window preached: 120 years of warning refused, so the world's "knew not" (_Matt. 24:39_) was willful; they had Noah's preaching and rejected it.
- _Heb. 11:7._ "By faith Noah, being warned of God of things not seen as yet, moved with fear, prepared an ark to the saving of his house; by the which he condemned the world, and became heir of the righteousness which is by faith" — the ark itself, built by faith, "condemned the world": Noah's obedience redrew the line the world had blurred, and his righteousness is the righteousness of faith.
- _Gen. 6:22._ "Thus did Noah; according to all that God commanded him, so did he" — the obedience that condemned the world stated plainly: ALL that God commanded. The peculiar people are marked by keeping the commandments the mingled world neglected.
- _Matt. 24:39._ "And knew not until the flood came, and took them all away; so shall also the coming of the Son of man be" — the mingled world's ignorance is willful ignorance; the judgment remakes the distinction they had erased. Christ fixes the pattern on the last day.
- _Gen. 7:16._ "And they that went in, went in male and female of all flesh, as God had commanded him: and the LORD shut him in" — "the LORD shut him in": the distinction the mingling dissolved is remade by God's own hand at the judgment. Probation closes by divine act, not human choice — the shut-in faithful, the shut-out world.
- _EP 55.4._ White: "Noah and his household were within the ark, 'and the Lord shut him in.' The massive door, impossible for those within to close, was slowly swung to its place by unseen hands. Noah was shut in and the rejecters of God's mercy were shut out" — confirms _Gen. 7:16_ as the close of probation dividing the two classes: one door, two sides, God's hand on it.
- _EW 284.1._ White: "When the rain descended and the flood came, Noah and his family had entered the ark, and God had shut them in... God would not suffer the wicked to destroy those who... would not bow to the decree of the beast or receive his mark" — the inspired bridge to the end: the shut-in distinction of Noah's day is the very deliverance of those who refuse the beast's decree — the last mingling and the last separation (see "As in the Days of Noah — the Last Mingling"; "The Seal or the Mark — the Distinction Made Visible").

**DEFINITION — THE DAYS OF NOAH — THE INSTITUTIONS OF EDEN UNDER ATTACK =** before sin God gave three gifts, the institutions of Eden: marriage — one man, one woman, one flesh (_Gen. 2:24_); the appointed diet — the seed-bearing herbs, fruits, and grains, flesh absent (_Gen. 1:29_; enlarged to the herb of the field after the fall, _Gen. 3:18_); and the Sabbath — the seventh day blessed and sanctified (_Gen. 2:1-3_) — marriage and the Sabbath "twin institutions" born in Eden (_AH 340.4_), Sabbath and family "indissolubly linked" (_Ed 250.2_), the Creator's chosen diet confirmed (_CD 81.2_). By Noah's day the serpent's seed had struck them in order: marriage by Lamech's polygamy in Cain's line (_Gen. 4:19_) and then by the covenant line itself — the sons of God, the line of Seth (_Luke 3:38_; _Deut. 14:1_), taking "wives of all which they chose" while "the restrictions of the seventh commandment were disregarded" (_Gen. 6:2_; _PP 81.2_); appetite by eating and drinking made the whole of life (_Matt. 24:38_; _Luke 17:27_) — and since flesh was not granted until after the flood (_Gen. 9:3_; _3SM 289.1_), the antediluvian table itself ran against the still-standing grant of Eden; and appointed worship by the way of Cain's self-chosen offering (_Gen. 4:3-5_) — worship on man's terms against worship on God's terms — a war that meets the Sabbath only at the end (Rev. 13-14). The fruit was total: "every imagination of the thoughts of his heart was only evil continually" (_Gen. 6:5_) — save Noah, "a preacher of righteousness" (_2 Pet. 2:5_) through the 120-year window God Himself had set (_Gen. 6:3_; _4T 308.3_), whose ark-building faith "condemned the world" (_Heb. 11:7_) because he did "according to all that God commanded him" (_Gen. 6:22_). Then the distinction the mingling had erased was remade by God's own hand: "the LORD shut him in" (_Gen. 7:16_; _EP 55.4_) while the world that "knew not" was taken away (_Matt. 24:39_) — and Christ stakes the last age on the same pattern, when those who will not bow to the beast's decree are shut in as Noah was (_EW 284.1_): "so shall also the coming of the Son of man be."

**Symbols defined here:**

- **the institutions of Eden** = the three gifts God established before sin: MARRIAGE (one man and one woman made one flesh), the APPOINTED DIET (the seed-bearing bounty given for meat), and the SABBATH (the seventh day blessed and sanctified) — all three predating the fall, all three assaulted by the serpent's seed: polygamy against marriage, indulgence against the diet, self-appointed worship against the appointed, the last war ending at the Sabbath (_Gen. 2:24_; _Gen. 1:29_; _Gen. 2:1-3_; _Gen. 4:19_; _Gen. 6:2_).
- **the original diet** = the _Gen. 1:29_ grant — fruits, grains, herbs, and after the fall the herb of the field — with flesh not granted until after the flood, so that the vegetable grant stood through the whole antediluvian age and the days-of-Noah appetite ran against, not within, God's appointment (_Gen. 1:29_; _Gen. 3:18_; _Gen. 9:3_).
- **eating and drinking** = Christ's mark of the heedless class before the flood: lawful appetite — eating, drinking, marrying — made the whole of life, so that the ordinary swallowed the eternal and men "knew not" until judgment fell; not gross crime but total absorption in the permitted, the sign of a world that has erased the distinction between the holy and the common (_Matt. 24:38-39_; _Luke 17:27_).

**Symbols carried:** the sons of God, the daughters of men, and the mingling — the alliance by which the peculiar people lost their peculiarity (see "The Mingling — How the Peculiar People Lost Their Peculiarity"); Cain's offering and the way of Cain — the first war on appointed worship, whose antediluvian record is the altar, not the Sabbath (see "Cain and Abel — the Two Worships"); the city of Cain and Lamech's polygamy — the civilization that carried the attack (see "The Two Lineages — the City and the Name"); the two seeds and the God-put enmity the mingling dissolved (see "The Enmity — Two Seeds from One Fall"). Forward: Christ's days-of-Noah pattern for the last generation (see "As in the Days of Noah — the Last Mingling") and the worship war's terminus at seal versus mark (see "The Seal or the Mark — the Distinction Made Visible").

**For discussion:**

1. Every count against the antediluvian world in _Matt. 24:38_ is a LAWFUL thing — "eating and drinking, marrying and giving in marriage" — yet "the flood came, and took them all away." How does the permitted become the destroyer, and where in your own week has a lawful thing quietly become the whole of life?
2. Flesh was not granted until after the flood (_Gen. 9:3_), so the antediluvians' indulgence ran against a standing grant of God (_Gen. 1:29_). Where do we treat God's later concessions as if they erased His original appointments — in appetite, in marriage, in rest — and what would returning toward "the life of Eden" (_Ed 250.2_) cost this week?
3. Noah "condemned the world" not by argument but by obedience — an ark built to "all that God commanded him" (_Heb. 11:7_; _Gen. 6:22_) — and then "the LORD shut him in" (_Gen. 7:16_). If the distinction between the two classes is finally remade by God's hand and not ours, what is our part now, before the door — and what was the world's part during Noah's 120 years of warning?

# Part III — The Witnesses Repeat

## 6. Babel — Congregating for Convenience

> The first post-flood act of the serpent's seed is to rebuild the city: "let us build us a city... lest we be scattered" is open defiance of "replenish the earth," and God answers the congregation with the very scattering it was built to prevent — naming the whole system Babel, CONFUSION, the name it carries all the way to Revelation.

**The command Babel defies — "replenish the earth" — Gen. 9:1, 19:**

- _Gen. 9:1._ "And God blessed Noah and his sons, and said unto them, Be fruitful, and multiply, and replenish the earth" — God's post-flood word is centrifugal: fill the earth, spread out. Every clause of Babel's manifesto is written against this one command; the whole plot is congregation against dispersal.
- _Gen. 9:19._ "These are the three sons of Noah: and of them was the whole earth overspread" — God's design is that the whole earth be OVERSPREAD from Noah's sons. Scattering is the blessing, not the curse; the Babel-builders want the exact opposite.

**The city is Cain's signature — and the Name is Seth's — Gen. 4:16-17, 26:**

- _Gen. 4:16-17._ "And Cain went out from the presence of the LORD... and he builded a city" — the first city-builder in Scripture is the first man who went out from the presence of the LORD. City-founding is the serpent-seed's signature act — safety and a name apart from God (the city of Cain is defined in "The Two Lineages — the City and the Name"); Babel is the same impulse resurrected after the flood.
- _Gen. 4:26._ "then began men to call upon the name of the LORD" — Seth's line calls upon the NAME OF THE LORD. Set this exactly against Babel's "let us make us a name" (_Gen. 11:4_): the two seeds are divided by whose name is invoked — the Name called upon, or the name made.

**Nimrod — the founder named — Gen. 10:8-12; Mic. 5:6:**

- _Gen. 10:8-9._ "And Cush begat Nimrod: he began to be a mighty one in the earth. He was a mighty hunter before the LORD" — the founder named: a mighty hunter before — in the face of — the LORD; might in defiance, not in worship.
- _Gen. 10:10._ "And the beginning of his kingdom was Babel, and Erech, and Accad, and Calneh, in the land of Shinar" — Babel is the HEADWATER: the first post-flood kingdom, founded by Nimrod son of Cush on the plain of Shinar in the first generations after the ark.
- _Gen. 10:11-12._ "Out of that land went forth Asshur, and builded Nineveh" — from Shinar the city-system multiplies outward, city upon city compounding into "a great city" (_Gen. 10:12_). The confederacy of great cities is the fruit of Nimrod's Babel, and the pattern runs straight to "Babylon the great, that great city" of Revelation.
- _Mic. 5:6._ "And they shall waste the land of Assyria with the sword, and the land of Nimrod in the entrances thereof" — Scripture itself calls the enemy-land "the land of Nimrod": the Bible's own witness that Nimrod's Babel names the archetypal oppressor-kingdom, the confederated power against God's people.

**Down from the mountains to the plain of Shinar — Gen. 11:1-2:**

- _Gen. 11:1._ "And the whole earth was of one language, and of one speech" — the pre-Babel condition: total unity of speech. Unity is not itself the sin; the sin is unity harnessed against the command of God.
- _Gen. 11:2._ "as they journeyed from the east, that they found a plain in the land of Shinar; and they dwelt there" — they stop journeying and settle on the plain: the geography of Cain's line, the lowland against the mountains of the faithful (see "The Two Lineages — the City and the Name"). To "dwell there" is to refuse to be scattered.
- _EP 70.3._ White: "For a time, the descendants of Noah continued to dwell among the mountains where the ark had rested. As their numbers increased, apostasy led to division... After a time they decided to separate. Accordingly they journeyed to Shinar" — the mountain/plain division of the two classes re-enacted after the flood: the apostate line separates from the worshipers of God and descends to the plain.
- _8MR 42.1._ White: "Those who desired to forget their Creator, and to cast off the restraint of His law, decided to separate from the worshipers of God... Thus they hoped to make themselves independent of God" — the split named at its headwater: apostasy led to division, and Babel is the serpent-seed's congregation, built to be "independent of God."

**Brick for stone, a tower for safety, a name for themselves — Gen. 11:3-4:**

- _Gen. 11:3._ "Go to, let us make brick, and burn them throughly. And they had brick for stone, and slime had they for morter" — man-made brick for God-made stone, slime for mortar: the whole enterprise is substitution, human manufacture standing in for what God provides — a self-made security.
- _Gen. 11:4._ "Go to, let us build us a city and a tower, whose top may reach unto heaven; and let us make us a name, lest we be scattered abroad upon the face of the whole earth" — the manifesto in one verse: (1) "a city and a tower" — congregation for convenience and safety in numbers; (2) "make us a name" — self-exaltation against calling on the LORD's name (_Gen. 4:26_); (3) "lest we be scattered" — explicit defiance of _Gen. 9:1_. Every clause is against a word of God.
  - **Babel / Babylon** = the serpent-seed's confederated city-system: congregation for convenience and self-security in defiance of God's command to fill the earth, and self-naming in defiance of calling on the Name of the LORD (_Gen. 11:4_ against _Gen. 9:1_ and _Gen. 4:26_); founded by Nimrod on the plain of Shinar — "the beginning of his kingdom was Babel" (_Gen. 10:10_) — named CONFUSION by God's own judgment (_Gen. 11:9_), and carried by the Spirit to its end-time head as "BABYLON THE GREAT" (_Rev. 17:5_). Defined in full at the close.
  - **the tower** = man-made security against God's judgment and against God's command to scatter and fill the earth — brick-and-slime presumption trusted for the safety and the name that belong to the LORD alone, so small the LORD must "come down" to see it, answered by the very scattering it was built to prevent (_Gen. 11:3-5_; _Gen. 11:8_). Defined in full at the close.
- _PP 119.1._ White: "One object before them in the erection of the tower was to secure their own safety in case of another deluge... The whole undertaking was designed to exalt still further the pride of its projectors and to turn the minds of future generations away from God and lead them into idolatry" — the tower-symbol confirmed exactly: man-made security against God's judgment, born of pride and unbelief in God's covenant word. The same paragraph supplies the seed-continuity: "their hearts, like that of Cain, rose up in rebellion against Him" (_PP 119.1_) — the Babel-builders' hearts are Cain's heart, one line from Cain's city (_Gen. 4:17_) to Nimrod's plain (see "The Enmity — Two Seeds from One Fall").
- _EP 70.4._ White: "God had directed men to disperse throughout the earth, but these Babel builders determined to keep their community united and to found a monarchy that should embrace the whole earth" — the defiance of _Gen. 9:1_ named: God said disperse; Babel said stay united and build a universal empire — the metropolis and the monument of its builders' power.

**"Go to, let us go down" — the confederacy broken — Gen. 11:5-9:**

- _Gen. 11:5._ "And the LORD came down to see the city and the tower, which the children of men builded" — the tower that was to "reach unto heaven" is so small the LORD must "come down" to see it. The irony judges the pride: man cannot climb to God by masonry.
- _Gen. 11:6._ "Behold, the people is one... and now nothing will be restrained from them, which they have imagined to do" — unbroken confederacy makes evil limitless. God scatters not from spite but to restrain corporate rebellion, as He divided the seeds at the first (see "The Enmity — Two Seeds from One Fall").
- _Gen. 11:7._ "Go to, let us go down, and there confound their language" — God answers "Go to, let us build" with His own "Go to, let us go down." The confounding of speech is the instrument of the scattering the builders feared.
- _Gen. 11:8._ "So the LORD scattered them abroad from thence upon the face of all the earth: and they left off to build the city" — the exact thing they built to prevent, God accomplishes. The distinction between the two classes is remade by judgment, as at Eden's gate and at the flood.
- _Gen. 11:9._ "Therefore is the name of it called Babel; because the LORD did there confound the language of all the earth" — they sought a name; God gave them one — Babel, CONFUSION. The self-made name becomes the byword of judgment, and this naming is inherited by the whole city-system of Revelation.
- _EP 71.1._ White: "Angels were sent to bring to naught the purpose of the builders... the language was confounded so that the directions delivered were often the reverse of those that had been given... Lightnings from heaven as an evidence of God's displeasure broke off the upper portion of the tower" — corroborates _Gen. 11:7-8_: God's direct judgment confounds the speech and breaks the tower, accomplishing the scattering the builders built against.

**From Babel to Babylon the great — the city-system at full stature — Rev. 17:5; 18:4:**

- _Rev. 17:5._ "And upon her forehead was a name written, MYSTERY, BABYLON THE GREAT, THE MOTHER OF HARLOTS AND ABOMINATIONS OF THE EARTH" — the headwater of Shinar grown to the end-time confederacy of the serpent's seed: still self-named, still congregated against God, and Scripture names her by Babel's name — "the mother" of the whole system.
- _TMR 37.2._ Andrews: "The word Babylon signifies confusion, and is derived from Babel... being the chosen term of the Holy Spirit to designate 'that mighty city'... was doubtless selected with especial reference to its signification" — the pioneer definition sealing the Babel-to-Babylon line: Babylon = confusion = Babel, the Spirit's own chosen name for the Revelation city, "composed of professed worshipers of God."
- _TMR 47.2._ Andrews: "Since the great apostasy, the majority of the professed followers have busied themselves in attempting to climb up to heaven some other way. They have been confounded in the attempt, and scattered abroad... with creeds as discordant as the languages of those who were dispersed at the ancient tower" — the antitype drawn: the great apostasy repeats Babel's tower, climbing to heaven another way, and ends in the same confusion.
- _Rev. 18:4._ "Come out of her, my people, that ye be not partakers of her sins, and that ye receive not of her plagues" — the final answer to Babel's "lest we be scattered": God still scatters His people OUT of the city of confusion before He judges it — the everlasting separation of the two seeds (see "Come Out, My People — the Pilgrim Call").

**DEFINITION — BABEL — CONGREGATING FOR CONVENIENCE =** the first post-flood act of the serpent's seed is to rebuild the city, and every clause of the rebuilding is written against a word of God. God's blessing was centrifugal — "replenish the earth" (_Gen. 9:1_), the whole earth "overspread" from Noah's sons (_Gen. 9:19_) — but the apostate line, hearts "like that of Cain" (_PP 119.1_; _Gen. 4:16-17_), separated from the worshipers of God, descended from the mountains to the plain of Shinar (_Gen. 11:2_; _EP 70.3_; _8MR 42.1_), and under Nimrod, the mighty hunter before the LORD whose kingdom began at Babel (_Gen. 10:8-10_; _Mic. 5:6_), raised a city and a tower of brick and slime (_Gen. 11:3_): congregation for convenience ("let us build us a city"), self-exaltation against the Name called upon by Seth's line ("let us make us a name," _Gen. 11:4_ against _Gen. 4:26_), and open defiance of the scattering ("lest we be scattered abroad"). The tower was man-made security against God's judgment and monument to its builders' pride (_PP 119.1_; _EP 70.4_) — yet so small the LORD must "come down" to see it (_Gen. 11:5_). Because unbroken confederacy makes evil limitless (_Gen. 11:6_), God answered "let us build" with "let us go down" (_Gen. 11:7_), confounded the speech, broke the tower (_EP 71.1_), and accomplished by judgment the very scattering the congregation was built to prevent — the distinction between the two classes remade, as at Eden's gate and at the flood, and the self-made name turned to CONFUSION: Babel (_Gen. 11:8-9_). That name the Spirit carries to the end: the city-system of Shinar reaches full stature as "BABYLON THE GREAT, THE MOTHER OF HARLOTS" (_Rev. 17:5_; _TMR 37.2_), the great apostasy repeating the tower — climbing to heaven another way, confounded into discordant creeds (_TMR 47.2_) — until God's final answer to "lest we be scattered" falls: "Come out of her, my people" (_Rev. 18:4_). The witnesses repeat: the serpent's seed congregates for convenience; God's people are content to be scattered at His word — the same drama replayed inside one family in "Abraham and Lot — the Tent and the Plain," and consummated in "Come Out, My People — the Pilgrim Call."

**Symbols defined here:**

- **Babel / Babylon** = the serpent-seed's confederated city-system: congregation for convenience and self-security in defiance of God's command to fill the earth, and self-naming in defiance of calling on the Name of the LORD; founded by Nimrod on the plain of Shinar, named CONFUSION by God's judgment, and carried forward by the Spirit to "Babylon the great" (_Gen. 11:4_; _Gen. 11:8-9_; _Gen. 10:8-10_; _Gen. 9:1_; _Gen. 4:26_; _Rev. 17:5_).
- **the tower** = man-made security against God's judgment and against His command to scatter and fill the earth — brick-and-slime presumption trusted for the safety and the name that belong to the LORD alone, exposed by the LORD's "coming down" and answered by the confusion of tongues and the very scattering it was built to prevent (_Gen. 11:3-5_; _Gen. 11:8_).

**Symbols carried:** the seed of the serpent and the enmity — the God-put division Babel's confederacy tries to erase, mercifully remade by judgment (see "The Enmity — Two Seeds from One Fall"); the city of Cain, calling on the name of the LORD, and the mountains-and-plains geography of the two classes, all resurrected at Shinar (see "The Two Lineages — the City and the Name"); the distinction remade by judgment, as at the flood (see "The Days of Noah — the Institutions of Eden Under Attack"); Babel's plain becomes the plain toward which Lot pitches his tent (see "Abraham and Lot — the Tent and the Plain"), and Babylon the great receives her final sentence and the last call of separation (see "The Seal or the Mark — the Distinction Made Visible" and "Come Out, My People — the Pilgrim Call").

**For discussion:**

1. Babel says "let us make us a name" (_Gen. 11:4_) while Seth's line "began... to call upon the name of the LORD" (_Gen. 4:26_) — the two classes divided by whose name is invoked. Where in your own life or in the church do we build a name for ourselves — reputation, platform, institution, security — where God is asking us to call on His?
2. The builders congregated "lest we be scattered" (_Gen. 11:4_) in direct defiance of "replenish the earth" (_Gen. 9:1_) — safety in numbers, convenience over command. What forms of congregating for convenience look reasonable to us today, and how do we test whether a gathering is unity in God's word or a confederacy against it (_Gen. 11:6_)?
3. God's final answer to "lest we be scattered" is "Come out of her, my people" (_Rev. 18:4_). If God still scatters His people out of the city of confusion before He judges it, what would it mean this week to accept the scattering rather than the tower — to come out in body and in spirit now, while the call is mercy and not yet plague?

## 7. Abraham and Lot — the Tent and the Plain

> The two classes divide one family: Abraham, called out of the Babel-lands, dwells in a tent with an altar at every pitch and confesses himself a pilgrim; Lot pitches his tent toward Sodom and drifts from the plain to the gate — same stock, two classes, and the difference is the direction of the tent door.

**The call OUT of the city-civilization — Gen. 12:1; Heb. 11:8:**

- _Gen. 12:1._ "Now the LORD had said unto Abram, Get thee out of thy country, and from thy kindred, and from thy father's house, unto a land that I will shew thee" — the covenant people begin with a command to SEPARATE. Abraham is called out of Ur of the Chaldees — the Babel-lands of Mesopotamia where the serpent's seed congregated (see "Babel — Congregating for Convenience") — before he is given a promise: separation precedes worship. The witness of the woman's seed repeats here, c. 2000 BC.
- _Heb. 11:8._ "By faith Abraham, when he was called to go out into a place which he should after receive for an inheritance, obeyed; and he went out, not knowing whither he went" — the going out was the act of faith itself: he obeyed with nothing in hand but the word, leaving the established land for a country not yet seen. (The full pilgrim confession of Heb. 11 is gathered below.)
- _MWV2 266.5._ Miller: "Abraham was called to go out from the land of his fathers 'into a strange land, not knowing whither he went'... dwelling in tabernacles with Isaac and Jacob, the heirs with him of the same promise; for he looked for a city which hath foundations, whose Builder and Maker is God... in the midst of idolatrous nations, among whom they sojourned, not one of them lost their faith, or became impure in their worship" — the pioneer reads the call OUT as the standing pattern: God's people kept pure precisely because they dwelt as pilgrims among the idolaters, not as citizens of their cities.

**The tent and the altar — worship travels with the pilgrim — Gen. 12:7-8; 13:4, 18:**

- _Gen. 12:7-8._ "there builded he an altar unto the LORD, who appeared unto him... and pitched his tent... and there he builded an altar unto the LORD, and called upon the name of the LORD" — the pattern set at the first pitch: everywhere the tent goes up, an altar goes up beside it. Abraham "called upon the name of the LORD" — the dividing act of the faithful seed carried straight down from Seth's line (see "The Two Lineages — the City and the Name").
  - **the tent and the altar** = the pilgrim life — dwelling loose from the world with worship at the center, portable, needing no city or temple; Abraham builds an altar at every pitch, so his worship travels with him, and he dwells in tents because he looks for the city God builds (_Gen. 12:7-8_; _Gen. 13:4, 18_; _Heb. 11:9-10_; _John 4:23-24_). Defined in full at the close.
- _Gen. 13:4._ "Unto the place of the altar, which he had made there at the first: and there Abram called on the name of the LORD" — returning from Egypt, Abraham goes first to the ALTAR, not to a market. Worship, not gain, is the center of his camp.
- _Gen. 13:18._ "Then Abram removed his tent, and came and dwelt in the plain of Mamre, which is in Hebron, and built there an altar unto the LORD" — tent removed, altar rebuilt: the whole life is portable worship. The tent moves; the altar always goes up. Abraham too can dwell in a plain — the contrast here is not topography but the altar and the tent-door's direction: Mamre gets an altar; Sodom's plain never does.
- _PP 132.1._ White: "Lot was still with him, and again they came to Bethel, and pitched their tents by the altar which they had before erected... Although the whole land had been given him by God Himself, he courteously waived this right" — confirms the tent-and-altar pattern of _Gen. 13:3-4_, and the pilgrim's non-grasping spirit: Abraham holds so loosely that he waives even God-given rights.

**Worship DE-localized — the portable altar validated — John 4:21-24:**

- _John 4:21._ "the hour cometh, when ye shall neither in this mountain, nor yet at Jerusalem, worship the Father" — Christ Himself unbinds worship from place: NEITHER this mountain NOR Jerusalem. The city temple was always provisional — a shadow serving its appointed hour; the pilgrim's portable altar anticipated the worship in spirit and truth that Christ here unbinds from every address.
- _John 4:23-24._ "the true worshippers shall worship the Father in spirit and in truth: for the Father seeketh such to worship him. God is a Spirit: and they that worship him must worship him in spirit and in truth" — worship is bound to SPIRIT and TRUTH, not to an address. This is why Abraham could worship at every pitch of the tent: the woman's seed never needed the serpent-seed's city to find God.
- _CE 74.1._ White: "The Teacher from heaven, no less a personage than the Son of God, came to earth to reveal the character of the Father to men, that they might worship him in spirit and in truth" — confirms the "spirit and truth" definition of true worship; the de-localizing word itself (_John 4:21_) the Scripture carries alone, as it is fully able to do.

**Lot's choice by SIGHT — the well-watered plain — Gen. 13:10-13:**

- _Gen. 13:10-12._ "And Lot lifted up his eyes, and beheld all the plain of Jordan, that it was well watered every where... even as the garden of the LORD... Then Lot chose him all the plain of Jordan... and pitched his tent toward Sodom" — the choice by SIGHT: Lot "lifted up his eyes" and chose what looked like Eden — "as the garden of the LORD" — and no altar is named in his choosing. The tent door now faces the doomed city; the drift begins with a direction.
  - **the plain of Sodom** = choice by sight — the well-watered convenience, "as the garden of the LORD," chosen with the eyes and no altar named; it ends inside the doomed city, for "toward Sodom" becomes "dwelt in Sodom" becomes "sat in the gate of Sodom" (_Gen. 13:10-13_; _Gen. 14:12_; _Gen. 19:1_). The same eye-led choosing that mingled the sons of God before the flood — "saw... that they were fair... of all which they chose" (see "The Mingling — How the Peculiar People Lost Their Peculiarity"). Defined in full at the close.
- _Gen. 13:13._ "But the men of Sodom were wicked and sinners before the LORD exceedingly" — Scripture names the corruption in the same breath as the convenience: what looked like the garden of the LORD was a condemned city. The well-watered plain and the exceeding wickedness are one address.
- _PP 133.1._ White: "Dazzled with visions of worldly gain, Lot overlooked the moral and spiritual evils that would be encountered there. The inhabitants of the plain were 'sinners before the Lord exceedingly;' but of this he was ignorant, or, knowing, gave it but little weight. He 'chose him all the plain of Jordan,' and 'pitched his tent toward Sodom.' How little did he foresee the terrible results of that selfish choice!" — confirms the symbol exactly: the choice was by sight of worldly gain, and the city's named corruption was weighed as nothing.
- _1MCP 339.1._ White: "Lot chose Sodom as a place of residence because he looked more to the temporal advantages he would gain than to the moral influences that would surround himself and his family... his possessions were destroyed, part of his children perished in the destruction of that wicked city, his wife was turned to a pillar of salt by the way, and he himself was saved 'so as by fire'" — the full harvest of the sight-choice tallied in one sentence: goods, children, wife — everything but the bare soul.

**The drift — from "toward" to "dwelt" to "sat in the gate" — Gen. 14:12; 19:1:**

- _Gen. 14:12._ "And they took Lot, Abram's brother's son, who dwelt in Sodom, and his goods, and departed" — the tent-door direction has become a street address: "pitched his tent TOWARD Sodom" (_Gen. 13:12_) is now "DWELT IN Sodom" — and already the city's wars sweep him and his goods away.
- _Gen. 19:1._ "And there came two angels to Sodom at even; and Lot sat in the gate of Sodom" — the final stage: the gate is the seat of the magistrate. Lot is now an officer of the city he once merely faced. Toward, in, gate — one unbroken drift, and not one altar named on the whole road.
- _CL 7.1._ White: "Lot trod the plain with unwilling and tardy steps. He had so long associated with evil workers that he could not see his peril until his wife stood on the plain a pillar of salt forever" — confirms the drift's blinding power: long mingling with Sodom dulled the very sense that should have fled it.

**The powerless witness and the divided heart — Gen. 19:14, 26; 2 Pet. 2:7-8; Luke 17:32:**

- _Gen. 19:14._ "Up, get you out of this place; for the LORD will destroy this city. But he seemed as one that mocked unto his sons in law" — the wages of the mingled life: when the hour comes to bear the come-out message, Lot's testimony is powerless. "As one that mocked" — a witness who lived in Sodom's gate cannot be believed against Sodom, even by his own family.
- _2 Pet. 2:7-8._ "And delivered just Lot, vexed with the filthy conversation of the wicked: (For that righteous man dwelling among them, in seeing and hearing, vexed his righteous soul from day to day with their unlawful deeds;)" — the Spirit's verdict guards the point: Lot was GENUINELY righteous. He is not the serpent's seed; he is the woman's seed lodged in the serpent-seed's city — "vexed... from day to day," saved himself, and losing everything else. The two classes are not decided by pedigree but by where the tent door points.
- _Gen. 19:26._ "But his wife looked back from behind him, and she became a pillar of salt" — her body left Sodom; her heart never did. The backward look monumentalizes the divided soul: half out of the city is still in it. The plain kept what the plain had won.
- _Luke 17:32._ "Remember Lot's wife." — Christ distills the whole section into three words and nails them to the end of the age: the peril of the divided heart at the very moment of deliverance. The last generation is commanded to carry this witness (taken up in "As in the Days of Noah — the Last Mingling").
- _PP 169.2._ White: "Those who secure for their children worldly wealth and honor at the expense of their eternal interests, will find in the end that these advantages are a terrible loss. Like Lot, many see their children ruined, and barely save their own souls. Their lifework is lost; their life is a sad failure" — confirms _2 Pet. 2:7-8_: the righteous soul lodged in the wrong class is saved "so as by fire," and the lifework burns.

**The pilgrim confession — strangers seeking a city — Heb. 11:8-10, 13-16; 1 Pet. 2:11:**

- _Heb. 11:8._ "By faith Abraham, when he was called to go out into a place which he should after receive for an inheritance, obeyed; and he went out, not knowing whither he went" — the coming out was the act of FAITH: he obeyed before he saw, leaving the established land with nothing in hand but the word.
- _Heb. 11:9-10._ "By faith he sojourned in the land of promise, as in a strange country, dwelling in tabernacles... For he looked for a city which hath foundations, whose builder and maker is God" — the tent explained: Abraham dwells in tabernacles BECAUSE he looks for the city God builds. The tent is a confession about where home is; the pilgrim refuses Cain's city because he is waiting for God's.
- _Heb. 11:13-14._ "These all died in faith... and confessed that they were strangers and pilgrims on the earth. For they that say such things declare plainly that they seek a country" — the confession itself is the mark: to say "strangers and pilgrims" is to "declare plainly" that earth is not home.
  - **strangers and pilgrims** = the confessed identity of the woman's seed in every age — those who own that earth is not home and openly seek a better, heavenly country; Abraham is the pattern, and the New Testament church wears the same title (_Heb. 11:13-16_; _1 Pet. 2:11_; _Gen. 12:1_). Defined in full at the close.
- _Heb. 11:15-16._ "if they had been mindful of that country from whence they came out, they might have had opportunity to have returned. But now they desire a better country, that is, an heavenly: wherefore God is not ashamed to be called their God: for he hath prepared for them a city" — the road back to Ur stayed open; the pilgrim's not-returning is a daily choice. And the reward is exact: for those who refuse man's city, "he hath prepared for them a CITY."
- _1 Pet. 2:11._ "Dearly beloved, I beseech you as strangers and pilgrims, abstain from fleshly lusts, which war against the soul" — the church of the last days inherits Abraham's title verbatim: "strangers and pilgrims" is not patriarchal nostalgia but the standing self-understanding of the woman's seed.
- _PP 169.3._ White: "The heritage that God has promised to His people is not in this world. Abraham had no possession in the earth, 'no, not so much as to set his foot on.' Acts 7:5. He possessed great substance, and he used it to the glory of God and the good of his fellow men; but he did not look upon this world as his home" — confirms _Heb. 11:9-13_: the pilgrim may hold great substance, but he holds it loosely; possession without citizenship.
- _PREX1 24.2._ Litch: "It is the city for which the patriarch Abraham looked, (Hebrews 11:10,) 'which hath foundations, whose builder and maker is God.' For this city he looked as the promised inheritance of the land of Canaan" — the pioneer names the pilgrim's looked-for city: the New Jerusalem, the true inheritance behind the Canaan promise.
- _AA 522.1._ White: "Dearly beloved, I beseech you as strangers and pilgrims, abstain from fleshly lusts, which war against the soul; having your conversation honest among the Gentiles: that, whereas they speak against you as evildoers, they may by your good works, which they shall behold, glorify God in the day of visitation" — in AA 522.1 White plants this very text at the head of her charge to the church: the apostolic charter carried forward verbatim.

**DEFINITION — ABRAHAM AND LOT — THE TENT AND THE PLAIN =** the two classes traced through one family, proving the division is drawn by direction, not descent. Abraham is called OUT of the Babel-lands ("Get thee out of thy country," _Gen. 12:1_) and lives the rest of his life as a tent-dweller with an altar at every pitch (_Gen. 12:7-8_; _13:4, 18_) — the TENT AND THE ALTAR, portable worship needing no city temple, vindicated by Christ's own word that true worship is bound "neither in this mountain, nor yet at Jerusalem" but to "spirit and in truth" (_John 4:21-24_); he dwells in tabernacles because "he looked for a city which hath foundations, whose builder and maker is God" (_Heb. 11:9-10_), and dies confessing himself among the "strangers and pilgrims on the earth" who "desire a better country" (_Heb. 11:13-16_) — the confession the New Testament church inherits verbatim (_1 Pet. 2:11_; _AA 522.1_). Lot, of the same righteous stock (_2 Pet. 2:7-8_), lifts up his eyes and chooses THE PLAIN OF SODOM by sight — well watered, "as the garden of the LORD," no altar named (_Gen. 13:10-12_) — though its men were "sinners before the LORD exceedingly" (_Gen. 13:13_); and the tent door's direction becomes a destiny: "toward Sodom" (_Gen. 13:12_), "dwelt in Sodom" (_Gen. 14:12_), "sat in the gate of Sodom" (_Gen. 19:1_). The harvest is total: a testimony powerless "as one that mocked" (_Gen. 19:14_), a wife turned to a pillar of salt by the backward look of a divided heart (_Gen. 19:26_), children and goods consumed, the man himself "vexed from day to day" and saved so as by fire (_2 Pet. 2:8_; _1MCP 339.1_; _PP 169.2_). Christ seals the witness for the last generation in three words: "Remember Lot's wife" (_Luke 17:32_). The witnesses repeat because the classes repeat: the pilgrim by the altar, the drifter at the gate — and the difference between them, first and last, is the direction the tent door faces.

**Symbols defined here:**

- **the tent and the altar** = the pilgrim life — dwelling loose from the world with worship at the center, portable, needing no city or temple; an altar at every pitch, because worship is bound to spirit and truth, not to a place, and the tent confesses that home is the city God builds (_Gen. 12:7-8_; _Gen. 13:4, 18_; _Heb. 11:9-10_; _John 4:23-24_).
- **the plain of Sodom** = choice by sight — the well-watered convenience, "as the garden of the LORD," chosen with the eyes and no altar named, ending inside the doomed city by one unbroken drift: toward, in, gate (_Gen. 13:10-13_; _Gen. 14:12_; _Gen. 19:1_).
- **strangers and pilgrims** = the confessed identity of the woman's seed in every age — those who own that earth is not home and declare plainly that they seek a better, heavenly country; Abraham the pattern, the church the heir of the title (_Heb. 11:13-16_; _1 Pet. 2:11_; _Gen. 12:1_).

**Symbols carried:** the seed of the woman and the seed of the serpent, the two classes this family divides into (see "The Enmity — Two Seeds from One Fall"); the city of Cain and calling on the name of the LORD — Sodom is the city-congregation full grown, and Abraham's altar-call continues Seth's dividing act (see "The Two Lineages — the City and the Name"); Babel / Babylon, the city-system of Ur out of which Abraham was called (see "Babel — Congregating for Convenience"); the mingling and its choice by sight — Lot's eye-led choosing repeats the sons of God who "saw... and took" (see "The Mingling — How the Peculiar People Lost Their Peculiarity"); "Remember Lot's wife" is carried to the last generation in "As in the Days of Noah — the Last Mingling"; the pilgrim's looked-for city and the better country are claimed at last in "Come Out, My People — the Pilgrim Call".

## 8. Israel Mingles — "Like All the Nations"

> What Balaam's curse could not do from without, his counsel did from within — the daughters, the feast, the strange altar — and the peculiar people finished the work themselves, demanding a king "like all the nations" they were set apart from; the Spirit's post-mortem on the whole national history is one line: "[they] were mingled among the heathen, and learned their works."

**The charter of peculiarity — distinctness IS the covenant identity — Exo. 19:5-6; Deut. 14:2; 7:2-6:**

- _Exo. 19:5-6._ "if ye will obey my voice indeed, and keep my covenant, then ye shall be a peculiar treasure unto me above all people... And ye shall be unto me a kingdom of priests, and an holy nation" — God's founding charter for the covenant nation: obedience makes Israel His peculiar treasure above all people, a kingdom of priests. Distinctness is not an accessory to the covenant; it IS the covenant identity.
  - **peculiar people** = God's purchased, set-apart treasure — distinctness is the covenant identity itself, not an ornament upon it; Israel is "a peculiar treasure above all people" and "a kingdom of priests" precisely by keeping the covenant that unlikes them from the nations, and the same charter passes almost word for word to the church redeemed by Christ's blood; to be conformed is to forfeit the identity (_Exo. 19:5-6_; _Deut. 14:2_; _Deut. 7:6_; _Titus 2:14_; _1 Pet. 2:9_).
- _Deut. 14:2._ "the LORD hath chosen thee to be a peculiar people unto himself, above all the nations that are upon the earth" — the symbol named in the law itself: chosen TO BE peculiar. The election and the unlikeness are one act.
- _Deut. 7:2-4._ "thou shalt make no covenant with them... Neither shalt thou make marriages with them... For they will turn away thy son from following me, that they may serve other gods" — the non-mingling statutes with the reason stated on their face: no covenants, no marriages, because the mingled tie "will turn away thy son." The exact mechanism that dissolved the antediluvian sons of God (see "The Mingling — How the Peculiar People Lost Their Peculiarity") is here written into national law.
- _Deut. 7:6._ "For thou art an holy people unto the LORD thy God: the LORD thy God hath chosen thee to be a special people unto himself, above all people that are upon the face of the earth" — the ground of the statute is the identity: because thou ART holy and special, therefore no mingling. The law guards what the election made.

**Balaam's forced confession — the appointed posture is dwelling alone — Num. 23:9; Deut. 33:28:**

- _Num. 23:9._ "lo, the people shall dwell alone, and shall not be reckoned among the nations" — Balaam's own hired mouth confesses God's design: the covenant people are appointed to dwell alone, not reckoned among the nations. Separation is the divinely set posture of the woman's seed among the kingdoms.
  - **dwelling alone** = the appointed posture of the woman's seed among the nations — separation as safety, not isolation as loss; even the prophet hired to curse must confess it, and Moses seals it as shelter, "Israel then shall dwell in safety alone"; the demand for a king "like all the nations" is the deliberate reversal of this posture (_Num. 23:9_; _Deut. 33:28_; _1 Sam. 8:5, 20_).
- _Deut. 33:28._ "Israel then shall dwell in safety alone: the fountain of Jacob shall be upon a land of corn and wine" — Moses' dying blessing frames dwelling alone as SAFETY, not deprivation. The aloneness is the shelter of the peculiar people, joined to fountain, corn, wine, and dew — separation with abundance.
- _PP 439.2._ White: "Balaam knew that his curse could not harm Israel. God was on their side, and so long as they were true to Him no adverse power of earth or hell could prevail against them" — the premise of all that follows: no outside curse can touch the people who dwell alone with God. If the enemy is to reach them at all, he must reach them by mingling from within.

**The breach at Shittim — what could not be cursed is corrupted — Num. 25:1-3; 31:16; Rev. 2:14:**

- _Num. 25:1-2._ "the people began to commit whoredom with the daughters of Moab. And they called the people unto the sacrifices of their gods: and the people did eat, and bowed down to their gods" — the breach at Baal-peor (Shittim, Israel's last camp before Canaan, c. 1407 BC) runs the antediluvian sequence (_Gen. 6:2_; _Matt. 24:38_; _Gen. 4:3-5_): the daughters, the feasts ("did eat"), the false worship ("bowed down"). The mingling mechanism of the old world repeats point for point at the border of the promised land — entering by invitation, not invasion.
- _Num. 25:3._ "And Israel joined himself unto Baalpeor: and the anger of the LORD was kindled against Israel" — the end of the sequence: the people who could not be cursed are JOINED to Baal-peor by their own act. What Balaam's enchantments could not do, the mingling did.
- _Num. 31:16._ "these caused the children of Israel, through the counsel of Balaam, to commit trespass against the LORD in the matter of Peor" — Scripture names the author: Baal-peor was not drift but design, "the counsel of Balaam" — the serpent's standing strategy against the peculiar people: corrupt what you cannot curse.
  - **the doctrine of Balaam** = corrupting by mingling what cannot be cursed — the serpent's standing strategy against the peculiar people; unable to bring a curse from without (_Num. 23:9_), Balaam counsels Balak to draw Israel into the daughters, the feasts, and the worship of Moab (_Num. 25:1-3_; _Num. 31:16_), so that the people curse themselves by their own transgression; Christ names it a permanent doctrine and lays it against a New Testament church (_Rev. 2:14_).
- _Rev. 2:14._ "thou hast there them that hold the doctrine of Balaam, who taught Balac to cast a stumblingblock before the children of Israel, to eat things sacrificed unto idols, and to commit fornication" — Christ Himself codifies the mechanism as "the doctrine of Balaam" and finds it HELD inside a Christian church. Mingling — the idol feast and the strange marriage — is the enemy's transhistorical method, carried forward to the end (see "As in the Days of Noah — the Last Mingling").
- _PP 454.2._ White: "It was the object of these women, in their association with the Hebrews, to seduce them into transgression of the law of God... These motives were studiously concealed under the garb of friendship" — the breach narrated: the corruption entered "under the garb of friendship," not by open assault. Mingling, not war, is the method.
- _PP 455.1._ White: "Their iniquitous practices did that for Israel which all the enchantments of Balaam could not do—they separated them from God" — the doctrine of Balaam stated exactly: the mingling accomplished what the enchantments could not. The curse that heaven refused, the people pronounced upon themselves — and tens of thousands fell by the plague.
- _4aSG 48.2._ White: "there was no way to bring a curse upon them but by seducing them to transgression... He counseled Balak to proclaim an idolatrous feast in honor of their idol gods... and then the most beautiful Midianitish women should entice the Israelites to transgress the law of God... This Satanic counsel succeeded too well" — the counsel of Balaam itemized as a deliberate plan: feast, women, idol sacrifice — the daughters-feasts-worship mechanism in full.
- _DAR 356.1._ Smith, on _Rev. 2:14_: "not being permitted by the Lord to curse them, he resolved to accomplish essentially the same thing, though in a different way. He therefore counseled Balak to seduce them, by means of the females of Moab... The plan succeeded" — the pioneer commentary defining the doctrine of Balaam as corrupting-by-mingling what could not be cursed: the very symbol this section owns.

**The vote at Ramah — "make us a king like all the nations" — 1 Sam. 8:5, 7, 19-20:**

- _1 Sam. 8:5._ "now make us a king to judge us like all the nations" — c. 1050 BC the peculiar people vote away their peculiarity: the open demand to be reckoned among the very nations Balaam confessed they were set apart from (_Num. 23:9_). The mingling of Shittim has become a national platform.
- _1 Sam. 8:7._ "they have not rejected thee, but they have rejected me, that I should not reign over them" — God reads the demand for likeness as rejection of His own reign. To want to be like the nations is to unseat the King who made them unlike the nations; the breach is measured at the throne.
- _1 Sam. 8:19-20._ "Nay; but we will have a king over us; That we also may be like all the nations" — the demand doubled and made explicit against Samuel's warning. What the enemy worked by seduction at Peor, the people here choose with open eyes: the peculiar treasure begging to be common coin.
- _GSAM 46.1._ Loughborough: "In their dissatisfaction they requested of Samuel that he appoint a king over them, and the Lord said to Samuel, 'They have rejected me, that I should not reign over them'" — the pioneer witness reading the king demand as the rejection of God's reign for likeness to the nations.
- _PP 607.2._ White: "God had separated the Israelites from every other people, to make them His own peculiar treasure. But they, disregarding this high honor, eagerly desired to imitate the example of the heathen! And still the longing to conform to worldly practices and customs exists among the professed people of God" — the section's thesis in her own words, tied by her directly to _1 Pet. 2:9_: "for the sake of earthly distinction they sacrifice the unspeakable honor" of showing forth His praises. The king demand is the peculiar treasure despising its own peculiarity, and the same conformity-hunger lives on in the modern church.

**The crowning case — Solomon and the statute proved true — 1 Kings 11:1-8:**

- _1 Kings 11:1-2._ "But king Solomon loved many strange women... Of the nations concerning which the LORD said unto the children of Israel, Ye shall not go in to them... for surely they will turn away your heart after their gods: Solomon clave unto these in love" — c. 930 BC the wisest king breaks the exact marriage statute of _Deut. 7:3-4_, with the statute's own words quoted over his head as he does it.
- _1 Kings 11:4._ "when Solomon was old, that his wives turned away his heart after other gods: and his heart was not perfect with the LORD his God" — the statute's stated reason proved true to the letter: "they will turn away thy son" — and they turned away the son of David. No wisdom exempts from the law of mingling; the man best equipped to think himself the exception was not.
- _1 Kings 11:7-8._ "Then did Solomon build an high place for Chemosh, the abomination of Moab, in the hill that is before Jerusalem, and for Molech... And likewise did he for all his strange wives" — the fruit: the temple-builder erects high places to Chemosh and Molech in the face of the temple itself. Moab's daughters at Shittim, Moab's god above Jerusalem — mingling always ends at the strange altar, the way of Cain rebuilt by the son of David (see "Cain and Abel — the Two Worships").
- _PK 54.1._ White: "Solomon flattered himself that his wisdom and the power of his example would lead his wives from idolatry to the worship of the true God... Vain hope! Solomon's mistake in regarding himself as strong enough to resist the influence of heathen associates was fatal" — the missionary-by-mingling rationalization named and sentenced: alliance does not convert; it corrupts. The world does not rise; the church descends.

**The inspired post-mortem — one line over the whole history — Ps. 106:35-36:**

- _Ps. 106:35-36._ "But were mingled among the heathen, and learned their works. And they served their idols: which were a snare unto them" — the Spirit's verdict on the entire national record, from Shittim to the captivity, in one sentence: mingled, learned, served, snared. The two-classes law in four steps — mingling breeds imitation, imitation breeds idolatry, idolatry becomes the snare.
- _2T 125.1._ White: "Many of the professed, peculiar people of God are so conformed to the world that their peculiar character is not discerned, and it is difficult to distinguish 'between him that serveth God and him that serveth Him not'" — the Psalm 106 breach applied to the church: mingling erases the discernible line between the two classes. Distinctness lost is identity lost.

**The charter re-issued — the peculiar people carried into the church — Titus 2:14; 1 Pet. 2:9:**

- _Titus 2:14._ "Who gave himself for us, that he might redeem us from all iniquity, and purify unto himself a peculiar people, zealous of good works" — the symbol carried forward: Christ's blood purchases "a peculiar people." The set-apart treasure is the redeemed's identity, not an Old Testament relic; the cross re-founds the charter of Sinai.
- _1 Pet. 2:9._ "But ye are a chosen generation, a royal priesthood, an holy nation, a peculiar people; that ye should shew forth the praises of him who hath called you out of darkness" — the _Exo. 19:5-6_ charter re-issued to the Christian church almost word for word: chosen, royal priesthood, holy nation, peculiar people. Distinctness remains the covenant identity to the end, and its purpose is witness — "shew forth the praises."
- _AA 521.3._ White: "Many of the believers to whom Peter addressed his letters, were living in the midst of heathen, and much depended on their remaining true to the high calling of their profession... 'Ye are a chosen generation... a royal priesthood, an holy nation, a peculiar people'" — the charter confirmed as a live command to Christians "living in the midst of heathen": the symbol is portable, and the posture it demands is the same.
- _CG 449.3._ White: "Our only safety is to stand as God's peculiar people. We must not yield one inch to the customs and fashions of this degenerate age, but stand in moral independence, making no compromise with its corrupt and idolatrous practices" — the peculiar-people symbol defined as SAFETY, the echo of "dwell in safety alone" (_Deut. 33:28_), and non-mingling — "no compromise" — named as the standing posture of the last church.

**DEFINITION — ISRAEL MINGLES — "LIKE ALL THE NATIONS" =** the two-classes law applied to the covenant NATION, and proved across its whole history from Shittim (c. 1407 BC) to Samuel (c. 1050 BC) to Solomon (c. 930 BC). God's charter made distinctness the identity itself: obedience to the covenant constitutes Israel "a peculiar treasure... above all people, a kingdom of priests" (_Exo. 19:5-6_), "a peculiar people... above all the nations" (_Deut. 14:2_), fenced by non-mingling statutes whose reason is stated on their face — no covenants, no marriages, "for they will turn away thy son from following me" (_Deut. 7:2-6_). Even Balaam's hired mouth must confess the appointed posture: "the people shall dwell alone, and shall not be reckoned among the nations" (_Num. 23:9_), a dwelling alone that is safety, not loss (_Deut. 33:28_). And because no curse from without can touch such a people (_PP 439.2_), the serpent's seed works by its standing method: mingling. At Baal-peor the days-of-Noah sequence repeats — the daughters, the feasts, the bowing down (_Num. 25:1-3_; _Gen. 6:2_; _Matt. 24:38_; _Gen. 4:3-5_) — by "the counsel of Balaam" (_Num. 31:16_), accomplishing "that... which all the enchantments of Balaam could not do" (_PP 455.1_), and Christ codifies the method as "the doctrine of Balaam" still held inside a New Testament church (_Rev. 2:14_). What entered as seduction matures into policy: "make us a king to judge us like all the nations" (_1 Sam. 8:5, 19-20_) — the peculiar people voting away their peculiarity, which God weighs at the throne: "they have rejected me, that I should not reign over them" (_1 Sam. 8:7_; _PP 607.2_; _GSAM 46.1_). Solomon is the crowning case: the wisest king breaks the exact marriage statute, his wives turn away his heart precisely as the statute foretold, and the temple-builder ends building high places to Chemosh and Molech before Jerusalem (_1 Kings 11:1-8_) — the way of Cain rebuilt by the son of David — the missionary-by-mingling hope pronounced "vain... fatal" (_PK 54.1_). The Spirit's post-mortem on it all is one line: "[they] were mingled among the heathen, and learned their works... which were a snare unto them" (_Ps. 106:35-36_). Yet the charter does not die with the nation: Christ redeems and purifies "unto himself a peculiar people" (_Titus 2:14_), and Peter re-issues Sinai's charter to the church almost word for word (_1 Pet. 2:9_; _AA 521.3_), so that "our only safety is to stand as God's peculiar people... making no compromise" (_CG 449.3_; _2T 125.1_). The witness repeats: the mingling that emptied Eden's line before the flood emptied the covenant nation after it — the doctrine of Balaam runs forward into the church's marriage to the empire (see "The Church in the Wilderness — the Mountains Keep the Faith") and waits for the last church (see "As in the Days of Noah — the Last Mingling," "The Seal or the Mark — the Distinction Made Visible").

## 9. The Church in the Wilderness — the Mountains Keep the Faith

> The two classes cross into the Christian era unchanged: when the church married the empire, the true church — the woman of Revelation 12 — fled into the wilderness, and for 1260 prophetic days the mountains kept the faith that the mingled majority in the plains and cities had surrendered.

**Scripture likens God's people to a woman — the true church defined — Jer. 6:2; Rev. 12:1-2; Eph. 5:25, 27:**

- _Jer. 6:2._ "I have likened the daughter of Zion to a comely and delicate [woman]" — Scripture's own key, laid down before the symbol is used: God Himself likens His covenant people, the daughter of Zion, to a woman. The Bible defines its own figure.
- _Rev. 12:1._ "And there appeared a great wonder in heaven; a woman clothed with the sun, and the moon under her feet, and upon her head a crown of twelve stars" — the woman appears: arrayed in Christ's righteousness, standing on the covenant, crowned with the twelve. She is a corporate body of the faithful, not a city and not a numbers-majority.
  - **the woman** = the true church, the seed of the woman corporate — the pure bride of Christ, kept distinct by loyalty, not by numbers; God's own likeness for the daughter of Zion (_Jer. 6:2_; _Rev. 12:1-2_; _Eph. 5:25, 27_; _Rev. 12:17_).
- _Rev. 12:2._ "And she being with child cried, travailing in birth, and pained to be delivered" — the true church bears Christ into the world through suffering; her identity is a birthing loyalty, not comfort or crowds. She is the woman of the enmity — the seed-bearing line of Genesis 3:15 traced from "The Enmity — Two Seeds from One Fall."
- _Eph. 5:25._ "Husbands, love your wives, even as Christ also loved the church, and gave himself for it" — the church is Christ's bride, the pure woman; this is why Scripture figures apostasy as adultery — the woman who marries another has forsaken her true Husband.
- _Eph. 5:27._ "That he might present it to himself a glorious church, not having spot, or wrinkle, or any such thing; but that it should be holy and without blemish" — the true woman is defined by purity kept, not popularity gained: "not having spot" — the exact opposite of the mingled majority.

**Two women, two classes — the bride and the harlot — Rev. 17:5; James 4:4; COL 283.3:**

- _Rev. 17:5._ "And upon her forehead [was] a name written, MYSTERY, BABYLON THE GREAT, THE MOTHER OF HARLOTS AND ABOMINATIONS OF THE EARTH" — the counter-woman. Prophecy sets two women over against each other: the faithful bride, and the harlot who DID marry the world — Babylon, the city-system of "Babel — Congregating for Convenience" wearing a woman's name. Two women, two classes; there is no third.
- _James 4:4._ "Ye adulterers and adulteresses, know ye not that the friendship of the world is enmity with God? whosoever therefore will be a friend of the world is the enemy of God" — the great mingling named in one line: a church seeking the friendship of the world commits spiritual adultery. This is the exact mechanism of the fourth-century union.
- _COL 283.3._ White: "There are only two classes in the world today, and only two classes will be recognized in the judgment—those who violate God's law and those who obey it. Christ gives the test by which to prove our loyalty or disloyalty" — the two-classes doctrine in bare form: the line runs on obedience to God's commandments, exactly the mark of the woman's remnant seed (_Rev. 12:17_).

**The great mingling repeats — the church marries the empire — GC 43.1; GC 45.1; PP 81.2:**

- _GC 43.1._ White: "Most of the Christians at last consented to lower their standard, and a union was formed between Christianity and paganism... the Christian religion became corrupted, and the church lost her purity and power. There were some, however, who were not misled by these delusions" — the historical fact: under Constantine the "converted" pagan masses brought their customs in, and the church that stopped fleeing the world lost her purity — while a faithful few refused, splitting the two classes again.
- _GC 45.1._ White: "But there is no union between the Prince of light and the prince of darkness, and there can be no union between their followers... He then brought his power to bear more fully upon these, and inspired them to persecute those who remained true to God" — the union of convenience turns persecuting: Satan, having led the compromised church further from truth, "inspired them to persecute those who remained true to God." This is precisely what drives the woman to flee.
- _PP 81.2._ White: "For some time the two classes remained separate. The race of Cain... dispersed over the plains and valleys where the children of Seth had dwelt; and the latter, in order to escape from their contaminating influence, withdrew to the mountains... So long as this separation continued, they maintained the worship of God in its purity. But in the lapse of time they ventured, little by little, to mingle with the inhabitants of the valleys" — the master pattern from before the flood (see "The Mingling — How the Peculiar People Lost Their Peculiarity"): Cain's race in the plains, the faithful in the mountains, worship kept pure only while separate. The Christian era now repeats it point for point.

**The woman flees — the wilderness and the 1260 days — Rev. 12:6, 14; MWM 213.1:**

- _Rev. 12:6._ "And the woman fled into the wilderness, where she hath a place prepared of God, that they should feed her there a thousand two hundred [and] threescore days" — the section's title text. When the empire married the church, the true church did not conquer the plains — she FLED, kept distinct by separation, fed by God in a place He prepared, for the 1260 prophetic days.
  - **the wilderness** = the place of preservation-by-separation for the true church during the mingled centuries — not a geography but a condition: God's provided refuge, entered by flight rather than conquest, where the woman is fed away from the serpent through the 1260 days/years (_Rev. 12:6_; _Rev. 12:14_; _Heb. 11:38_; _2 Cor. 6:17_).
- _Rev. 12:14._ "And to the woman were given two wings of a great eagle, that she might fly into the wilderness, into her place, where she is nourished for a time, and times, and half a time, from the face of the serpent" — the flight restated: God's own provision (eagle's wings, cf. _Ex. 19:4_) carries the church into HER place, nourished away from the serpent for time + times + half a time — the same 1260, the medieval night.
- _MWM 213.1._ Miller: "Three times we have witnessed... the fulfilment of the 'time, times, and an half,' in the accomplishment of the 'forty-two months,' in the completion of the 'twelve hundred and three-score days'" — the pioneer reading: time, times, and a half = forty-two months = 1260 days, one period, its fulfilment already witnessed.

**The mountains keep the faith — Heb. 11:37-38; GC 64.1; 65.2-3; 267.2:**

- _Heb. 11:37._ "They were stoned, they were sawn asunder, were tempted, were slain with the sword: they wandered about in sheepskins and goatskins; being destitute, afflicted, tormented" — the roll of the faithful reaches the persecuted witnesses: the same class as the wilderness woman, marked by loss for the truth's sake.

And the verdict is inverted from all appearance — the mingled majority that held the cities and called itself the church was the world "not worthy" of the exiles it hunted. The mountain-and-plain geography of the two classes holds (see "The Two Lineages — the City and the Name").
- _GC 64.1._ White: "There were some, however, who refused to yield to the authority of pope or prelate... A separation took place. - _GC 267.2._ White: "God's witnesses remained in a state of obscurity... hunted, betrayed, tortured, buried in dungeon cells, martyred for their faith, or compelled to flee to mountain fastnesses, and to dens and caves of the earth—then the faithful witnesses prophesied in sackcloth. **No cathedral — worship in spirit and in truth — John 4:21, 23-24; GC 66.1-2:**

- _John 4:21._ "Woman, believe me, the hour cometh, when ye shall neither in this mountain, nor yet at Jerusalem, worship the Father" — Christ DE-localizes worship: it is bound to no city, no temple, no shrine. - _John 4:23._ "But the hour cometh, and now is, when the true worshippers shall worship the Father in spirit and in truth: for the Father seeketh such to worship him" — the true worshippers carry a portable altar — the pilgrim worship of Abraham's tent-altars in "Abraham and Lot — the Tent and the Plain" — and this is exactly how the woman worships in the wilderness: no building, full acceptance with God.
- _John 4:24._ "God [is] a Spirit: and they that worship him must worship [him] in spirit and in truth" — the definition of acceptable worship is inward loyalty, not architecture: the ground for reading the wilderness centuries as genuine, God-accepted worship.
- _GC 66.1._ White: "God had provided for His people a sanctuary of awful grandeur, befitting the mighty truths committed to their trust. To those faithful exiles the mountains were an emblem of the immutable righteousness of Jehovah... God's servants should be as firm as the unchanging hills" — no cathedral, and none needed: God's own provision was their sanctuary, and the mountains preached His law's immutability to those who kept it.

**Separation is the standing command — the line runs to the remnant — 2 Cor. 6:17-18; Rev. 12:17; 18:4; MWM 316.2:**

- _2 Cor. 6:17._ "Wherefore come out from among them, and be ye separate, saith the Lord, and touch not the unclean [thing]; and I will receive you" — the command that defines the class in every age: SEPARATION. The wilderness flight is this imperative obeyed under persecution.
- _2 Cor. 6:18._ "And will be a Father unto you, and ye shall be my sons and daughters, saith the Lord Almighty" — the reward of separation is sonship: the place "prepared of God" is where He receives and fathers those who came out.
- _MWM 316.2._ Miller: "A church, according to the Scriptures, is a religious assembly, selected and called out of the world, by the doctrine of the gospel, to worship the true God according to his word... we have... 'the church in the wilderness,' Acts 7:38" — the pioneer definition: the church is by nature CALLED OUT of the world; "the church in the wilderness" is one of Scripture's own names for her.
- _Rev. 12:17._ "And the dragon was wroth with the woman, and went to make war with the remnant of her seed, which keep the commandments of God, and have the testimony of Jesus Christ" — the wilderness woman is one continuous line ending in a commandment-keeping remnant. The mark of the true class is never numbers but obedience and testimony — the seed of the woman of "The Enmity — Two Seeds from One Fall," carried forward to "The Seal or the Mark — the Distinction Made Visible."
- _Rev. 18:4._ "And I heard another voice from heaven, saying, Come out of her, my people, that ye be not partakers of her sins, and that ye receive not of her plagues" — the same come-out call re-sounded at the end. The wilderness principle is not medieval-only: it is the standing summons out of the church-world union, from Constantine's day to the final call of "Come Out, My People — the Pilgrim Call."

# Part IV — The Days of the Son of Man

## 10. As in the Days of Noah — the Last Mingling

> Christ appointed BOTH Genesis judgments — the flood and Sodom — as one picture of the last age: lawful things made the whole of life, the covenant line mingled with the world until "knew not," and sudden separation by judgment "the same day" the righteous go out; the last mingling is not out in the world but inside the church, and against it the old calls of separation sound again as present truth.

**Christ appoints the FIRST Genesis judgment as the picture of the last age — Luke 17:26-27; Matt. 24:37-39:**

- _Luke 17:26._ "And as it was in the days of Noe, so shall it be also in the days of the Son of man" — Christ Himself fixes the type: the antediluvian world is the mirror of the last generation. He does not reach for a new figure; He reaches back to the first judgment.
  - **the days of the Son of man** = the last age, defined by Christ Himself through the matched Noah/Lot pattern — heedless absorption in lawful things ("they did eat, they drank... bought... sold... planted... builded"), mingled worship as the covenant line blends with the world, and sudden separation by judgment on "the same day" the righteous go out; the mark is not open rebellion but "knew not" (_Luke 17:26-30_; _Matt. 24:37-39_; _Gen. 6:2, 5_; _Gen. 13:12-13_). Defined in full at the close.
- _Luke 17:27._ "They did eat, they drank, they married wives, they were given in marriage, until the day that Noe entered into the ark, and the flood came, and destroyed them all" — four verbs, every one lawful. Eating, drinking, and marrying made the whole of life right up to the day of separation; not one open crime is named — the indictment is absorption.
- _Matt. 24:37._ "But as the days of Noe were, so shall also the coming of the Son of man be." — the parallel account fixes the same appointment; the days of Noah are not an illustration but a stated equivalence.
- _Matt. 24:38-39._ "they were eating and drinking, marrying and giving in marriage... And knew not until the flood came, and took them all away; so shall also the coming of the Son of man be" — the diagnosis in two words: "knew not." Heedlessness, not defiance, is the mark of the age (eating and drinking as the mark of the heedless class is defined in "The Days of Noah — the Institutions of Eden Under Attack").
- _DA 633.1._ White: "Christ continues, pointing out the condition of the world at His coming: 'As the days of Noah were, so shall also the coming of the Son of man be'... He tells us that as it was in Noah's day, so will it be when the Son of man comes again." — confirms that the Noah saying is Christ's own definition of the state of the world at His coming, not merely its suddenness.
- _AH 121.3._ White: "There is in itself no sin in eating and drinking, or in marrying and giving in marriage... But in the days of Noah men married without consulting God..." — the precise edge of the charge: the condemned things were LAWFUL, carried on without God until they became the whole of life.

**The SECOND judgment added as a matched pair — the days of Lot — Luke 17:28-30:**

- _Luke 17:28._ "Likewise also as it was in the days of Lot; they did eat, they drank, they bought, they sold, they planted, they builded" — six verbs now: to the antediluvian four Christ adds buying, selling, planting, building — the busy commerce of the city. The last age is pictured as uninterrupted business.
- _Luke 17:29-30._ "But the same day that Lot went out of Sodom it rained fire and brimstone from heaven, and destroyed them all. Even thus shall it be in the day when the Son of man is revealed" — "the same day": the going out of the righteous and the fall of judgment are one event, as at the ark door. The commerce runs to the very hour.
- _CD 17.6._ White: "Our Saviour warned His disciples that just prior to His second coming a state of things would exist very similar to that which preceded the flood. Eating and drinking would be carried to excess... more and more like the doomed inhabitants of Sodom" — joins BOTH judgments, flood and Sodom, as one last-days picture of appetite and pleasure — exactly Luke 17's pairing.
- _CD 435.2._ White: "The world's Redeemer, who knows well the state of society in the last days, represents eating and drinking as the sins that condemn this age" — names the lawful-made-ultimate as the condemning sin of the age, per the Noah pattern.

**What "the days of Noah" actually were — the mingling behind the meals — Gen. 6:2, 5:**

- _Gen. 6:2._ "That the sons of God saw the daughters of men that they were fair; and they took them wives of all which they chose" — behind the eating and marrying stood the MINGLING: the covenant line of Seth blended with the line of Cain, and the distinction dissolved (the sons of God, the daughters of men, and the mingling are defined in "The Mingling — How the Peculiar People Lost Their Peculiarity"). The attack fell on the Eden institution of marriage.
- _Gen. 6:5._ "And GOD saw that the wickedness of man was great in the earth, and that every imagination of the thoughts of his heart was only evil continually" — the result once the two classes blended: universal corruption. The mingling, not merely open sin, produced the world the flood swept away.
- _PP 81.2._ White: "For some time the two classes remained separate... So long as this separation continued, they maintained the worship of God in its purity. But in the lapse of time they ventured, little by little, to mingle with the inhabitants of the valleys... and they lost their peculiar, holy character" — the master witness: separation maintained the pure worship, and gradual mingling — "little by little" — dissolved the peculiar character. THIS is the substance of "as it was in the days of Noe."
- _PP 85.4._ White: Enoch, "fearing that their infidelity might lessen his reverence for God... avoided constant association with them, and spent much time in solitude, giving himself to meditation and prayer" — the counter-example inside the same generation: deliberate separation from the mingled society preserved the walk with God.

**The last mingling is from INSIDE — a form of godliness, Laodicea at ease — 2 Tim. 3:1-5; Rev. 3:17:**

- _2 Tim. 3:1-2._ "This know also, that in the last days perilous times shall come. For men shall be lovers of their own selves, covetous..." — Paul dates the catalogue "in the last days," and the peril is a character-list, not a persecution-list: the danger comes from what men LOVE.
- _2 Tim. 3:4-5._ "lovers of pleasures more than lovers of God; Having a form of godliness, but denying the power thereof: from such turn away" — the crisis of the list: these are the PROFESSED. The Noah/Lot mingling is relocated from the world into the church — appetite and pleasure ruling under an exterior of piety — and the command is still separation: "from such turn away."
  - **having a form of godliness** = the last-days condition inside the church: an outward shape of religion emptied of its power, the professed mingled with the world in appetite and pleasure while keeping the exterior of piety — the Noah/Lot mingling moved from the world into the sanctuary (_2 Tim. 3:1-5_; _Rev. 3:17_; _Matt. 24:39_). Defined in full at the close.
- _Rev. 3:17._ "Because thou sayest, I am rich, and increased with goods, and have need of nothing; and knowest not that thou art wretched, and miserable, and poor, and blind, and naked" — Laodicea: the church at ease in the city, and — like the antediluvians — "knowest not." The days-of-Noah heedlessness has entered the sanctuary.
- _COL 158.3._ White: "The Lord says, 'Because thou sayest, I am rich, and increased with goods, and have need of nothing...' The gold tried in the fire is faith that works by love" — applies _Rev. 3:17_ to the church's self-satisfied wealth; the remedy is not more goods but tried faith.
- _MYP 390.2._ White: "The eternal God has drawn the line of distinction between the saint and the sinner... The two classes do not blend into each other imperceptibly, like the colors of a rainbow, but are as distinct as midday and midnight" — carries the two-classes line into the end time, and names the modern mingling precisely: "God's people cannot with safety enter into intimate associations with those who know the truth, but do not practice it."

**Against it, the old calls sound again — out of the cities — Gen. 13:12-13; CH 268.1:**

- _Gen. 13:12-13._ "Abram dwelled in the land of Canaan, and Lot dwelled in the cities of the plain, and pitched his tent toward Sodom. But the men of Sodom were wicked and sinners before the LORD exceedingly" — the two-classes divergence in one verse: Abraham in the pilgrim land, Lot drifting cityward while the city's wickedness is named in the same breath (the tent-and-altar life and the plain of Sodom are defined in "Abraham and Lot — the Tent and the Plain").
- _PP 168.1._ White: "In how wide contrast to the life of Abraham was that of Lot! Once they had been companions, worshiping at one altar... Lot had chosen Sodom for its pleasure and profit... he had permitted his children to mingle with a corrupt and idolatrous people" — the narrative of the drift: one altar shared, then the city chosen for pleasure and profit, and the children lost to mingling.
- _CH 268.1._ White: "'Out of the cities,' is my message... I hope and pray and believe that they will now arouse to the importance of getting out into the country" — the present-truth reissue of the old separations: her repeated turn-of-the-century "Out of the cities" appeals reissued the antediluvian separation as present truth for the last generation — the modern tent-pitching.
- _CL 5.2._ White: "Parents flock with their families to the cities, because they fancy it easier to obtain a livelihood there... they stay where they are, until Satan gains full control of their children" — the modern Lot-toward-Sodom drift named: cityward for livelihood, exactly as Lot chose the plain, and the children lost to the mingling exactly as in Sodom.
- _John 4:21, 23-24._ "neither in this mountain, nor yet at Jerusalem... the true worshippers shall worship the Father in spirit and in truth... God is a Spirit: and they that worship him must worship him in spirit and in truth" — the call out of the city costs no worship: Christ de-localizes it. The peculiar people are bound to no city or temple; they carry the true worship with them, as Abraham carried his altar.

**The return toward the Eden table — Gen. 1:29:**

- _Gen. 1:29._ "Behold, I have given you every herb bearing seed, which is upon the face of all the earth, and every tree, in the which is the fruit of a tree yielding seed; to you it shall be for meat" — the original grant, one of the institutions of Eden with marriage and the Sabbath (the institutions of Eden and the original diet are defined in "The Days of Noah — the Institutions of Eden Under Attack"); flesh was not granted until after the flood (_Gen. 9:3_).
- _Ev 663.4._ White: "In the beginning, fruit was pronounced by God as 'good for food.' The permission to eat flesh meat was a consequence of the fall. Not till after the Flood was man given permission to eat the flesh of animals" — anchors the health message to Genesis: _Gen. 1:29_ the original table, flesh a post-fall, post-flood concession.
- _CG 383.1._ White: "Is it not time that all should aim to dispense with flesh foods?... Let them, rather, return to the wholesome and delicious food given to man in the beginning" — the reform framed explicitly as a RETURN toward Eden's institution: not salvation by menu, but the peculiar people re-learning the institutions of Eden while the world perfects the way of Cain (see "Cain and Abel — the Two Worships").

**The last-generation text — Remember Lot's wife — Luke 17:32; Gen. 19:26:**

- _Luke 17:32._ "Remember Lot's wife." — three words, Christ's own memorial nailed into the days-of-the-Son-of-man discourse itself. The warning is not against staying in the city; she had left it. It is against a divided leaving of it — a separation of the feet without a separation of the heart.
  - **Remember Lot's wife** = the last-generation warning against a divided separation — out of the city with the body, in it with the heart; she obeyed the call to come out, and one backward look fixed her forever as the standing type of the professor whose separation never reached the affections (_Luke 17:32_; _Gen. 19:26_; _Gen. 13:12-13_). Defined in full at the close.
- _Gen. 19:26._ "But his wife looked back from behind him, and she became a pillar of salt" — the literal fate the command points back to: her feet left Sodom, her heart lingered, and the lingering look was fatal. Separation of the body without separation of the heart is no separation at all.

**DEFINITION — AS IN THE DAYS OF NOAH — THE LAST MINGLING =** Christ appointed both Genesis judgments as one picture of the last age: "as it was in the days of Noe... likewise also as it was in the days of Lot" (_Luke 17:26-30_; _Matt. 24:37-39_) — lawful things made the whole of life ("they did eat, they drank, they married wives... they bought, they sold, they planted, they builded"), carried on "until the day" the righteous went out and judgment fell "the same day," with the fatal diagnosis "knew not." Behind the antediluvian meals and marriages stood the mingling: the sons of God took the daughters of men (_Gen. 6:2_), the two classes blended, "every imagination of the thoughts of his heart was only evil continually" (_Gen. 6:5_) — for "so long as this separation continued, they maintained the worship of God in its purity," and mingling "little by little" cost them their "peculiar, holy character" (_PP 81.2_). The last generation meets this same mingling from INSIDE the profession: "lovers of pleasures more than lovers of God; having a form of godliness, but denying the power thereof" (_2 Tim. 3:1-5_), Laodicea "rich, and increased with goods... and knowest not" (_Rev. 3:17_) — the church at ease in the city, distinct from the world "as midday and midnight" in God's intent (_MYP 390.2_) yet blending in fact. Against it the old calls of Eden and of Abraham sound again as present truth: "'Out of the cities,' is my message" (_CH 268.1_; _CL 5.2_) — the modern tent-pitching against the modern drift toward the plain (_Gen. 13:12-13_; _PP 168.1_) — and the return toward the Eden table of _Gen. 1:29_ (_Ev 663.4_; _CG 383.1_), the peculiar people re-learning the institutions of Eden while the world perfects the way of Cain, carrying worship "in spirit and in truth" out of every city (_John 4:21-24_). And over the whole age stands Christ's shortest sermon: "Remember Lot's wife" (_Luke 17:32_; _Gen. 19:26_) — for the days of the Son of man end as Noah's and Lot's did, in sudden separation by judgment, and the only separation that survives it is one of the heart. The two classes here re-formed become fully and finally visible in the seal and the mark (see "The Seal or the Mark — the Distinction Made Visible"), under the last form of the old call: "Come out of her, my people" (see "Come Out, My People — the Pilgrim Call").

**Symbols defined here:**

- **the days of the Son of man** = the last age, defined by Christ through the matched Noah/Lot pattern — heedless absorption in lawful things (eating, drinking, marrying, buying, selling, planting, building), mingled worship as the covenant line blends with the world, and sudden separation by judgment on "the same day" the righteous go out; the mark of the age is "knew not" (_Luke 17:26-30_; _Matt. 24:37-39_; _Gen. 6:2, 5_; _Gen. 13:12-13_).
- **Remember Lot's wife** = the last-generation warning against a divided separation — out of the city with the body, in it with the heart; she obeyed the call to come out, and one backward look fixed her forever as the standing type of the professor whose separation never reached the affections (_Luke 17:32_; _Gen. 19:26_; _Gen. 13:12-13_).
- **having a form of godliness** = the last-days condition inside the church: an outward shape of religion emptied of its power, the professed mingled with the world in appetite and pleasure while keeping the exterior of piety — the Noah/Lot mingling relocated from the world into the sanctuary, Laodicea "rich, and increased with goods" yet "knowest not" (_2 Tim. 3:1-5_; _Rev. 3:17_; _Matt. 24:39_).

**Symbols carried:** the mingling, the sons of God, and the daughters of men — the mechanism by which the peculiar people lose their peculiarity (see "The Mingling — How the Peculiar People Lost Their Peculiarity"); the institutions of Eden, the original diet, and eating and drinking as the mark of the heedless class (see "The Days of Noah — the Institutions of Eden Under Attack"); the tent and the altar, the plain of Sodom, and the pilgrim identity behind the out-of-the-cities call (see "Abraham and Lot — the Tent and the Plain"); the peculiar people (see "Israel Mingles — 'Like All the Nations'"); the way of Cain, the self-chosen worship the world perfects while the remnant returns to the appointed way (see "Cain and Abel — the Two Worships"); the two classes made finally visible in seal and mark (see "The Seal or the Mark — the Distinction Made Visible"); and the last call out of the city (see "Come Out, My People — the Pilgrim Call").

**For discussion:**

1. Every verb in Christ's picture of the last age is lawful — "they did eat, they drank... they bought, they sold, they planted, they builded" (_Luke 17:27-28_) — and the fatal word is "knew not" (_Matt. 24:39_). What does the Sethites' little-by-little descent to the valleys look like among us now — in associations, in appetite, in what we love (_MYP 390.2_)?

## 11. The Seal or the Mark — the Distinction Made Visible

> The war on appointed worship that opened at Cain's altar terminates at the Sabbath: the two classes reach their final, universal, visible form — all the world marked for the beast's self-appointed worship, or sealed in the forehead with the sign of Him that made heaven and earth — and judgment makes the distinction permanent.

**The war on worship goes universal — Rev. 13:3-4:**

- _Rev. 13:3._ "his deadly wound was healed: and all the world wondered after the beast" — the power whose fourth-century church-world union supplanted the Creator's appointed day (the union traced in "The Church in the Wilderness — the Mountains Keep the Faith"; _GC_ chs. 3-4) recovers its dominion, and the wondering is now worldwide. The two classes are about to become visible on a world scale.
- _Rev. 13:4._ "they worshipped the dragon which gave power unto the beast: and they worshipped the beast, saying, Who is like unto the beast? who is able to make war with him?" — the text names the issue itself: WORSHIP. The serpent's seed does not end in mere irreligion but in a rival worship, dragon-empowered, universal — the enmity of the two seeds (see "The Enmity — Two Seeds from One Fall") fought out at the last altar.

**The mark enforced — and the siege of buying and selling — Rev. 13:16-17:**

- _Rev. 13:16._ "he causeth all, both small and great, rich and poor, free and bond, to receive a mark in their right hand, or in their foreheads" — the rival worship is ENFORCED on every class of men; the badge of allegiance is set where choice lives (the forehead) or where conduct complies (the hand).
  - **the mark of the beast** = allegiance to the self-appointed worship-system enforced in place of the Creator's appointed worship — the way of Cain full grown, the substitute offering (_Gen. 4:3-5_) become world law; received in forehead or hand (_Rev. 13:16_), set opposite "the commandments of God" (_Rev. 14:9-12_), and enforced by economic siege (_Rev. 13:17_). Defined in full at the close.
- _Rev. 13:17._ "that no man might buy or sell, save he that had the mark, or the name of the beast, or the number of his name" — the city-system's final weapon against the separate class: the confederated commerce of Babel turned into a siege engine (see "Babel — Congregating for Convenience"; see "The Two Lineages — the City and the Name"). Cain's congregation no longer merely excludes the separate; it starves them. The pilgrims (see "Abraham and Lot — the Tent and the Plain") are pressed toward conformity.
- _CS 59.3._ White: "The time is coming when we cannot sell at any price. The decree will soon go forth prohibiting men to buy or sell of any man save he that hath the mark of the beast" — confirms _Rev. 13:17_ as a literal, coming decree against the class that will not conform.

**The first angel answers — worship Him that MADE — Rev. 14:6-7; Exo. 20:8-11; Gen. 2:1-3:**

- _Rev. 14:7._ "Fear God, and give glory to him; for the hour of his judgment is come: and worship him that made heaven, and earth, and the sea, and the fountains of waters" — heaven's counter-summons: worship the CREATOR. The phrase is lifted from the fourth commandment; the angel answers the beast's worship by pointing all the world back to the Creator's own memorial.
- _Exo. 20:8, 10._ "Remember the sabbath day, to keep it holy... the seventh day is the sabbath of the LORD thy God" — the commandment the first angel echoes; the day is the LORD'S, not man's to appoint.
- _Exo. 20:11._ "For in six days the LORD made heaven and earth, the sea, and all that in them is... wherefore the LORD blessed the sabbath day, and hallowed it" — the exact ground _Rev. 14:7_ stands on: "made heaven, and earth, and the sea." To worship Him that made is to keep the memorial He appointed for the making.
- _Gen. 2:3._ "And God blessed the seventh day, and sanctified it: because that in it he had rested from all his work which God created and made" — the Sabbath instituted in Eden, before any fall or any nation — one of the three institutions of Eden the serpent's seed has warred on from the beginning (see "The Days of Noah — the Institutions of Eden Under Attack"). The end-time battle over worship is a fight over the original, appointed day.
- _CCh 334.6._ White: "The Sabbath question is to be the issue in the great final conflict in which all the world will act a part... Each sabbath institution bears the name of its author, an ineffaceable mark that shows the authority of each" — each day carries its author's authority: the Creator's appointed Sabbath against the self-chosen day — Abel's appointed offering against Cain's substitute, on a world scale (see "Cain and Abel — the Two Worships").

**God names the Sabbath His SIGN — Exo. 31:13-17; Eze. 20:12, 20:**

- _Exo. 31:13._ "Verily my sabbaths ye shall keep: for it is a sign between me and you throughout your generations; that ye may know that I am the LORD that doth sanctify you" — God Himself names the Sabbath His SIGN: the mark of the God who sanctifies, set between Him and His people.
- _Exo. 31:17._ "It is a sign... for ever: for in six days the LORD made heaven and earth, and on the seventh day he rested, and was refreshed" — the sign is "for ever," and its content is creation — the same credential the first angel proclaims (_Rev. 14:7_).
- _Eze. 20:12._ "I gave them my sabbaths, to be a sign between me and them, that they might know that I am the LORD that sanctify them" — the sign identifies WHOSE people they are, and that God — not they themselves — sanctifies them.
- _Eze. 20:20._ "And hallow my sabbaths; and they shall be a sign between me and you, that ye may know that I am the LORD your God" — the sign marks those who acknowledge the Creator's authority against every rival: the self-appointed system carries the authority of its own maker.

**The sign set in the forehead is the seal — Rev. 7:2-3; 14:1; Eze. 9:4-6:**

- _Rev. 7:2-3._ "having the seal of the living God... Hurt not the earth, neither the sea, nor the trees, till we have sealed the servants of our God in their foreheads" — the judgment winds are held back until the servants of God are sealed in their FOREHEADS. A sign set in the forehead is a seal; the Sabbath-sign of _Exo. 31_ and _Eze. 20_ is here fixed on the separate class before the plagues fall.
  - **the seal of God** = the Creator's sign of ownership and sanctification set in the forehead — the seventh-day Sabbath kept by faith. Scripture calls the Sabbath God's "sign" that He is the LORD who made heaven and earth and who sanctifies His people (_Exo. 31:13-17_; _Eze. 20:12, 20_); the sign in the forehead is the seal (_Rev. 7:2-3_), and the sealed bear the Father's name (_Rev. 14:1_). Defined in full at the close.
- _Rev. 14:1._ "a Lamb stood on the mount Sion, and with him an hundred forty and four thousand, having his Father's name written in their foreheads" — the sealed bear the Father's NAME — His character and authority — the exact opposite of the beast's name and mark (_Rev. 13:17_).
- _Eze. 9:4._ "set a mark upon the foreheads of the men that sigh and that cry for all the abominations that be done in the midst thereof" — the Old Testament type of the sealing: its mark — the seal-type — falls on the MOURNERS over abomination, not on the comfortable majority of the professed people.
- _Eze. 9:6._ "but come not near any man upon whom is the mark; and begin at my sanctuary" — the slaying spares only the marked, and it begins at the sanctuary: the distinction is drawn inside the professed church first, then made permanent by judgment — the flood-pattern of the two classes repeated (see "As in the Days of Noah — the Last Mingling").
- _SSP 132.2._ Haskell: "this knowledge of God, is the seal placed in the foreheads of the chosen generation... this, Jehovah has placed in His Sabbath... The seal placed in the forehead by the angel, cannot be read by man; only God and heavenly beings can read it. For this reason, no civil law can enforce Sabbath keeping. Man may keep the form one day in seven, but only a knowledge of God can give the seal in the forehead" — the pioneer exposition binding _Rev. 7_ to the Sabbath-sign of _Exo. 31_ and _Eze. 20_: the seal is a knowledge of God, and precisely because it is inward, no decree can counterfeit it — only the mark can be enforced by law.

**The third angel names the two classes — Rev. 14:9-12:**

- _Rev. 14:9-10._ "If any man worship the beast and his image, and receive his mark in his forehead, or in his hand, The same shall drink of the wine of the wrath of God" — the third angel's warning defines the mark by its object: WORSHIP of the beast and his image — allegiance to the self-appointed system enforced in place of the Creator's appointed worship. The way of Cain (see "Cain and Abel — the Two Worships") stands full grown: a substitute worship, now persecuting the true.
- _Rev. 14:12._ "Here is the patience of the saints: here are they that keep the commandments of God, and the faith of Jesus" — the sealed class named against the mark-bearers: commandment-keepers (the Sabbath memorial included) holding the faith of Jesus — Abel's line in its final, visible form.
- _TMR 112.1._ Andrews: "the worship of the beast and his image, and the reception of his mark, is the opposite of keeping the commandments of God and the faith of Jesus. And we have seen that one of the commandments of God has been superseded by an institution of the Romish apostasy" — the pioneer statement of the antithesis: the mark is the supplanting of the Sabbath commandment itself.
- _GC 605.2._ White: "The Sabbath will be the great test of loyalty... While one class, by accepting the sign of submission to earthly powers, receive the mark of the beast, the other, choosing the token of allegiance to divine authority, receive the seal of God" — the master statement: the Sabbath is the final test that draws the visible line between the two classes.
- _8T 117.4._ White: "The sign, or seal, of God is revealed in the observance of the seventh-day Sabbath, the Lord's memorial of creation.... The mark of the beast is the opposite of this—the observance of the first day of the week. This mark distinguishes those who acknowledge the supremacy of the papal authority from those who acknowledge the authority of God" (compiled in _LDE 224.3_) — seal = the Creator's memorial; mark = its opposite: the badge distinguishing the two allegiances.
- _Ev 235.2._ White: "When you obey the decree that commands you to cease from labor on Sunday and worship God, while you know that there is not a word in the Bible showing Sunday to be other than a common working day, you consent to receive the mark of the beast, and refuse the seal of God" — the mark is received KNOWINGLY: informed submission to the enforced substitute over the Creator's appointed day.
- _SR 383.3._ White: "In the issue of the great contest, two distinct, opposite classes are developed. One class 'worship the beast and his image, and receive his mark'... The other class, in marked contrast to the world, 'keep the commandments of God, and the faith of Jesus'" — the two-classes thesis of the whole study stated in its final-conflict form.

**The door shuts — the distinction fixed forever — Rev. 22:11:**

- _Rev. 22:11._ "He that is unjust, let him be unjust still... and he that is holy, let him be holy still" — probation closes and the two classes are fixed forever. As at the flood the LORD shut the door and judgment fixed the separation the mingled world had blurred (see "As in the Days of Noah — the Last Mingling"), so the distinction made visible by seal and mark is made permanent by judgment. After this word there is no third class and no crossing over.

**DEFINITION — THE SEAL OR THE MARK — THE DISTINCTION MADE VISIBLE =** the final, universal form of the two classes, drawn at the point of appointed worship. The healed beast carries the war on worship to all the world (_Rev. 13:3-4_) and enforces a mark — allegiance to a self-appointed worship-system, the way of Cain full grown — on forehead and hand, with an economic siege against all who refuse (_Rev. 13:16-17_). Heaven answers by the first angel's summons to "worship him that made heaven, and earth" (_Rev. 14:7_), the very language of the fourth commandment (_Exo. 20:8-11_), pointing to the memorial planted in Eden before any fall or nation (_Gen. 2:1-3_). God has already named that memorial His SIGN — the sign that He is the LORD who made all and who sanctifies His people (_Exo. 31:13-17_; _Eze. 20:12, 20_) — and a sign set in the forehead is a seal: the seal of the living God fixed on His servants before the winds are loosed (_Rev. 7:2-3_), the Father's name written within (_Rev. 14:1_), typed by the mark set on the men that sigh and cry over abomination before the slaying begins at the sanctuary (_Eze. 9:4-6_). The third angel names the two companies plainly: mark-bearers who worship the beast, and saints who "keep the commandments of God, and the faith of Jesus" (_Rev. 14:9-12_; _TMR 112.1_) — and the testimony confirms what the verses built: "The Sabbath will be the great test of loyalty," one class receiving the mark of the beast, the other the seal of God (_GC 605.2_; _8T 117.4_; _Ev 235.2_), a knowledge of God no civil law can enforce (_SSP 132.2_). Then the door shuts: "he that is holy, let him be holy still" (_Rev. 22:11_) — the distinction, made visible at last, is made permanent by judgment. Two altars, two names in the forehead, two classes — and after the sealing, no third.

**Symbols defined here:**

- **the seal of God** = the Creator's sign of ownership and sanctification set in the forehead — the seventh-day Sabbath kept by faith; God's own "sign" that He is the LORD who made heaven and earth and who sanctifies His people, marking the separate class before the judgment falls (_Rev. 7:2-3_; _Rev. 14:1_; _Exo. 31:13-17_; _Exo. 20:8-11_; _Gen. 2:1-3_; _Eze. 20:12, 20_; _Eze. 9:4_).
- **the mark of the beast** = allegiance to the self-appointed worship-system enforced in place of the Creator's appointed worship — the way of Cain grown to world scale; received in forehead or hand, set opposite "the commandments of God, and the faith of Jesus," and enforced by the decree that "no man might buy or sell" (_Rev. 13:16-17_; _Rev. 14:9-11_; _Rev. 14:12_; _Rev. 13:3-4_; _Rev. 14:7_).

**Symbols carried:** the way of Cain and the two offerings — the self-chosen worship against the appointed lamb, here full grown into the mark (see "Cain and Abel — the Two Worships"); the enmity and the two seeds, now in their last, visible array (see "The Enmity — Two Seeds from One Fall"); the institutions of Eden — the Sabbath of _Gen. 2:1-3_, the last of the three under attack (see "The Days of Noah — the Institutions of Eden Under Attack"); the city-system whose confederated commerce becomes the buy-or-sell siege (see "Babel — Congregating for Convenience"; see "The Two Lineages — the City and the Name"); pilgrims and strangers, the class the siege is aimed at (see "Abraham and Lot — the Tent and the Plain"); the days of the Son of man, the heedless age in which the sealing quietly closes and whose mingling judgment ends (see "As in the Days of Noah — the Last Mingling"); the church-world union that supplanted the appointed day (see "The Church in the Wilderness — the Mountains Keep the Faith"). The summons that gathers the sealed out of Babylon before the plagues is the study's close (see "Come Out, My People — the Pilgrim Call").

If the sealing distinguishes mourners from the comfortable INSIDE the professed people of God, what am I sighing and crying over now — and what am I comfortable with?

The mark is received by those who obey the decree "while you know that there is not a word in the Bible" behind it (_Ev 235.2_), and the siege says "no man might buy or sell" without it (_Rev. 13:17_). What would it cost you, concretely — income, standing, security — to stand with them "that keep the commandments of God, and the faith of Jesus" (_Rev. 14:12_), and what are you doing now so that the cost is already counted before _Rev. 22:11_ fixes the classes forever?

# Part V — Come Out

## 12. Come Out, My People — the Pilgrim Call

> Every separation in this study — the ark door, the angels' hands on Lot, Abraham's "get thee out," the woman's flight to the wilderness — gathers at last into one voice from heaven, "Come out of her, my people"; those who obey take up the pilgrim confession of the whole cloud of witnesses, and the two classes end at two cities: Babylon thrown down, and the New Jerusalem coming down.

**The last separation-call — a voice from heaven to MY PEOPLE — Rev. 18:1-4:**

- _Rev. 18:1._ "And after these things I saw another angel come down from heaven, having great power; and the earth was lightened with his glory" — the closing scene opens with light, not doom: before Babylon falls, the whole earth is lightened, that the call which follows may be heard by every soul still inside.
- _Rev. 18:2._ "And he cried mightily with a strong voice, saying, Babylon the great is fallen, is fallen, and is become the habitation of devils, and the hold of every foul spirit, and a cage of every unclean and hateful bird" — the city of Cain in its end-form: Babylon, the serpent-seed's confederated city-system (see "Babel — Congregating for Convenience"), become at last what it tended toward from Cain's first wall — the habitation of devils. The city that began with a fugitive from God's presence ends as the cage of every unclean bird.
- _Rev. 18:3._ "For all nations have drunk of the wine of the wrath of her fornication, and the kings of the earth have committed fornication with her, and the merchants of the earth are waxed rich through the abundance of her delicacies" — the mingling gone universal: all nations drunk, kings in alliance, merchants enriched (see "The Mingling — How the Peculiar People Lost Their Peculiarity"). No one is outside her by geography; the only way out of Babylon is the way of the call.
- _Rev. 18:4._ "And I heard another voice from heaven, saying, Come out of her, my people, that ye be not partakers of her sins, and that ye receive not of her plagues" — the last separation-call of the whole study, addressed to MY PEOPLE — God still has a people INSIDE Babylon. Two mercies attach to the coming out: that they partake not of her sins, and that they receive not of her plagues — and the call sounds before the plagues fall on the marked class (see "The Seal or the Mark — the Distinction Made Visible").
- _GC 604.1._ White: "But God still has a people in Babylon; and before the visitation of His judgments these faithful ones must be called out... the call is heard: 'Come out of her, My people'" — the call is present truth, uniting with the third angel's message as "the final warning to be given to the inhabitants of the earth"; it is spoken to faithful souls yet inside.
- _BP2 69.3._ Bates: "the cry is made in the Protestant churches, 'Come out of her, my people.' What is now the response? Thousands on thousands dissolve their connection" — the historical fact: the come-out cry sounded audibly in the 1844 Advent movement and was answered; "the last, and crowning test which God ever gave his people to come away and separate themselves."

**Every earlier call was this call — the ark door, the mountain, the tent, the wilderness — Gen. 7:1; 19:17; 12:1; Rev. 12:6:**

- _Gen. 7:1._ "Come thou and all thy house into the ark; for thee have I seen righteous before me in this generation" — the first "come": the righteous called IN while the world is left out. The ark door is "Come out of her, my people" in its first, physical form — one door dividing two classes (see "The Days of Noah — the Institutions of Eden Under Attack").
- _Gen. 19:17._ "Escape for thy life; look not behind thee, neither stay thou in all the plain; escape to the mountain, lest thou be consumed" — the angels' hands on Lot: flee the doomed city, no lingering, no backward look. The mountain answers the plain — the geography of the two classes from the beginning (see "The Two Lineages — the City and the Name"; "Abraham and Lot — the Tent and the Plain").
- _Gen. 12:1._ "Get thee out of thy country, and from thy kindred, and from thy father's house, unto a land that I will shew thee" — Abraham's "get thee out," the call that made him father of the faithful; the covenant people begin with a come-out, separation from country and kindred toward a land not yet seen.
- _Rev. 12:6._ "And the woman fled into the wilderness, where she hath a place prepared of God, that they should feed her there a thousand two hundred and threescore days" — the true church kept by flight through the mingled centuries: the woman flees the city-power exactly as Lot fled to the mountain, and God prepares her place (see "The Church in the Wilderness — the Mountains Keep the Faith"). The come-out class has never been without a prepared refuge.
- _MWV2 266.5._ Miller: "God has called his people into the wilderness state, time and again. 1st. Abraham was called to go out from the land of his fathers 'into a strange land'... for he looked for a city which hath foundations, whose Builder and Maker is God" — the pioneer reading: the come-out-into-the-wilderness pattern runs from Abraham forward, and the pilgrim's eye is on the city God builds.
- _GC 65.3._ White: "Behind the lofty bulwarks of the mountains—in all ages the refuge of the persecuted and oppressed—the Waldenses found a hiding place... Here, for a thousand years, witnesses for the truth maintained the ancient faith" — the historical anchor: the mountain class of Gen. 19:17 and Rev. 12:6 walked through history, and the truth was kept a thousand years by the separated — "witnesses for the truth" in the very cloud of Heb. 12:1.

**Separation is a settled law — and its reward is adoption — Isa. 52:11; 2 Cor. 6:14-18:**

- _Isa. 52:11._ "Depart ye, depart ye, go ye out from thence, touch no unclean thing; go ye out of the midst of her; be ye clean, that bear the vessels of the LORD" — the Old Testament root of both texts that follow: going out of her midst and touching no unclean thing is a settled law of God's people, not a novelty of the last message. Note the reason attached — "that bear the vessels of the LORD": the separated are separated FOR service.
- _2 Cor. 6:14._ "Be ye not unequally yoked together with unbelievers: for what fellowship hath righteousness with unrighteousness? and what communion hath light with darkness?" — the unequal yoke is the very mingling by which the peculiar people lost their peculiarity (see "The Mingling — How the Peculiar People Lost Their Peculiarity"); the questions admit no third answer.
- _2 Cor. 6:15-16._ "And what concord hath Christ with Belial?... ye are the temple of the living God; as God hath said, I will dwell in them, and walk in them; and I will be their God, and they shall be my people" — the ground of the separation: the believer is God's dwelling; the temple cannot be yoked to idols. "They shall be my people" — the same "my people" the voice from heaven addresses in Babylon.
- _2 Cor. 6:17._ "Wherefore come out from among them, and be ye separate, saith the Lord, and touch not the unclean thing; and I will receive you" — Isa. 52:11 quoted into the church: God's reception is conditioned on the coming out. He does not receive a people still yoked to Babylon.
- _2 Cor. 6:18._ "And will be a Father unto you, and ye shall be my sons and daughters, saith the Lord Almighty" — separation is not loss but ADOPTION. What Babylon calls exile, heaven calls a family; the unequal yoke forfeits the sonship, the come-out receives it.

**The pilgrim confession — the cloud of witnesses and the better country — Heb. 11:13-16; 12:1; 13:14:**

- _Heb. 11:13._ "These all died in faith, not having received the promises... and confessed that they were strangers and pilgrims on the earth" — one confession on every tongue in this study: Abel at his altar, Enoch walking with God, Noah in the ark, Abraham in his tent, the church in the wilderness — the pilgrims-and-strangers identity (see "Abraham and Lot — the Tent and the Plain").
- _Heb. 11:14._ "For they that say such things declare plainly that they seek a country" — the confession is a declaration: to confess "stranger here" is to declare "citizen there."
  - **the better country** = the heavenly inheritance every pilgrim witness confessed and sought — God's answer to the man-built city of Cain; not any place on this earth, for "here have we no continuing city," but the prepared city to come (_Heb. 11:14_; _Heb. 11:16_; _Heb. 13:14_). Defined in full at the close.
- _Heb. 11:15._ "And truly, if they had been mindful of that country from whence they came out, they might have had opportunity to have returned" — the come-out is kept in the MIND: the road back stood open all their lives, and the pilgrims refused it. Lot's wife took it — the backward look that unmade an escape (_Gen. 19:17, 26_; see "As in the Days of Noah — the Last Mingling").
- _Heb. 11:16._ "But now they desire a better country, that is, an heavenly: wherefore God is not ashamed to be called their God: for he hath prepared for them a city" — the desire names the class, and God answers the desire with a CITY. Man built the first city in rebellion; God builds the last one for the pilgrims — and He is "not ashamed" to be the God of pilgrims; He owns no other class.
- _Heb. 12:1._ "Wherefore seeing we also are compassed about with so great a cloud of witnesses, let us lay aside every weight, and the sin which doth so easily beset us, and let us run with patience the race that is set before us" — the seal on the whole arc: the reader now stands compassed about by every come-out witness of the study and is summoned to run the same separated, pilgrim race.
- _Heb. 13:14._ "For here have we no continuing city, but we seek one to come" — the pilgrim confession stated as doctrine: no continuing city HERE — not Cain's city of Enoch (Gen. 4:17), not Babel, not Sodom, not Babylon. The seed of the woman seeks the one to come.
- _PP 81.1._ White: Cain stands "at the head of that great class of men who worship the god of this world," while Abel led a pastoral life, and the descendants of Seth followed the same course, "counting themselves 'strangers and pilgrims on the earth,' seeking 'a better country, that is, an heavenly.' Hebrews 11:13, 16" — the two classes at the fountainhead named in EGW's own words, from this very spine text: the pilgrim confession is as old as the second generation from Eden.
- _PP 170.2._ White: "We must dwell as pilgrims and strangers here if we would gain 'a better country, that is, an heavenly'... Those who are children of Abraham will be seeking the city which he looked for, 'whose builder and maker is God'" — the confession made present-tense: to gain the country, dwell as pilgrims now.

**The come-out class named — peculiar, priestly, at war with the flesh — Titus 2:14; 1 Pet. 2:9-11:**

- _Titus 2:14._ "Who gave himself for us, that he might redeem us from all iniquity, and purify unto himself a peculiar people, zealous of good works" — the purpose of the cross is a purified, PECULIAR people (the covenant identity itself, see "Israel Mingles — 'Like All the Nations'"); the separation is not idleness in the mountain but zeal in good works — separated FOR service, as Isa. 52:11's vessel-bearers.
- _1 Pet. 2:9._ "But ye are a chosen generation, a royal priesthood, an holy nation, a peculiar people; that ye should shew forth the praises of him who hath called you out of darkness into his marvellous light" — the identity in full, and its verb: a people CALLED OUT — of darkness into light — for the purpose of witness. The come-out class exists to shew forth His praises, not to hide.
- _1 Pet. 2:10._ "Which in time past were not a people, but are now the people of God: which had not obtained mercy, but now have obtained mercy" — the "my people" of Rev. 18:4 defined: made a people by mercy, not by blood or city; no one is born inside it, and no one still yoked to Babylon is outside its reach.
- _1 Pet. 2:11._ "Dearly beloved, I beseech you as strangers and pilgrims, abstain from fleshly lusts, which war against the soul" — the pilgrim confession bound to appetite: the very "eating and drinking" indulgence that marked the heedless antediluvian class wars against the pilgrim soul (see "The Days of Noah — the Institutions of Eden Under Attack").
- _CD 379.2._ White: "Our habits of eating and drinking show whether we are of the world or among the number whom the Lord by His mighty cleaver of truth has separated from the world. These are His peculiar people, zealous of good works" — the separation made visible at the table; Titus 2:14 and 1 Pet. 2:9 joined to a real, present distinction, and the "cleaver of truth" is the Lord's own.
- _AA 109.4._ White: the members of God's church are "to be zealous of good works, separating from worldly ambition and walking in the footsteps of Him who went about doing good" — set apart FOR good works, not merely apart.
- _CG 449.3._ White: "Our only safety is to stand as God's peculiar people... making no compromise with its corrupt and idolatrous practices" — the peculiar identity as the ONLY safety: no inch yielded — the come-out call lived out daily.

**Two cities end the study — one thrown down, one coming down — Rev. 18:20-21; 21:2:**

- _Rev. 18:20._ "Rejoice over her, thou heaven, and ye holy apostles and prophets; for God hath avenged you on her" — heaven's verdict on the city that persecuted the pilgrim class from righteous Abel forward (see "Cain and Abel — the Two Worships"): avenged.
- _Rev. 18:21._ "And a mighty angel took up a stone like a great millstone, and cast it into the sea, saying, Thus with violence shall that great city Babylon be thrown down, and shall be found no more at all" — the end of the first city: the city-system Cain began and Nimrod raised again at Babel is "found no more at all." This is the urgency of the call — the city cannot be reformed, only left.
  - **the two cities** = the two destinations of the two classes: Babylon, the end-form of the city of Cain, "become the habitation of devils" and thrown down to be found no more; and the New Jerusalem, the city God "hath prepared" for the pilgrims, coming down from God out of heaven (_Rev. 18:2_; _Rev. 18:21_; _Rev. 21:2_; _Heb. 11:16_). Defined in full at the close.
- _Rev. 21:2._ "And I John saw the holy city, new Jerusalem, coming down from God out of heaven, prepared as a bride adorned for her husband" — the end of the second city: the pilgrims who sought a country receive a city; "he hath prepared for them a city" (_Heb. 11:16_) kept to the letter. Note the directions: Babylon is thrown DOWN; the New Jerusalem comes DOWN — one city falls by judgment, the other descends as a gift.
- _COL 152.1._ White: "The Pharisee and the publican represent two great classes... Their first two representatives are found in the first two children that were born into the world" — the thesis of the whole study confirmed at its close: from the first two children born to the last two cities standing, there are two classes, and only two; the come-out call is where the reader chooses between them.

**DEFINITION — COME OUT, MY PEOPLE — THE PILGRIM CALL =** the last form of every separation this study has traced. The voice from heaven — "Come out of her, my people, that ye be not partakers of her sins, and that ye receive not of her plagues" (_Rev. 18:4_) — is the ark door of Noah ("Come thou... into the ark," _Gen. 7:1_), the angels' hands on Lot ("escape to the mountain," _Gen. 19:17_), Abraham's "Get thee out" (_Gen. 12:1_), and the woman's flight to the wilderness (_Rev. 12:6_) spoken once more, worldwide, to God's OWN people still inside Babylon — the city of Cain "become the habitation of devils" (_Rev. 18:2_; _GC 604.1_). It is no novelty but a settled law: "go ye out of the midst of her; be ye clean" (_Isa. 52:11_), "come out from among them, and be ye separate" (_2 Cor. 6:17_) — and its reward is not exile but ADOPTION: "And will be a Father unto you, and ye shall be my sons and daughters" (_2 Cor. 6:18_) — the unequal yoke forfeits the sonship, the separation receives it. The whole cloud of witnesses seals the call: every witness of the study "confessed that they were strangers and pilgrims on the earth" (_Heb. 11:13_; _PP 81.1_), refused the open road back (_Heb. 11:15_), and desired "a better country, that is, an heavenly" — wherefore God, "not ashamed to be called their God... hath prepared for them a city" (_Heb. 11:16_; _Heb. 13:14_; _PP 170.2_) — and the reader, "compassed about with so great a cloud of witnesses" (_Heb. 12:1_) — Abel at his altar, Enoch walking with God, Noah at the ark door, Abraham in his tent, the woman in the wilderness, the Waldenses behind the mountains (_GC 65.3_; _MWV2 266.5_) — is summoned to run the same race. The come-out class is Christ's own purchase, "a peculiar people, zealous of good works" (_Titus 2:14_), "called... out of darkness into his marvellous light," beseeched "as strangers and pilgrims" to abstain from the fleshly lusts of the heedless line (_1 Pet. 2:9-11_; _CD 379.2_; _CG 449.3_) — separated from the world's ambition and appetite, and separated FOR service. The testimony fixes it as present truth — "God still has a people in Babylon... these faithful ones must be called out" (_GC 604.1_) — and history proves it a cry that has already sounded once and was answered by thousands, when in 1844 "thousands on thousands" dissolved their connection at the call (_BP2 69.3_). And the study ends where the two classes end — at two cities: Babylon "thrown down... found no more at all" (_Rev. 18:21_), the holy city "coming down from God out of heaven" (_Rev. 21:2_). One city falls; one descends. From the first two children born into the world (_COL 152.1_; _PP 81.1_) the classes have been two and only two; the pilgrim call sets the choice before every soul — which seed, which city, which class.

**Symbols defined here:**

- **the better country** = the heavenly inheritance every pilgrim witness of the study confessed and sought — God's answer to the man-built city of Cain; those who came out refused to return, "desire a better country, that is, an heavenly," and receive the city God prepared, for "here have we no continuing city, but we seek one to come" (_Heb. 11:13-16_; _Heb. 13:14_; _PP 170.2_).
- **the two cities** = the two destinations of the two classes: Babylon, the end-form of the city of Cain — "the habitation of devils," thrown down and "found no more at all" — and the New Jerusalem, the prepared city, "coming down from God out of heaven"; the come-out call sets every reader between them (_Rev. 18:2_; _Rev. 18:21_; _Rev. 21:2_; _Heb. 11:16_).

**Symbols carried:** Babel / Babylon, the confederated city-system here reaching its fall (see "Babel — Congregating for Convenience"); the city of Cain and the mountains-and-plains geography that Gen. 19:17 replays (see "The Two Lineages — the City and the Name"); the two worships from the first two altars, avenged at last in Rev. 18:20 (see "Cain and Abel — the Two Worships"); the mingling / the unequal yoke that Rev. 18:3 shows gone universal (see "The Mingling — How the Peculiar People Lost Their Peculiarity"); pilgrims and strangers, the confessed identity of Heb. 11:13, with the tent and the altar against the plain (see "Abraham and Lot — the Tent and the Plain"); the peculiar people, the covenant identity restored in Titus 2:14 and 1 Pet. 2:9 (see "Israel Mingles — 'Like All the Nations'"); the woman and the wilderness of Rev. 12:6 (see "The Church in the Wilderness — the Mountains Keep the Faith"); eating and drinking / the fleshly lusts of 1 Pet. 2:11 (see "The Days of Noah — the Institutions of Eden Under Attack"); the backward look of Lot's wife, the escape unmade (see "As in the Days of Noah — the Last Mingling"); the plagues from which the come-out delivers, falling on the marked class (see "The Seal or the Mark — the Distinction Made Visible"); and the two seeds themselves, whose God-put enmity this call consummates (see "The Enmity — Two Seeds from One Fall").

**For discussion:**

1. The voice of Rev. 18:4 is addressed to "MY PEOPLE" — God owns them as His even while they stand in the wrong city — and 2 Cor. 6:17-18 promises that the one who comes out is received as a son or daughter. What does that say about how we regard souls still in Babylon — and if separation is adoption and not loss, what makes remaining feel safer than coming out?
2. The pilgrims "might have had opportunity to have returned" (_Heb. 11:15_) yet refused to be "mindful of that country," while Lot's wife left the city with her body and kept it in her heart (_Gen. 19:17, 26_). Since the come-out is kept in the mind and the desire (_Heb. 11:16_), where does your own "opportunity to return" still stand open — and what would it mean this week to stop being "mindful of that country" you came out of?
3. The come-out class is "a peculiar people, zealous of good works" (_Titus 2:14_), called out of darkness "that ye should shew forth the praises of him" (_1 Pet. 2:9_) — separated FOR witness, not merely FROM the city. How do you tell the difference, in practice, between a separation that shews forth His praises and a withdrawal that merely hides — and which city does each one serve?

## Appendix — Symbol Dictionary

Every symbol defined in this handbook, alphabetically (leading articles ignored), with its receipts and owning section.

- **Abel's offering (the firstlings of his flock)** = the appointed lamb brought by faith — righteousness by faith enacted at the world's first altar, the firstling's blood confessing the coming Lamb of God who takes away sin; accepted because it comes God's appointed way, trusting wholly in the promised atonement (_Gen. 4:4_; _Heb. 11:4_; _John 1:29_; _Gen. 3:15_). — defined in "Cain and Abel — the Two Worships".
- **Babel / Babylon** = the serpent-seed's confederated city-system: congregation for convenience and self-security in defiance of God's command to fill the earth, and self-naming in defiance of calling on the Name of the LORD; founded by Nimrod on the plain of Shinar, named CONFUSION by God's judgment, and carried forward by the Spirit to "Babylon the great" (_Gen. 11:4_; _Gen. 11:8-9_; _Gen. 10:8-10_; _Gen. 9:1_; _Gen. 4:26_; _Rev. 17:5_). — defined in "Babel — Congregating for Convenience".
- **the better country** = the heavenly inheritance every pilgrim witness of the study confessed and sought — God's answer to the man-built city of Cain; those who came out refused to return, "desire a better country, that is, an heavenly," and receive the city God prepared, for "here have we no continuing city, but we seek one to come" (_Heb. 11:13-16_; _Heb. 13:14_; _PP 170.2_). — defined in "Come Out, My People — the Pilgrim Call".
- **Cain's offering (the fruit of the ground)** = will-worship: the self-chosen, bloodless way substituted for God's appointed way — the first false worship; partial obedience (an altar, a gift) that omits the essential thing, faith in the promised Redeemer's blood, and comes in the worshiper's own merits, and to which God has not respect (_Gen. 4:3_; _Gen. 4:5_; _Gen. 4:7_; _Col. 2:23_). — defined in "Cain and Abel — the Two Worships".
- **calling on the name of the LORD** = the dividing act of the faithful seed — open, avowed worship, a public profession of loyalty that marks off the Sethite line from the city; crowned in Enoch, who walked with God in the corrupt age and preached judgment to the city of Cain (_Gen. 4:26_; _Gen. 5:24_; _Jude 1:14-15_; _PP 80.2_). — defined in "The Two Lineages — the City and the Name".
- **the city of Cain** = the serpent-seed's congregation — fortified convenience built by the fugitive from God's presence; man staying put in the cursed earth, distinguished in arts, industry, and violence-as-culture while God is forgotten; the civilization whose spirit prevails in the eating-drinking-marrying world Christ names as the type of the last age (_Gen. 4:16-17_; _Gen. 4:21-23_; _Matt. 24:38_; _PP 81.1_). — defined in "The Two Lineages — the City and the Name".
- **the daughters of men** = the Cainite line, the world — the daughters of the multiplying "men" of _Gen. 6:1_, the class of the man who went out from the presence of the LORD, already practicing polygamy (_Gen. 6:1-2_; _Gen. 4:16_; _Gen. 4:19_). — defined in "The Mingling — How the Peculiar People Lost Their Peculiarity".
- **the days of the Son of man** = the last age, defined by Christ through the matched Noah/Lot pattern — heedless absorption in lawful things (eating, drinking, marrying, buying, selling, planting, building), mingled worship as the covenant line blends with the world, and sudden separation by judgment on "the same day" the righteous go out; the mark of the age is "knew not" (_Luke 17:26-30_; _Matt. 24:37-39_; _Gen. 6:2, 5_; _Gen. 13:12-13_). — defined in "As in the Days of Noah — the Last Mingling".
- **the doctrine of Balaam** = corrupting by mingling what cannot be cursed — the serpent's standing strategy against the peculiar people; barred from cursing from without, Balaam counsels the daughters, the feasts, and the worship of Moab, so the people curse themselves by their own transgression; Christ names it a permanent doctrine and finds it held in a New Testament church (_Num. 31:16_; _Num. 25:1-3_; _Num. 23:9_; _Rev. 2:14_). — defined in "Israel Mingles — "Like All the Nations"".
- **dwelling alone** = the appointed posture of the woman's seed among the nations — separation as safety, not isolation as loss; Balaam's own mouth confesses the people "shall dwell alone, and shall not be reckoned among the nations," Moses seals it as "dwell in safety alone," and the demand for a king "like all the nations" is the deliberate reversal of the posture (_Num. 23:9_; _Deut. 33:28_; _1 Sam. 8:5, 20_). — defined in "Israel Mingles — "Like All the Nations"".
- **eating and drinking** = Christ's mark of the heedless class before the flood: lawful appetite — eating, drinking, marrying — made the whole of life, so that the ordinary swallowed the eternal and men "knew not" until judgment fell; not gross crime but total absorption in the permitted, the sign of a world that has erased the distinction between the holy and the common (_Matt. 24:38-39_; _Luke 17:27_). — defined in "The Days of Noah — the Institutions of Eden Under Attack".
- **the enmity** = the God-put antagonism between the two seeds — grace, not curse: fallen man is naturally at peace with the serpent, so "I will put enmity" is a supernatural interposition of implanted grace, creating "conflict where hitherto had been peace"; to lose it by befriending the world is to become "the enemy of God," and the world's hatred of the saints is the same enmity running outward (_Gen. 3:15_; _Jas. 4:4_; _John 15:19_; _GC 505.2_; _GC 506.2_). — defined in "The Enmity — Two Seeds from One Fall".
- **having a form of godliness** = the last-days condition inside the church: an outward shape of religion emptied of its power, the professed mingled with the world in appetite and pleasure while keeping the exterior of piety — the Noah/Lot mingling relocated from the world into the sanctuary, Laodicea "rich, and increased with goods" yet "knowest not" (_2 Tim. 3:1-5_; _Rev. 3:17_; _Matt. 24:39_). — defined in "As in the Days of Noah — the Last Mingling".
- **the institutions of Eden** = the three gifts God established before sin: MARRIAGE (one man and one woman made one flesh), the APPOINTED DIET (the seed-bearing bounty given for meat), and the SABBATH (the seventh day blessed and sanctified) — all three predating the fall, all three assaulted by the serpent's seed: polygamy against marriage, indulgence against the diet, self-appointed worship against the appointed, the last war ending at the Sabbath (_Gen. 2:24_; _Gen. 1:29_; _Gen. 2:1-3_; _Gen. 4:19_; _Gen. 6:2_). — defined in "The Days of Noah — the Institutions of Eden Under Attack".
- **the mark of the beast** = allegiance to the self-appointed worship-system enforced in place of the Creator's appointed worship — the way of Cain grown to world scale; received in forehead or hand, set opposite "the commandments of God, and the faith of Jesus," and enforced by the decree that "no man might buy or sell" (_Rev. 13:16-17_; _Rev. 14:9-11_; _Rev. 14:12_; _Rev. 13:3-4_; _Rev. 14:7_). — defined in "The Seal or the Mark — the Distinction Made Visible".
- **the mingling** = the alliance of the two classes by which the peculiar people lose their peculiarity — the standing mechanism of apostasy: choice by sight, union with the world, one-directional descent, ending in "all flesh had corrupted his way"; forbidden as the unequal yoke, named as the friendship of the world (_Gen. 6:1-5_; _Gen. 6:12_; _2 Cor. 6:14_; _1 Cor. 15:33_; _Jas. 4:4_). — defined in "The Mingling — How the Peculiar People Lost Their Peculiarity".
- **mountains and plains** = the geography of the two classes — the serpent's seed congregating over the plains and valleys for convenience, the faithful withdrawing to the mountains to keep their peculiar character and their worship pure; the geography of separation, not of appointed worship, given its biblical voice in "Escape to the mountain, lest thou be consumed" (_Gen. 4:16_; _Gen. 6:1-2_; _Gen. 19:17_; _PP 81.2_; _GC 65.3_). — defined in "The Two Lineages — the City and the Name".
- **the original diet** = the _Gen. 1:29_ grant — fruits, grains, herbs, and after the fall the herb of the field — with flesh not granted until after the flood, so that the vegetable grant stood through the whole antediluvian age and the days-of-Noah appetite ran against, not within, God's appointment (_Gen. 1:29_; _Gen. 3:18_; _Gen. 9:3_). — defined in "The Days of Noah — the Institutions of Eden Under Attack".
- **peculiar people** = God's purchased, set-apart treasure — distinctness is not an accessory to the covenant identity, it IS the covenant identity; Israel is a "peculiar treasure above all people" and "a kingdom of priests" precisely by keeping the covenant that unlikes them from the nations, and the same charter passes unchanged to the church redeemed by Christ's blood; to be conformed is to forfeit the identity (_Exo. 19:5-6_; _Deut. 14:2_; _Deut. 7:6_; _Titus 2:14_; _1 Pet. 2:9_). — defined in "Israel Mingles — "Like All the Nations"".
- **the plain of Sodom** = choice by sight — the well-watered convenience, "as the garden of the LORD," chosen with the eyes and no altar named, ending inside the doomed city by one unbroken drift: toward, in, gate (_Gen. 13:10-13_; _Gen. 14:12_; _Gen. 19:1_). — defined in "Abraham and Lot — the Tent and the Plain".
- **Remember Lot's wife** = the last-generation warning against a divided separation — out of the city with the body, in it with the heart; she obeyed the call to come out, and one backward look fixed her forever as the standing type of the professor whose separation never reached the affections (_Luke 17:32_; _Gen. 19:26_; _Gen. 13:12-13_). — defined in "As in the Days of Noah — the Last Mingling".
- **the seal of God** = the Creator's sign of ownership and sanctification set in the forehead — the seventh-day Sabbath kept by faith; God's own "sign" that He is the LORD who made heaven and earth and who sanctifies His people, marking the separate class before the judgment falls (_Rev. 7:2-3_; _Rev. 14:1_; _Exo. 31:13-17_; _Exo. 20:8-11_; _Gen. 2:1-3_; _Eze. 20:12, 20_; _Eze. 9:4_). — defined in "The Seal or the Mark — the Distinction Made Visible".
- **the seed of the serpent** = a spiritual paternity, not a bloodline — those who belong to the devil by doing his works: "of your father the devil" by doing his lusts, "the children of the wicked one" sown as tares, Cain "of that wicked one" though Eve's own son; the test is practice — "he that committeth sin is of the devil," "whosoever doeth not righteousness is not of God" (_John 8:44_; _Matt. 13:38-39_; _1 John 3:12_; _1 John 3:8_; _1 John 3:10_). — defined in "The Enmity — Two Seeds from One Fall".
- **the seed of the woman** = Christ first, and in Him His people — the singular seed "which is Christ," reckoned to all who are Christ's, sown by the Son of man as the children of the kingdom, sharing the head-bruising victory, and terminating in "the remnant of her seed" who keep the commandments of God and have the testimony of Jesus (_Gen. 3:15_; _Gal. 3:16_; _Gal. 3:29_; _Matt. 13:37-38_; _Rom. 16:20_; _Rev. 12:17_). — defined in "The Enmity — Two Seeds from One Fall".
- **the sons of God** = the covenant line of Seth, the professed people of God — Scripture's own idiom for Adam's faithful line ("the son of God," _Luke 3:38_) and the covenant people ("the children of the LORD your God," _Deut. 14:1_); read literally, not angels, for angels marry not (_Gen. 6:2_; _Luke 3:38_; _Deut. 14:1-2_; _Matt. 22:30_). — defined in "The Mingling — How the Peculiar People Lost Their Peculiarity".
- **strangers and pilgrims** = the confessed identity of the woman's seed in every age — those who own that earth is not home and declare plainly that they seek a better, heavenly country; Abraham the pattern, the church the heir of the title (_Heb. 11:13-16_; _1 Pet. 2:11_; _Gen. 12:1_). — defined in "Abraham and Lot — the Tent and the Plain".
- **the tent and the altar** = the pilgrim life — dwelling loose from the world with worship at the center, portable, needing no city or temple; an altar at every pitch, because worship is bound to spirit and truth, not to a place, and the tent confesses that home is the city God builds (_Gen. 12:7-8_; _Gen. 13:4, 18_; _Heb. 11:9-10_; _John 4:23-24_). — defined in "Abraham and Lot — the Tent and the Plain".
- **the tower** = man-made security against God's judgment and against His command to scatter and fill the earth — brick-and-slime presumption trusted for the safety and the name that belong to the LORD alone, exposed by the LORD's "coming down" and answered by the confusion of tongues and the very scattering it was built to prevent (_Gen. 11:3-5_; _Gen. 11:8_). — defined in "Babel — Congregating for Convenience".
- **the two cities** = the two destinations of the two classes: Babylon, the end-form of the city of Cain — "the habitation of devils," thrown down and "found no more at all" — and the New Jerusalem, the prepared city, "coming down from God out of heaven"; the come-out call sets every reader between them (_Rev. 18:2_; _Rev. 18:21_; _Rev. 21:2_; _Heb. 11:16_). — defined in "Come Out, My People — the Pilgrim Call".
- **the way of Cain** = self-appointed worship that hardens into a lineage and ends in persecuting the true — the false worshiper hating and slaying the true because his own works are evil and his brother's righteous; the pattern that opens the martyr stream from righteous Abel onward (_Jude 1:11_; _1 John 3:12_; _Gen. 4:8_; _Matt. 23:35_). — defined in "Cain and Abel — the Two Worships".
- **the wilderness** = the place of preservation-by-separation for the true church during the mingled centuries — a condition, not a geography: God's provided refuge, entered by flight and not conquest, where the woman is fed away from the serpent through the 1260 days/years; needing no cathedral because worship is "in spirit and in truth," and located by Scripture itself "in deserts, and in mountains, and in dens and caves of the earth" (_Rev. 12:6, 14_; _Heb. 11:38_; _John 4:23-24_; _2 Cor. 6:17_). — defined in "The Church in the Wilderness — the Mountains Keep the Faith".
- **the woman** = the true church, the seed of the woman corporate — the pure bride of Christ, kept distinct by loyalty rather than numbers; God likens the daughter of Zion to a woman, Christ loves the church as a husband his wife "not having spot," and her remnant seed keep the commandments of God and the testimony of Jesus, over against the harlot Babylon who married the world (_Jer. 6:2_; _Rev. 12:1-2, 17_; _Eph. 5:25, 27_; _Rev. 17:5_). — defined in "The Church in the Wilderness — the Mountains Keep the Faith".
