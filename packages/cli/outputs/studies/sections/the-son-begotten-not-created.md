## The Son — Begotten, Not Created; Did Christ Have a Beginning?

> The Word already WAS in the beginning — a distinct Person "with God" and Himself "was God"; yet He is a true Son, brought forth OF the Father in the days of eternity. Begotten, not created: derived from the Father as fountain, but so far back as to be, to us, without beginning.

**The goings forth of old — the Word who already WAS — Micah 5:2; John 1:1, 14, 18:**

- _Micah 5:2._ "out of thee shall he come forth unto me that is to be ruler in Israel; whose goings forth have been from of old, from everlasting" — the Ruler born in Bethlehem is no newcomer; His "goings forth" (origins, proceedings) reach back "from of old, from everlasting" (margin: "from the days of eternity"). He came forth, yet so far back as to be to us without beginning.
  - **the Son / the Word** = the literal, only-begotten divine Person — distinct from the Father yet "with God" and Himself "was God," of the Father's own substance; He existed "in the beginning," came forth from the Father in the days of eternity, and was made flesh as the only begotten of the Father (_John 1:1_; _John 1:14_; _Micah 5:2_; _John 8:58_; _Col. 1:17_). A real Son, truly brought forth, fully divine. Defined in full at the close.
- _John 1:1._ "In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God" — the Word already WAS at the beginning; co-existent "with God" (a distinct Person) and fully "was God" (the same divine nature). Personal distinction and full deity held together in one verse.
- _John 1:14._ "the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us... the glory as of the only begotten of the Father" — the Word incarnate is "the only begotten of the Father," a true Son sharing the Father's own glory, not an adopted or created being.
- _John 1:18._ "the only begotten Son, which is in the bosom of the Father, he hath declared him" — the Son rests "in the bosom of the Father," the language of a real, intimate filial relation; He alone declares the unseen God because He is OF Him.
- _CHR 9.1._ Waggoner: "It is not given to men to know when or how the Son was begotten; but we know that he was the Divine Word, not simply before He came to this earth to die, but even before the world was created... it was so far back in the ages of eternity as to be far beyond the grasp of the mind of man" — the Divine Word before the world was, begotten in unknowable ages of eternity; binds _John 1:1_, _Micah 5:2_, _John 8:42_.
- _PP 34.1._ White: "Christ, the Word, the only begotten of God, was one with the eternal Father—one in nature, in character, in purpose... 'His goings forth have been from of old, from everlasting.' Micah 5:2" — the whole spine in one paragraph: _John 1:1_, only begotten, one in nature with the Father, _Micah 5:2_.
- _EP 9.3._ White: "Christ the Word was one with the eternal Father, one in nature, in character, in purpose. 'His name shall be called... The mighty God'... His 'goings forth have been from of old, from everlasting.' Micah 5:2" — the Son one in NATURE with the Father and titled "The mighty God"; full deity of the One whose goings forth are everlasting.

**Self-existent deity, yet proceeded forth from God — John 8:58, 42; 16:28:**

- _John 8:58._ "Before Abraham was, I am" — Christ claims the divine self-existent name "I AM" (_Exo. 3:14_), asserting deity reaching before Abraham; the Jews took it as blasphemy and reached for stones (v. 59).
- _John 8:42._ "I proceeded forth and came from God; neither came I of myself, but he sent me" — "I proceeded forth and came from God": Christ's existence derives FROM the Father as fountain; He is not self-originated but truly came forth of God.
  - **only begotten / begotten not created** = truly brought forth OF the Father (not made, not created), and therefore of the very substance and nature of God, sharing His attributes by birth and inheritance; the word Scripture uses is "begotten" — said of no angel — and it places the Son above all creation as Creator, not within it (_John 1:14_; _John 1:18_; _Heb. 1:5_; _Psa. 2:7_; _Col. 1:15-16_; _John 8:42_). Defined in full at the close.
- _John 16:28._ "I came forth from the Father, and am come into the world: again, I leave the world, and go to the Father" — His mission framed as a procession from the Father's own presence into the world and a return to it — derivation, not creation.
- _CHR 21.2._ Waggoner: "The Scriptures declare that Christ is 'the only begotten son of God.' He is begotten, not created. As to when He was begotten, it is not for us to inquire... There was a time when Christ proceeded forth and came from God, from the bosom of the Father... but that time was so far back in the days of eternity that to finite comprehension it is practically without beginning" — the central pioneer statement: begotten, NOT created; brought forth from the Father in the days of eternity, practically without beginning. Anchors _Micah 5:2_ + _John 8:42_ + _John 1:18_.

**Life in Himself — divine life by derivation — John 5:26:**

- _John 5:26._ "as the Father hath life in himself; so hath he given to the Son to have life in himself" — the Father is the absolute fountain who "hath life in himself"; the Son also "hath life in himself," but this life is GIVEN, inhering by the Father's bestowal. Full divine life by derivation, not independent self-origination.
- _PREX1 15.2._ Litch (1842): "As the Son of God, the only begotten of the Father, he is also possessed of a self-existent living principle... 'For as the Father hath life in himself, so hath he given to the Son to have life in himself.'... derived from the Father" — an installed pioneer reading _John 5:26_ exactly as this section does: the Son's life is self-living yet DERIVED from the Father.
- _DA 530.3._ White: "In Christ is life, original, unborrowed, underived. 'He that hath the Son hath life.' 1 John 5:12. The divinity of Christ is the believer's assurance of eternal life" — corroborates the Son's FULL divinity (life original, unborrowed, underived), guarding against making the begotten Son a lesser or created being.
- _1SM 296.2._ White: "The Word, who was with God, and who was God, had this life... the life of Christ was unborrowed. No one can take this life from Him. 'I lay it down of myself' (John 10:18)... In Him was life, original, unborrowed, underived" — ties _John 1:1-4_ + _John 10:18_ to the Son's inherent life, the same "life in Himself" of _John 5:26_.

**Begotten, not created — the word said of no angel — Heb. 1:5; Psa. 2:7:**

- _Heb. 1:5._ "unto which of the angels said he at any time, Thou art my Son, this day have I begotten thee?" — Scripture's own word is "begotten," what was never said to any angel; Christ's sonship is unique — truly begotten of the Father, in a class above all created beings.
- _Psa. 2:7._ "the LORD hath said unto me, Thou art my Son; this day have I begotten thee" — the decree the apostles apply to Christ (_Acts 13:33_; _Heb. 1:5_); the begetting is the ground of His Sonship.
- _Heb. 1:8._ "unto the Son he saith, Thy throne, O God, is for ever and ever" — the Father directly addresses the Son as "O God" with an everlasting throne; the begotten Son is explicitly called God by the Father Himself.
- _CHR 22.1._ Waggoner: "He has by inheritance a more excellent name than the angels... since He is the only-begotten son of God, He is of the very substance and nature of God and possesses by birth all the attributes of God... the express image of His Person, the brightness of His glory, and filled with all the fullness of the Godhead. So He has 'life in Himself'" — defines "begotten not created": of the very substance and nature of God, all the attributes by birth; ties _Heb. 1:3_ + _John 5:26_ to the begotten Son.

**Firstborn means heirship, not first-created — Col. 1:15-18; Psa. 89:27; Heb. 1:2-3, 6:**

- _Col. 1:15-16._ "the firstborn of every creature: For by him were all things created... all things were created by him, and for him" — "firstborn of every creature" is immediately defined as Creator of all things; so "firstborn" means heirship and preeminence, NOT first-created.
  - **firstborn / firstbegotten** = a title of preeminence, heirship, and express-image standing — NOT first in creation. Scripture defines "firstborn" as "higher than the kings of the earth" (_Psa. 89:27_) and immediately calls the "firstborn of every creature" the Creator of all (_Col. 1:16_); the "firstbegotten" receives the worship of angels (_Heb. 1:6_). Defined in full at the close.
- _Col. 1:17-18._ "he is before all things, and by him all things consist... who is the beginning, the firstborn from the dead" — the same word "firstborn" frames Him as "firstborn from the dead," a rank of preeminence "that in all things he might have the preeminence."
- _Psa. 89:27._ "I will make him my firstborn, higher than the kings of the earth" — God Himself defines "firstborn" as a rank of supremacy, "higher than the kings of the earth," establishing the term as preeminence and heirship, not chronological first-creation.
- _Heb. 1:2-3._ "his Son, whom he hath appointed heir of all things, by whom also he made the worlds; Who being the brightness of his glory, and the express image of his person, and upholding all things by the word of his power" — the Son is "heir of all things," Maker of the worlds, "the brightness of his glory, and the express image of his person" — the radiance of the Father's own substance, not a separate lesser being.
- _Heb. 1:6._ "when he bringeth in the firstbegotten into the world, he saith, And let all the angels of God worship him" — the "firstbegotten" receives the worship of all the angels, a worship due to deity alone, proving the begotten Son is no creature but God to be adored.
- _CHR 21.1._ Waggoner: "Neither should we imagine that Christ is a creature, because Paul calls Him (Colossians 1:15) 'The First-born of every creature' for the very next verses show Him to be Creator and not a creature... He is above all creation and not a part of it" — directly defines "firstborn of every creature" as Creator, NOT creature.
- _PP 36.2._ White: "the King declared that none but Christ, the Only Begotten of God, could fully enter into His purposes... The Son of God had wrought the Father's will in the creation of all the hosts of heaven; and to Him, as well as to God, their homage and allegiance were due" — the "Only Begotten of God" as Creator of the heavenly hosts, due homage as God; confirms the worship of the firstbegotten in _Heb. 1:6_.

**Brought forth before all His works — Prov. 8:22-25:**

- _Prov. 8:22._ "The LORD possessed me in the beginning of his way, before his works of old" — Wisdom personified, applied by the pioneers to Christ, was possessed by the Lord before any work of His hands.
- _Prov. 8:23-25._ "I was set up from everlasting, from the beginning... When there were no depths, I was brought forth... before the hills was I brought forth" — "set up from everlasting" and twice "brought forth" before creation: the language of being begotten, brought forth out of the Father in the days of eternity, before any of His works.
- _PP 34.1._ White: "And the Son of God declares concerning Himself: 'The Lord possessed Me in the beginning of His way... I was set up from everlasting'" — White herself applies _Prov. 8_ to Christ, joining it to _John 1:1_, the only begotten, and _Micah 5:2_.

**DEFINITION — THE SON: BEGOTTEN, NOT CREATED =** Christ is the only-begotten divine Person, the Word who "in the beginning" already WAS — "with God" as a distinct Person and "was God" in full deity (_John 1:1_), self-existent "I AM" before Abraham (_John 8:58_), with "life in himself" (_John 5:26_), original, unborrowed, underived (_DA 530.3_; _1SM 296.2_). Yet He is a true Son: He "proceeded forth and came from God" (_John 8:42_; _John 16:28_), "brought forth" before all the works of God (_Prov. 8:22-25_), His "goings forth... from of old, from everlasting" — the days of eternity (_Micah 5:2_). Scripture's own word is "begotten," said of no angel (_Heb. 1:5_; _Psa. 2:7_), and "begotten" means brought forth OF the Father's very substance and nature, possessing by birth all the attributes of God (_CHR 22.1_) — hence begotten, NOT created (_CHR 21.2_). "Firstborn" is no clue to first-creation: it is heirship and preeminence, for the "firstborn of every creature" is the Creator of all (_Col. 1:15-16_), "higher than the kings of the earth" (_Psa. 89:27_), the "firstbegotten" worshiped by every angel (_Heb. 1:6_) and addressed by the Father as "O God" (_Heb. 1:8_). His begetting lies so far back in the days of eternity that to finite minds it is "practically without beginning" (_CHR 21.2_; _CHR 9.1_): truly derived from the Father as fountain, yet to us without beginning — fully God, the only begotten Son.

**Symbols defined here:**

- **the Son / the Word** = the literal, only-begotten divine Person — distinct from the Father yet "with God" and "was God," of the Father's own substance; existed "in the beginning," came forth from the Father in the days of eternity, made flesh as the only begotten of the Father (_John 1:1_; _John 1:14_; _Micah 5:2_; _John 8:58_; _Col. 1:17_).
- **only begotten / begotten not created** = truly brought forth OF the Father (not made, not created), of the very substance and nature of God, sharing His attributes by birth; the word Scripture uses is "begotten," said of no angel, placing the Son above creation as Creator (_John 1:14_; _John 1:18_; _Heb. 1:5_; _Psa. 2:7_; _Col. 1:15-16_; _John 8:42_).
- **firstborn / firstbegotten** = a title of preeminence, heirship, and express-image standing — NOT first in creation; defined by Scripture as "higher than the kings of the earth" (_Psa. 89:27_), and the "firstborn of every creature" as Creator of all (_Col. 1:16_), the "firstbegotten" worshiped by angels (_Heb. 1:6_) (_Col. 1:15-18_; _Heb. 1:2-3_).

**Symbols carried:** the **express image** and the **brightness of His glory** (_Heb. 1:3_) — the Son's deity and substance from the Father — belong to "The Father and the Son" as the owning thread of Part I.

**For discussion:**

1. Scripture calls Christ both self-existent "I AM" (_John 8:58_) and One who "proceeded forth and came from God" with life "given" Him (_John 8:42_; _John 5:26_). How do "begotten, not created" and "life in Himself, original, unborrowed, underived" (_DA 530.3_) hold together without making the Son either a creature or a second self-originated God?
2. Twice the text forbids reading "firstborn" as first-created — _Col. 1:16_ calls the firstborn the Creator of all, and _Psa. 89:27_ defines it as "higher than the kings of the earth." Where else do we let a single word (like "firstborn") carry an assumption Scripture itself never makes, and how does letting the Bible define its own figures guard us?
3. The Father addresses the Son as "O God" (_Heb. 1:8_) and commands every angel to worship Him (_Heb. 1:6_). If the begotten Son receives the worship due to deity alone, what does that settle for how we are to honor Christ today (cf. _John 5:23_)?
