## Why Prophecy Is History Written in Advance

> The pioneers did not invent the history of the trumpets — they pointed to it. The chain is named out loud: **Gibbon (secular history) -> Keith (the believing commentator) -> White and Smith (the pioneers).**

**Historicist frame — prophecy = pre-written history.**

- _Rev. 1:1._ Book = coming history, sent in advance: "things which must shortly come to pass." SSTR 1.
- _Rev. 8:6._ Seven angels prepare to sound = seven future events; only question = which history fulfils them. SSTR 2.
  > "instead of predicting the rise and triumph of either of those four great governments, it was his part to give the prophetic history of the fall of the last of the four..."
  > — James White, _The Sounding of the Seven Trumpets_, 1
- _Rev. 8:7-12._ First four trumpets — hail/fire, burning mountain, falling star, smitten sun — demand a real history to answer. Gibbon's record of Rome's fall = unknowing commentary on Rev. 8-9. DAR 455.
  > "...The chapters of the skeptical philosopher that treat directly of the matter, need but a text to be prefixed... to form a series of expository lectures on the eighth and ninth chapters of Revelation." "Little or nothing is left for the professed interpreter to do but to point to the pages of Gibbon."
  > — Uriah Smith, _Daniel and the Revelation_, 455, quoting Keith

> 📖 **TRUMPET** = a war-alarm, hence a war/plague (_Num. 10:9_) -> the seven successive wars that brought Rome to ruin (_Rev. 8:6_; SSTR 2).

**Named scholarship behind every reading — trail open for inspection.**

- _Keith, Signs of the Times_ — running source for first five trumpets; named by both White and Smith (DAR 455, 456).
- _Elliott, Horae Apocalypticae_ I.357-360 — cited by page for Western empire's extinction under 4th trumpet. DAR 465.

- _Gibbon, Decline and Fall_ — raw history under all the rest; _DAR_'s whole trumpet section built on White's earlier tract. DAR 455.

**Rule 13 — history must fulfil EVERY word.** One word left over = wrong. Test on "the third part," repeated six times (_Rev. 8:7-12; 9:15, 18_): at Constantine's death empire split three ways — sons Constantius, Constantine II, Constans — numbers fit, reading holds. SSTR 2; DAR 457.

## But Is This Letting the Bible Be Its Own Expositor? (Answering "Hath God Said?")

The objection: calling a star Attila = Origen's hidden sense? No.

- Text-marked figure = _illustration_ (honest).
- Reader-imported meaning = _interpretation_ (the counterfeit).
- Trumpet method stays on the honest side.

**1. Meaning comes FROM the Book, never from the interpreter (Rule 12).** Each figure traced to the verse that defines it; that meaning, no other.

- _Rev. 17:15._ Waters = "peoples, and multitudes, and nations, and tongues" — the angel's own dictionary.
- _Rev. 1:20._ "The seven stars are the angels of the seven churches" — star = messenger/ruler over a people.
- _Joel 2:25._ Locust = "my great army which I sent among you" — God names the figure.

> 📖 **SEA / WATERS** = peoples, multitudes, nations, and tongues (_Rev. 17:15_) -> the maritime peoples and nations struck through the trumpets.

> "The Bible is its own expositor. One passage will prove to be a key that will unlock other passages... the true meaning of the Scriptures will be made evident."
> — Ellen G. White, _Christian Education_, 85

- Origen _added_ unmarked meanings; trumpet method _receives_ the text's meaning and goes no further.

**2. The text MARKS these as figures, so reading them as figures is obedience, not violence (Rule 11).** Trumpet burning literal grass, a named star ("Wormwood") poisoning rivers, locusts as armored cavalry — flat reading = absurd; language forces the figure. Literal first; figure only where the text compels.

- Hostile editor on Miller: "Mr. Miller is a great stickler for literal interpretations; never admitting the figurative, unless absolutely required to make correct sense or meet the event which is intended to be pointed out." — _Miller's Works_, vol. 1, 16.
- Origen despised the literal for a hidden sense — made it doctrine: "the literal is the baser sense of Scripture" (the serpent's move).
- Here: literal held until the inspired text raises the figure (obedience).

**3. The dates came from "Bible its own expositor," not from history (Rule 5).** The day-year key yielding the 150 years and 391 years 15 days = Scripture interpreting Scripture.

- _Num. 14:34._ "Each day for a year" — God's own rod for prophetic time. GC 324.
- _Ezek. 4:6._ "I have appointed thee each day for a year" — same key, twice; chronology is the Bible's, not the historian's. GC 324.

> "Following his rule of making Scripture its own interpreter, Miller learned that a day in symbolic prophecy represents a year (Numbers 14:34; Ezekiel 4:6); he saw that the period of 2300 prophetic days, or literal years, would extend far beyond the close of the Jewish dispensation, hence it could not refer to the sanctuary of that dispensation."
> — Ellen G. White, _The Great Controversy_, 324

- Prophecy's own numbers through prophecy's own day-year key — no external code laid over the text.

**4. The reading is FALSIFIABLE and verified; the hidden sense answers to nothing (Rule 13).** Origen's secret key = uncheckable by definition. Historicist reading staked on datable public events — and tested:

- 391 years 15 days ran out August 11, 1840, to the day — exactly as Litch published two years before.
- Test both by the sermon's measure: "to the law and to the testimony" (Isa 8:20), and to the record of history the prophecy invites.
- Hidden sense flunks (submits to no test); trumpet reading passes (met the hardest test — a dated prediction — word for word).
- A method that stands or falls by history answering every word = antithesis of a secret key answering to nothing.

**5. Shared ground the sermon stood on.** Not a special rule for prophecy — the one rule the Reformers and pioneers recovered.

- Luther "submitted himself to the Bible... He saw that it must be its own interpreter... held it his duty to learn what is its direct and obvious teaching." — _The Great Controversy_, 173.
- "Scripture must be its own expositor, since it is a rule of itself." — _Miller's Works_, vol. 1, 20.

**In one line:** "Hath God said?" _loosens_ the word to import a hidden sense; historicism _holds_ the word — Bible defines its own figures (Rule 12), keeps its own literal default (Rule 11), dates its own prophecies by its own day-year key (Rule 5), submits the whole to the test of history (Rule 13).

**DEFINITION — HISTORICIST READING vs. SPIRITUALIZING =** historicist takes every symbol's meaning FROM the Bible (Rule 12), reads literally unless the text forces a figure (Rule 11), stands or falls by whether history answers every word (Rule 13) — testable, Bible-supplied. Spiritualizing IMPORTS a hidden sense the text never marks, answerable to nothing but the interpreter — the serpent's and Origen's method. Trumpets = the first; this study = the second's opposite.

## How to Read a Symbol — The Bible's Own Lexicon (Rules 11-12)

Two rules = complete dictionary of the trumpet-symbols, handed over before John lifts a horn.

**DEFINITION — A PROPHETIC SYMBOL =** a figure whose meaning the Bible itself supplies; read literally unless it does violence to nature (Rule 11), and interpreted only by tracing it to where Scripture explains it (Rule 12) — never guessed, never imported.

### Part 1 — The Rule

- **Rule 11 — literal unless it does violence to nature.** Literal trumpet + literal hail/fire burning literal trees/grass (_Rev. 8:7_) = violence to nature -> figurative. Literal wherever it stands; figure only where nature refuses it.
- **Rule 12 — trace the figure through the Bible.** Trace the word; where Scripture explains it, that's the meaning. Dictionary = the Bible; interpreter only looks it up.
- _DAR 463._ Smith — "sun, moon, and stars... for they are undoubtedly here used as symbols — evidently denote the great luminaries of the Roman government, — its emperors, senators, and consuls."
- _DAR 482._ Smith again — "fire... stands for red... jacinth, or hyacinth, for blue; and brimstone, for yellow" — colors, not substances (red/blue/yellow do no violence; flaming horses would).

## What Is a Trumpet?

> The trumpet is the Bible's own alarm of war and of coming judgment. Define the figure by Scripture (Rule 12), then watch the history fill every word.

**Scripture's settled use — trumpet = war-alarm:**

- _Num. 10:9._ Blow an alarm when ye go to war — God's own definition.
- _Amos 3:6._ Trumpet in the city, evil the LORD hath done — warns of judgment, the rod in His hand.
- _Joel 2:1._ Blow the trumpet in Zion — day of the LORD cometh.
- _Zeph. 1:14-16._ Great day of the LORD near — day of trumpet and alarm against fenced cities and high towers.
- _Hos. 8:1._ Set the trumpet to thy mouth — heathen power as God's rod against apostate Israel; pattern of the woes.

- _Rev. 8:2._ Seven trumpets given to seven angels.

- _Rev. 8:7._ First trumpet: hail and fire mingled with blood on earth; third part of trees and all green grass burnt — the Goths under Alaric.

First trumpet = the Goths under Alaric: "a deluge of barbarians" out of the North, the poet Claudian who "pathetically lamented the fate of his contemporary trees, which must blaze in the conflagration of the whole country [note the words of the prophecy, — 'The third part of trees was burned up']; and the emperor of the Romans fled before the king of the Goths" (DAR 456). ## The First Four Trumpets — Four Wars That Broke Rome

Each trumpet = one named invader, one real campaign. Proof "trumpet" = "war" (Rule 13).

- _Rev. 8:8-9._ Second trumpet — burning mountain into the sea, third of sea to blood, third of ships destroyed: Genseric + the Vandals, a naval war (DAR 458).
- **mountain** = a kingdom (_Dan. 2:35_; _Jer. 51:25_); **sea** = peoples/nations (_Rev. 17:15_) -> warring kingdom (Vandals) hurled on the maritime peoples.

  > "His conquests were, for the most part, naval; and his triumphs were 'as it were a great mountain burning with fire, cast into the sea.'... The next great invasion was that of 'the terrible Genseric,' at the head of the Vandals. His career occurred during the years A. D. 428-468."
  > — Uriah Smith, _Daniel and the Revelation_, 458

- _Rev. 8:10-11._ Third trumpet — great star fell, burning as a lamp, named Wormwood: Attila + the Huns, "the Scourge of God" (DAR 461).
- **star** = a ruler/leader (_Rev. 1:20_; _Dan. 8:10_) -> Attila, meteor-king of the Huns.

  > "'In the manner of his appearance, he strongly resembled a brilliant meteor flashing in the sky. He came from the East gathering his Huns, and poured them down... with the rapidity of a flashing meteor, suddenly on the empire.'"
  > — Uriah Smith, _Daniel and the Revelation_, 461

- _Rev. 8:11._ Star named Wormwood — bitterness, total ruin; Attila styled himself "The Scourge of God." DAR 462.
- _Rev. 8:12._ Fourth trumpet — third of sun, moon, stars smitten: extinction of the Western emperors under Odoacer, A.D. 476 (DAR 463).

## Seven Within a Seven — Jericho and the Seventh Trumpet

7th trumpet = 7th vial, one event: both "the seventh angel," both "It is done" from the opened temple, both close in "voices, thunders, lightnings, and a great earthquake" (_Rev. 11:15-19_ ↔ _16:17-18_). So the last trumpet _contains_ the seven last plagues — a seven within a seven, the Bible's pattern for a doomed city's fall, typified at Jericho.

**The Jericho type — seven priests, seven trumpets, the seventh day, seven circuits, the shout, the wall:**

- _Josh. 6:3-5._ Seven priests, seven trumpets; seventh day, seven circuits; long blast, all shout, "the wall of the city shall fall down flat" — six days single circuits, seventh day holds seven.
- _Josh. 6:15-16._ Seventh day, seven circuits; at the seventh, priests blow, Joshua: "Shout; for the LORD hath given you the city" — seventh circuit = moment of conquest.
- _Josh. 6:20._ People shout, priests blow, "the wall fell down flat" — trumpet, shout, wall fallen.

EGW draws the climax in seventh-trumpet terms:

> "As the **seventh circuit** was completed... The trumpets... now broke forth in a **blast that shook the very earth**. The walls of solid stone, with their massive **towers** and battlements, tottered and heaved from their foundations, and with a crash **fell in ruin** to the earth." — PP 491.

- _Isa. 30:25._ the day "when the towers fall."
- _Rev. 16:19-20._ "great Babylon came in remembrance before God"; "every island fled away."

**A pioneer draws the line — Miller reads the trumpet by Jericho** (his proof-text the same _Amos 3:6_ this study uses):

> "the **sounding of trumpets is always used to denote the downfall** of some empire, nation, or place... **At the fall of Jericho, the trumpet was the instrument**... which **cast down her walls, destroyed the city**... 'Shall a trumpet be blown in the city, and the people not be afraid?'... The seven trumpets mentioned in Revelation... indicate the final overthrow of the powers spoken of in the prophecy." — _Miller's Works_, vol. 2, 115.

James White completes it — west fell under first four, east under fifth/sixth:

> "but **under the seventh trumpet great Babylon entire will sink to rise no more at all**." — SSTR 2.

**Why the seventh contains seven — the covenant's sevenfold rod:**

- _Lev. 26:21._ "I will bring **seven times more plagues** upon you" (cf. _Lev. 26:28_, "I will chastise you seven times") — covenant rod = sevenfold plague on the unrepentant.
- _Rev. 15:1._ "seven last plagues; for in them is filled up the wrath of God" — the sevenfold rod to full measure.
- _2 Kgs. 5:14._ Naaman dips seven times, made whole — a lesser seven confirming completion lives in the seventh.

Twice over: trumpet that fells a city = sevenfold, climaxing seventh (Jericho); plague chastising the covenant-breaker = sevenfold (Lev 26). Revelation joins them — seventh trumpet = Jericho's seventh circuit = the seven last plagues poured out without mixture; the wall of Babylon falls flat.

**The loud cry = encircling trumpet; the shout = the fall.** Israel compassed Jericho six days untouched (long warning); at the seventh the shout fells the wall. So the loud cry (watchman's trumpet of Ezek 33, already defined) compasses the doomed city; proclamation finished, seventh trumpet sounds, shout goes up, Babylon falls.

- _Rev. 18:2._ "Babylon the great **is fallen, is fallen**" — the loud cry's announcement, the shout before the wall.
- _Rev. 18:4._ "**Come out of her**, my people... that ye receive not of her plagues" — God's people called out before the wall falls in the day of slaughter.

**DEFINITION — SEVEN WITHIN A SEVEN =** the Bible's own structure for a doomed city's downfall, typified at Jericho (Josh 6): seven priests, seven trumpets, and on the seventh day seven circuits, climaxing in the shout and the falling wall (PP 491). Miller fixes the prophetic trumpet by this type — "at the fall of Jericho, the trumpet was the instrument... which cast down her walls" (_Miller's Works_, vol. 2, 115). So Revelation's seven trumpets are Jericho's antitype: they sound the fall of great Babylon, and the SEVENTH trumpet — the seventh circuit — contains the SEVEN last plagues (the sevenfold covenant-rod of Lev 26:21), at whose pouring the shout goes up (Rev 18:2) and the wall falls flat (Rev 16:18-19). ## Appendix — Symbol Dictionary

Every prophetic figure in this study, defined by Scripture's own usage, keyed to its trumpet/event.

- 📖 **A FALLEN STAR** = a fallen, apostate power, cut down yet armed with a key (_Isa. 14:12_) -> the power loosed with the key of the pit under the 5th trumpet.
- 📖 **BEAST** = a kingdom or governing power (_Dan. 7:17, 23_) -> the master key for every beast/power named across the trumpets.
- 📖 **BLOOD** = slaughter (_Ezek. 14:19_) -> the bloodshed of the 1st and 2nd trumpets.
- 📖 **BRIMSTONE** = the fire of divine judgment (_Gen. 19:24_) -> the 6th-trumpet gunpowder fire, and the third angel's penalty.
- 📖 **DAY = YEAR** = one prophetic day stands for one literal year (_Num. 14:34; Ezek. 4:6_) -> the 150 years and 391 years 15 days dated by Litch to August 11, 1840 (DAR 479).
- 📖 **FIRE** = devouring war and judgment (_Joel 2:3_) -> the war that consumes under the 1st and 2nd trumpets.
- 📖 **GRASS / FLESH** = the common people, the mass of the population (_Isa. 40:6-7_) -> the populace burnt up under the 1st trumpet.
- 📖 **HAIL** = sudden, sweeping destruction falling from the north (_Isa. 28:17_) -> the Goths who came down on Rome under the 1st trumpet.
- 📖 **HORSES** = strength for the day of battle, the mounted war-power (_Prov. 21:31_) -> the Arab and Turkish cavalry of the 5th and 6th trumpets.
- 📖 **JUDGMENT OF THE LIVING** = the closing phase of Christ's work in the heavenly sanctuary, the judgment now begun (_Dan. 7:9-10_) -> the investigative judgment now in session, determining who is sealed before the four winds loose as the day of slaughter (1 Pet. 4:17).
- 📖 **LION** = a roaring conqueror / God's deploying rod (_Amos 3:8; Jer. 50:17_) -> the lion-toothed locust and lion-headed cavalry of the 5th and 6th trumpets (Rev. 9:8, 17).
- 📖 **LOCUSTS** = an invading army God commissions (_Joel 2:25_) -> the Saracen/Arab hosts of the 5th trumpet (Rev. 9:3).
- 📖 **MOUNTAIN** = a kingdom or seat of power (_Jer. 51:25; Dan. 2:35_) -> the burning mountain cast into the sea, a kingdom overthrown under the 2nd trumpet (Rev. 8:8).
- 📖 **RAIN / LATTER RAIN** = doctrine, truth taught and received — the latter rain being its fulness (_Deut. 32:2; Isa. 55:10-11_) -> the Spirit unveiling completed truth into prepared hearts at the close, the inward side of the loud cry, fitting God's people to stand in the day of slaughter.
- 📖 **RICHES / MERCHANDISE** = trust in wealth that cannot deliver in the day of wrath (_Prov. 11:4; Zeph. 1:18; Ezek. 7:19_) -> the merchant-Babylon whose riches "come to nought" in one hour, watched burning afar (_Rev. 18:11, 17_); the day-of-slaughter herald.
- 📖 **RIVERS / THE EUPHRATES** = the supporting population of the power that dwells on it; dry up the river and the power is undone (_Rev. 16:12_) -> the Ottoman sultanies loosed under the 6th trumpet (Rev. 9:14).
- 📖 **SCORPIONS** = power to sting and to chastise (_1 Kgs. 12:11_) -> the stinging tails of the 5th trumpet (Rev. 9:10).
- 📖 **SEA / WATERS** = peoples, multitudes, nations, and tongues (_Rev. 17:15_) -> the maritime peoples and nations struck through the trumpets.
- 📖 **SMOKE** = the rising token of torment and destruction (_Rev. 14:11_) -> the smoke from the pit under the 5th trumpet, and the third angel's penalty.
- 📖 **STAR** = a ruler/leader/messenger set over a people (_Rev. 1:20_) -> the meteor-king Attila and the fallen star Wormwood of the trumpets (Rev. 8:10-11).
- 📖 **SUN / MOON / STARS** = the great luminaries of a government — its emperors, senators, and consuls (_Gen. 37:9-10_) -> the Roman government smitten under the 4th trumpet.
- 📖 **SWORD** = war / the slaying stroke of judgment (_Jer. 46:10_) -> the devouring rod of the day of the LORD, set on the Euphrates (Isa. 66:16).
- 📖 **THE BOTTOMLESS PIT** = the deep, a dark power rising into view (_Rev. 9:1-2_) -> the desert wastes whence the Saracens issued under the 5th trumpet.
- 📖 **THE NORTH** = the quarter the invading rod comes from, tied to the Euphrates (_Jer. 1:14; 6:1; 25:9; 46:10_) -> the slaughter-weapons of Ezekiel 9 (from "toward the north," _Ezek. 9:2_) and the eastern/Euphratean rod of the third woe.
- 📖 **THE THIRD PART** = one of the three divisions of the Roman empire under the scourge (_Rev. 8:7-12; 9:15, 18_) -> Rome's tripartite split among Constantine's three sons, A.D. 337 (SSTR 2; DAR 457).
- 📖 **TOWER** = the lofty and proud brought low in the day of the LORD (_Isa. 2:15; 30:25; Ezek. 26:4_) -> the high towers/fenced walls felled in the day of slaughter (_Isa. 30:25_), antitype of Jericho's towers (_PP 491_).
- 📖 **TREES / CEDARS** = great men and rulers, the lofty and lifted-up (_Isa. 2:13_) -> the rulers burnt up under the 1st trumpet.
- 📖 **TRUMPET** = a war-alarm, hence a war/plague (_Num. 10:9_) -> the seven successive wars that brought Rome to ruin (Rev. 8:6).
- 📖 **WHIRLWIND** = the storm-rush of God's judgment-army (_Jer. 25:32_) -> the four winds loosed for the day of slaughter (Rev. 7:1).
- 📖 **WIND / FOUR WINDS** = strife, war, commotion among nations (_Dan. 7:2_) -> the political winds whose holding and loosing govern the trumpet-wars (Rev. 7:1).
- 📖 **WINEPRESS** = the treading-out of the wrath of God (_Jer. 25:30_) -> the unmixed wine of the third angel and the seventh trumpet's vials (Rev. 14:10, 19).
