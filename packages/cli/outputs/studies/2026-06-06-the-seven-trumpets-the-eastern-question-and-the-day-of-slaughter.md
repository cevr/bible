# Bible Study: The Seven Trumpets, the Eastern Question, and the Day of Slaughter

**A Miller's-Rules, word-by-word study that builds one definition at a time — trumpet, woe, seal, vial, the two questions, the four winds, the loud cry — until the chain shows that the third woe, the eastern question, and the day of slaughter are one thing, and that the loud cry is the warning to escape it before the sealing closes.**

_Method: take the Bible as it reads; let Scripture be its own expositor (Miller's Rule 5); gather every passage on the subject before drawing the thesis (Rule 4); trace each figure through the Bible until it is explained (Rule 12); and test the history against the prophecy word for word (Rule 13). The pioneers — Litch, Smith, Andrews — are quoted in full, not merely cited._

## Why Prophecy Is History Written in Advance

Before the first trumpet sounds, the class is right to press the question: how does Smith conclude a _war_ in Western Rome, that a _star_ = a people, that _locusts_ = the Arabs — what gives any of it a historical basis? The answer is not a private synthesis; it is the pioneers' own disclosed method and their own citation trail. They did not invent the history — they pointed to it. The frame is plainest in **James White**, whose tract on the trumpets (the _Sounding of the Seven Trumpets_, the foundational pioneer exposition that Uriah Smith later expanded into _Daniel and the Revelation_) names the task of Revelation as the writing of history in advance.

**1. The historicist frame, stated by a pioneer.** White opens by setting John's commission against Daniel's: not prediction of empires rising, but **the prophetic history of an empire falling**.

- _Rev. 1:1._ "The Revelation of Jesus Christ... to shew unto his servants things which must shortly come to pass." — the book is given as _coming history_, sent in advance.

  > "Accordingly, instead of predicting the rise and triumph of either of those four great governments, it was his part to give the prophetic history of the fall of the last of the four, and give us the various means by which that great persecuting system should come to ruin."
  > — James White, _The Sounding of the Seven Trumpets_, 1

- _Rev. 8:6._ "And the seven angels which had the seven trumpets prepared themselves to sound." — seven future events; the question is only _which_ history fulfils them, and where the interpreter got it.
  > "In giving an outline of this subject, I shall, for the most part, follow Keith, in his “Signs of the Times,” on the first four trumpets. I should be glad to give his remarks and historical quotations entire, would my limits, which are prescribed for this work, admit it."
  > — James White, _The Sounding of the Seven Trumpets_, 2

This is the strongest grounding statement in the whole study, because it is a _pioneer_ disclosing his sources, not a borrowed British scholar speaking for himself. White does not claim private revelation for the identification of Goths, Vandals, Huns; he says plainly: I follow Keith, who follows Gibbon. The chain is **Gibbon (secular history) → Keith (the believing commentator) → White and Smith (the pioneers)** — and it is named out loud.

**2. The corroborating principle: fulfilled prophecy is pre-written history.** Smith takes up the same method and states the historicist axiom — that the secular historian, writing with no interest in Scripture, unknowingly wrote the commentary on Revelation 8 and 9.

- _Rev. 8:7-12._ The first four trumpets — hail and fire, a burning mountain, a falling star, a smitten sun. — symbols that demand a real history to answer them.
  > "“None could elucidate the texts more clearly, or expound them more fully, than the task has been performed by Gibbon. The chapters of the skeptical philosopher that treat directly of the matter, need but a text to be prefixed, and a few unholy words to be blotted out, to form a series of expository lectures on the eighth and ninth chapters of Revelation.” “Little or nothing is left for the professed interpreter to do but to point to the pages of Gibbon.”"
  > — Uriah Smith, _Daniel and the Revelation_, 455, quoting Keith

So the "historical basis" is exactly this: the prophecy was written first; the history came after; and when the unbelieving Gibbon recorded the fall of Rome, he was — without knowing it — writing expository lectures on Revelation. The interpreter's job is to _point_, not invent.

**3. The named scholarship behind every conclusion.** That the readings are not guessed is shown by the pioneers naming, in print, the histories and commentaries they leaned on. The trail is open for inspection:

- _Keith, Signs of the Times_ — the running source for the first five trumpets, named by both White and Smith.
  > "I know not how the history of the sounding of the first trumpet can be more impressively set forth than by presenting the graphic rehearsal of the facts which are stated in Gibbon’s 456 History, by Mr. Keith, in his Signs of the Times, Vol. I, pp. 221-233: —"
  > — Uriah Smith, _Daniel and the Revelation_, 455
- _Elliott, Horae Apocalypticae_ — cited by page for the extinction of the Western empire under the fourth trumpet.
  > "Elliott (Horae Apocalypticae, Vol. I, pp. 357-360) speaks of the fulfillment of this portion of the prophecy in the extinction of the Western empire, as follows: —"
  > — Uriah Smith, _Daniel and the Revelation_, 465
- _Litch, Prophetic Expositions / Christ's Second Coming_ — the named author of the dated calculation that carried the prophecy through the woes to the seventh trumpet.
  > "In the foregoing, Josiah Litch has brought us down through the prophecy of the trumpets, and the woes, to the last. We now wish to briefly notice some of the events to occur under the sounding of the seventh angel."
  > — James White, _The Sounding of the Seven Trumpets_, 67
  > "The calculation which follows, founded on this starting-point, was made and published in a work entitled, Christ’s Second Coming, etc., by J. Litch, in 1838."
  > — Uriah Smith, _Daniel and the Revelation_, 479
- _Gibbon, Decline and Fall_ — the raw history itself, the source under all the rest. Smith even discloses that _Daniel and the Revelation_'s whole trumpet section is built on White's earlier tract:
  > "A full exposition of the seven trumpets is given in a work entitled, An Exposition of the Seven Trumpets of Revelation VIII and IX, published at the Review and Herald Office, Battle Creek, Mich., to which the reader is referred for a more extended examination of the subject. To that work we are chiefly indebted for the extracts given below."
  > — Uriah Smith, _Daniel and the Revelation_, 455

**4. The test that validates a reading: Rule 13 — history must fulfil EVERY word.** A historicist reading stands only if the history answers each detail of the prophecy; one word left over, and the interpretation is wrong. Watch it work on the small phrase repeated through every trumpet — "the **third part**." Why a _third_? Because at Constantine's death the empire was literally split three ways, and the prophecy's numbers track that real division:

- _Rev. 8:7-12; 9:15, 18._ "the third part" — repeated six times, an odd detail demanding a real cause.
  > "The empire, after Constantine, was divided into three parts; and hence the frequent remark, “a third part of men,” etc., in allusion to the third part of the empire which was under the scourge. Under the first four trumpets the two western divisions fell, and under the fifth and sixth the eastern empire was crushed; but under the seventh trumpet great Babylon entire will sink to rise no more at all."
  > — James White, _The Sounding of the Seven Trumpets_, 2
  > "The Roman empire, after Constantine, was divided into three parts; and hence the frequent remark, “a third part of men,” etc., in allusion to the third part of the empire which was under the scourge. This division of the Roman kingdom was made at the death of Constantine, between his three sons, Constantius, Constantine II, and Constans... Of this well-known historical fact, Elliott, as quoted by Albert Barnes... says: “Twice, at least, before the Roman empire became divided permanently into the two parts... there was a tripartite division of the empire... the other, A. D. 337, on the death of Constantine, when it was divided between his three sons.”"
  > — Uriah Smith, _Daniel and the Revelation_, 457

That is Rule 13 in miniature: the prophecy said _a third_, the history produced _exactly three_ parts, the numbers fit — so the reading holds. Where the history would _not_ fit every word, the reading would fall.

**5. The method's built-in humility — fulfilled vs. unfulfilled.** The same discipline that speaks with certainty about _past_ fulfilment refuses to speak with that certainty about what is _not yet_ fulfilled. White states the rule himself, and it is the reason this study later labels its eastern-rod / present-day application as _present-truth synthesis_ rather than asserted fact:

- _Rev. 11:14._ "The second woe is past; and, behold, the third woe cometh quickly." — two woes are history (positive); the third is future (modesty).
  > "While we may speak of fulfilled prophecy with positiveness, we would apply unfulfilled prophecy with becoming modesty."
  > — James White, _The Sounding of the Seven Trumpets_, 68

So the certainty in this study belongs to the dated, fulfilled chain — the first six trumpets, the 150 years, the 391 years and 15 days to August 11, 1840. The _modesty_ belongs to the third woe's present-day marker. That distinction is not a hedge added by us; it is the pioneers' own rule.

**6. What keeps symbols from being arbitrary.** A star, a locust, a mountain — these are not guessed at. Two of Miller's rules govern them, and they hand directly to the Symbol Lexicon that follows:

- **Rule 11** — _literal unless it does violence to nature._ An angel's trumpet that burns literal trees does violence to nature, so the language is figurative; but the _event_ it points to is literal, datable history.
- **Rule 12** — _trace the figure: a symbol means what the Bible elsewhere says it means._ A "trumpet" is the war-alarm (Num 10:9); "winds" are strife (Dan 7:2); "stars" and "waters" are defined by the text itself. Symbols are Bible-defined, not invented — which is exactly why the next section is a Symbol Lexicon drawn from Scripture's own definitions, not from imagination.

So the answer to the class is this: the pioneers concluded a war in Western Rome because (a) the Bible defines "trumpet" as war (Rule 12), (b) the symbol is figurative because a literal angel-trumpet burning trees does violence to nature (Rule 11), and (c) the recorded history of Alaric, Genseric, Attila, and Odoacer — written by Gibbon, marshalled by Keith and Elliott, dated by Litch — answers every word of the prophecy (Rule 13). Prophecy is history written in advance; the interpreter only points to the page where it came true.

**DEFINITION — HISTORICISM = the pioneers' disclosed method (James White, _SSTR_ 1, 2, 68; Smith/Keith, _DAR_ 455, 457): prophecy is fulfilled in literal, datable history — "the prophetic history of the fall" of Rome written before it happened, so that the secular historian (Gibbon) unknowingly composed "a series of expository lectures on the eighth and ninth chapters of Revelation" (Keith, _DAR_ 455). Its symbols are read by the Bible's own definitions, not guessed (Rule 12), and are taken figuratively only where a literal sense does violence to nature (Rule 11). Its discipline is to speak of _fulfilled_ prophecy "with positiveness" and apply _unfulfilled_ prophecy "with becoming modesty" (White, _SSTR_ 68). And its test is Rule 13: an interpretation stands only when the history answers EVERY word — proven, for example, when the prophecy's "third part" met Rome's tripartite division under Constantine's three sons (White, _SSTR_ 2; _DAR_ 457).**

## But Is This Letting the Bible Be Its Own Expositor? (Answering "Hath God Said?")

A careful listener has every right to stop the study here and press a hard question. We have just said a star is Attila, a trumpet is the Gothic war, the locusts are the Saracens. _Isn't that exactly the thing we condemned in the serpent and in Origen — reaching past the plain word for a "higher," hidden meaning?_ The objection is fair and it must be answered, not dodged: if assigning these historical fulfillments is importing a secret sense the text never gave, then historicism is only spiritualizing with dates on it. The answer is the opposite — and it is the _same_ method the "Hath God Said?" sermon laid down, now applied to the trumpets. The line that sermon drew settles it: a figure the text itself marks is _illustration_ (honest); a meaning the reader imports is _interpretation_ (the counterfeit). The trumpet method stays entirely on the honest side of that line.

**First — the meaning comes FROM the Book, never from the interpreter (Rule 12).** This is the whole difference. We never decide what a symbol "really" means and then read it in. We trace each figure to the place where Scripture itself defines it, and put _that_ meaning — and no other — on the figure. The Symbol Lexicon section did exactly this:

- _Rev. 17:15._ "And he saith unto me, The waters which thou sawest, where the whore sitteth, are **peoples, and multitudes, and nations, and tongues**." — the Bible's own dictionary: sea/waters = peoples. We did not supply that; the angel did.
- _Rev. 1:20._ "The seven stars are the **angels** of the seven churches." — Scripture's own definition: a star stands for the messenger/ruler set over a people. The meaning is in the verse, not in our heads.
- _Joel 2:25._ "the locust... my **great army** which I sent among you." — God Himself calls the locust His army. So when locusts swarm in Revelation 9, the figure already carries the meaning God assigned it.

[→] This is the precise opposite of the "hidden sense." Origen's allegory _added_ meanings the text never marked; the trumpet method _receives_ the meaning the text supplies and refuses to go past it. The same rule the sermon recovered governs here:

> "The Bible is its own expositor. One passage will prove to be a key that will unlock other passages, and in this way light will be shed upon the hidden meaning of the word. By comparing different texts treating on the same subject, viewing their bearing on every side, the true meaning of the Scriptures will be made evident."
> — Ellen G. White, _Christian Education_, 85

**Second — the text itself MARKS these as figures, so reading them as figures is obedience, not violence (Rule 11).** We are not free to call anything we please a symbol; the text tells us when it is speaking figuratively, by doing violence to nature if read flat. An angel's trumpet that burns up literal grass and trees, a star with a name ("Wormwood") that poisons rivers, locusts shaped like armored cavalry with crowns and men's faces — these cannot be read literally without absurdity. The language forces the figure. So here is the exact reversal of Origen: he took the _literal_ and despised it for a hidden sense the text never marked; we read literally first and admit the figure _only_ where the text itself compels it.

- This is Miller's settled discipline — literal-first, figurative only under compulsion, reported even by a hostile editor:
  > "Mr. Miller is a great stickler for literal interpretations; never admitting the figurative, unless absolutely required to make correct sense or meet the event which is intended to be pointed out. He doubtless believes, most unwaveringly, all he teaches to others. His lectures are interspersed with powerful admonitions to the wicked, and he handles Universalism with gloves of steel."
  > — _Miller's Works_, vol. 1, 16

[→] Mark the contrast exactly. "Hath God said?" _loosens_ the plain word to admit a secret meaning the words do not carry — and Origen made a doctrine of it: "the literal is the baser sense of Scripture." The trumpet method does the reverse: it _holds_ the literal as the default and bows to the figure only where the inspired text would otherwise be made absurd. Despising the literal for a hidden sense is the serpent's move; honoring the literal until the text itself raises the figure is obedience to the text.

**Third — the dates themselves came from "Bible its own expositor," not from history (Rule 5).** The objection's sharpest form is: "Fine, the symbols are defined — but the _chronology_ (150 years, 391 years 15 days) is surely an outside key you bring to the prophecy." It is not. The day-year principle that yields those numbers is itself the Bible interpreting the Bible. The same rule that opened Daniel 8:14 opens the trumpets:

> "Following his rule of making Scripture its own interpreter, Miller learned that a day in symbolic prophecy represents a year (Numbers 14:34; Ezekiel 4:6); he saw that the period of 2300 prophetic days, or literal years, would extend far beyond the close of the Jewish dispensation, hence it could not refer to the sanctuary of that dispensation."
> — Ellen G. White, _The Great Controversy_, 324

- _Num. 14:34._ "After the number of the days in which ye searched the land, even forty days, **each day for a year**." — God's own rod for measuring prophetic time.
- _Ezek. 4:6._ "I have appointed thee **each day for a year**." — the same key, stated twice, so the chronology is the Bible's, not the historian's.

[→] So the five months (150 years) and the hour-day-month-year (391 years 15 days) are _not_ history imposed on the prophecy. They are the prophecy's own numbers run through the prophecy's own day-year key. The chronology is the Bible interpreting the Bible — the very thing the sermon called for — not an external code laid over the text.

**Fourth — the reading is FALSIFIABLE and verified; the hidden sense answers to nothing (Rule 13).** Here is the deepest difference of all. Origen's hidden sense can never be _tested_ — it answers to nothing outside the interpreter's own mind, so it can never be proved wrong and never be proved right. A secret key is, by definition, uncheckable. The historicist reading is the exact opposite: it stakes itself on datable, public events, so it _can_ be checked against reality — and it was. The 391 years and 15 days ran out on August 11, 1840, to the day, exactly as Litch had published two years before it happened. Every word of the prophecy was met by an event you could put on a calendar. A method that stands or falls by whether history answers every word is the antithesis of a secret key that answers to nothing.

[→] Test the two methods by the sermon's own measure. "To the law and to the testimony" — and add: to the record of history, which the prophecy itself invites. The hidden sense flunks because it submits to no test; the trumpet reading passes because it submitted to the hardest test (a dated prediction) and met it word for word.

**Fifth — this is the shared ground the sermon stood on.** We are not inventing a special rule for prophecy. We are applying the one rule the Reformers and pioneers recovered:

> "He submitted himself to the Bible as the word of God, the only sufficient, infallible rule. He saw that it must be its own interpreter. He dared not attempt to explain Scripture to sustain a preconceived theory or doctrine, but held it his duty to learn what is its direct and obvious teaching."
> — Ellen G. White, _The Great Controversy_, 173

> "Scripture must be its own expositor, since it is a rule of itself. If I depend on a teacher to expound it to me, and he should guess at its meaning, or desire to have it so on account of his sectarian creed, or to be"
> — _Miller's Works_, vol. 1, 20

[→] **The contrast in one line.** "Hath God said?" _loosens_ the word to import a hidden sense; historicism _holds_ the word and lets the Bible define its own figures (Rule 12), keep its own literal default (Rule 11), and date its own prophecies by its own day-year key (Rule 5) — then submits the whole to the test of history (Rule 13). Same method as the sermon — now applied to the trumpets.

**DEFINITION — HISTORICIST READING vs. SPIRITUALIZING = a historicist reading takes every symbol's meaning FROM the Bible (Rule 12), reads literally unless the text forces a figure (Rule 11), and stands or falls by whether history answers every word (Rule 13) — it is testable and Bible-supplied. Spiritualizing IMPORTS a hidden sense the text never marks, answerable to nothing but the interpreter — the serpent's and Origen's method. The trumpets are the first; this study is the second's opposite.**

## How to Read a Symbol — The Bible's Own Lexicon (Rules 11-12)

Before a single trumpet is interpreted, the question must be settled: _when_ is John's language literal and _when_ is it figurative — and once it is figurative, _how_ is the figure's meaning fixed? The pioneers did not guess. They followed two of Miller's rules, and those two rules hand the reader a complete dictionary of the trumpet-symbols before John ever lifts a horn to his lips. This section states the rule, shows Smith using it, and then lays out the lexicon — every symbol the trumpets use, defined by the Bible itself.

### Part 1 — The Rule: When Is It Figurative, and How Is the Figure Read?

**Rule 11 — literal unless it does violence to nature.** If a passage makes good sense as it stands, and does no violence to the simple laws of nature, then it is to be understood literally; if not, figuratively. An angel sounding a literal trumpet so that literal hail and fire burn up literal trees and literal grass (Rev 8:7) does violence to the simple laws of nature — fire and hail do not fall _because_ a trumpet blows. So the language is figurative. The rule does not let the reader spiritualize at will; it forces the literal reading wherever the literal reading stands, and only releases the figure where nature itself refuses the literal.

**Rule 12 — trace the figure through the Bible.** To learn the meaning of a figure, trace the word through the Bible; and where you find it explained, put it on the figure; and if it makes good sense, you need look no further. The meaning is not imported from history, from the newspaper, or from the interpreter's ingenuity — it is read off the page where Scripture itself supplies it. The dictionary is the Bible. The interpreter only looks the word up.

**Smith applies the rule, he does not merely state it.** Watch the method at work in _Daniel and the Revelation_. On the fourth trumpet's "sun, moon, and stars," Smith pauses to flag that these are symbols (Rule 11 — the literal sun cannot be smitten "a third part") and then fixes their meaning by Scripture's own usage (Rule 12):

> "We understand that this trumpet symbolizes the career of Odoacer, the barbarian monarch who was so intimately connected with the downfall of Western Rome. The symbols, sun, moon, and stars — for they are undoubtedly here used as symbols — evidently denote the great luminaries of the Roman government, — its emperors, senators, and consuls."
> — Uriah Smith, _Daniel and the Revelation_, 463

And on the sixth-trumpet horsemen, Smith reads "fire, jacinth, brimstone" not as substances but as _colors_ — exactly the disciplined, sense-making reading Rule 11 demands (red, blue, yellow do no violence to nature; flaming horses would):

> "Fire, representing a color, stands for red, “as red as fire” being a frequent form of expression; jacinth, or hyacinth, for blue; and brimstone, for yellow. And these colors greatly predominated in the dress of these warriors; so that the description, according to this view, would be accurately met in the Turkish uniform, which was composed largely of red, or scarlet, blue, and yellow."
> — Uriah Smith, _Daniel and the Revelation_, 482

[→] This is the whole secret. The trumpets are not decoded by cleverness from outside; they are decoded by looking each figure up in the Bible's own glossary. Here is that glossary — every symbol the trumpets use, with the verse where God Himself defines it.

### Part 2 — The Lexicon: The Bible Hands You Every Key

#### Peoples and Polities — Who the Symbols Stand For

- _Rev. 17:15._ "And he saith unto me, The waters which thou sawest, where the whore sitteth, are **peoples, and multitudes, and nations, and tongues**." — God's own dictionary entry: **WATERS / SEA = peoples and nations**. Unlocks the **2nd trumpet** ("the third part of the sea became blood").
- _Rev. 16:12._ "And the sixth angel poured out his vial upon the great river **Euphrates**; and the water thereof was dried up, that the way of the kings of the east might be prepared." — **RIVERS / THE EUPHRATES = the supporting population** of the power that dwells on it; dry up the river and the power is undone. Smith fixes the four-angel Euphrates as the Ottoman sultanies, not the literal stream:
  > "The Four Angels. — These were the four principal sultanies of which the Ottoman empire was composed, located in the country watered by the great river Euphrates. These sultanies were situated at Aleppo, Iconium, Damascus, and Bagdad. Previously they had been restrained; but God commanded, and they were loosed."
  > — Uriah Smith, _Daniel and the Revelation_, 480
  > Unlocks the **6th trumpet** ("the four angels which are bound in the great river Euphrates," Rev 9:14).
- _Isa. 40:6-7._ "All flesh is **grass**, and all the goodliness thereof is as the flower of the field... surely **the people is grass**." (cf. _1 Pet. 1:24_, "all flesh is as grass"). — **GRASS / FLESH = the common people**, the mass of the population. Unlocks the **1st trumpet** ("all green grass was burnt up").
- _Isa. 2:13._ "And upon all the **cedars** of Lebanon, that are high and lifted up, and upon all the oaks of Bashan." — and Scripture interprets its own tree-figure plainly: _Dan. 4:20-22_, "The **tree** that thou sawest... It is thou, O king... for thy greatness is grown, and reacheth unto heaven, and thy dominion to the end of the earth"; _Ezek. 31:3_, "the Assyrian was a **cedar** in Lebanon." — **TREES / CEDARS = great men and rulers**, the lofty and lifted-up. Unlocks the **1st trumpet** ("the third part of trees was burnt up").
- _Dan. 2:35, 44._ "the stone that smote the image became a great **mountain**, and filled the whole earth... shall the God of heaven set up a **kingdom**." (cf. _Jer. 51:25_, "O destroying **mountain**... which destroyest all the earth"). — **MOUNTAIN = a kingdom**. Unlocks the **2nd trumpet** ("a great mountain burning with fire was cast into the sea").
- _Dan. 7:17, 23._ "These great **beasts**, which are four, are four kings... The fourth beast shall be the fourth **kingdom** upon earth." — **BEAST = a kingdom or governing power**. This is the master key for every beast in the Revelation.

#### Rulers and Governing Powers — Who Leads Them

- _Rev. 1:20._ "The seven **stars** are the angels of the seven churches." (cf. _Dan. 8:10_, "it cast down some of the host and of the **stars** to the ground"). — **STARS = rulers, leaders, those set in authority**. Unlocks the **3rd trumpet** ("there fell a great star from heaven").
- _Gen. 37:9-10._ "the **sun** and the **moon** and the eleven **stars** made obeisance to me... Shall I and thy mother and thy brethren indeed come to bow down ourselves to thee to the earth?" — Joseph's own family reads the figure: **SUN / MOON / STARS = the great luminaries of a government — its emperors, senators, and consuls** — and Smith applies it to Rome:
  > "We understand that this trumpet symbolizes the career of Odoacer, the barbarian monarch who was so intimately connected with the downfall of Western Rome. The symbols, sun, moon, and stars — for they are undoubtedly here used as symbols — evidently denote the great luminaries of the Roman government, — its emperors, senators, and consuls."
  > — Uriah Smith, _Daniel and the Revelation_, 463
  > Unlocks the **4th trumpet** ("the third part of the sun was smitten").
- _Isa. 14:12._ "How art thou **fallen** from heaven, O Lucifer, son of the morning! how art thou cut down to the ground." (cf. _Rev. 9:1_, "I saw a **star fall** from heaven unto the earth: and to him was given the key of the bottomless pit"). — **A FALLEN STAR = a fallen, apostate power**, cut down yet armed with a key. Unlocks the **5th trumpet** (the star given the key of the pit).

#### War and Judgment — The Trumpet-Stroke Vocabulary

- _Num. 10:9._ "And if ye go to **war** in your land against the enemy that oppresseth you, then ye shall blow an **alarm** with the trumpets." (cf. _Amos 3:6_, "Shall a trumpet be blown in the city, and the people not be afraid?"; _Joel 2:1_, "Blow ye the trumpet in Zion... for the day of the LORD cometh"). — **TRUMPET = the alarm of war and coming judgment.** This is the master figure that names the whole series.
- _Jer. 49:36._ "And upon Elam will I bring the four **winds** from the four quarters of heaven, and will **scatter** them toward all those winds." (cf. _Dan. 7:2_, "the four **winds** of the heaven **strove** upon the great sea"). — **WIND = strife and war let loose** among the nations. ## What Is a Trumpet?

The seven trumpets are seven literal wars God sent against Rome — pagan and imperial under the first four trumpets, then apostate Eastern Christendom under the woes. But first, the figure must be defined by the Bible itself (Rule 12: trace the word through Scripture and where you find it explained, put that meaning on the figure) — and Scripture's own settled use of the trumpet is **the alarm of war and of coming judgment**.

- _Num. 10:9._ "And if ye go to **war** in your land against the enemy that oppresseth you, then ye shall blow an **alarm** with the trumpets... and ye shall be saved from your enemies." — God's own definition: the trumpet is the war-alarm.
- _Amos 3:6._ "Shall a **trumpet** be blown in the city, and the people not be afraid? shall there be **evil** in a city, and the LORD hath not done it?" — the trumpet warns of judgment, and the judgment is the LORD's own doing (the rod in His hand).
- _Joel 2:1._ "**Blow ye the trumpet** in Zion, and sound an **alarm** in my holy mountain... for the **day of the LORD** cometh." — the trumpet announces the day of the LORD.
- _Zeph. 1:14-16._ "The great **day of the LORD** is near... a day of **the trumpet and alarm** against the fenced cities, and against the **high towers**." — trumpet, day of wrath, and falling towers in one breath.
- _Hos. 8:1._ "Set the **trumpet** to thy mouth. He shall come as an eagle **against the house of the LORD**, because they have transgressed my covenant." — the trumpet announces a heathen power coming as God's rod against apostate Israel — the very pattern of the woes.

[→] So when John hears seven trumpets sound, the Bible has already taught him what a trumpet means: an alarm of war, of judgment the LORD Himself sends. Now watch the history fill every word.

## With Mixture vs Without Mixture — The Vials Are the Trumpets Without Mercy

The first six trumpets struck "a third part" — partial, mixed, mercy still restraining. The seven vials strike the whole — unmixed wrath, mercy withdrawn. And vial by vial they fall on the very same targets as the trumpets, in the same order.

- _Rev. 8:7-12; 9:15, 18._ "the third part" — six times. The trumpets are judgment **with mixture**: a third, not the whole; warning still mingled with mercy.
- _Rev. 14:10._ "the same shall drink of the wine of the wrath of God, which is poured out **without mixture** into the cup of his indignation." — the third angel threatens unmixed wrath.
- _Rev. 15:1._ "seven angels having the seven last plagues; for in them is filled up the wrath of God." — the vials are the wrath "filled up," complete, no longer a third.

  > "It follows, therefore, that the wine of the wrath of God is the seven last plagues... the wrath of God, as threatened by the third angel, is poured out in the seven last plagues; for the first plague is inflicted on the very class that the third angel threatens. Compare Revelation 14:9, 10; 16:1, 2."
  > — J. N. Andrews, _The Three Messages of Revelation 14:6-12_, 115

  > "We have shown that the plagues and the wrath of God without mixture are the same. And wrath without mixture must be wrath with nothing else; that is, wrath without mercy. God has not yet visited the earth with unmixed wrath; nor can he while our great High Priest ministers in the heavenly Sanctuary, and stays the wrath of God by his intercession for sinful men. When the plagues are poured out, mercy has given place to vengeance."
  > — J. N. Andrews, _The Three Messages of Revelation 14:6-12_, 116

- _The vials fall on the same targets as the trumpets, in the same order:_
  - _Rev. 16:2 ↔ 8:7_ — 1st vial / 1st trumpet: upon **the earth**.
  - _Rev. 16:3 ↔ 8:8-9_ — 2nd vial / 2nd trumpet: upon **the sea**, it became blood.
  - _Rev. 16:4 ↔ 8:10-11_ — 3rd vial / 3rd trumpet: upon **the rivers and fountains of waters**.

## The Same Symbols Point to the Eastern Question — Fire, Brimstone, Smoke

Here is the neglected half. The third angel's penalty is described in the same three words that mark the first and second woes — fire, brimstone, smoke. Let us trace the figure through the whole Bible (Rule 12) and weigh every use (Rule 10) before drawing the meaning.

- _Rev. 14:10-11._ "tormented with **fire and brimstone**... the **smoke** of their torment ascendeth up." — the penalty of the third angel.

- _Rev. 9:17-18._ Second woe — "out of their mouths issued **fire and smoke and brimstone**... by the fire, and by the smoke, and by the brimstone." — the same three agents, exactly.

[→] **Weigh the figure (Rule 10 — a figure can carry more than one meaning; let context and harmony decide).** The words "fire, brimstone, smoke" carry two related senses across Revelation:

- _The agent of war_ — the literal gunpowder, artillery, and cannon-smoke of the sixth-trumpet armies (Rev 9:17-18; cf. Smith, DAR 482-483 above). Fire/smoke/brimstone = the rod God uses.
- _The penalty itself_ — the unmixed wrath and final perdition: _Rev. 19:3_ "her **smoke** rose up for ever and ever"; _Rev. 20:10_ "the lake of **fire and brimstone**... tormented... for ever and ever"; _Rev. 21:8_ "the lake which burneth with **fire and brimstone**: which is the second death."

The distinctive Rev 14:11 phrase — "smoke of their torment ascendeth up **for ever and ever**" — lines up with the _penalty_ sense (Rev 19:3; 20:10), the final fire. So the third angel's "fire and brimstone, smoke" is, in Andrews' reading, the day of vengeance and final perdition:

> "He shall be tormented with fire and brimstone in the presence of the holy angels, and in the presence of the Lamb; and the smoke of their torment ascendeth up forever and ever... The final perdition of ungodly men in the lake of fire is without doubt the subject of these awful words."
> — J. N. Andrews, _The Three Messages of Revelation 14:6-12_, 124

> "The prophet Isaiah (chap. 34) describes the final conflagration of our globe in language which is a complete parallel to that of the third angel... 'For it is the day of the Lord's vengeance, and the year of recompenses for the controversy of Zion... the smoke thereof shall go up forever.'"
> — J. N. Andrews, _The Three Messages of Revelation 14:6-12_, 125, quoting Isaiah 34:8-10

[→] **The point the figure makes.** Both senses are one judgment seen at two ranges. The same three symbols that named God's rod in the woes (the agent) name His wrath in the third angel's penalty (the day of vengeance, Isa 34) — so the third angel's warning is cast in the very language of the woes, pointing the reader back to the rod that executes the wrath. ## The Rod of His Indignation — A Heathen Power as God's Rod

The first two woes were the rod (Muslims, Ottomans). Scripture's settled figure: God uses a heathen power as His rod and staff to punish His apostate people. The thesis of this study is that the same kind of rod returns for the third woe.

**DEFINITION — THE ROD OF HIS INDIGNATION = a heathen power that God raises up to punish His apostate, idol-serving people (named in Scripture: Assyria, Babylon — both "lions," Isa 10:5; Jer 50:17; and the Saracen and Ottoman of the two past woes). ## The Seal of God and the Sealing — In the Day of Slaughter, the Slain Are the Unsealed

The trumpets already taught it: the fifth trumpet could hurt "only those men which have not the seal of God." Sealing and slaughter are the two opposite outcomes of the one closing crisis. But first — what IS the seal?

The only commandment of the ten that carries all three — "the LORD" (name), "made" (Creator, His title), "heaven and earth" (His dominion) — is the fourth. - _Rev. 14:1._ the 144,000 stand "having his **Father's name** written in their foreheads" — the seal is the Father's name/character, the opposite of the mark.

- _Ezek. 9:5-6._ "smite: let not your eye spare... but come not near any man upon whom is the mark; and begin at my sanctuary." — the slaughter falls on the unmarked, beginning at the church.
- _Rev. 7:3._ "Hurt not the earth, neither the sea, nor the trees, till we have **sealed** the servants of our God in their foreheads." — the same: no hurt until the sealing is complete.
- Ellen White joins Ezekiel 9, the slaughter weapons, and the slain-of-the-Lord (Jer 25:33) into one scene:

Each of the three angels' messages was lit by a heaven-sent, publicly-verified event — and the fourth angel now lights the third. The loud cry is the message of the four winds: escape the day of slaughter before the sealing closes, or be found among the slain — that is, the unsealed.

[→] **And this is our work.** The loud cry IS the blowing of the trumpet — and Scripture has already defined the trumpet as the war-alarm (Num 10:9; Joel 2:1). So the loud cry is the watchman's trumpet of Ezekiel 33: the warning of the coming sword, and a charge that binds the warner under blood-accountability.

- _Ezek. 33:3-6._ The watchman who sees the sword and blows the trumpet; the hearer who will not heed dies in his own iniquity; the watchman who will not blow has the blood required at his hand.
  > "If when he seeth the **sword** come upon the land, he **blow the trumpet, and warn the people**; Then whosoever heareth the sound of the trumpet, and **taketh not warning**; if the sword come, and take him away, **his blood shall be upon his own head**... But he that **taketh warning shall deliver his soul**. But if the watchman see the sword come, and **blow not the trumpet**, and the people be not warned... his blood will I require at the **watchman's hand**." (Ezekiel 33:3-6)
- _Ezek. 9:4 ↔ 33:6._ Same prophet, same sword: in Ezekiel 9 the unmarked are slain; in Ezekiel 33 the unwarned are slain — and the slain in the day of slaughter are, of necessity, the unsealed (Rev 9:4). To warn is to seal; to be silent is to share the blood.
- _Amos 3:6._ "Shall a **trumpet** be blown in the city, and the people not be afraid?" — the warning is meant to make men flee. That fear-then-flight is mercy, not cruelty.

### The Latter Rain — the Fulness of Doctrine (rain = doctrine, Rule 12)

Before we can say where the latter rain belongs, we must do what this study has done with every figure: define the symbol by the Bible itself (Rule 12 — trace the word through Scripture and where you find it explained, put that meaning on the figure). And the Bible's own settled use of _rain_ is **doctrine — truth taught and received.**

**First, define the symbol (Rule 12).**

- _Deut. 32:2._ "My doctrine shall drop as the rain, my speech shall distil as the dew, as the small rain upon the tender herb, and as the showers upon the grass:" — Moses' own song sets the equation in the plainest terms: rain = **doctrine**, the falling speech of God upon the tender herb.
- _Isa. 55:10-11._ "For as the rain cometh down, and the snow from heaven, and returneth not thither, but watereth the earth, and maketh it bring forth and bud, that it may give seed to the sower, and bread to the eater: So shall my word be that goeth forth out of my mouth: it shall not return unto me void, but it shall accomplish that which I please, and it shall prosper [in the thing] whereto I sent it." — the figure is interpreted _in the text_: as the rain waters the earth, **so** the WORD goes forth and prospers. Rain = God's word.
- _Hos. 6:3._ "Then shall we know, [if] we follow on to know the LORD: his going forth is prepared as the morning; and he shall come unto us as the rain, as the latter [and] former rain unto the earth." — the rain is tied to **knowing** the LORD: He comes as the rain in the measure that we follow on to know Him. Rain falls as truth received into a heart that pursues it.

So Scripture defines its own figure: **rain = doctrine, truth taught and received.**

**Therefore the LATTER rain = the FULNESS of doctrine** — the ripening, completing truth poured out just before the harvest.

- _Joel 2:23._ "Be glad then, ye children of Zion, and rejoice in the LORD your God: for he hath given you the former rain moderately, and he will cause to come down for you the rain, the former rain, and the latter rain in the first [month]." — two rains, former and latter: the truth that began the work, and the truth that finishes it.
- _James 5:7._ "Be patient therefore, brethren, unto the coming of the Lord. Behold, the husbandman waiteth for the precious fruit of the earth, and hath long patience for it, until he receive the early and latter rain." — the husbandman waits for **both** rains to ripen the grain before harvest. The latter rain is the closing-of-the-crop rain, set at the coming of the Lord.
- _James 5:5._ "Ye have lived in pleasure on the earth, and been wanton; ye have nourished your hearts, as in a day of slaughter." — and James 5:5 (the **day of slaughter**, already defined in this study) is the SAME harvest context as James 5:7, two verses apart. The latter rain that ripens the grain and the day of slaughter that reaps it are the **same closing moment** — the harvest at the coming of the Lord.

**The diagnostic — why the latter rain must be doctrine and not a light-show.** Here is the test that settles it. If the latter rain were a purely supernatural display — fire, sound, an indiscriminate miracle — no one standing in it could miss it. But the testimony says it can fall **unrecognized**:

- _TM 507.1._ The latter rain falls on hearts that are not advancing — and they do not even discern it.
  > "Many have in a great measure failed to receive the former rain. They have not obtained all the benefits that God has thus provided for them. They expect that the lack will be supplied by the latter rain. When the richest abundance of grace shall be bestowed, they intend to open their hearts to receive it. They are making a terrible mistake. The work that God has begun in the human heart in giving His light and knowledge must be continually going forward. Every individual must realize his own necessity. The heart must be emptied of every defilement and cleansed for the indwelling of the Spirit. It was by the confession and forsaking of sin, by earnest prayer and consecration of themselves to God, that the early disciples prepared for the outpouring of the Holy Spirit on the Day of Pentecost. The same work, only in greater degree, must be done now. Then the human agent had only to ask for the blessing, and wait for the Lord to perfect the work concerning him. It is God who began the work, and He will finish His work, making man complete in Jesus Christ. But there must be no neglect of the grace represented by the former rain. Only those who are living up to the light they have will receive greater light. Unless we are daily advancing in the exemplification of the active Christian virtues, we shall not recognize the manifestations of the Holy Spirit in the latter rain. It may be falling on hearts all around us, but we shall not discern or receive it."
  > — Ellen G. White, _Testimonies to Ministers and Gospel Workers_, 507

This only makes sense if the latter rain is the Holy Spirit **unveiling doctrine into a prepared heart** — truth received, conditional on living up to present light: "Only those who are living up to the light they have will receive greater light." Some receive it while others standing right beside them "shall not discern or receive it." That is exactly what doctrine-unveiled-to-the-prepared looks like, and it is NOT what an indiscriminate miracle looks like — a miracle compels every eye; truth opens only to the heart that has followed on to know the LORD (Hos 6:3).

**Therefore the latter rain belongs IN the loud cry.** The loud cry **proclaims** the fulness of doctrine; the latter rain is that same fulness **received**. They are one event, the message gone out and the message come home:

- _EW 279.1._ The latter rain and the last great warning (the loud cry) are named together as a single moment at the closing of the third angel's message:

  > "I was pointed down to the time when the third angel's message was closing. The power of God had rested upon His people; they had accomplished their work and were prepared for the trying hour before them. They had received the latter rain, or refreshing from the presence of the Lord, and the living testimony had been revived. The last great warning had sounded everywhere, and it had stirred up and enraged the inhabitants of the earth who would not receive the message."
  > — Ellen G. White, _Early Writings_, 279

- _EW 71.2._ The refreshing — the latter rain — is what fits God's people to stand in the day of the Lord; but it is conditional, not automatic. Those who neglect the needful preparation reach the falling of the plagues with no shelter and no Mediator — exactly the diagnostic of TM 507.1, read forward to its end:
  > "I saw that many were neglecting the preparation so needful and were looking to the time of "refreshing" and the "latter rain" to fit them to stand in the day of the Lord and to live in His sight. Oh, how many I saw in the time of trouble without a shelter! They had neglected the needful preparation; therefore they could not receive the refreshing that all must have to fit them to live in the sight of a holy God. Those who refuse to be hewed by the prophets and fail to purify their souls in obeying the whole truth, and who are willing to believe that their condition is far better than it really is, will come up to the time of the falling of the plagues, and then see that they needed to be hewed and squared for the building. But there will be no time then to do it and no Mediator to plead their cause before the Father. Before this time the awfully solemn declaration has gone forth, "He that is unjust, let him be unjust still: and he which is filthy, let him be filthy still: and he that is righteous, let him be righteous still: and he that is holy, let him be holy still." I saw that none could share the "refreshing" unless they obtain the victory over every besetment, over pride, selfishness, love of the world, and over every wrong word and action. We should, therefore, be drawing nearer and nearer to the Lord and be earnestly seeking that preparation necessary to enable us to stand in the battle in the day of the Lord. Let all remember that God is holy and that none but holy beings can ever dwell in His presence."
  > — Ellen G. White, _Early Writings_, 71

The latter rain fits them "to stand in the day of the Lord" — the very day of slaughter the loud cry warns of. It is conditional, not automatic: the same neglect that misses the day of slaughter's warning also forfeits the refreshing that would have carried one through it. The proclamation and the reception are two sides of the one closing crisis.

**DEFINITION — THE LATTER RAIN = the fulness of doctrine (rain = doctrine: Deut 32:2; Isa 55:10-11; Hos 6:3) — the Holy Spirit unveiling the completed truth into prepared hearts to ripen them for the harvest (Joel 2:23; James 5:7). Because it is truth RECEIVED, not an indiscriminate supernatural display, it can fall on hearts all around us unrecognized (TM 507.1); it is the inward side of the loud cry — the message proclaimed (loud cry) and the message received (latter rain) — given to fit God's people to stand in the day of slaughter (EW 71.2; 279.1).**

**DEFINITION — THE LOUD CRY = the watchman's trumpet (Ezek 33:3-6) — the message of the four winds, warning that the third woe / seventh trumpet / day of slaughter (the eastern question, by the study's thesis) is at hand, calling God's people to escape (Rev 18:4) before the sealing closes. It is OUR commission: the watchman who blows it delivers souls and his own; the watchman who keeps silent has the blood of the slain required at his hand. To hear it and refuse is to be found among the slain in that day; and the slain in the day of slaughter are, by Rev 9:4 and Ezekiel 9:6, the unsealed — those who received the mark, who drink the wine of wrath under the rod of His indignation.**

## The Thesis, Assembled

Every definition is now filled in. Read them in a single breath:

- A **trumpet** is a real war God sends as a judgment (a "plague," Rev 9:18, 20).
- The first six trumpets fell **with mixture** — a third part — mercy still restraining.
- The first two **woes** were the rod of His indignation: the Saracens (1st woe) and the Ottomans (2nd woe, ended to the day on August 11, 1840).
- Trumpet = plague = war (the same _kind_ of judgment, Rev 9:20; 16:9), so the **third woe** will be the same kind of thing — a real rod — but now **without mixture**: the seven last plagues, which (Andrews, _TMR_ 115-116) are "the wrath of God without mixture," sounded by the seventh trumpet.
- The **third angel's message** announces this unmixed wrath and deals with **both questions**: the **western** (the beast from the earth, the image, the mark — Rev 13) and the **eastern** — casting its penalty in the very symbols of the first two woes, **fire, brimstone, smoke** (Rev 14:10-11 ↔ Rev 9:2, 17-18), which point back to the rod that executes the wrath.
- The **eastern question** is the third woe / day of vengeance; and (by the study's figure-transfer thesis) the rod that executes it is the eastern power, the Turk — a "lion" like the past rods (Rev 9:8, 17; Jer 50:17) — against all who take the mark.
- The **four winds**, the **sealing**, and the **day of slaughter** are the three consecutive phases of one closing crisis: the winds are held until the sealing is finished (Rev 7:1-3; EW 38), then loosed as the day of slaughter (EW 44; Jer 25:32-33). The sealing and the slaughter are its two opposite outcomes — so whoever is slain in that day is, by Rev 9:4 and Ezekiel 9:6, **unsealed**.
- The **loud cry** is the message of the four winds — the watchman's trumpet (Ezek 33:3-6): the warning that the day of slaughter is at hand, and the call to escape it (Rev 18:4; EW 277-278) before the sealing closes. It is our work to blow it; the silent watchman bears the blood of the slain.

[TANGENT — present application, beyond where the pioneers stopped] The pioneers traced the eastern question to August 11, 1840, where the prophetic time ran out and the second woe ended. The thesis of this study carries the chain forward: that the holding back of the four winds, and the moment they begin to be loosed, finds its modern marker in the loosing of strife from the east — September 11, 2001 — as the visible sign that the sealing time is closing and the third woe, the eastern rod against the mark, is at the door. This is offered as present-truth application of the proven symbol-chain, not as a pioneer date-calculation. The certainty belongs to the chain; the marker belongs to watchfulness (Rev 16:15).

## Appeal

- The **judgment of the living** — the closing phase of Christ's work in the heavenly sanctuary, the judgment now begun (Dan 7:9-10; 1 Pet 4:17, "judgment must begin at the house of God") — has begun. If, in the day of slaughter, those upon whom that judgment has begun are found **slain by the rod of His indignation**, then — by the plain word of Rev 9:4 and Ezekiel 9:6, where the rod strikes only the unmarked — they are among the **unsealed**: they received the mark, they drink the wine of His wrath.
- The loud cry is mercy's last warning to escape: to flee by faith in the message, out of the doomed cities, into the shelter God draws over His sealed people in the day of slaughter (EW 44).
- And it is laid on us to sound it. We are the watchmen of Ezekiel 33: if we see the sword and blow the trumpet, we deliver souls and our own; if we hold our peace, their blood is required at our hand. _"He that taketh warning shall deliver his soul"_ (Ezekiel 33:5).
- "Come out of her, my people, that ye be not partakers of her sins, and that ye receive not of her plagues" (Rev 18:4).
- _"Behold, I come as a thief. Blessed is he that watcheth, and keepeth his garments, lest he walk naked, and they see his shame"_ (Rev 16:15).
