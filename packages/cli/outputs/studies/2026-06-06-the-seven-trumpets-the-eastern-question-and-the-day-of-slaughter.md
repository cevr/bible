## Why Prophecy Is History Written in Advance

> The pioneers did not invent the history of the trumpets — they pointed to it. The chain is named out loud: **Gibbon (secular history) -> Keith (the believing commentator) -> White and Smith (the pioneers).**

**The historicist frame — prophecy is pre-written history.**

- _Rev. 1:1._ The book is given as coming history, sent in advance: "things which must shortly come to pass." SSTR 1.
- _Rev. 8:6._ Seven angels prepare to sound — seven future events; the only question is which history fulfils them. SSTR 2.
  > "instead of predicting the rise and triumph of either of those four great governments, it was his part to give the prophetic history of the fall of the last of the four..."
  > — James White, _The Sounding of the Seven Trumpets_, 1
- _Rev. 8:7-12._ The first four trumpets — hail and fire, a burning mountain, a falling star, a smitten sun — demand a real history to answer them. When the unbelieving Gibbon recorded the fall of Rome he was, unknowing, writing the commentary on Revelation 8 and 9. DAR 455.
  > "...The chapters of the skeptical philosopher that treat directly of the matter, need but a text to be prefixed... to form a series of expository lectures on the eighth and ninth chapters of Revelation." "Little or nothing is left for the professed interpreter to do but to point to the pages of Gibbon."
  > — Uriah Smith, _Daniel and the Revelation_, 455, quoting Keith

> 📖 **TRUMPET** = a war-alarm, hence a war/plague (_Num. 10:9_) -> the seven successive wars that brought Rome to ruin (_Rev. 8:6_; SSTR 2).

**The named scholarship behind every reading — the trail is open for inspection.**

- _Keith, Signs of the Times_ — running source for the first five trumpets, named by both White and Smith (DAR 455, 456).
- _Elliott, Horae Apocalypticae_ I.357-360 — cited by page for the extinction of the Western empire under the fourth trumpet. DAR 465.
- _Litch, Christ's Second Coming (1838) / Prophetic Expositions_ — author of the dated calculation that carried the prophecy through the woes to the seventh trumpet. SSTR 67; DAR 479.
- _Gibbon, Decline and Fall_ — the raw history under all the rest; _DAR_'s whole trumpet section is built on White's earlier tract. DAR 455.

**Rule 13 — the history must fulfil EVERY word.** A reading stands only if the history answers each detail; one word left over and it is wrong. Test it on "the third part," repeated six times (_Rev. 8:7-12; 9:15, 18_): at Constantine's death the empire was literally split three ways, between his sons Constantius, Constantine II, and Constans — so the numbers fit and the reading holds. SSTR 2; DAR 457.

> "The empire, after Constantine, was divided into three parts; and hence the frequent remark, 'a third part of men,' etc., in allusion to the third part of the empire which was under the scourge..."
> — James White, _The Sounding of the Seven Trumpets_, 2

> 📖 **THE THIRD PART** = one of the three divisions of the Roman empire under the scourge (_Rev. 8:7-12; 9:15, 18_) -> Rome's tripartite split among Constantine's three sons, A.D. 337 (SSTR 2; DAR 457).

**Rule 13's twin — fulfilled with positiveness, unfulfilled with modesty.** The same discipline that speaks with certainty of _past_ fulfilment refuses that certainty for what is _not yet_ fulfilled. This is why the study later labels its eastern-rod / present-day application as _present-truth synthesis_ (the thesis), not as an asserted, dated fact.

- _Rev. 11:14._ Two woes are history (positive); the third is future (modesty). SSTR 68.
  > "While we may speak of fulfilled prophecy with positiveness, we would apply unfulfilled prophecy with becoming modesty."
  > — James White, _The Sounding of the Seven Trumpets_, 68

So the certainty belongs to the dated, fulfilled chain (the first six trumpets); the modesty belongs to the third woe's present-day marker.

**What keeps symbols from being arbitrary — Miller's Rules 11 and 12.**

- **Rule 11** — _literal unless it does violence to nature._ An angel's trumpet burning literal trees does violence to nature, so the language is figurative; the _event_ it points to is literal, datable history.
- **Rule 12** — _trace the figure: a symbol means what the Bible elsewhere says it means._ A "trumpet" is the war-alarm (_Num. 10:9_); "winds" are strife (_Dan. 7:2_); stars and waters are defined by the text itself — Bible-defined, not invented.

> 📖 **STAR** = a ruler/leader/messenger set over a people (_Rev. 1:20_) -> the meteor-king Attila and the fallen star "Wormwood" of the trumpets (_Rev. 8:10-11_).

> 📖 **MOUNTAIN** = a kingdom or seat of power (_Jer. 51:25_; _Dan. 2:35_) -> the burning mountain cast into the sea, a kingdom overthrown under the 2nd trumpet (_Rev. 8:8_).

> 📖 **WIND / FOUR WINDS** = strife, war, commotion among nations (_Dan. 7:2_) -> the political winds whose holding and loosing govern the trumpet-wars (_Rev. 7:1_).

> 📖 **LOCUSTS** = an invading army God commissions (_Joel 2:25_) -> the Saracen/Arab hosts of the 5th trumpet (_Rev. 9:3_).

> 📖 **DAY = YEAR** = one prophetic day stands for one literal year (_Num. 14:34_; _Ezek. 4:6_) -> the 150 years and the 391 years 15 days dated by Litch to August 11, 1840 (DAR 479).

So the pioneers concluded a war in Western Rome because (a) the Bible defines "trumpet" as war (Rule 12), (b) a literal angel-trumpet burning trees does violence to nature, so the figure is compelled (Rule 11), and (c) the recorded history of Alaric, Genseric, Attila, and Odoacer — written by Gibbon, marshalled by Keith and Elliott, dated by Litch — answers every word (Rule 13). The interpreter only points to the page where it came true.

**DEFINITION — HISTORICISM =** the pioneers' disclosed method (White, _SSTR_ 1, 2, 68; Smith/Keith, _DAR_ 455, 457): prophecy fulfilled in literal, datable history — "the prophetic history of the fall" of Rome written before it happened, so the secular Gibbon unknowingly composed "a series of expository lectures on the eighth and ninth chapters of Revelation" (Keith, _DAR_ 455); its symbols read by the Bible's own definitions (Rule 12), figurative only where a literal sense does violence to nature (Rule 11); its discipline to speak of _fulfilled_ prophecy "with positiveness" and apply _unfulfilled_ "with becoming modesty" (_SSTR_ 68); its test Rule 13 — the history must answer EVERY word, as the prophecy's "third part" met Rome's tripartite division under Constantine's three sons (_SSTR_ 2; _DAR_ 457).

## But Is This Letting the Bible Be Its Own Expositor? (Answering "Hath God Said?")

The fair objection: is calling a star Attila the very thing we condemned in Origen — reaching past the plain word for a hidden sense? No: a figure the text itself marks is _illustration_ (honest); a meaning the reader imports is _interpretation_ (the counterfeit). The trumpet method stays on the honest side.

**1. The meaning comes FROM the Book, never from the interpreter (Rule 12).** Each figure is traced to the verse where Scripture defines it; that meaning, and no other, is put on the figure.

- _Rev. 17:15._ Waters = "peoples, and multitudes, and nations, and tongues" — the angel's own dictionary, not ours.
- _Rev. 1:20._ "The seven stars are the angels of the seven churches" — a star = the messenger/ruler set over a people.
- _Joel 2:25._ The locust = "my great army which I sent among you" — God Himself names the figure.

> 📖 **SEA / WATERS** = peoples, multitudes, nations, and tongues (_Rev. 17:15_) -> the maritime peoples and nations struck through the trumpets.

> "The Bible is its own expositor. One passage will prove to be a key that will unlock other passages... the true meaning of the Scriptures will be made evident."
> — Ellen G. White, _Christian Education_, 85

Origen's allegory _added_ meanings the text never marked; the trumpet method _receives_ the meaning the text supplies and goes no further.

**2. The text itself MARKS these as figures, so reading them as figures is obedience, not violence (Rule 11).** A trumpet that burns literal grass, a named star ("Wormwood") poisoning rivers, locusts shaped like armored cavalry — cannot be read flat without absurdity; the language forces the figure. Read literally first; admit the figure only where the text compels it.

- Miller's settled discipline, reported by a hostile editor: "Mr. Miller is a great stickler for literal interpretations; never admitting the figurative, unless absolutely required to make correct sense or meet the event which is intended to be pointed out." — _Miller's Works_, vol. 1, 16.

Origen took the literal and despised it for a hidden sense — he made a doctrine of it: "the literal is the baser sense of Scripture" (the serpent's move). Here the literal is held until the inspired text itself raises the figure (obedience).

**3. The dates themselves came from "Bible its own expositor," not from history (Rule 5).** The day-year key that yields the 150 years and the 391 years 15 days is itself Scripture interpreting Scripture.

- _Num. 14:34._ "Each day for a year" — God's own rod for measuring prophetic time. GC 324.
- _Ezek. 4:6._ "I have appointed thee each day for a year" — the same key, stated twice; the chronology is the Bible's, not the historian's. GC 324.

> "Following his rule of making Scripture its own interpreter, Miller learned that a day in symbolic prophecy represents a year (Numbers 14:34; Ezekiel 4:6); he saw that the period of 2300 prophetic days, or literal years, would extend far beyond the close of the Jewish dispensation, hence it could not refer to the sanctuary of that dispensation."
> — Ellen G. White, _The Great Controversy_, 324

The prophecy's own numbers run through the prophecy's own day-year key — not an external code laid over the text.

**4. The reading is FALSIFIABLE and verified; the hidden sense answers to nothing (Rule 13).** Origen's secret key is by definition uncheckable. The historicist reading stakes itself on datable public events — and was tested: the 391 years 15 days ran out on August 11, 1840, to the day, exactly as Litch had published two years before it happened. Test the two methods by the sermon's own measure — "to the law and to the testimony" (Isa 8:20), and add: to the record of history, which the prophecy itself invites. The hidden sense flunks because it submits to no test; the trumpet reading passes because it submitted to the hardest test (a dated prediction) and met it word for word. A method that stands or falls by whether history answers every word is the antithesis of a secret key that answers to nothing.

**5. This is the shared ground the sermon stood on.** Not a special rule for prophecy — the one rule the Reformers and pioneers recovered.

- Luther "submitted himself to the Bible... He saw that it must be its own interpreter... held it his duty to learn what is its direct and obvious teaching." — _The Great Controversy_, 173.
- "Scripture must be its own expositor, since it is a rule of itself." — _Miller's Works_, vol. 1, 20.

**In one line:** "Hath God said?" _loosens_ the word to import a hidden sense; historicism _holds_ the word and lets the Bible define its own figures (Rule 12), keep its own literal default (Rule 11), date its own prophecies by its own day-year key (Rule 5), then submits the whole to the test of history (Rule 13).

**DEFINITION — HISTORICIST READING vs. SPIRITUALIZING =** a historicist reading takes every symbol's meaning FROM the Bible (Rule 12), reads literally unless the text forces a figure (Rule 11), and stands or falls by whether history answers every word (Rule 13) — testable and Bible-supplied. Spiritualizing IMPORTS a hidden sense the text never marks, answerable to nothing but the interpreter — the serpent's and Origen's method. The trumpets are the first; this study is the second's opposite.

## How to Read a Symbol — The Bible's Own Lexicon (Rules 11-12)

The two rules below hand the reader a complete dictionary of the trumpet-symbols before John ever lifts a horn to his lips.

**DEFINITION — A PROPHETIC SYMBOL =** a figure whose meaning the Bible itself supplies; read literally unless it does violence to nature (Rule 11), and interpreted only by tracing it to where Scripture explains it (Rule 12) — never guessed, never imported.

### Part 1 — The Rule

- **Rule 11 — literal unless it does violence to nature.** A literal trumpet making literal hail and fire burn literal trees and grass (_Rev. 8:7_) does violence to the simple laws of nature; so the language is figurative. The rule forces the literal reading wherever it stands and only releases the figure where nature refuses it.
- **Rule 12 — trace the figure through the Bible.** Trace the word through Scripture; where you find it explained, put that meaning on the figure. The dictionary is the Bible; the interpreter only looks the word up.
- _DAR 463._ Smith applies it — "sun, moon, and stars... for they are undoubtedly here used as symbols — evidently denote the great luminaries of the Roman government, — its emperors, senators, and consuls."
- _DAR 482._ Smith applies it again — "fire... stands for red... jacinth, or hyacinth, for blue; and brimstone, for yellow" — colors, not substances (red/blue/yellow do no violence to nature; flaming horses would).

## What Is a Trumpet?

> The trumpet is the Bible's own alarm of war and of coming judgment. Define the figure by Scripture (Rule 12), then watch the history fill every word.

**Scripture's settled use — trumpet = war-alarm:**

- _Num. 10:9._ Blow an alarm with the trumpets when ye go to war against the enemy — God's own definition.
- _Amos 3:6._ A trumpet blown in the city, and the evil the LORD Himself hath done — the trumpet warns of judgment, the rod in His hand.
- _Joel 2:1._ Blow the trumpet in Zion, for the day of the LORD cometh.
- _Zeph. 1:14-16._ The great day of the LORD near — a day of the trumpet and alarm against the fenced cities and high towers.
- _Hos. 8:1._ Set the trumpet to thy mouth; a heathen power comes as God's rod against apostate Israel — the very pattern of the woes.
- _1 Cor. 14:8._ An uncertain trumpet, and none prepares for battle — Paul takes trumpet = battle-call as a given.

**The seven trumpets sound — and the history fits:**

- _Rev. 8:2._ The seven trumpets given to the seven angels.
- _Rev. 8:6._ The angels prepare to sound — one war after another, in sequence.
- _Rev. 8:7._ First trumpet: hail and fire mingled with blood cast on the earth; the third part of trees and all green grass burnt up — the Goths under Alaric.

First trumpet = the Goths under Alaric: "a deluge of barbarians" out of the North, the poet Claudian who "pathetically lamented the fate of his contemporary trees, which must blaze in the conflagration of the whole country [note the words of the prophecy, — 'The third part of trees was burned up']; and the emperor of the Romans fled before the king of the Goths" (DAR 456). The word "Africa" then signals the second trumpet — "instead of a storm of hail being cast upon the earth, a burning mountain was cast into the sea" (DAR 457).

- _Symbols (Rule 12):_ **hail** = sweeping destruction from the north (_Isa. 28:17_) — the Goths' northern origin; **fire** = devouring war (_Joel 2:3_); **blood** = slaughter (_Ezek. 14:19_); **trees** = great men/rulers (_Ezek. 31:3_; _Dan. 4:20-22_); **green grass** = the common people (_Isa. 40:7_). So the first trumpet consumes Rome's leaders and people together.

**DEFINITION — TRUMPET =** the Bible's own alarm of war and of coming judgment (_Num. 10:9_; _Amos 3:6_; _Joel 2:1_; _Zeph. 1:16_; _Hos. 8:1_) — here a literal war sent of God as judgment against Rome (pagan under the first four trumpets, apostate Christendom under the woes). Figurative by Rule 11 (an angel's trumpet burning literal trees does violence to nature), meaning "war" by Rule 12 (Scripture so defines it), the history fitting every word by Rule 13.

## The First Four Trumpets — Four Wars That Broke Rome

Each trumpet = one named invader, one real campaign — the proof that "trumpet" means "war" (Rule 13: the history meets every word).

- _Rev. 8:8-9._ Second trumpet — a burning mountain cast into the sea, a third of the sea turned to blood, a third of the ships destroyed: Genseric and the Vandals, a naval war (DAR 458).
- **mountain** = a kingdom (_Dan. 2:35_; _Jer. 51:25_) and **sea** = peoples/nations (_Rev. 17:15_) -> a warring kingdom (the Vandals) hurled on the maritime peoples.

  > "His conquests were, for the most part, naval; and his triumphs were 'as it were a great mountain burning with fire, cast into the sea.'... The next great invasion was that of 'the terrible Genseric,' at the head of the Vandals. His career occurred during the years A. D. 428-468."

## The Day of the Lord IS the Plague — One Force Through All the Prophets

> The chain so far runs **trumpet = plague = war**. Now gather every prophet (Rule 4): the "day of the LORD" — the refrain of Joel, Isaiah, Jeremiah, Zephaniah, Amos, Obadiah, Zechariah, Malachi, Nahum, Habakkuk — is described in the exact symbol-vocabulary the trumpets and vials use. It is the plague; the plague is the third woe; the third woe is the seventh trumpet. "The day of the LORD" and "the seven last plagues" are the same day seen from two ends of the Book.

### Part 1 — The Prophets Name the Day in Plague-Language

Every plague-symbol the trumpets used is the prophets' standing vocabulary for the day of the LORD.

- _Joel 2:1-2._ "Blow ye the trumpet in Zion, and sound an alarm... for the day of the LORD cometh... A day of darkness." — opens with the trumpet-alarm and the fifth trumpet's darkness (Rev 9:2).
- _Joel 2:10-11._ "the sun and the moon shall be dark, and the stars shall withdraw their shining: And the LORD shall utter his voice before his army." — the fourth trumpet's smitten lights (Rev 8:12), the LORD's army loosed.
- _Joel 2:30-31._ "blood, and fire, and pillars of smoke... before the great and the terrible day of the LORD come." — the trumpets' precise triad (Rev 8:7; 9:2, 17-18) set expressly before the day of the LORD.
- _Zeph. 1:14-18._ Trumpet, alarm, "high towers," "blood... poured out as dust," land "devoured by the fire" — the day of the LORD is the plague-stroke (cf. Isa 30:25, the falling towers).
- _Isa. 13:6, 9-13._ "the day of the LORD is at hand... the stars... shall not give their light: the sun shall be darkened... I will shake the heavens, and the earth shall remove." — fourth-trumpet lights plus the seventh-trumpet earthquake (Rev 11:19; 16:18).
- _Isa. 34:8-10._ "the day of the LORD's vengeance... the dust thereof into brimstone... the smoke thereof shall go up for ever." — the sixth-trumpet/third-angel triad named flatly as the day of the LORD. Andrews used this verse to define the third angel's penalty, TMR 125.
- _Amos 5:18-20._ "the day of the LORD is darkness... As if a man did flee from a lion... and a serpent bit him." — darkness, and a fleeing man met by a lion and a stinging serpent: the very lion-teeth and scorpion-sting force of the fifth and sixth trumpets (Rev 9:8, 10, 17).

> 📖 **LION** = a roaring conqueror / God's deploying rod (_Amos 3:8; Jer. 50:17_) -> the lion-toothed locust and lion-headed cavalry of the 5th and 6th trumpets (_Rev. 9:8, 17_).

- _Nahum 1:5-6._ "The mountains quake at him... his fury is poured out like fire." — fury "poured out" (the vials' own verb, Rev 16:1).
- _Obad. 15; Zech. 14:1; Hab. 3:11-12._ "near upon all the heathen"; "the day of the LORD cometh"; "the sun and moon stood still... thou didst thresh the heathen in anger." — every minor prophet sings the one day in the one vocabulary.

### Part 2 — The Same Day Is Set on the Euphrates and the Slain of the LORD

Two details nail the day of the LORD to the trumpet-and-vial Euphrates and to the day of slaughter — a word-match, not a mood-match.

- _Jer. 46:10._ "this is the day of the Lord GOD of hosts, a day of vengeance... the sword shall devour... a sacrifice in the north country by the river Euphrates." — the day of vengeance set by name on the Euphrates (Rev 9:14; 16:12); the eastern question stands inside the day-of-the-LORD text itself.

- _Jer. 25:30-33._ "The LORD shall roar from on high... a shout, as they that tread the grapes... a great whirlwind... And the slain of the LORD shall be at that day from one end of the earth even unto the other." — the winepress-shout (Rev 14:10), the whirlwind (Rev 7:1), the slain end to end: the day of slaughter is the day of the LORD.

> 📖 **WINEPRESS** = the treading-out of the wrath of God (_Jer. 25:30_) -> the unmixed wine of the third angel and the seventh trumpet's vials (_Rev. 14:10, 19_).

> 📖 **WHIRLWIND** = the storm-rush of God's judgment-army (_Jer. 25:32_) -> the four winds loosed for the day of slaughter (_Rev. 7:1_).

Joel's locust-army "which I sent" (Joel 2:25) — lion-teeth, horses, chariots, devouring fire (Joel 1:6; 2:3-5) — is, image for image, the fifth- and sixth-trumpet cavalry (Rev 9:7-9, 17) and the seventh-trumpet vials (Rev 16): first woe = torment only (Rev 9:5), second = slay a third (Rev 9:15), third = slay without mixture (Rev 16). ## With Mixture vs Without Mixture — The Vials Are the Trumpets Without Mercy

The trumpets strike "a third" — mercy still mingled; the vials strike the whole — mercy withdrawn; and vial by vial they fall on the trumpets' own targets, in order.

- _Rev. 8:7-12; 9:15, 18._ "The third part" — six times. Judgment **with mixture**: a third, not the whole; warning mingled with mercy.
- _Rev. 14:10._ "Without mixture" — the third angel threatens wrath "poured out without mixture into the cup of his indignation."
- _Rev. 15:1._ Seven last plagues: the wrath "filled up," complete, no longer a third — the vials. TMR 115.

> "It follows, therefore, that the wine of the wrath of God is the seven last plagues... the wrath of God, as threatened by the third angel, is poured out in the seven last plagues; for the first plague is inflicted on the very class that the third angel threatens. Compare Revelation 14:9, 10; 16:1, 2."
> — J. N. Andrews, _The Three Messages of Revelation 14:6-12_, 115

> "We have shown that the plagues and the wrath of God without mixture are the same... God has not yet visited the earth with unmixed wrath; nor can he while our great High Priest ministers in the heavenly Sanctuary... When the plagues are poured out, mercy has given place to vengeance."
> — J. N. Andrews, _The Three Messages of Revelation 14:6-12_, 116

The vials fall on the trumpets' targets, in order:

- _Rev. 16:2 ↔ 8:7._ 1st vial / 1st trumpet — upon **the earth**.
- _Rev. 16:3 ↔ 8:8-9._ 2nd vial / 2nd trumpet — upon **the sea**, become blood.
- _Rev. 16:4 ↔ 8:10-11._ 3rd vial / 3rd trumpet — upon **the rivers and fountains of waters**.
- _Rev. 16:8 ↔ 8:12._ 4th vial / 4th trumpet — upon **the sun**.
- _Rev. 16:10 ↔ 9:2._ 5th vial / 5th trumpet — both bring **darkness** (beast's seat / the pit); a thematic match in effect, not target.
- _Rev. 16:12 ↔ 9:14._ 6th vial / 6th trumpet — upon **the great river Euphrates**.
- _Rev. 16:17-18 ↔ 11:15, 19._ 7th vial / 7th trumpet (keystone) — both "the seventh angel," both speak from the opened **temple** ("It is done" / "the kingdoms of this world are become... our Lord's"), both close in "voices... thunders... lightnings... a great earthquake." One event.

**DEFINITION — WITH MIXTURE / WITHOUT MIXTURE =** the trumpets strike a third, mercy still mingled (Rev 8:7-12; 9:15, 18); the vials strike the same _kind_ of judgment ("plagues," Rev 9:20; 16:9) on the same targets in the same order, now in full, mercy withdrawn — for mercy is withdrawn only when the High Priest ceases to intercede, the close of probation (Andrews _TMR_ 116). The pairing is an interpretive harmony, not a mechanical lockstep: anchored hardest at the 1st-4th, 6th, and 7th pairs; the 5th matches in effect, not target.

What the seventh trumpet contains: it is the consummation-trumpet, covering far more than the plagues — the mystery of God finished (Rev 10:7), the kingdom transferred (Rev 11:15), the dead judged and saints rewarded (Rev 11:18), probation closed as the temple opens (Rev 11:19). The seven last plagues fall _within_ it, after probation shuts, as its executing wrath.

- _Rev. 10:7; 11:15, 18._ "The mystery of God should be finished"; "The kingdoms of this world are become the kingdoms of our Lord"; "the time of the dead, that they should be judged... reward unto thy servants... destroy them which destroy the earth." Kingdom-transfer, judgment, reward, destruction — all under one trumpet. DAR 507.

> "Having introduced the seventh trumpet in verse 15, the first great event that strikes the mind of the seer is the transfer of the kingdom from earthly to heavenly rule... we are pointed back, in verse 18, to the state of the nations, the judgment to fall upon them, and the final destiny of both saints and sinners... the last scene in the work of mercy for a guilty world. The temple is opened."
> — Uriah Smith, _Daniel and the Revelation_, 507

**DEFINITION — THE SEVENTH TRUMPET =** the consummation-trumpet (Rev 10:7; 11:15-19; Smith DAR 507): Christ takes the kingdom, the dead are judged, the saints rewarded, probation closes (the temple opened), the wicked destroyed. The seven last plagues / day of slaughter are the executing-wrath phase WITHIN it — the third woe — not the whole trumpet.

## Seven Within a Seven — Jericho and the Seventh Trumpet

The seventh trumpet and seventh vial are one event (both "the seventh angel," both "It is done" from the opened temple, both closing in "voices, thunders, lightnings, and a great earthquake," Rev 11:15-19 ↔ 16:17-18). Hence: the last of the seven trumpets _contains_ the full seven last plagues — a seven within a seven, the Bible's own pattern for a doomed city's fall, typified at Jericho.

**The Jericho type — seven priests, seven trumpets, the seventh day, seven circuits, the shout, the wall:**

- _Josh. 6:3-5._ Seven priests, seven trumpets; on the seventh day, seven circuits; at the long blast all shout and "the wall of the city shall fall down flat" — six days of single circuits, then a seventh day holding seven, ending in shout and falling wall.
- _Josh. 6:15-16._ On the seventh day, seven circuits; at the seventh time the priests blow, Joshua says "Shout; for the LORD hath given you the city" — the seventh circuit of the seventh day is the moment of conquest.
- _Josh. 6:20._ The people shout, the priests blow, "the wall fell down flat" — trumpet, shout, wall fallen.

EGW draws the climax in seventh-trumpet terms:

> "As the **seventh circuit** was completed... The trumpets... now broke forth in a **blast that shook the very earth**. The walls of solid stone, with their massive **towers** and battlements, tottered and heaved from their foundations, and with a crash **fell in ruin** to the earth." — PP 491.

Read beside the seventh trumpet/vial: "a great earthquake, such as was not since men were upon the earth" (_Rev. 16:18_); the day "when the towers fall" (_Isa. 30:25_); "great Babylon came in remembrance before God" and "every island fled away" (_Rev. 16:19-20_). Jericho's earth-shaking blast that felled the towers is the seventh-trumpet earthquake that fells Babylon.

**A pioneer draws the line — Miller reads the trumpet by Jericho** (his proof-text the same Amos 3:6 this study uses):

> "the **sounding of trumpets is always used to denote the downfall** of some empire, nation, or place... **At the fall of Jericho, the trumpet was the instrument**... which **cast down her walls, destroyed the city**... 'Shall a trumpet be blown in the city, and the people not be afraid?'... The seven trumpets mentioned in Revelation... indicate the final overthrow of the powers spoken of in the prophecy." — _Miller's Works_, vol. 2, 115.

James White completes it: under the first four trumpets the two western divisions fell, under the fifth and sixth the eastern empire was crushed, "but **under the seventh trumpet great Babylon entire will sink to rise no more at all**." — SSTR 2.

**Why the seventh contains seven — the covenant's sevenfold rod:**

- _Lev. 26:21._ "I will bring **seven times more plagues** upon you" (cf. _Lev. 26:28_, "I will chastise you seven times") — God's covenant rod is a sevenfold plague upon the unrepentant.
- _Rev. 15:1._ The "seven last plagues; for in them is filled up the wrath of God" — the sevenfold covenant-rod brought to full measure.
- _2 Kgs. 5:14._ Naaman dips seven times and is made whole — a lesser seven confirming completion lives in the seventh.

So the structure is the Bible's twice over: the trumpet that fells a city is sevenfold, climaxing on the seventh (Jericho); the plague chastising the covenant-breaker is sevenfold (Lev 26). Revelation joins them — the seventh trumpet, Jericho's seventh circuit, contains the seven last plagues poured out without mixture, and the wall of Babylon falls flat.

**The loud cry is the encircling trumpet; the shout is the fall.** Israel compassed Jericho six days, the city untouched — a long warning — then at the seventh the shout fells the wall. So the loud cry (the watchman's trumpet of Ezek 33, already defined) compasses the doomed city; when the proclamation is finished the seventh trumpet sounds, the shout goes up, and Babylon falls.

- _Rev. 18:2._ "Babylon the great **is fallen, is fallen**" — the loud cry's own announcement, the shout before the wall.
- _Rev. 18:4._ "**Come out of her**, my people... that ye receive not of her plagues" — God's people called out before the wall falls in the day of slaughter.

**DEFINITION — SEVEN WITHIN A SEVEN =** the Bible's own structure for a doomed city's downfall, typified at Jericho (Josh 6): seven priests, seven trumpets, and on the seventh day seven circuits, climaxing in the shout and the falling wall (PP 491). Miller fixes the prophetic trumpet by this type — "at the fall of Jericho, the trumpet was the instrument... which cast down her walls" (_Miller's Works_, vol. 2, 115). So Revelation's seven trumpets are Jericho's antitype: they sound the fall of great Babylon, and the SEVENTH trumpet — the seventh circuit — contains the SEVEN last plagues (the sevenfold covenant-rod of Lev 26:21), at whose pouring the shout goes up (Rev 18:2) and the wall falls flat (Rev 16:18-19). The loud cry is the encircling trumpet of warning; the seventh-trumpet plagues are the shout that brings the city down.

## The Third Angel's Message — A Warning of the Judgment Without Mixture

The third angel's penalty = the seven last plagues = the third woe, sounded by the seventh trumpet.

- _Rev. 14:9-10._ Worshippers of the beast and his image drink "the wine of the wrath of God, which is poured out without mixture into the cup of his indignation" — the warning names unmixed wrath.

- 📖 **JUDGMENT OF THE LIVING** = the closing phase of Christ's work in the heavenly sanctuary, the judgment now begun (_Dan. 7:9-10_) -> the investigative judgment now in session, determining who is sealed before the four winds loose as the day of slaughter (1 Pet. 4:17).
- 📖 **LION** = a roaring conqueror / God's deploying rod (_Amos 3:8; Jer. 50:17_) -> the lion-toothed locust and lion-headed cavalry of the 5th and 6th trumpets (Rev. 9:8, 17).
- 📖 **LOCUSTS** = an invading army God commissions (_Joel 2:25_) -> the Saracen/Arab hosts of the 5th trumpet (Rev. 9:3).
- 📖 **MOUNTAIN** = a kingdom or seat of power (_Jer. 51:25; Dan. 2:35_) -> the burning mountain cast into the sea, a kingdom overthrown under the 2nd trumpet (Rev. 8:8).
- 📖 **RAIN / LATTER RAIN** = doctrine, truth taught and received — the latter rain being its fulness (_Deut. 32:2; Isa. 55:10-11_) -> the Spirit unveiling completed truth into prepared hearts at the close, the inward side of the loud cry, fitting God's people to stand in the day of slaughter.
- 📖 **RIVERS / THE EUPHRATES** = the supporting population of the power that dwells on it; dry up the river and the power is undone (_Rev. 16:12_) -> the Ottoman sultanies loosed under the 6th trumpet (Rev. 9:14).
- 📖 **SCORPIONS** = power to sting and to chastise (_1 Kgs. 12:11_) -> the stinging tails of the 5th trumpet (Rev. 9:10).
- 📖 **SEA / WATERS** = peoples, multitudes, nations, and tongues (_Rev. 17:15_) -> the maritime peoples and nations struck through the trumpets.
- 📖 **SMOKE** = the rising token of torment and destruction (_Rev. 14:11_) -> the smoke from the pit under the 5th trumpet, and the third angel's penalty.
- 📖 **STAR** = a ruler/leader/messenger set over a people (_Rev. 1:20_) -> the meteor-king Attila and the fallen star Wormwood of the trumpets (Rev. 8:10-11).
- 📖 **SUN / MOON / STARS** = the great luminaries of a government — its emperors, senators, and consuls (_Gen. 37:9-10_) -> the Roman government smitten under the 4th trumpet.
- 📖 **SWORD** = war / the slaying stroke of judgment (_Jer. 46:10_) -> the devouring rod of the day of the LORD, set on the Euphrates (Isa. 66:16).
- 📖 **THE BOTTOMLESS PIT** = the deep, a dark power rising into view (_Rev. 9:1-2_) -> the desert wastes whence the Saracens issued under the 5th trumpet.
- 📖 **THE THIRD PART** = one of the three divisions of the Roman empire under the scourge (_Rev. 8:7-12; 9:15, 18_) -> Rome's tripartite split among Constantine's three sons, A.D. 337 (SSTR 2; DAR 457).
- 📖 **TREES / CEDARS** = great men and rulers, the lofty and lifted-up (_Isa. 2:13_) -> the rulers burnt up under the 1st trumpet.
- 📖 **TRUMPET** = a war-alarm, hence a war/plague (_Num. 10:9_) -> the seven successive wars that brought Rome to ruin (Rev. 8:6).
- 📖 **WHIRLWIND** = the storm-rush of God's judgment-army (_Jer. 25:32_) -> the four winds loosed for the day of slaughter (Rev. 7:1).
- 📖 **WIND / FOUR WINDS** = strife, war, commotion among nations (_Dan. 7:2_) -> the political winds whose holding and loosing govern the trumpet-wars (Rev. 7:1).
- 📖 **WINEPRESS** = the treading-out of the wrath of God (_Jer. 25:30_) -> the unmixed wine of the third angel and the seventh trumpet's vials (Rev. 14:10, 19).
