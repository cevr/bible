# Four and Three — How Scripture Divides the Seven

**Thesis.** The number seven, the Bible's number of covenant fulness and completeness, is again and again broken into FOUR and THREE — and the break is not arbitrary. Four is the number of the earth (the four winds, the four corners, the four quarters, the four beasts from the sea), and three is the number of heaven and of God (the thrice-holy, the three Persons of the divine name). So seven = four + three = the earthly joined to the heavenly, the creation under its Creator. The three great sevenfold series of the Revelation show the seam in the open: the seven seals divide 4 (the four horsemen) + 3; the seven trumpets divide 4 + 3 (the last three named "Woe, woe, woe"); and the seven churches carry the same fourfold/threefold signature. Read the seam, and the structure of each vision opens.

**Method.** After Haskell's _Bible Handbook_: ref → gloss, scannable; every number and symbol defined first from Scripture (Miller's Rule — the Bible defines its own figures). The doctrine of four (earth), three (heaven), and seven (their union) is built from the text; the pioneer/EGW witnesses (especially Uriah Smith on the 4+3 of the seals and trumpets) corroborate only, after Scripture has carried the point.

# Part I — The Numbers Defined

## 1. Seven — the Number of Covenant Fulness

> Seven is the Bible's own measure of completeness, perfection, and covenant — set at creation, sworn at the well, sprinkled in the sanctuary, and crowned in the Revelation; and Scripture keeps breaking the figure into four and three.

**Set at creation — Gen. 2:1-3; Exo. 20:11:**

- _Gen. 2:1._ "Thus the heavens and the earth were finished, and all the host of them." — creation is declared **finished**; completeness is the threshold of the seventh day.
- _Gen. 2:2-3._ "And on the seventh day God ended his work which he had made; and he rested on the seventh day... And God blessed the seventh day, and sanctified it" — on the SEVENTH day God ended his work and rested, blessed and sanctified; seven both completes and consecrates the whole. This is the founding pattern of the number.
  - **seven** = the number of completeness, perfection, and covenant — set at creation, where God ENDED his work and rested on the seventh day, so that seven marks a finished, consecrated whole (_Gen. 2:1-3_; _Exo. 20:11_).
- _Exo. 20:11._ "For in six days the LORD made heaven and earth... and rested the seventh day: wherefore the LORD blessed the sabbath day, and hallowed it." — the fourth commandment re-grounds the seventh-day rest in creation: six of work, the seventh hallowed — the rhythm written into time itself. The number's meaning is fixed before any symbol is built on it.

**Sworn in covenant — Gen. 21:28-31:**

- _Gen. 21:28-30._ "And Abraham set seven ewe lambs of the flock by themselves... For these seven ewe lambs shalt thou take of my hand, that they may be a witness unto me, that I have digged this well." — seven ewe lambs seal the oath between Abraham and Abimelech; the witness of a covenant is counted in sevens.
- _Gen. 21:31._ "Wherefore he called that place Beersheba; because there they sware both of them." — there they SWARE, and named the place "well of the oath." In the Hebrew the verb to swear (_shaba_) is built on the word for seven (_sheba_), so that to bind a covenant-oath is, in the language, a "sevening" — the narrative supplies the link (seven lambs, "they sware," Beersheba), and the lexicon confirms the wordplay.
  - **seven** (carried) = the number of covenant: at Beersheba seven lambs witness the oath and there "they sware" (_Gen. 21:28-31_).

**Sprinkled in the sanctuary — Lev. 16:14, 19:**

- _Lev. 16:14._ "and before the mercy seat shall he sprinkle of the blood with his finger seven times." — on the Day of Atonement the blood is sprinkled SEVEN TIMES before the mercy seat; sevenfold sprinkling for full cleansing.
- _Lev. 16:19._ "And he shall sprinkle of the blood upon it with his finger seven times, and cleanse it, and hallow it from the uncleanness of the children of Israel." — seven sprinklings **cleanse** AND **hallow** the altar; seven is the measure of completed purification. What was complete at creation is now complete in atonement.

**Built into holy time — Lev. 23:3, 15-16:**

- _Lev. 23:3._ "Six days shall work be done: but the seventh day is the sabbath of rest, an holy convocation... it is the sabbath of the LORD in all your dwellings." — the feast calendar opens on the seventh-day sabbath; the whole rhythm of holy time is built on seven.
- _Lev. 23:15-16._ "ye shall count unto you from the morrow after the sabbath... seven sabbaths shall be complete: Even unto the morrow after the seventh sabbath shall ye number fifty days." — Pentecost is reckoned by SEVEN sabbaths COMPLETE plus one — a week of weeks; seven measures the fulness of the feast count itself.

**Stacked for divine victory — Josh. 6:4:**

- _Josh. 6:4._ "And seven priests shall bear before the ark seven trumpets of rams' horns: and the seventh day ye shall compass the city seven times, and the priests shall blow with the trumpets." — Jericho falls under seven priests, seven trumpets, the seventh day, seven circuits. Sevens are stacked upon sevens to mark a work that is wholly God's and wholly complete, won by the act of God and not of man.

**The one Spirit named in sevenfold fulness — Isa. 11:2; Zech. 4:10; Rev. 1:4; 4:5; 5:6:**

- _Isa. 11:2._ "And the spirit of the LORD shall rest upon him, the spirit of wisdom and understanding, the spirit of counsel and might, the spirit of knowledge and of the fear of the LORD" — the Spirit upon Messiah is named in a sevenfold fulness: the Spirit of the LORD plus six attributes, the seedbed of Revelation's sevenfold Spirit.
- _Zech. 4:10._ "they shall... see the plummet in the hand of Zerubbabel with those seven; they are the eyes of the LORD, which run to and fro through the whole earth." — the SEVEN are the eyes of the LORD running through the whole earth: the OT root of the Lamb's seven eyes, the Spirit's complete reach.
  - **the sevenfold Spirit** = not seven separate spirits but the one Holy Spirit named in sevenfold fulness and perfection (_Rev. 4:5_; _Rev. 5:6_; rooted in _Isa. 11:2_; _Zech. 4:10_).
- _Rev. 1:4._ "and from the seven Spirits which are before his throne" — grace and peace are invoked from the SEVEN SPIRITS before the throne: the one Spirit in sevenfold fulness, sent to the seven churches.
- _Rev. 4:5._ "and there were seven lamps of fire burning before the throne, which are the seven Spirits of God." — Revelation interprets its own figure: the seven lamps of fire ARE the seven Spirits of God. Scripture defines the symbol.
- _Rev. 5:6._ "a Lamb as it had been slain, having seven horns and seven eyes, which are the seven Spirits of God sent forth into all the earth." — the Lamb's seven horns and seven eyes ARE the seven Spirits: full power and full wisdom, full reach. Again Scripture names the seven Spirits.
- _DAR 395.3._ Smith confirms, after Scripture has carried the point: "Horns are symbols of power, eyes of wisdom; and seven is a number denoting completeness, or perfection... perfect power and perfect wisdom inhere in the Lamb, through the operation of the Spirit of God, called the seven Spirits of God, to denote the fulness and perfection of its operation." — the pioneer reads exactly what the text said: seven = completeness/perfection, and the sevenfold Spirit = the fulness and perfection of the Spirit's operation.

**Crowned in the Revelation — the church, the book, and the trumpets seen in sevens — Rev. 1:12, 16; 5:1; 8:2:**

- _Rev. 1:12._ "And being turned, I saw seven golden candlesticks" — the complete church is seen as seven lamps. The sevenfold church is walked church by church in "The Seven Churches — the Fourfold and Threefold Signature."
- _Rev. 1:16._ "And he had in his right hand seven stars" — the messengers of the seven churches, the whole held in sevens in Christ's hand.
- _Rev. 5:1._ "a book written within and on the backside, sealed with seven seals." — the book of destiny is SEALED WITH SEVEN SEALS: the completeness of the divine record, opened only by the Lamb. The series is walked in "The Seven Seals — the Four Horsemen and the Last Three."
- _Rev. 8:2._ "And I saw the seven angels which stood before God; and to them were given seven trumpets." — seven angels, seven trumpets: the full series of judgments, paired with the seven seals as the great sevens of the book. The series is walked in "The Seven Trumpets — the Four, Then the Three Woes."

**The seam within the seven — four and three:**

- The same seven that Revelation lays on churches, seals, and trumpets is repeatedly divided FOUR then THREE — the four horsemen before the last three seals (_Rev. 6:1-8_), the four trumpets before the three woes (_Rev. 8:13_). Seven is whole, but it is a whole made of two parts: four, the number of the earth ("Four — the Number of the Earth"), joined to three, the number of heaven ("Three — the Number of Heaven and of God"). What that union means is gathered in "Four Plus Three — Earth Joined to Heaven in One Seven."

**DEFINITION — SEVEN =** the Bible's number of completeness, perfection, and covenant, self-defined by its first use: God ENDED His work and rested on the seventh day, blessing and sanctifying it (_Gen. 2:1-3_; _Exo. 20:11_), so seven marks a finished, consecrated whole. To make a covenant is to "seven oneself" — at Beersheba seven ewe lambs witness the oath and there "they sware" (_Gen. 21:28-31_). Seven governs sanctuary cleansing (blood sprinkled seven times, _Lev. 16:14, 19_), holy time (the seventh-day sabbath and the seven complete sabbaths to Pentecost, _Lev. 23:3, 15-16_), and victory by divine act (Jericho's stacked sevens, _Josh. 6:4_). The Revelation crowns it — seven churches, candlesticks, stars, seals, trumpets, and the one Spirit named in sevenfold fulness (_Rev. 1:4, 12, 16_; _4:5_; _5:1, 6_; _8:2_) — completeness throughout; and it is repeatedly shown as four (earth) + three (heaven).

**Symbols defined here:**

- **seven** = the number of completeness, perfection, and covenant — finished and consecrated at creation, sworn in covenant, sprinkled in the sanctuary, measuring holy time and divine victory, and crowning Revelation's visions (_Gen. 2:1-3_; _Gen. 21:28-31_; _Lev. 16:14, 19_; _Lev. 23:3, 15-16_; _Josh. 6:4_; _Rev. 1:4, 12_; _5:1_; _8:2_).
- **the sevenfold Spirit** = the one Holy Spirit named in sevenfold fulness and perfection — the seven lamps before the throne and the Lamb's seven horns and seven eyes, full power, full wisdom, full reach (_Rev. 4:5_; _5:6_; _1:4_; rooted in _Isa. 11:2_; _Zech. 4:10_).

**Symbols carried:** four = the number of the earth ("Four — the Number of the Earth"); three = the number of heaven and of God ("Three — the Number of Heaven and of God"); the four-plus-three division of the seven ("Four Plus Three — Earth Joined to Heaven in One Seven").

**For discussion:**

1. Genesis grounds the seventh day in God's own finished, blessed, sanctified work (_Gen. 2:1-3_; _Exo. 20:11_). If seven is the number of a _finished_ work, what does keeping the seventh day confess about whose work saves us (_Heb. 4:9-10_) — and how does that shape the way you read every later "seven" (the sprinklings, the feasts, the seals) as pointing toward a completed, consecrated work of God rather than a mere count?
2. At Beersheba, to swear a covenant was to "seven oneself" (_Gen. 21:28-31_), and the blood is sprinkled seven times to cleanse and hallow (_Lev. 16:14, 19_). What does it mean that the number of the covenant is also the number of full cleansing — and how does it deepen your trust to know God binds Himself to us by oath (_Heb. 6:13-18_)?
3. Revelation defines its own symbol: the seven lamps and the Lamb's seven eyes "are the seven Spirits of God" sent into all the earth (_Rev. 4:5_; _5:6_; _Zech. 4:10_). Where are you tempted to settle for a partial work of the Spirit, when Scripture offers the _full_ Spirit of God in sevenfold fulness?

## 2. Four — the Number of the Earth

> Four is Scripture's number of the earth — its four corners, its four winds, its four quarters, its four faces — the whole created world in its compass; and the four winds are the strife let loose over that world.

**The four winds and the four beasts — Daniel 7:**

- _Dan. 7:2._ "I saw in my vision by night, and, behold, the four winds of the heaven strove upon the great sea" — four winds over the great sea: the powers of the whole earth striving together, the universal compass of the world from which the kingdoms rise.
  - **four** = the number of the earth, the created world in its full compass — the four corners/quarters and four winds covering the entire globe, the four beasts that "arise out of the earth," the four rivers reaching out from Eden, the four living creatures facing every direction of the created world (_Dan. 7:3, 17_; _Rev. 7:1_; _Isa. 11:12_; _Gen. 2:10_; _Eze. 1:6, 10_). Four = the created world in its fullness.
  - **the four winds** = the powers and strife let loose over the whole earth — the winds "of heaven" / "of the earth" blowing from the four quarters, the agencies that strive over the world, held in check by four angels until loosed (_Dan. 7:2_; _Jer. 49:36_; _Jer. 25:32_, wind = "evil... from nation to nation"; _Rev. 7:1_).
  - **sea / waters** = peoples, nations (_Rev. 17:15_, "The waters... are peoples, and multitudes, and nations") — here, the great sea of mankind from which the kingdoms rise.
- _Dan. 7:3._ "And four great beasts came up from the sea, diverse one from another" — four great beasts out of the sea: the totality of world-empire arising from the peopled earth; four marks the complete earthly sequence.
- _Dan. 7:17._ "These great beasts, which are four, are four kings, which shall arise out of the earth" — Scripture's own equation: the four beasts = four kings that "arise out of the earth"; the number is bound by the text itself directly to the earth's powers.

**The four winds named — strife, war, and scattering over all nations:**

- _Jer. 25:32._ "Behold, evil shall go forth from nation to nation, and a great whirlwind shall be raised up from the coasts of the earth" — Scripture's own key: wind/whirlwind = evil and strife going forth nation to nation, raised up from the earth.
- _Jer. 49:36._ "Upon Elam will I bring the four winds from the four quarters of heaven, and will scatter them toward all those winds; and there shall be no nation whither the outcasts of Elam shall not come" — the four winds from the four quarters scatter "toward all those winds," over the whole earth; four winds = total dispersion over the world.
- _Dan. 8:8._ "the great horn was broken; and for it came up four notable ones toward the four winds of heaven" — the kingdom divided toward the four winds of heaven, that is, toward every direction of the earth; four winds = the universal compass.
- _Zech. 6:5._ "These are the four spirits of the heavens, which go forth from standing before the Lord of all the earth" — four spirits (winds) of the heavens going forth over "all the earth"; four = heaven's agency reaching the whole earth.
  - _Confirmation (after Scripture)._ Uriah Smith reads the figure straight from these same verses: "Winds, in the Bible, symbolize political commotion, strife, and war. Daniel 7:2; Jeremiah 25:32. The four winds, held by four angels standing in the four quarters of the earth, must denote all the elements of strife and commotion that exist in the world; and when they are all loosed, and all blow together, it will constitute the great whirlwind just referred to in the prophecy of Jeremiah" (_DAR 436.3_) — exactly the Bible's own definition, no more.

**The four corners and four quarters — the whole inhabited earth:**

- _Rev. 7:1._ "I saw four angels standing on the four corners of the earth, holding the four winds of the earth, that the wind should not blow on the earth, nor on the sea, nor on any tree" — four angels at the four corners hold the four winds of the earth in check; four = the entire earth in its quarters, the winds the universal strife held back over it.
- _Isa. 11:12._ "shall... gather together the dispersed of Judah from the four corners of the earth" — gathering from the four corners = from everywhere on the globe; four corners = the entire inhabited earth.
- _Rev. 20:8._ "to deceive the nations which are in the four quarters of the earth, Gog and Magog... the number of whom is as the sand of the sea" — nations in the four quarters = all nations everywhere; four quarters = the entire globe.
- _Mark 13:27._ "shall gather together his elect from the four winds, from the uttermost part of the earth to the uttermost part of heaven" — Christ's own equation: "from the four winds" = "from the uttermost part of the earth"; four winds = the whole earth.
- _Eze. 37:9._ "Come from the four winds, O breath, and breathe upon these slain, that they may live" — the breath summoned from the four winds, that is, from every quarter of the earth; four winds = the whole compass of heaven over the world.

**Four in creation — the river and the living creatures:**

- _Gen. 2:10._ "a river went out of Eden to water the garden; and from thence it was parted, and became into four heads" — the river of the garden parts into four heads watering the created world; four rivers reach out to the whole earth from Eden — four = the created order's compass.
- _Eze. 1:5._ "out of the midst thereof came the likeness of four living creatures... they had the likeness of a man" — four living creatures before the throne, the created order represented in fourfold form.
- _Eze. 1:6._ "every one had four faces, and every one had four wings" — four faces and four wings: fourfold completeness, facing every direction of the earth.
- _Eze. 1:10._ "they four had the face of a man, and the face of a lion... the face of an ox... the face of an eagle" — man, lion, ox, eagle: the four faces representing the whole of created, animate life; four = the created world in its fullness.
- _Eze. 1:17._ "When they went, they went upon their four sides: and they turned not when they went" — movement on their four sides, facing all four directions of the earth at once; four = the full compass of motion over the earth.
- _Rev. 4:6._ "in the midst of the throne, and round about the throne, were four beasts full of eyes before and behind" — four living creatures round the throne, the created order in worship; the New-Testament echo of Ezekiel's four faces.
- _Rev. 4:7._ "the first beast was like a lion, and the second beast like a calf, and the third beast had a face as a man, and the fourth beast was like a flying eagle" — lion, calf, man, eagle: the same fourfold representation of created life as Ezekiel 1:10; four = the whole created world before God.
- _Rev. 4:4._ "round about the throne were four and twenty seats: and upon the seats I saw four and twenty elders" — the four and twenty elders, the redeemed company round the throne; the term "four and twenty" belongs to this section's vocabulary, though twenty-four is a multiple (the tribes and the apostles) and not a pure figure of four — the four-as-earth doctrine rests on the winds, corners, beasts, rivers, and faces, not on the elders.

**DEFINITION — FOUR =** the Bible's own number of the earth in its whole compass. Scripture counts to four wherever it means the created world entire: the four corners and four quarters that hold every nation (_Rev. 7:1_; _Isa. 11:12_; _Rev. 20:8_), the four winds that Christ equates with "the uttermost part of the earth" (_Mark 13:27_; _Eze. 37:9_), the four beasts and four kings that "arise out of the earth" (_Dan. 7:3, 17_), the four rivers parting out of Eden to water the world (_Gen. 2:10_), and the four living creatures with four faces facing every direction of the created order (_Eze. 1:5-6, 10, 17_; _Rev. 4:6-7_). Bound up with it stand the four winds: the universal powers of strife, commotion, and war (_Jer. 25:32_, the Bible's own key that wind = strife going forth nation to nation) blowing from the four quarters over all nations, held in check by four angels until loosed (_Dan. 7:2_; _Jer. 49:36_; _Rev. 7:1_; confirmed _DAR 436.3_). Four = the created world in its fullness; the four winds = the world's powers in motion.

**Symbols defined here:**

- **four** = the number of the earth, the created world in its full compass (_Dan. 7:3, 17_; _Rev. 7:1_; _Isa. 11:12_; _Mark 13:27_; _Rev. 20:8_; _Gen. 2:10_; _Eze. 1:6, 10_; _Rev. 4:7_).
- **the four winds** = the powers and strife let loose over the whole earth (_Dan. 7:2_; _Jer. 25:32_; _Jer. 49:36_; _Rev. 7:1_; _Dan. 8:8_; _Zech. 6:5_).
- **the four corners / four quarters of the earth** = the entire inhabited globe (_Rev. 7:1_; _Isa. 11:12_; _Rev. 20:8_).
- **the four living creatures** = the created order in fourfold form, facing every direction (_Eze. 1:5-6, 10, 17_; _Rev. 4:6-7_).

**Symbols carried:**

- **sea / waters** = peoples, nations — owned by another section; used here for the great sea of mankind from which the four beasts rise.
- **three** = the number of heaven and of God — defined in "Three — the Number of Heaven and of God," the heavenward counterpart to four's earthward reach.
- **seven** = covenant fulness — defined in "Seven — the Number of Covenant Fulness," the whole that four and three divide; see "Four Plus Three — Earth Joined to Heaven in One Seven."

**For discussion:**

1. Christ Himself equates "the four winds" with "the uttermost part of the earth" (_Mark 13:27_). How does His own equation guard us from reading the number four mystically rather than geographically — as the plain compass of the created world?
2. Jeremiah gives the key that wind = "evil... from nation to nation" and "a great whirlwind" (_Jer. 25:32_); Revelation 7:1 shows four angels holding the four winds back. What does it mean for our day that the strife of all nations is being held in check, and what is the four angels' restraint waiting upon (_Rev. 7:1-3_)?
3. Four rivers water the garden (_Gen. 2:10_) and four living creatures face every direction before the throne (_Eze. 1:10_; _Rev. 4:7_). If four is the signature of the created earth, and three the number of heaven (see "Three — the Number of Heaven and of God"), how should the worship of "every creature" in heaven and earth (_Rev. 5:13_) shape the way we read these fourfold figures — and what should we expect when the two numbers are joined in one seven (see "Four Plus Three — Earth Joined to Heaven in One Seven")?

## 3. Three — the Number of Heaven and of God

> Three is the number stamped on the divine side of every reckoning — the heavenly witness, the threefold name, the thrice-holy, the third day of God's intervention — so that when seven divides as four and three, three is the heaven joined to earth's four.

**three** = the number of God / of heaven / of the divine fulness — the divine side of every reckoning, fixed by the threefold saving name (_Matt. 28:19_), the threefold benediction (_2 Cor. 13:14_), and the threefold blessing (_Num. 6:24-26_), and corroborated by the heavenly witness, "there are three that bear record in heaven... and these three are one" (_1 John 5:7_), with the fulness of the Godhead complete in Christ (_Col. 2:9_).

**The heavenly number is threefold — Matt. 28:19; Num. 6:24-26; (cf. 1 John 5:7; Col. 2:9):**

- _Matt. 28:19._ The one saving _name_ is threefold — Father, Son, and Holy Ghost named as the single divine authority into which converts are sealed (developed below under "The one name is threefold"), and so three is fixed as the number of God.
- _Num. 6:24-26._ The priestly blessing names the LORD three times in three lines, and so puts the divine name on Israel (v. 27) — three as the seal of God (developed below under "The blessing names the LORD three times").
- _1 John 5:7._ "For there are three that bear record in heaven, the Father, the Word, and the Holy Ghost: and these three are one." — a corroborating heavenly witness: three is named as the _heavenly_ number — the Father, the Word, the Holy Ghost — and these three are one, the divine fulness counted out as three.
- _Col. 2:9._ "For in him dwelleth all the fulness of the Godhead bodily." — the divine fulness, the Godhead complete, dwells in Christ; three names the totality of God, the heavenly side of all reckoning.

> Note on _1 John 5:7_: this verse (the Johannine Comma) is textually disputed and absent from the earliest Greek manuscripts; it is cited from the KJV/Received Text as this corpus's text, but the doctrine of three-in-heaven stands independently on _Matt. 28:19_, _2 Cor. 13:14_, and _Num. 6:24-26_ even if it be set aside.

**The one name is threefold — Matt. 28:19; 2 Cor. 13:14:**

- _Matt. 28:19._ "baptizing them in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Ghost" — the one saving _name_ is threefold; three persons are named as the single divine authority into which converts are sealed.
- _2 Cor. 13:14._ "The grace of the Lord Jesus Christ, and the love of God, and the communion of the Holy Ghost, be with you all." — the apostolic benediction blesses in three: grace of the Son, love of the Father, communion of the Spirit — the divine threefold pattern set over the church.
  - _Ev 615.1._ White (confirmation only, after Scripture has carried the point): "There are three living persons of the heavenly trio; in the name of these three great powers—the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit—those who receive Christ by living faith are baptized" — the threefold name of _Matt. 28:19_ read as three persons of the heavenly fulness.

**The blessing names the LORD three times — Num. 6:24-26:**

- _Num. 6:24-26._ "The LORD bless thee, and keep thee: The LORD make his face shine upon thee, and be gracious unto thee: The LORD lift up his countenance upon thee, and give thee peace." — the priestly blessing names the LORD three times in three lines, and so puts the divine name on Israel (v. 27) — three as the seal of God.

**The thrice-holy — heaven's own anthem — Isa. 6:3; Rev. 4:8:**

- _Isa. 6:3._ "And one cried unto another, and said, Holy, holy, holy, is the LORD of hosts: the whole earth is full of his glory." — the seraphim cry holiness in a threefold acclamation; three is the measure of the fulness of God's holiness, and from heaven that glory fills the whole earth.
  - **the thrice-holy** = the fulness of God's holiness — the threefold "Holy, holy, holy" is heaven's own confession of God's complete, unbounded holiness, sung above the throne (_Isa. 6:3_) and before the throne without rest (_Rev. 4:8_), the same anthem in earth's temple-vision and in heaven.
- _Rev. 4:8._ "and they rest not day and night, saying, Holy, holy, holy, Lord God Almighty, which was, and is, and is to come." — the living creatures before the throne echo the same thrice-holy without ceasing; heaven's own anthem fixes three as the seal of the divine.

**The third day — when heaven breaks into earth — Hos. 6:2; 1 Cor. 15:4:**

- _Gen. 22:4._ "Then on the third day Abraham lifted up his eyes, and saw the place afar off." — on the third day Isaac is received back as from the dead (_Heb. 11:19_) — the earliest pattern of three as the day of divine deliverance.
- _Exod. 19:11._ "be ready against the third day: for the third day the LORD will come down in the sight of all the people upon mount Sinai." — on the third day the LORD descends on Sinai; three is the appointed day of divine descent and manifestation.
- _Hos. 6:2._ "After two days will he revive us: in the third day he will raise us up, and we shall live in his sight." — the third day is the day of God's reviving and raising; divine intervention falls on the third, the heavenly day of resurrection.
  - **the third day** = the day God intervenes from heaven — the appointed span of burial before deliverance, of descent, of reviving, of resurrection, and of consummation (_Gen. 22:4_; _Exod. 19:11_; _Hos. 6:2_; _Jonah 1:17_; _Matt. 12:40_; _1 Cor. 15:4_; _Luke 13:32_).
- _Jonah 1:17._ "And Jonah was in the belly of the fish three days and three nights." — three days and three nights in the deep, the LORD's appointed span of burial before deliverance, the type of divine rescue.
- _Matt. 12:40._ "so shall the Son of man be three days and three nights in the heart of the earth." — Christ binds Jonah's three days and nights to His own; the great sign of resurrection, three as heaven's seal on victory over death.
- _1 Cor. 15:4._ "and that he rose again the third day according to the scriptures" — Christ rose the third day "according to the scriptures"; the resurrection, heaven breaking into earth, is dated to three.
- _Luke 13:32._ "the third day I shall be perfected." — Christ marks His work's completion to the third day; three as the number of divine consummation.

**DEFINITION — THREE =** the number of God, of heaven, of the divine fulness — the divine side of every reckoning. It is fixed by the threefold name (_Matt. 28:19_), benediction (_2 Cor. 13:14_), and blessing (_Num. 6:24-26_), and corroborated by the heavenly witness itself: "there are three that bear record in heaven, the Father, the Word, and the Holy Ghost: and these three are one" (_1 John 5:7_), so that the doctrine stands even if that disputed verse be set aside. The whole fulness of the Godhead dwells bodily in Christ (_Col. 2:9_). Three also marks the day God intervenes from heaven — the third day of descent, reviving, and resurrection (_Gen. 22:4_; _Exod. 19:11_; _Hos. 6:2_; _Jonah 1:17_; _Matt. 12:40_; _1 Cor. 15:4_; _Luke 13:32_). So three is the seal of the divine; when seven divides as four-and-three, three is the heavenly side joined to earth's four.

**DEFINITION — THE THRICE-HOLY =** the threefold "Holy, holy, holy," heaven's own confession of the complete, unbounded holiness of God — sung by the seraphim above the throne (_Isa. 6:3_) and by the living creatures before the throne without rest (_Rev. 4:8_), the same anthem in earth's temple-vision and in heaven. The repetition by three is the heavenly measure of God's holiness: three names the fulness, so the thrice-holy declares the totality of who God is, and the whole earth is full of His glory.

**Symbols defined here:**

- **three** = the number of God / of heaven / of the divine fulness (_1 John 5:7_; _Matt. 28:19_; _Num. 6:24-26_; _2 Cor. 13:14_; _Col. 2:9_; _Hos. 6:2_; _1 Cor. 15:4_; _Exod. 19:11_; _Matt. 12:40_).
- **the thrice-holy** = the fulness of God's holiness (_Isa. 6:3_; _Rev. 4:8_).
- **the third day** = the day God intervenes from heaven — descent, reviving, resurrection, consummation (_Gen. 22:4_; _Exod. 19:11_; _Hos. 6:2_; _Jonah 1:17_; _Matt. 12:40_; _1 Cor. 15:4_; _Luke 13:32_).

**Symbols carried:** none — this section owns the heavenly number and supplies the divine side wherever seven and four are reckoned; for the earthly number it joins, see "Four — the Number of the Earth"; for their union in one seven, see "Four Plus Three — Earth Joined to Heaven in One Seven."

# Part II — The Seam in the Sevens

## 4. The Seven Churches — the Fourfold and Threefold Signature

> The seven letters carry a hidden architecture: the position of one refrain — "he that hath an ear" — splits the seven into three and four. Formula-then-promise for the first three, promise-then-formula for the last four: the same fourfold/threefold division the Spirit stamped on the seals and the trumpets, here in 3+4 (mirror) order — the inverse of the seals' and trumpets' 4+3.

**The series named and the symbols self-defined — Rev. 1:11-20:**

- _Rev. 1:11._ "What thou seest, write in a book, and send it unto the seven churches which are in Asia; unto Ephesus, and unto Smyrna, and unto Pergamos, and unto Thyatira, and unto Sardis, and unto Philadelphia, and unto Laodicea." — the order is fixed and must be held; the structural seam will fall between the third and fourth, splitting the seven into 3+4.
  - **the seven churches** = the one church of Christ seen through its seven successive ages — the seven candlesticks self-defined as the seven churches (_Rev. 1:20_); seven, the number of completeness, covers the whole course, and each message is addressed "unto the churches," plural (_Rev. 2:7_), not to one alone.
- _Rev. 1:20._ "The seven stars are the angels of the seven churches: and the seven candlesticks which thou sawest are the seven churches." — Scripture decodes its own figures on the spot.
  - **the seven golden candlesticks** = the seven churches themselves (_Rev. 1:20_, self-defined) — the lampstand is the church bearing light to the world (_Matt. 5:14-16_), Christ walking "in the midst" of them in every age (_Rev. 2:1_).
  - **the seven stars** = the angels (messengers) of the churches, held in Christ's right hand (_Rev. 1:20_; _Rev. 2:1_) — the church's ministry through every age, sustained by Him.
- _Rev. 2:1._ "These things saith he that holdeth the seven stars in his right hand, who walketh in the midst of the seven golden candlesticks" — every letter re-anchors on the candlesticks and stars of 1:20: Christ is present in each age, not a distant overseer. This refrain and the "I know thy works" of every letter (_Rev. 2:2, 2:9, 2:13, 2:19, 3:1, 3:8, 3:15_) form the fixed frame against which the shifting seam stands out.

**The two refrains that build the pattern:**

- **the overcomer (he that overcometh)** = the faithful believer who endures to the end. Scripture defines him directly: born of God, conquering the world by faith in Jesus the Son of God (_1 John 5:4-5_, "this is the victory that overcometh the world, even our faith"); rewarded in every church-age and finally inheriting all things and sonship (_Rev. 21:7_). Christ sets the pattern — the overcomer overcomes "even as I also overcame" (_Rev. 3:21_).
- **he that hath an ear (the ear to hear)** = not the physical ear but the spiritual capacity, given by grace, to receive the Spirit's word — Christ's own parable-call from the Gospels (_Matt. 13:9_, "Who hath ears to hear, let him hear"; _Matt. 13:16_, "blessed are your eyes... and your ears, for they hear"). It marks the true hearer (the overcomer) from the merely present. Its POSITION relative to the overcomer-promise is the seam.

**The first triad — Pattern A: ear-formula FIRST, then promise:**

- _Rev. 2:7._ EPHESUS (1st). "He that hath an ear, let him hear what the Spirit saith unto the churches; To him that overcometh will I give to eat of the tree of life" — the ear-formula comes FIRST, the overcomer-promise follows. Note the formula is corporate, "unto the churches," proving each message is for the whole church through all ages.
- _Rev. 2:11._ SMYRNA (2nd). "He that hath an ear, let him hear what the Spirit saith unto the churches; He that overcometh shall not be hurt of the second death" — Pattern A again: formula FIRST, then promise. The persecuted church (_Rev. 2:10_) is promised escape from the second death.
- _Rev. 2:17._ PERGAMOS (3rd). "He that hath an ear, let him hear what the Spirit saith unto the churches; To him that overcometh will I give to eat of the hidden manna" — Pattern A a third time, closing the first triad: three churches, all formula-before-promise.

**The seam — at the fourth church the order inverts:**

- _Rev. 2:26._ THYATIRA (4th) — THE SEAM. "And he that overcometh, and keepeth my works unto the end, to him will I give power over the nations" — the overcomer-promise now comes FIRST (2:26-28), and only after it does the ear-formula follow. The order has inverted at the fourth church.
- _Rev. 2:29._ "He that hath an ear, let him hear what the Spirit saith unto the churches." — Thyatira closes with the formula AFTER the promise — Pattern B. From here to the end the formula trails the promise. The seam falls between Pergamos (#3) and Thyatira (#4) — a 3|4 division, the mirror image of the 4|3 seam in the seven seals and the seven trumpets.

**The last four — Pattern B: promise FIRST, then ear-formula:**

- _Rev. 3:5._ SARDIS (5th). "He that overcometh, the same shall be clothed in white raiment; and I will not blot out his name out of the book of life" — Pattern B: promise FIRST.
- _Rev. 3:6._ "He that hath an ear, let him hear what the Spirit saith unto the churches." — Sardis' formula trails the promise — Pattern B confirmed.
- _Rev. 3:12._ PHILADELPHIA (6th). "Him that overcometh will I make a pillar in the temple of my God... the name of the city of my God, which is new Jerusalem" — Pattern B: promise FIRST.
- _Rev. 3:13._ "He that hath an ear, let him hear what the Spirit saith unto the churches." — Philadelphia's formula trails the promise — Pattern B.
- _Rev. 3:21._ LAODICEA (7th). "To him that overcometh will I grant to sit with me in my throne, even as I also overcame, and am set down with my Father in his throne" — Pattern B: promise FIRST, and the highest of all, sharing Christ's own throne. Christ defines the overcomer by His own victory: the overcomer overcomes the same way Christ did.
- _Rev. 3:22._ "He that hath an ear, let him hear what the Spirit saith unto the churches." — Laodicea closes the series with the formula AFTER the promise — Pattern B, the fourth time. The tally is complete: churches 1-3 = formula-then-promise; churches 4-7 = promise-then-formula. A 3+4 signature woven into the seven.

**Where all the promises converge:**

- _1 John 5:4-5._ "whatsoever is born of God overcometh the world: and this is the victory that overcometh the world, even our faith... Who is he that overcometh the world, but he that believeth that Jesus is the Son of God?" — the "to him that overcometh" of the seven churches is no different victor; it is the believer's faith that conquers.
- _Rev. 21:7._ "He that overcometh shall inherit all things; and I will be his God, and he shall be my son." — the seven scattered overcomer-promises (tree of life, no second death, hidden manna, the throne) gather here into one inheritance and the sonship of God.

**Confirmation (after Scripture has carried the point):**

- _BHB 122.4._ Haskell: "God chose seven of them to represent the seven periods of His church during the remainder of time" — the historicist read that lets the demonstrable 3+4 literary seam stand as a prophetic outline of church history. (That this 3+4 seam is a mirror of the seals' and trumpets' 4+3 fourfold/threefold signature is interpretive synthesis built on the text's own structure — supported by Scripture, not stated by it.)

**DEFINITION — THE FOURFOLD AND THREEFOLD SIGNATURE =** the 3+4 architecture demonstrable in the seven letters by the position of the ear-formula: in Ephesus, Smyrna, and Pergamos the formula stands FIRST and the overcomer-promise follows (Pattern A); from Thyatira onward — Thyatira, Sardis, Philadelphia, Laodicea — the promise stands FIRST and the formula trails it (Pattern B) (_Rev. 2:7, 2:11, 2:17_ against _Rev. 2:26-29, 3:5-6, 3:12-13, 3:21-22_). The seam falls between Pergamos (#3) and Thyatira (#4) — a 3+4 division, here in mirror (inverted) order to the seven seals and the seven trumpets, which carry the same fourfold/threefold structure in 4+3 order. This is not an instance of "four = earth, three = heaven" but a structural cousin in reverse: the same two-part signature, the parts simply reversed. The whole frame is held steady by the candlestick-Christ who walks among the churches and the "I know thy works" refrain (_Rev. 2:1_), so the moving seam can be seen. The one constant through all seven is the overcomer — born of God, conquering by faith, overcoming "even as I also overcame" (_1 John 5:4-5_; _Rev. 3:21_), who finally inherits all things (_Rev. 21:7_).

**Symbols defined here:**

- **the seven churches** = the one church through its seven successive ages; candlesticks self-defined as churches (_Rev. 1:20_; _Rev. 1:11_; _Rev. 2:7_).
- **the seven golden candlesticks** = the seven churches, the church bearing light, Christ walking in their midst (_Rev. 1:20_; _Matt. 5:14-16_; _Rev. 2:1_).
- **the seven stars** = the angels/messengers (ministry) of the churches, held in Christ's right hand (_Rev. 1:20_; _Rev. 2:1_).
- **the overcomer (he that overcometh)** = the believer born of God who conquers the world by faith, overcoming as Christ overcame, rewarded in every age and finally inheriting all things (_1 John 5:4-5_; _Rev. 3:21_; _Rev. 21:7_).
- **he that hath an ear (the ear to hear)** = the spiritual, grace-given capacity to receive the Spirit's word — the parable-call of the Gospels; its position relative to the overcomer-promise is the structural seam (_Matt. 13:9, 13:16_; _Rev. 2:7, 2:29, 3:22_).

**Symbols carried:** the same fourfold/threefold signature of the seals, in mirror order — defined in "The Seven Seals — the Four Horsemen and the Last Three"; the same signature in the trumpets — defined in "The Seven Trumpets — the Four, Then the Three Woes."

1. The ear-formula moves from before the promise to after it between the third and fourth churches (_Rev. 2:7, 2:11, 2:17_ vs. _Rev. 2:26-29_ onward). What does it teach us that the same fourfold/threefold signature marks the churches, the seals, and the trumpets — even though the churches carry it in mirror (3+4) order — that one Author is writing one history?

## 5. The Seven Seals — the Four Horsemen and the Last Three

> The seals break in two: four horsemen ride the earth in a four-quartered course, then the pattern snaps — no horse, no rider — and the last three turn heavenward to martyr-cry, the day of wrath, and silence.

**The Lamb opens the seals — Rev. 6:1:**

- _Rev. 6:1._ "And I saw when the Lamb opened one of the seals, and I heard, as it were the noise of thunder, one of the four beasts saying, Come and see." — the same Lamb who took the book now breaks its seals, and a living creature summons each scene forward.

**The first four — the horsemen go forth — Rev. 6:1-8:**

- _Rev. 6:2._ "And I saw, and behold a white horse: and he that sat on him had a bow; and a crown was given unto him: and he went forth conquering, and to conquer." — the first seal: a horse rides out in early purity and victory. This first-seal white horse is the gospel and the early church going forth in apostolic purity — not the white horse of Rev. 19:11, whose rider is Christ Himself in final conquest; the shared color names the same righteousness, not the same referent.
  - **a horse** = a power or agency commissioned and sent forth, here in conquest and war — sent out from before the Lord to walk to and fro through the earth (_Zech. 6:5_, "the four spirits of the heavens, which go forth from standing before the Lord of all the earth"; _Zech. 1:10_, "These are they whom the LORD hath sent to walk to and fro through the earth"; _Rev. 6:2_, the rider "went forth conquering, and to conquer"; _Rev. 19:11_, the white-horse rider who "in righteousness he doth judge and make war"). Scripture defines its own figure: the horse is a force going forth, heavenly in commission yet earthly in theatre — the rider's color naming its work.
  - **white** = victorious going-forth, righteousness (_Rev. 6:2_; _Rev. 19:11_, heaven's own white horse whose rider is "called Faithful and True"; _Rev. 19:14_, the armies that follow "clothed in fine linen, white and clean").
- _Rev. 6:3-4._ "And there went out another horse that was red: and power was given to him that sat thereon to take peace from the earth, and that they should kill one another: and there was given unto him a great sword." — the second seal: peace taken, slaughter, a great sword.
  - **red** = bloodshed and war — the color names the work, "take peace from the earth" and "a great sword" (_Rev. 6:4_; the chariot-red of _Zech. 6:2_).
- _Rev. 6:5-6._ "And I beheld, and lo a black horse; and he that sat on him had a pair of balances in his hand... A measure of wheat for a penny, and three measures of barley for a penny; and see thou hurt not the oil and the wine." — the third seal: scarcity weighed out and priced by the balance.
  - **black** = famine and darkness, the loss of light — bread rationed by weight, the dearth that follows the sword (_Rev. 6:5-6_).
- _Rev. 6:7-8._ "And I looked, and behold a pale horse: and his name that sat on him was Death, and Hell followed with him. And power was given unto them over the fourth part of the earth, to kill with sword, and with hunger, and with death, and with the beasts of the earth." — the fourth seal closes the quartet with the only named rider, Death.
  - **pale** = death, the sickly hue of mortality — the rider is named outright, "his name... was Death" (_Rev. 6:8_).
  - **the four horsemen (white, red, black, pale)** = the earthly, four-quartered course of the church/world in conflict across successive periods — four = the earthly compass, sealed by the fourth rider's "power over the fourth part of the earth" (_Rev. 6:1-8_; _Rev. 6:8_; _Zech. 6:1-3_, the same fourfold colored horses as four chariots). The colors name each phase: white = victorious going-forth, red = bloodshed, black = famine, pale = death.

Scripture supplies its own key to these riders. The colored horses are not invented in Revelation; Zechariah saw them first, and the angel interpreted them outright.

- _Zech. 6:1-3._ "there came four chariots out from between two mountains... In the first chariot were red horses; and in the second chariot black horses; And in the third chariot white horses; and in the fourth chariot grisled and bay horses." — the fourfold colored horses, defined before John ever uses the figure.
- _Zech. 6:5._ "These are the four spirits of the heavens, which go forth from standing before the Lord of all the earth." — the horses are agencies heavenly in commission yet sent out to traverse all the earth; the going-forth, not their dispatch, is the figure Rev. 6 shares. The earthly weight of the four horsemen rests on their sphere of operation — "power over the fourth part of the earth" (Rev. 6:8) — not on a denial of their heavenly origin.
- _Zech. 1:8-10._ "a man riding upon a red horse... and behind him were there red horses, speckled, and white... These are they whom the LORD hath sent to walk to and fro through the earth." — the same colored horses, expressly the commissioned power moving through the earth.
- _Rev. 19:11._ "behold a white horse; and he that sat upon him was called Faithful and True, and in righteousness he doth judge and make war." — Revelation's own later white horse confirms the figure: a rider going forth to conquer.

**The seam — the pattern breaks at the fifth seal — Rev. 6:9-11:**

- _Rev. 6:9-10._ "And when he had opened the fifth seal, I saw under the altar the souls of them that were slain for the word of God, and for the testimony which they held: And they cried with a loud voice, saying, How long, O Lord, holy and true, dost thou not judge and avenge our blood on them that dwell on the earth?" — the fifth seal breaks the rhythm: no creature summons, no horse rides; instead the slaughtered martyrs cry from beneath the altar.
  - **souls under the altar** = the slain martyrs represented by their blood poured beneath the altar of sacrifice — not living disembodied spirits, but the blood crying for justice (_Lev. 4:7_, the sacrificial blood poured "at the bottom of the altar of the burnt offering"; _Gen. 4:10_, "the voice of thy brother's blood crieth unto me from the ground"; _Rev. 6:10_, "avenge our blood"). The blood, not a conscious spirit, is the voice — exactly as Abel's blood cried from the ground.
- _DAR 409.4._ Smith, a confirmation after the sanctuary-types have carried the point: "This cannot denote any altar in heaven, as it is evidently the place where these victims had been slain, — the altar of sacrifice... The souls are represented under the altar, just as victims slain upon it would pour out their blood beneath it, and fall by its side." — the altar is the altar of sacrifice; the "souls" are poured-out blood, not the conscious dead.
- _Rev. 6:11._ "And white robes were given unto every one of them; and it was said unto them, that they should rest yet for a little season, until their fellowservants also and their brethren, that should be killed as they were, should be fulfilled." — the answer to "How long": the verdict of righteousness given now ("white robes"), but judgment waits until the full number of the martyrs is complete.
  - **white robes** = the verdict of righteousness given the martyrs now, before the judgment falls (_Rev. 6:11_; cf. the white of the first horse, righteousness).

**The sixth seal — the heavenward turn to the day of wrath — Rev. 6:12-17:**

- _Rev. 6:12-14._ "there was a great earthquake; and the sun became black as sackcloth of hair, and the moon became as blood; And the stars of heaven fell unto the earth, even as a fig tree casteth her untimely figs, when she is shaken of a mighty wind; And the heaven departed as a scroll when it is rolled together; and every mountain and island were moved out of their places." — the last three seals run heavenward: earthquake, sun blackened, moon as blood, stars falling, heaven itself departing as creation unmakes itself before the day of wrath.
- _Rev. 6:16-17._ "Fall on us, and hide us from the face of him that sitteth on the throne, and from the wrath of the Lamb: For the great day of his wrath is come; and who shall be able to stand?" — the Lamb who opened seal one now appears in wrath; the great of earth flee His face. The sixth seal poses the question the seventh answers in silence: **who shall be able to stand?**

**The seventh seal — silence in heaven — Rev. 8:1:**

- _Rev. 8:1._ "And when he had opened the seventh seal, there was silence in heaven about the space of half an hour." — the last seal is not a scene but a silence: no creature, no horse, no vision, only stillness as the Lamb finishes the roll. The heavenward climax of the sevens arrives hushed.
  - **silence in heaven** = the seventh seal's pause, heaven stilled — the whole content of the last seal, the answer to "who shall be able to stand?" given not in words but in the hush of heaven (_Rev. 8:1_; _Rev. 6:17_).

**DEFINITION — THE SEVEN SEALS (4+3) =** the one prophetic roll, broken in two movements under the Lamb's opening hand. The first four are the four horsemen — an earthly, four-quartered course of the church/world in conflict, white to pale, each summoned by a living creature, each a power going forth (_Zech. 6:5_; _Zech. 1:10_), the rider's color naming its work: white = victorious going-forth, red = bloodshed, black = famine, pale = death — sealed as earthly and four-fold by "power over the fourth part of the earth" (_Rev. 6:1-8_; _Zech. 6:1-3_). At the fifth the pattern snaps — no horse, no creature — and the last three turn heavenward: the martyrs' poured-out blood crying "How long" beneath the altar of sacrifice (_Rev. 6:9-11_; _Lev. 4:7_; _Gen. 4:10_), the day of wrath in which earth's great hide from the Lamb and ask "who shall be able to stand?" (_Rev. 6:12-17_), and the silence in heaven that is the seventh seal entire (_Rev. 8:1_). The number itself preaches the structure: four for the earthly compass, three for the heavenward close. The historic span — apostolic purity, imperial persecution, doctrinal corruption, papal death, the Reformation's martyr-cry, the day of wrath — is the SDA-pioneer reading (_DAR_); Scripture itself states only the 4+3 structure and the symbols, not the dates.

**Symbols defined here:**

- **a horse** = a power or agency commissioned and going forth, here in conquest and war (_Zech. 6:5_; _Zech. 1:10_; _Rev. 6:2_; _Rev. 19:11_).
- **the four horsemen (white, red, black, pale)** = the earthly, four-quartered course of the church/world in conflict across successive periods — four = earthly scope; colors name each phase: white = going-forth, red = bloodshed, black = famine, pale = death (_Rev. 6:1-8_; _Rev. 6:8_; _Zech. 6:1-3_).
- **souls under the altar** = the slain martyrs' poured-out blood pleading for justice, not living disembodied spirits (_Rev. 6:9-10_; _Lev. 4:7_; _Gen. 4:10_).
- **white robes** = the verdict of righteousness granted the martyrs (_Rev. 6:11_).
- **silence in heaven** = the seventh seal's pause — heaven hushed, the sole content of the last seal, answering "who shall be able to stand?" (_Rev. 8:1_; _Rev. 6:17_).

**Symbols carried:** the Lamb who opens the seals and appears in wrath, and the four living creatures who summon each horseman — both introduced and used within this section (_Rev. 6:1_, the Lamb opening the seals; the four beasts summoning each horseman); four = the number of the earthly compass (defined in "Four — the Number of the Earth"); three = the heavenward number that closes the roll, the number under which the sixth seal's day of wrath in sun, moon, and stars belongs (defined in "Three — the Number of Heaven and of God").

**For discussion:**

1. The four horses are summoned by living creatures and named by color, then the fifth seal drops the horse entirely — no creature, no rider, only blood crying "How long?" Why does Scripture mark the seam here, and what does this 4+3 break teach about how Revelation uses number and pattern to carry meaning — four for the earth, three for heaven (_Rev. 6:1-8_ vs. _Rev. 6:9, 6:12, 8:1_)?
2. Read in light of Lev. 4:7 and Gen. 4:10, the "souls under the altar" are the martyrs' poured-out blood crying like Abel's from the ground (_Gen. 4:10_; _Rev. 6:10_). How does letting the Bible interpret its own symbol — soul as poured-out blood, not a conscious disembodied spirit — guard us against a false doctrine of the state of the dead?
3. The sixth seal asks "who shall be able to stand?" and the seventh answers only with silence (_Rev. 6:17_; _Rev. 8:1_). If the day of wrath is settled before a word is spoken, what must be settled in us now, while the roll is still being read?

## 6. The Seven Trumpets — the Four, Then the Three Woes

> Seven angels, seven alarms — but Scripture itself snaps the series in two: the first FOUR fall on the spheres of the created earthly world, and then an angel sets the last THREE apart as "Woe, woe, woe."

**The frame — Rev. 8:6:**

- _Rev. 8:6._ "And the seven angels which had the seven trumpets prepared themselves to sound." — the series is set: seven angels, seven trumpets. The number itself invites the listener to watch for an internal break, and the text will supply one (8:13).
  - **a trumpet** = an alarm — a divinely-appointed proclamation of war, judgment, or the day of the LORD (_Num. 10:2_, the silver trumpet "for the calling of the assembly, and for the journeying of the camps"; _Num. 10:9_, "ye shall blow an alarm with the trumpets... and ye shall be saved from your enemies"; _Joel 2:1_, "sound an alarm in my holy mountain... for the day of the LORD cometh"; _Eze. 33:3, 6_, the watchman who "blow[s] the trumpet" when he "see[s] the sword come"). So the seven trumpets of Revelation are seven successive divine judgment-alarms — proclamations of war against the powers that oppress.

**The four — Rev. 8:7-12:**

- _Rev. 8:7._ "The first angel sounded, and there followed hail and fire mingled with blood, and they were cast upon the earth: and the third part of trees was burnt up, and all green grass was burnt up." — the first of the FOUR; its judgment falls "upon the earth" — the first four strike the created earthly spheres.
  - **a third part** = a measured, partial stroke of judgment — not the whole, but a defined portion given over to the blow (_Eze. 5:2, 12_, the prophet's hair divided in thirds for the threefold ruin of Jerusalem; _Zech. 13:8_, "two parts therein shall be cut off and die; but the third shall be left therein"). The refrain runs through all four trumpets, binding them into one class.
- _Rev. 8:8._ "And the second angel sounded, and as it were a great mountain burning with fire was cast into the sea: and the third part of the sea became blood;" — the second trumpet, the sea; the same pattern, a "third part" struck, the blow landing on a created earthly sphere.
- _Rev. 8:10._ "And the third angel sounded, and there fell a great star from heaven, burning as it were a lamp, and it fell upon the third part of the rivers, and upon the fountains of waters;" — the third trumpet, the rivers and fountains; the "third part" refrain continues through the four.
- _Rev. 8:12._ "And the fourth angel sounded, and the third part of the sun was smitten, and the third part of the moon, and the third part of the stars; so as the third part of them was darkened..." — the fourth and LAST of the first group, the heavenly lights. With this the four are complete; the next verse marks the seam before the three.
  - The four move in order across the spheres of the created earthly world — **earth, sea, rivers, lights** — each suffering "the third part." They are blows of one order and one method, struck impersonally upon the created earthly spheres.

**The seam — Rev. 8:13:**

- _Rev. 8:13._ "And I beheld, and heard an angel flying through the midst of heaven, saying with a loud voice, Woe, woe, woe, to the inhabiters of the earth by reason of the other voices of the trumpet of the three angels, which are yet to sound!" — THE SEAM. Scripture itself divides 4+3: after the four, an angel sets the remaining three apart as "Woe, woe, woe" — "the trumpet of the three angels, which are yet to sound." The last three are of another order.
  - **the three woes** = the last three of the seven trumpets, set apart by Scripture itself as a distinct, graver class (_Rev. 8:13_, the threefold "Woe" against "the inhabiters of the earth"; numbered in _Rev. 9:12_; _11:14_; consummated in _11:15_). The threefold cry over the dwellers on earth answers Joel's alarm that the day of the LORD makes "the inhabitants of the land tremble" (_Joel 2:1_).
  - _Confirmation (secondary, after Scripture)._ The structural seam above is the text's own — the last three trumpets are judgments of another and final order. William Miller's eighth lecture, on "The three Woe Trumpets," reaches the same 4+3 division along a different axis — a historical, pagan-vs-papal one: "the first four having their accomplishment under Rome Pagan; to the last three under Rome Papal. These three trumpets and three woes are a description of the judgments that God has sent and will send" (_MWV2 115.4_). This 19th-century Advent exposition is offered only to corroborate the seam the text itself sets at 8:13; note that Miller's seam (pagan Rome vs. papal Rome) is a distinct claim from the structural seam (a graver, final order), not the same observation.

**The first woe — Rev. 9:1, 12:**

- _Rev. 9:1._ "And the fifth angel sounded, and I saw a star fall from heaven unto the earth: and to him was given the key of the bottomless pit." — the FIFTH trumpet = the FIRST woe; the judgment shifts from earth/sea/rivers/lights to the bottomless pit, confirming the woes are of a different character than the four.
- _Rev. 9:12._ "One woe is past; and, behold, there come two woes more hereafter." — the text counts the woes for us: the fifth trumpet was "one woe." Two remain (sixth, seventh). The 4+3 division is now numbered explicitly.

**The second woe — Rev. 9:13; 11:14:**

- _Rev. 9:13._ "And the sixth angel sounded, and I heard a voice from the four horns of the golden altar which is before God," — the SIXTH trumpet = the SECOND woe; it proceeds from the golden altar, so the woes answer the prayers of the saints (cf. _Rev. 8:3-5_), unlike the impersonal blows of the four.
- _Rev. 11:14._ "The second woe is past; and, behold, the third woe cometh quickly." — Scripture again numbers the seam: "the second woe is past" — the sixth trumpet closes, and the third woe (seventh trumpet) is announced. The 5th/6th/7th = first/second/third woe equation is the text's own.

**The third woe — Rev. 11:15, 18:**

- _Rev. 11:15._ "And the seventh angel sounded; and there were great voices in heaven, saying, The kingdoms of this world are become the kingdoms of our Lord, and of his Christ; and he shall reign for ever and ever." — the SEVENTH trumpet = the THIRD and final woe; its content is the climax of all history, the kingdoms passing to Christ. The third woe is the consummation, set utterly apart from the first four.
- _Rev. 11:18._ "And the nations were angry, and thy wrath is come, and the time of the dead, that they should be judged, and that thou shouldest give reward unto thy servants the prophets... and shouldest destroy them which destroy the earth." — unpacks the seventh trumpet: wrath, the judgment of the dead, reward for the saints, destruction of the destroyers. The third woe carries final judgment — proof the last trumpet stands on a different plane than the four.

**DEFINITION — THE SEVEN TRUMPETS, THE FOUR THEN THE THREE WOES =** the one series of seven judgment-alarms of Revelation 8-11 (trumpet = the Bible's alarm of war and the day of the LORD, _Num. 10:9_; _Joel 2:1_; _Eze. 33:6_), which Scripture itself structures 4+3. The first FOUR fall in order on the spheres of the created earthly world — earth, sea, rivers, heavenly lights — each striking "the third part" (_Rev. 8:7-12_). Then an angel cleaves the series in two, setting the last THREE apart as a graver class: "Woe, woe, woe, to the inhabiters of the earth... the trumpet of the three angels, which are yet to sound" (_Rev. 8:13_). The text then numbers the three: the fifth trumpet is the first woe ("One woe is past," _Rev. 9:12_), the sixth is the second woe ("The second woe is past," _Rev. 11:14_), and the seventh is the third and last woe — the consummation in which "the kingdoms of this world are become the kingdoms of our Lord" and final judgment falls (_Rev. 11:15, 18_). The 4+3 seam is not imposed; it is the text's own division, marking the last three trumpets as judgments of another and final order — a structural seam corroborated, along a separate historical axis, by Miller's grouping of the four under Rome Pagan and the three woes under Rome Papal (_MWV2 115.4_). (The historical identity of each trumpet — Saracen, Ottoman, and the rest — belongs to the historicist exposition and is beyond this section's structural scope.)

**Symbols defined here:**

- **a third part** = a measured, partial stroke of judgment — a defined portion given over to the blow, not the whole (_Eze. 5:2, 12_; _Zech. 13:8_).
- **a trumpet** = an alarm — a divinely-appointed proclamation of war, judgment, or the day of the LORD (_Num. 10:2_; _Num. 10:9_; _Joel 2:1_; _Eze. 33:3, 6_).
- **the three woes** = the last three of the seven trumpets (fifth, sixth, seventh), set apart by Scripture as a distinct, graver class — fifth = first woe, sixth = second woe, seventh = third woe (_Rev. 8:13_; _9:12_; _11:14-15_).

**Symbols carried:** the same 4+3 seam, gathered to its meaning at "Four Plus Three — Earth Joined to Heaven in One Seven"; four = the earth at "Four — the Number of the Earth"; three = heaven/the divine at "Three — the Number of Heaven and of God"; the parallel seal-seam at "The Seven Seals — the Four Horsemen and the Last Three"; the fourfold-and-threefold signature traced at "The Seven Churches — the Fourfold and Threefold Signature."

**For discussion:**

1. Rev. 8:13 makes the angel — not the interpreter — divide the seven trumpets into four and three, and the text itself numbers the woes for us (_Rev. 9:12_; _11:14_). Why does it matter for sound prophetic study that we let Scripture set its own seams rather than impose our own outline?
2. A trumpet in Israel "blew an alarm" so the people "shall be remembered before the LORD" and "saved from your enemies" (_Num. 10:9_), and Ezekiel's watchman blows it to warn of the coming sword (_Eze. 33:6_). If the trumpets are God's alarms of judgment, what merciful purpose is hidden inside even a "woe"?
3. The third woe ends not in ruin but in a coronation — "the kingdoms of this world are become the kingdoms of our Lord" (_Rev. 11:15_). How does ending the whole series on Christ's reign reframe the way we read every preceding judgment?

# Part III — The Meaning of the Seam

## 7. Four Plus Three — Earth Joined to Heaven in One Seven

> Seven breaks into four and three because four is read across Scripture as the number of the created earth and three as the number of God in heaven — so the divided seven is the gospel in arithmetic: the four joined to its Creator, the three crowning it.

**The original two — Gen. 1:1:**

- _Gen. 1:1._ "In the beginning God created the heaven and the earth." — the original order is two: heaven and earth; every later seam between three and four traces back to this primal joining of the Creator's realm and the created world.
  - **four (the earth-side)** = the number of the created, earthly order — fixed there not by a stated rule but by consistent usage: "the four corners of the earth... the four winds of the earth" (_Rev. 7:1_).
  - **three (the heaven-side)** = the number of God in His dwelling above — fixed there by the threefold "Holy, holy, holy" sung before the throne (_Isa. 6:3_; _Rev. 4:8_).

**The pattern laid in the creation week — Gen. 1:2-2:3:**

- _Gen. 1:2._ "And the earth was without form, and void... And the Spirit of God moved upon the face of the waters." — the week begins with the earth unformed and unfilled, the work that the made order must accomplish.
- _Gen. 1:31._ "And God saw every thing that he had made, and, behold, it was very good. And the evening and the morning were the sixth day." — the six days of making, the labor done in earth, close before the seventh; the created order's work is completed.
- _Gen. 2:1._ "Thus the heavens and the earth were finished, and all the host of them." — the forming-and-filling of the earthly creation is done; what remains is the day that joins it to God Himself.
- _Gen. 2:2-3._ "And on the seventh day God ended his work... And God blessed the seventh day, and sanctified it." — the seventh is the crowning member that brings God into the created week; the six are never read without it, and it alone is hallowed. The seventh is where heaven meets earth: the six labor-days plus the rest of God.
  - **the divided seven** = one key broken into a four and a three, read so that the earthly members are taken together and the heaven-ward members crown them — as the seventh day crowns the six (_Gen. 2:1-3_), the most holy crowns the sanctuary's first apartment (_Exo. 26:33_; _Heb. 9:2-3_), and the three God-ward petitions head the Lord's Prayer (_Matt. 6:9-10_). (The week's cleanest map is six-plus-seventh; the "forming then filling" of days 1-3 and 4-6 is a related division offered only as illustration.)

**The numbers fixed by usage — Rev. 7:1; Isa. 6:3; Rev. 4:8:**

- _Rev. 7:1._ "I saw four angels standing on the four corners of the earth, holding the four winds of the earth, that the wind should not blow on the earth, nor on the sea, nor on any tree." — Scripture attaches four to the earth: four corners, four winds "of the earth" — four is the number of the created, earthly order.
- _Isa. 6:3._ "Holy, holy, holy, is the LORD of hosts: the whole earth is full of his glory." — the threefold "Holy" belongs to the LORD in His heavenly throne-room; three attaches to God. And here that heavenly three fills "the whole earth" — the two realms joined.
- _Rev. 4:8._ "the four beasts... rest not day and night, saying, Holy, holy, holy, Lord God Almighty, which was, and is, and is to come." — in John's heaven-vision the four living creatures sing the threefold "Holy" before the throne; the four and the three are fused in a single verse, three crowning the scene as it crowns the seven.

**The same 3-over-earthly pattern in the model prayer — Matt. 6:9-13:**

- _Matt. 6:9-10._ "Our Father which art in heaven, Hallowed be thy name. Thy kingdom come. Thy will be done in earth, as it is in heaven." — the first three petitions are God-ward — thy name, thy kingdom, thy will — the heaven side, and they expressly join "in earth, as it is in heaven."
- _Matt. 6:11-13._ "Give us this day our daily bread. And forgive us our debts... And lead us not into temptation, but deliver us from evil." — the remaining petitions are man-ward — bread, forgiveness, deliverance — the earth side; the Lord's own prayer is built three (heaven) over the earthly needs — the heaven-crowns-earth pattern.

**The same seam stamped on the sanctuary — Exo. 26:33; Heb. 9:2-3:**

- _Exo. 26:33._ "the vail shall divide unto you between the holy place and the most holy." — the sanctuary is split by a vail: the outer holy place (with its court) for the daily, earthward ministry; the most holy where God dwells above the ark — the same divided pattern, now in furniture.
- _Heb. 9:2-3._ "the first... which is called the sanctuary. And after the second veil, the tabernacle which is called the Holiest of all." — the inspired commentary names the two apartments — the working sanctuary, then the Holiest of all where heaven touches the earthly tent — confirming the divided structure as God-given, not arbitrary.

**Why the seam exists — the doctrinal key — Eph. 1:10:**

- _Eph. 1:10._ "That in the dispensation of the fulness of times he might gather together in one all things in Christ, both which are in heaven, and which are on earth; even in him." — here is the reason the seven is divided. The whole purpose of God's dealings is to gather heaven and earth into one in Christ; the four is joined to the three so the created order is reunited to its Creator. The 4/3 is not numerology — it is the gospel.

**Where the seam closes — Rev. 21:1-3:**

- _Rev. 21:1._ "I saw a new heaven and a new earth: for the first heaven and the first earth were passed away; and there was no more sea." — the end answers _Gen. 1:1_: heaven and earth again, now renewed; the seam closes where the whole prophetic arc lands.
- _Rev. 21:2._ "I John saw the holy city, new Jerusalem, coming down from God out of heaven, prepared as a bride adorned for her husband." — heaven's city descends to the earth, the literal joining of the two realms toward which the divided sevens have always pointed.
- _Rev. 21:3._ "Behold, the tabernacle of God is with men, and he will dwell with them... and God himself shall be with them." — the final state names it: the tabernacle of God is WITH men — the most holy and the court no longer divided by a vail; earth joined to heaven, four crowned by three, forever.

**Confirmation in the pioneers' reading of the sevenfold visions** (secondary, after Scripture):

- _MWV2 115.4._ Miller: "The last three trumpets will claim our attention... the first four having their accomplishment under Rome Pagan; to the last three under Rome Papal." — a historical confirmation that the sevenfold trumpets were read as a divided seven, the first four set apart from the last three.
- _SSP 119.2._ Haskell: the sixth seal "differs from the first four seals, by showing events which mark prophetic time." — confirms the seven seals were likewise grouped as a divided seven, "the first four" treated as one set distinct from the later seals.

**DEFINITION — FOUR PLUS THREE =** the meaning of the divided seven. Seven divides as four-plus-three because four is read across Scripture as the number of the created earth and three the number of God in heaven, so the divided seven teaches that the whole of God's dealings join earth to its Creator — the four is never read alone, and the three crowns it. Four is fixed to the earth by the "four corners" and "four winds of the earth" (_Rev. 7:1_), and the work of making runs in sixes, finished before the seventh (_Gen. 1:31; 2:1_). Three is fixed to God by the threefold "Holy, holy, holy" sung before His throne (_Isa. 6:3_; _Rev. 4:8_). The strict four-plus-three seam appears in the sevenfold visions (the seals and trumpets divide 4+3; the churches carry the same signature in 3+4); and the same underlying logic — heaven set over the earthly — runs through the model prayer (three God-ward petitions heading the man-ward needs, _Matt. 6:9-13_, a three-over-the-rest), the tabernacle (holy place and court versus the most holy where God dwells, _Exo. 26:33_; _Heb. 9:2-3_), and the creation week (six days of forming-and-filling, then the seventh that brings God in, _Gen. 1:2-2:3_). All of it points to one end: "gather together in one all things... in heaven, and... on earth" (_Eph. 1:10_), realized when "the tabernacle of God is with men" (_Rev. 21:1-3_) — the divided seven made one.

**DEFINITION — THE DIVIDED SEVEN =** the structural key to the sevenfold visions: one seven broken into a four and a three, the four earthly members taken together and the three heaven-ward members crowning them — just as the seventh day crowns the six (_Gen. 2:1-3_), the most holy crowns the sanctuary's first apartment (_Exo. 26:33_; _Heb. 9:2-3_), and the three God-ward petitions head the Lord's Prayer (_Matt. 6:9-10_). The same 4/3 seam recurs across the sevens not by coincidence but because every sevenfold series carries the one principle of earth joined to heaven (_Eph. 1:10_), brought to its close when heaven descends to a renewed earth (_Rev. 21:1-3_).

**Symbols defined here:**

- **four** = the number of the created, earthly order — read from consistent usage, the "four corners" and "four winds of the earth" (_Rev. 7:1_).
- **three** = the number of God in heaven — read from the threefold "Holy, holy, holy" before the throne (_Isa. 6:3_; _Rev. 4:8_).
- **four plus three** = earth joined to heaven, the meaning of the divided seven; the four (created earth) crowned by the three (God in heaven) (_Rev. 7:1_; _Isa. 6:3_; _Rev. 4:8_; _Matt. 6:9-13_; _Exo. 26:33_; _Eph. 1:10_; _Rev. 21:3_).
- **the divided seven** = the structural key to the sevenfold visions — one key broken into a four and a three, the earthly members taken together and the heaven-ward members crowning them (_Gen. 2:1-3_; _Exo. 26:33_; _Heb. 9:2-3_; _Matt. 6:9-10_; _Eph. 1:10_; _Rev. 21:2_).

**Symbols carried:** the number four as the earth-side, established in "Four — the Number of the Earth"; the number three as the heaven-side, established in "Three — the Number of Heaven and of God"; the seven as the whole it crowns, established in "Seven — the Number of Covenant Fulness"; the 4/3 seam read across the sevenfold visions — the four churches and the three, the four horsemen and the last three, the four trumpets then the three woes — gathered here from "The Seven Churches — the Fourfold and Threefold Signature," "The Seven Seals — the Four Horsemen and the Last Three," and "The Seven Trumpets — the Four, Then the Three Woes."

**For discussion:**

1. Scripture binds four to "the four corners of the earth" and three to the "Holy, holy, holy" before the throne (_Rev. 7:1_; _Isa. 6:3_), and _Rev. 4:8_ fuses the two in one verse. How does seeing every seven as a four crowned by a three change the way you read the churches, the seals, and the trumpets?
2. The seventh day brings God into a week of human labor, and the most holy sits behind the vail of a working sanctuary (_Gen. 2:2-3_; _Exo. 26:33_). Where in your own week is the "seventh" — the member that joins your earthly work to heaven — and is it being kept?
3. Scripture's whole arc runs from "the heaven and the earth" of _Gen. 1:1_ to "the tabernacle of God is with men" of _Rev. 21:3_, the purpose being to "gather together in one all things... in heaven, and... on earth" (_Eph. 1:10_). If the divided seven is the gospel in arithmetic — the four reunited to the three — what does it mean for you that the seam was always meant to close?
