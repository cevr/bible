# Righteousness by Faith — the author's working definition / thesis

> This is Cristian's own framing. The handbook is to be built around this spine, with the
> sanctuary as the structural skeleton (Courtyard = Justification, Holy Place = Sanctification,
> Most Holy = Glorification), and the "image of God" as the governing thread.

## I. What was lost in sin: the IMAGE of God

What was lost in sin was the image of God (Gen 1:26, Gen 5:3), the presence of God (Isaiah 59:2),
and the life (Ezekiel 18:20) that comes from God — but in reality, these all stem from losing the image.

Exodus 33-34 teaches us that the image of God is His glory, is His name, is His character.

## II. The three are one: Law, Christ, Knowing God — all = LIFE = the IMAGE

The Bible teaches us that if a man does all the law, he shall live (Lev 18:5) — Jesus confirms this in Luke 10:25-28.

Jesus also says in John 17:3 that to know the only true God, and Jesus who He sent, is life eternal.
And finally, Jesus says in John 14:6 that He is the life.

How can we understand these three? Again, they are all one and the same.

Matthew 22:36-40 and Luke 10:25-28 both agree that the Law can be summarized into two principles —
love God, love your neighbour. Yet could we not further simplify it into one principle? Love.

In 1 John 4:8 — the Word teaches us that God _is_ love. Therefore, the law is an _image_ of God.
When 1 John 3:4 teaches us that sin is the transgression of the law, we can push it further as Jesus
did in Matthew 5, that anything that mars the character of God is a transgression of that law.

Why is Jesus the life? Hebrews 1:3 teaches us He is the express _image_ of God and the brightness
of His glory, and in John 16 He says that if you've _seen_ Him, you've seen the Father — He has the
same character as His Father.

Why is knowing the true God and knowing Jesus life? 2 Cor 3:18 teaches us that by beholding the
glory (character) of God, we are transformed into the same _image_ (character) of God.

## III. Bringing the points together

We were made in the image of God, having His character — sinning has marred that image in man —
causing our characters to ever incline towards Satan's own.

The Law being an Image of God, is our teacher (Gal 3:24-26) — teaching us the standard of God's
character. If one were to keep it perfectly, it means his character is a perfect reflection of God's.
Yet Rom 3:23 teaches us that we have all sinned.

Yet in Christ is Life, and He is Life. Being the express image of God, the first begotten of the
Father — He came to give Life (John 10:10), which, being understood, is He came to restore the
Image of God in man.

Why must we eat of His flesh and drink of His blood? Why must man not live on bread alone but by
every word that proceedeth out of the mouth of the Lord? Because for us to receive that Life from
Jesus, we must come to know Him. We must study His word, understand His truth, and see His character
that we may become transformed into that same Image.

Truth is that sanctifier (John 17:17) that transforms us day by day into His image.

## IV. Two things must be dealt with: DEATH and SEPARATION

Between man and God is an impassable gulf, one that cannot be crossed through any effort of man's
devising. Man must die, a slave to sin. Yet we know God desires to be with us again.

In Gen 28, Jacob saw a ladder whose bottom was on earth and top extended all the way to Heaven, to
the throne of God. He saw angels ascending and descending on it, and called the place from which it
stood the Gate of Heaven and the House of God.

In John 1:51, Jesus identifies Himself as the ladder that Jacob saw. In John 10:9, He also identifies
Himself as the Gate or Door. There is one other place on earth identified as the House of God — the
Sanctuary (1 Chronicles 28:10).

These parallels lead us to conclude that **Jesus = Ladder = Gate = Sanctuary** to God. Why did God
want a Sanctuary? Exodus 25:8: He desired to dwell with us. Therefore the purpose of the sanctuary is
to reverse the effects of sin — it explains how God leads man to Him, deals with the penalty of Sin,
and ultimately the cause of separation between man and God.

## V. The sanctuary's three compartments = three stages of salvation

Outer Courtyard = JUSTIFICATION · Holy Place = SANCTIFICATION · Most Holy Place = GLORIFICATION.

### Outer Courtyard — Justification (three pieces of furniture = three steps)

Mark 2:3-12 (the paralytic) and John 8:3-11 (the woman in adultery) outline the three steps:

1. **Gate = FAITH** — Jesus, seeing the man's faith / the woman saying "No man, Lord."
2. **Altar of Sacrifice = FORGIVENESS** — Jesus forgives his sins / "Neither do I condemn thee."
3. **Laver = POWER** — Jesus gives power to walk / "go and sin no more."

Healing is never separated from power. As Jesus forgives us our sins, He gives us power to go and
sin no more. Where does the faith come from? John 12:32 (Jesus draws all men), Eph 2:8-9 (faith is
the gift of God, by His Spirit).

The perfect Substitute pays man's debt of sin (His Life); alongside forgiveness He grants power
through the washing of regeneration (Titus 3:5) and makes us partakers of the divine nature (2 Peter 1:4).

### Holy Place — Sanctification (the daily walk to Heaven)

The man healed must now choose daily to go home (John 14:2-3); the path is narrow, the entrance strait.
The Holy Place has a Gate (= faith, Heb 11:6) and three pieces:

- **Table of Shewbread (north)** = daily eating the Word (John 6:53; Deut 8:3; Matt 4:4; John 1:1) —
  understanding God's Image, the means of being transformed.
- **Candlestick (south)** = good works / our light (Matt 5:14-16) — what we have seen and received we
  put into practice, leading others to faith.
- **Altar of Incense (west, set apart)** = prayer (Psalm 141:2; Rev 5:8) — by which we receive the Holy
  Spirit (Luke 11:13); without the Spirit we can neither understand the Word nor bear fruit (John 15:5;
  John 14:16-18; 2 Peter 1:21).

As the sanctuary service was daily, so daily we walk with God — Praying, Reading, Doing — transformed
more and more into His image. This is Sanctification.

### Most Holy Place — Glorification (the presence of God)

Isaiah 33:14-16: no sin can enter God's presence; His presence is a consuming fire. The Courtyard deals
with the penalty and the chosen nature of sin; the Holy Place sanctifies the heart; yet the flesh
remains, which Romans 8:3 calls sinful. At Christ's return our bodies are changed, made incorruptible
(1 Cor 15:51-52), and we shall be like Jesus glorified (1 John 3:2). Jesus completes restoration,
presenting us before the throne with exceeding joy (Jude 24-25).

## VI. The nature of Christ (to be folded in heavily)

He was fully tempted in all points as we were (Heb 4:15; Heb 2:14-18). The nature of man / nature of
Christ question (materialism vs. dualism, the source-video) bears directly on whether sin can be
overcome — the underlying theme of the plan of salvation. Christ took our nature, was tempted as we
are, and overcame — so in Him the image can be restored in us. (See the source transcript.)

## Governing thread for the whole handbook

IMAGE lost → LAW (image of God) is the teacher → CHRIST (express image) is the Life that restores →
TRUTH is the sanctifier that transforms → the SANCTUARY is the structure (Justification /
Sanctification / Glorification) by which the restoration is accomplished.
