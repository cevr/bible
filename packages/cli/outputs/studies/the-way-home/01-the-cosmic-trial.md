# Study 1: The Cosmic Trial — Why You Exist

**You were not an accident of divine boredom. Before this earth existed, the highest created being in heaven filed charges against the character and government of God — and you were created as living evidence in a real cosmic trial, a trial settled not by force but by demonstrated truth. The whole series ahead runs on two of those charges: "they only obey for reward," and "the law cannot be kept."**

## Prep Reading

- Ezekiel 28:12-17
- Isaiah 14:12-14
- Job 1:6-12
- Revelation 12:7-11

## How the Bible Tells Us Sin's Origin — A Dual Reference

**In Study 0 we settled that Scripture is its own expositor and that we take the plain sense. So before we read about Lucifer, notice the Bible's own method here: it describes two earthly kings — the king of Tyre and the king of Babylon — in language that overshoots any human king, and so points past him to the supernatural being who stood behind the throne. The text itself authorizes the dual reference; we are not importing a hidden meaning, we are following the words where they cannot stay on earth.**

- **The lament is addressed to "the king of Tyrus" — yet the words cannot fit a man**
  - "Son of man, take up a lamentation upon the king of Tyrus, and say unto him, Thus saith the Lord GOD; Thou sealest up the sum, full of wisdom, and perfect in beauty" (Eze 28:12)
  - "Thou hast been in Eden the garden of God" (Eze 28:13) — no king of Tyre was ever in Eden
  - "Thou art the anointed cherub that covereth; and I have set thee so: thou wast upon the holy mountain of God; thou hast walked up and down in the midst of the stones of fire" (Eze 28:14) — no earthly king was an anointed covering cherub on the mountain of God
  - [→] The plain words force us past the man of Tyre to the being he was a figure of. This is not allegory invented by us; the text describes someone created perfect, in Eden, a cherub on God's mountain — and only one creature ever fit that description.

- **The same dual reference in Isaiah — "the king of Babylon" who fell from heaven**
  - The chapter opens addressed to "the king of Babylon" (Isa 14:4), yet says: "How art thou fallen from heaven, O Lucifer, son of the morning! how art thou cut down to the ground, which didst weaken the nations!" (Isa 14:12)
  - No king of Babylon ever fell from heaven. The words break loose from the man and land on the being behind him.
  - [STR] Two prophets, two earthly thrones, one supernatural figure standing behind both — the Bible's own way of unmasking the power behind the powers. Compare spiritual with spiritual (1 Cor 2:13), and the two passages agree on one person.

[DYK🔎] "Lucifer" in Isaiah 14:12 is the Hebrew הֵילֵל (_helel_), "shining one / light-bearer," from a root meaning "to shine." He was designed to reflect God's glory outward. Instead he absorbed it inward. (This is illustration, not the proof — the proof is that the text names a being created perfect, in Eden, a covering cherub, who fell from heaven.)

## A Controversy Before Creation

**Following the Bible's dual reference, here is what Scripture plainly tells us about the being behind the thrones.**

- **His position — the highest created being**
  - "Thou sealest up the sum, full of wisdom, and perfect in beauty" (Eze 28:12)
  - "Thou art the anointed cherub that covereth; and I have set thee so: thou wast upon the holy mountain of God" (Eze 28:14)
  - Not a low-ranking rebel — the chief minister of heaven, set there by God Himself
  - [→] He had every advantage. Whatever went wrong did not go wrong for lack of light, position, or proximity to God.

- **Sin did not come from God — it was FOUND in a created being**
  - "Thou wast perfect in thy ways from the day that thou wast created, till iniquity was found in thee" (Eze 28:15)
  - [STR] Iniquity was not placed in him. It was _found_ in him. It originated _there_ — in a created will, not in the Creator. This single verse answers the oldest objection against God: "if God made everything, did God make sin?" No. He made a perfect being; the iniquity arose inside that being and is charged to that being.
  - This is the Bible breaking the charge before the charge is even spoken: God is not the author of evil; a creature is.

- **The five "I wills" — ambition dressed as grievance**
  - "For thou hast said in thine heart, I will ascend into heaven" (Isa 14:13)
  - "I will exalt my throne above the stars of God" (Isa 14:13)
  - "I will sit also upon the mount of the congregation, in the sides of the north" (Isa 14:13)
  - "I will ascend above the heights of the clouds" (Isa 14:14)
  - "I will be like the most High" (Isa 14:14)
  - Five declarations — every one aimed at displacing God, not serving Him
  - [→] Notice: he did not say "I will destroy God." He said "I will be _like_ God." The counterfeit always mimics, never openly opposes — remember that, because every counterfeit system in this series wears God's clothes.

- **The root of it — self-exaltation born of the very gifts God gave**
  - "Thine heart was lifted up because of thy beauty, thou hast corrupted thy wisdom by reason of thy brightness" (Eze 28:17)
  - The beauty God gave produced vanity; the wisdom God gave produced cunning
  - [→] Sin's first form is not murder or theft — it is a heart that turns inward, that takes what was meant to pass through and hoards it.

## War in Heaven

- **Not merely rebellion — war, and Satan is a literal personal being**
  - "And there was war in heaven: Michael and his angels fought against the dragon; and the dragon fought and his angels" (Rev 12:7)
  - "And prevailed not; neither was their place found any more in heaven" (Rev 12:8)
  - "And the great dragon was cast out, that old serpent, called the Devil, and Satan, which deceiveth the whole world: he was cast out into the earth, and his angels were cast out with him" (Rev 12:9)
  - [STR] Revelation here NAMES him for us — "the dragon," "that old serpent," "the Devil," "Satan" — four titles for one literal being. He is not a principle, not a metaphor for human evil, but a real personal adversary who waged a real war and lost.

- **A third of the angels sided with him**
  - "And his tail drew the third part of the stars of heaven, and did cast them to the earth" (Rev 12:4)
  - These were not ignorant beings — they were holy angels who had seen God's glory firsthand
  - [→] If beings who stood in God's own presence could be drawn away, what does that say about the subtlety of the arguments Satan was making? The rebellion spread by persuasion, not by force.

[Q] **"Why didn't God just destroy Satan immediately and end it?"**
→ Because the charge Satan raised was about God's _character_. Destroying the accuser would not answer the accusation — to a watching universe it would seem to confirm it ("see, He silences whoever questions Him"). So God chose to be _vindicated_, not merely victorious: "let God be true, but every man a liar... That thou mightest be justified in thy sayings, and mightest overcome when thou art judged" (Rom 3:4). Force can silence an objection; only truth can answer one. This is the hinge of the whole controversy — and the reason it has been allowed to run so long.

## God Submits to Being Judged — The Trial Frame

**Here is the most astonishing move in all of Scripture: the Judge of all the earth agrees to stand trial. The controversy is over His character, so He will not settle it by power — He puts His own justice on the record and invites the verdict.**

- **God consents to be judged — and to be justified by the evidence**
  - "let God be true, but every man a liar; as it is written, That thou mightest be justified in thy sayings, and mightest overcome when thou art judged" (Rom 3:4)
  - "be justified... overcome when thou art judged" — this is courtroom language. God is not afraid of scrutiny; He invites it. His character can withstand any cross-examination.

- **Force proves power, not righteousness**
  - God could have annihilated Satan in an instant — but an annihilated accuser is a silenced witness, not a refuted one
  - The watching universe (angels, unfallen worlds) needed to see the charges _answered_, not merely crushed
  - [→] This is why the controversy takes time. Truth must be demonstrated, not just declared. A God who only ever won by force would look exactly like the tyrant Satan accused Him of being.

[TANGENT] This same principle governs how God deals with _you_. He does not coerce obedience; He does not override your will. He demonstrates His character and waits for your free response. Love that is forced is not love at all — it is tyranny dressed in affection. The cross, not the lightning bolt, is how God answers an accuser.

## Satan's Charges Against God

**These are not merely ancient grievances filed and forgotten. They are the operating assumptions behind every temptation you will ever face, and the engine of every false system this series will expose. Read them straight from Scripture.**

### Charge: God's Law Is Arbitrary and Cannot Be Kept

- "Yea, hath God said...?" (Gen 3:1) — the first recorded words of the serpent on earth, not a denial of God but a question about His fairness (we traced this play in Study 0)
- The implication, spelled out: "For God doth know that in the day ye eat thereof, then your eyes shall be opened, and ye shall be as gods, knowing good and evil" (Gen 3:5) — that the command is a leash, that the rule exists to keep you small
- And then the seemingly clinching proof: fallen man genuinely _cannot_ keep it — "the carnal mind is enmity against God: for it is not subject to the law of God, neither indeed can be" (Rom 8:7)
- [→] Watch the trap close: Satan uses fallen humanity's real inability as evidence that the _law_ is the problem — that God requires the impossible and then condemns the failure. If that stands, God is unjust.

### Charge: Loyalty to God Is Bought, Not Genuine

- "Doth Job fear God for nought?" (Job 1:9)
- "Hast not thou made an hedge about him, and about his house, and about all that he hath on every side? thou hast blessed the work of his hands, and his substance is increased in the land" (Job 1:10)
- "But put forth thine hand now, and touch all that he hath, and he will curse thee to thy face" (Job 1:11)
- Escalated when the first test fails: "Skin for skin, yea, all that a man hath will he give for his life. But put forth thine hand now, and touch his bone and his flesh, and he will curse thee to thy face" (Job 2:4-5)
- The charge in one line: _no one serves God out of love — only out of self-interest._ Remove the benefits and the worship stops.
- [→] This is why suffering is permitted in the life of the faithful — not as punishment, but as the very evidence that disproves the charge. Love that survives the loss of every reward cannot be called bought.

[ILL] **Job's trial is the Bible's own staged courtroom exhibit.** A wealthy father wonders: do my children love me, or my money? He cannot know for certain — unless the money is removed. Job's calamity was not cruelty and not random; the text shows it convened in the heavenly council (Job 1:6) as a deliberate answer to Satan's accusation. When Job, stripped of everything, still says "though he slay me, yet will I trust in him" (Job 13:15), the charge "Doth Job fear God for nought?" is answered on the record, in front of the whole universe.

[Q] **"But these sound like the same accusation twice — isn't God just being slandered generally?"**
→ Look closely and you will see they aim at two different things. One charge attacks the law ("it can't be kept, so God is unjust to require it"). The other attacks the worshipper ("he only obeys because it pays"). One says God's standard is bad; the other says man's loyalty is fake. They cut from opposite sides toward the same goal: distrust of God. And — crucially — they need two different answers, which is exactly what the rest of this series will supply.

## A New Order of Witnesses Was Required

**The charges had to be answered — but by whom? Not by power, and as it turns out, not even by the loyal angels alone.**

- **Angels could not fully answer the charges**
  - "For verily he took not on him the nature of angels; but he took on him the seed of Abraham" (Heb 2:16)
  - When God moved to answer the controversy, He did not take up the nature of angels — He took human nature. Angels had never experienced weakness, mortality, or the pull of a fallen condition.
  - [→] An angel's loyalty proves a sinless being can stay loyal. It cannot prove that a _weak, tempted, suffering_ being can — and that was precisely the point in dispute.

- **The universe is the courtroom — and it is watching**
  - "For we are made a spectacle unto the world, and to angels, and to men" (1 Cor 4:9)
  - The word rendered "spectacle" is θέατρον (_theatron_), a theater. The redeemed are on the stage of the universe.
  - [→] This is not a figure of speech tacked on. The trial has an audience — "the world, and... angels, and... men" — and the evidence presented before that audience is the lives of redeemed sinners.

- **So a new kind of witness was created**
  - Beings who could experience the very things Satan said made obedience impossible — weakness, temptation, suffering, the loss of every reward — and still choose God
  - Beings through whom God's character could be demonstrated under exactly the conditions the charges named
  - [STR] That is why you exist. Not as an afterthought, but as testimony — God's answer to His accuser, written in human lives that hold to Him without a hedge and under fallen flesh.

## The Verdict Comes Through the Saints

**Scripture does not leave us to guess how the trial ends. It tells us the accuser is cast down — and names the three things that do it.**

- **The redeemed are the evidence that casts the accuser down**
  - "And they overcame him by the blood of the Lamb, and by the word of their testimony; and they loved not their lives unto the death" (Rev 12:11)
  - Blood of the Lamb = **legal standing** — Christ's sacrifice is what qualifies a sinner to testify at all (developed in Study 4)
  - Word of their testimony = **evidence** — not merely words, but the substance of transformed lives
  - Loved not their lives unto death = **credibility** — a witness who will die for the testimony cannot be bought
  - [→] That last clause is the direct answer to Job 1:9. Satan said loyalty has a price. A martyr proves it does not. "They loved not their lives unto the death" is the charge "they only obey for reward" disproved beyond appeal.

- **And so the accuser is cast down — by evidence, not by force**
  - "Now is come salvation, and strength, and the kingdom of our God, and the power of his Christ: for the accuser of our brethren is cast down, which accused them before our God day and night" (Rev 12:10)
  - "the accuser... which accused them before our God day and night" — Satan's role all along has been _prosecuting attorney_, pressing his charges in the court of heaven
  - He is cast _down_ — not silenced by a thunderbolt, but defeated in argument when the saints supply the evidence his charges denied was possible

- **You were made for this — and "glory" means character**
  - "Even every one that is called by my name: for I have created him for my glory, I have formed him; yea, I have made him" (Isa 43:7)
  - You were created _for God's glory_ — and across Scripture God's glory is His character (we prove this fully in Study 3, from Ex 33:18–34:7, where Moses asks to see God's "glory" and God answers by proclaiming His _character_)
  - To be made "for his glory," then, is to be made to display who God really is — to a universe watching the trial unfold

## The Two Charges That Drive the Whole Series

**Going forward, watch how the accusations consolidate into two operational charges that Satan presses through all of history. Mark them now; you will meet them in every study.**

- **Charge A: "They only obey for reward."** God's people are self-interested, not genuinely loyal (Job 1:9). Every counterfeit gospel that makes religion a transaction traces here.
- **Charge B: "The law cannot be kept."** Obedience is impossible, so God is unjust to require it (Gen 3:1; Rom 8:7). Every system that lowers, abolishes, or replaces God's law traces here.
- Every persecution, every false doctrine, every deception in the studies ahead will trace back to one or both of these.
- **The final verdict answers both at once:** "Here is the patience of the saints: here are they that keep the commandments of God, and the faith of Jesus" (Rev 14:12)
  - "keep the commandments of God" — Charge B refuted: in the last generation there stands a people who DO keep the law
  - "the faith of Jesus" — Charge A refuted: they hold to Him by faith and love, not for hire
- [→] Remember these two charges. They are the lens for everything that follows. The Way Home is, from start to finish, the story of how God answers His accuser — and how you become part of the answer.

## Appeal

- You are not here by accident, and you are not here for yourself
- You were created as a witness in a real cosmic trial — your character, your choices, your loyalty under pressure are evidence in a case about the goodness of God before a watching universe
- Two accusations drive everything ahead: "they only obey for reward," and "the law cannot be kept"
- Every day you live either confirms them or refutes them — and the God who submitted Himself to judgment will not coerce your verdict, but waits, having shown you the cross, for your free response
- Will you take the witness stand for the character of God?
