# Study 26: Conquering Canaan — How Transformation Really Works

**The Sabbath sealed you as God's own; coming out of Babylon cleared the ground. But a swept house left empty invites worse tenants. The question now is not "Will God change me?" but "By which covenant?" — for there are only two ways anyone ever tries to become righteous: "I will do it" and "God will do it in me." One built fig leaves at Eden and a golden calf at Sinai; the other wrote the law on Abraham's heart and will write it on yours. Scripture proves the engine — the new covenant, righteousness by faith, the fruit of the Spirit — in plain words. Then it hands us a forty-year war story as the map of how that engine works: the conquest of Canaan, where every giant is conquered and the land is possessed by little and little. The doctrine stands on the plain word; the conquest shows it to us in pictures.**

## Prep Reading

- Galatians 4:22-31
- Galatians 5:16-25
- Jeremiah 31:31-33
- Romans 4:1-5, 20-21
- Philippians 2:12-13
- Hebrews 3:14 – 4:11
- Joshua 5:13-15

## First the Doctrine: The Two Covenants — "I Will Do" vs. "God Will Do"

**Before any picture, the plain word. Paul does not leave the two covenants for us to deduce from a story — he names them, defines them, and tells us which is which. Everything in this study rests on these verses, not on the conquest narrative that will later illustrate them.**

- **Paul names the two covenants and marks his own reading as allegory — Galatians 4:22-26**
  - "For it is written, that Abraham had two sons, the one by a bondmaid, the other by a freewoman" (Gal 4:22)
  - "But he who was of the bondwoman was born after the flesh; but he of the freewoman was by promise" (Gal 4:23)
  - "Which things are an allegory: for these are the two covenants; the one from the mount Sinai, which gendereth to bondage, which is Agar" (Gal 4:24)
  - "But Jerusalem which is above is free, which is the mother of us all" (Gal 4:26)
  - [STR] This is not us spiritualizing a story. Paul himself says "which things are an allegory" and then states the lesson in plain doctrine: **the two covenants**. Ishmael, "born after the flesh," is the covenant of human effort — bondage. Isaac, born "by promise," is the covenant of God's doing — freedom. The text authorizes the figure and supplies the meaning.

- **The new covenant is God's promise to DO the work — Jeremiah 31:31-33**
  - "Behold, the days come, saith the LORD, that I will make a new covenant with the house of Israel, and with the house of Judah" (Jer 31:31)
  - "Not according to the covenant that I made with their fathers... which my covenant they brake, although I was an husband unto them, saith the LORD" (Jer 31:32)
  - "But this shall be the covenant that I will make with the house of Israel; After those days, saith the LORD, I will put my law in their inward parts, and write it in their hearts; and will be their God, and they shall be my people" (Jer 31:33)
  - [→] Count the verbs and whose they are. _I_ will make. _I_ will put. _I_ will write. The new covenant is not a list of resolutions you keep; it is a work God performs _in you_ — the law moved from stone to the heart. The old covenant broke because it ran on the wrong subject: "which my covenant _they_ brake."

- **Hebrews repeats it word for word — the new covenant is still "I will" — Hebrews 8:10**
  - "For this is the covenant that I will make with the house of Israel after those days, saith the Lord; I will put my laws into their mind, and write them in their hearts: and I will be to them a God, and they shall be to me a people" (Heb 8:10)
  - [→] The Spirit quotes Jeremiah into the New Testament and underlines it: the heart-writing is _God's_ act. Sanctification is not the law printed on a wall for you to obey by willpower; it is the law printed on the heart by the One who promised to do it.

[DYK🔎] Notice what the new covenant is _not_. It is not "no law." The very promise is "I will put **my law** in their inward parts" (Jer 31:33). The new covenant does not abolish the moral law established in Study 6 — it _internalizes_ it. The old covenant tried to keep that same law from the outside and failed; the new covenant keeps it from the inside, because God writes it there. Same law, different location, different power.

- **The old covenant: man's self-confident promise — Exodus 19:8**
  - "And all the people answered together, and said, All that the LORD hath spoken we will do" (Ex 19:8)
  - [→] There it is — the old covenant in one sentence: "_we_ will do." Not "Lord, do it in us," but "we will." Broken within forty days by the golden calf. This is not a different _law_ God offered them; it is the same law received by the wrong covenant — human promise instead of divine performance.
  - "O that there were such an heart in them, that they would fear me, and keep all my commandments always" (Deut 5:29) — God's grief was never the law; it was that they had no heart for it. The new covenant is the answer to that very grief: He gives the heart (Eze 36:26).

[Q] **"But doesn't God want us to DO something? Isn't this just passivity?"**

> No — and Scripture closes the loophole both ways. **"Work out your own salvation with fear and trembling. For it is God which worketh in you both to will and to do of his good pleasure"** (Phil 2:12-13). You _work out_ — there is real, strenuous effort. But notice the order and the engine: God works _in_ you first ("both to will and to do"); you work _out_ what He has worked in. Self-effort says "I do it, God helps." Passivity says "God does it, I wait." The new covenant says "God works in me, and therefore I work out." It is the most active thing in the world — but the power is His.

[→] So the doctrine is fixed before any picture is drawn: **there are two covenants, the old is "we will do," the new is "I — God — will do in you," and the new is righteousness God writes on the heart.** Now Scripture gives us the war story that shows this engine in motion. Canaan is the picture; the verses above are the proof.

## The Engine Proved: Righteousness Received, Not Achieved

**Two covenants is the frame; righteousness by faith is the engine inside it. Again — plain didactic texts first.**

- **Righteousness is counted to faith, not earned by works — Romans 4:3, 21**
  - "For what saith the scripture? Abraham believed God, and it was counted unto him for righteousness" (Rom 4:3)
  - "And being fully persuaded that, what he had promised, he was able also to perform" (Rom 4:21)
  - "Even as Abraham believed God, and it was accounted to him for righteousness" (Gal 3:6)
  - [→] Abraham's righteousness was not manufactured; it was _counted_. And the faith that received it was specifically confidence in God's power "to perform" — the new-covenant posture exactly: not "I am able," but "He is able."

- **Justification by faith yields peace — the standing the whole walk rests on — Romans 5:1**
  - "Therefore being justified by faith, we have peace with God through our Lord Jesus Christ" (Rom 5:1)
  - [→] The conquest begins from a position already secured. You do not fight your way _into_ God's favor; you fight _from_ it. Justified, at peace, accepted — then sent in to possess.

- **The same Abraham proves the danger of "helping" God — the Ishmael instinct — Galatians 4:23, 29**
  - "But he who was of the bondwoman was born after the flesh; but he of the freewoman was by promise" (Gal 4:23)
  - Sarah "helped": "go in unto my maid; it may be that I may obtain children by her" (Gen 16:2) — God's promise, attempted by human flesh, produced Ishmael.
  - "But as then he that was born after the flesh persecuted him that was born after the Spirit, even so it is now" (Gal 4:29)
  - [→] Helping God always produces an Ishmael. The flesh cannot manufacture the promise; it can only counterfeit it — and then the counterfeit persecutes the genuine. Old-covenant religion is forever at war with new-covenant grace.

[Q] **"The old came first and the new replaced it, right? Law then grace?"**

> The opposite. Paul's whole argument in Galatians 3 is that the _promise_ (new covenant) came to Abraham **four hundred and thirty years before Sinai** (the old-covenant transaction). The new covenant is the _original_ — God's promise in Eden, "I will put enmity... it shall bruise thy head" (Gen 3:15) — _I_ will. The old-covenant _experience_ ("we will do," Ex 19:8) is the human detour, not the foundation. The new covenant is older than the old.

[→] So the two oldest scenes on earth already stage the two covenants — and the next section reads them as Scripture's own first illustration before we ever reach Canaan.

## Two Covenants, Pictured at Eden: Fig Leaves, Coats of Skins, Two Brothers

**Now the pictures begin — but they only confirm what the verses above already proved. Genesis stages the two covenants twice before there is a single nation, a single law-giving, or a single battle.**

- **Fig leaves vs. God's coats of skins — Genesis 3:7, 21**
  - "And... they sewed fig leaves together, and made themselves aprons" (Gen 3:7) — **"we will do."** Human effort, self-covering, the old-covenant instinct born the same hour as sin.
  - "Unto Adam also and to his wife did the LORD God make coats of skins, and clothed them" (Gen 3:21) — **"God will do."** Divine provision, blood shed, a covering they did not make and could not make.
  - [→] The fig leaves came first in human experience; the coats of skins came first in God's plan (Gen 3:15 preceded the fall's remedy). The old covenant is humanity's reflex; the new covenant is God's initiative — and only His covering held.

- **Cain and Abel — the two covenants in two brothers — Genesis 4:3-5**
  - "Cain brought of the fruit of the ground an offering unto the LORD" (Gen 4:3) — the work of his own hands, no blood, no death: _I_ will provide.
  - "And Abel, he also brought of the firstlings of his flock and of the fat thereof. And the LORD had respect unto Abel and to his offering" (Gen 4:4) — God's prescribed offering, blood, faith: _God_ will provide.
  - "But unto Cain and to his offering he had not respect" (Gen 4:5)
  - [→] Same God, opposite covenants. One trusted God's method; the other substituted his own and was offended when his substitute was refused. Hebrews names the difference: "By faith Abel offered" — Abel's whole difference was faith, the new-covenant engine.

[STR] Mark the pattern that now runs through all of Scripture: **self-made covering vs. God-given covering; bloodless offering vs. blood offering; the flesh's child vs. the promise's child.** This is not numerology read into the stories — it is the two covenants Paul named in Galatians 4:24, showing up everywhere the Bible tells the human story. The doctrine came first; these are its illustrations.

## Canaan as the Map: Why the Conquest Is the God-Given Framework

**Here is the question the rest of the study answers in pictures: how does the new covenant actually proceed in a real life over real years? Scripture does not leave us guessing — it expressly hands us the Exodus-and-conquest as the framework. This is text-authorized typology, not a spiritualizing overlay.**

- **The Exodus story is given to us as our pattern — 1 Corinthians 10:11**
  - "Now all these things happened unto them for ensamples: and they are written for our admonition, upon whom the ends of the world are come" (1 Cor 10:11)
  - [→] Paul does not allow us to treat Israel's wilderness journey as mere ancient history. He says it was _written for us_ — an "ensample" (Greek _tupos_, a type). The map is authorized by the text itself.

- **Canaan is named a type of "the rest that remains" — Hebrews 3:14 – 4:11**
  - "For we are made partakers of Christ, if we hold the beginning of our confidence stedfast unto the end" (Heb 3:14)
  - "And to whom sware he that they should not enter into his rest, but to them that believed not? So we see that they could not enter in because of unbelief" (Heb 3:18-19)
  - "There remaineth therefore a rest to the people of God. For he that is entered into his rest, he also hath ceased from his own works, as God did from his" (Heb 4:9-10)
  - "Let us labour therefore to enter into that rest, lest any man fall after the same example of unbelief" (Heb 4:11)
  - [STR] Hebrews makes the connection for us: Canaan was _rest_; the believer's Canaan is _God's rest_ — "he that is entered into his rest... hath ceased from his own works." That is the new covenant exactly: ceasing from self-powered works to rest in God's performance. And the one thing that barred Israel from the land is named: **unbelief** — the failure of the new-covenant engine, faith.

- **Egypt → Exodus → Canaan maps the salvation we have traced**
  - Egypt = bondage to sin; the Exodus = deliverance by God's power alone (the whole series, Babylon left behind in Study 23); Canaan = the character God promised to give, possessed by faith.
  - "Understand therefore, that the LORD thy God giveth thee not this good land to possess it for thy righteousness; for thou art a stiffnecked people" (Deut 9:6) — the land was never earned. New-covenant logic stamped on the deed.
  - "The LORD thy God, he will go over before thee, and he will destroy these nations from before thee, and thou shalt possess them" (Deut 31:3) — **God** conquers; Israel's role is to march, trust, and possess what He conquers.
  - [→] This is sanctification's shape: God goes before and destroys the enemy; you possess the ground. He works in; you work out (Phil 2:13). The conquest is the new covenant told as a campaign.

[→] So the framework is licensed by Scripture, and it pictures a doctrine Scripture has already proved in plain words. Now we read the campaign — and at every turn the engine underneath is the verses above, not the story alone.

## The Spy Report: Faith vs. Unbelief (Numbers 13-14)

**The first battle is fought before a single sword is drawn — in the reconnaissance report. Twelve men saw the same land, the same giants. The difference was not data; it was the new-covenant engine: faith.**

- **Two reports, one reality**
  - all twelve spies saw the same land and the same giants (Num 13)
  - **ten spies — unbelief:** "We be not able to go up against the people; for they are stronger than we" (Num 13:31); "and there we saw the giants... and we were in our own sight as grasshoppers" (Num 13:33) — they magnified the problem and minimized God.
  - **two spies — faith:** "Let us go up at once, and possess it; for we are well able to overcome it" (Num 13:30); "If the LORD delight in us, then he will bring us into this land" (Num 14:8) — same giants, different focus.

- **"We be not able" is the old covenant talking**
  - [→] Hear the covenants in the two reports. The ten say "_we_ be not able" — and they are exactly right, on old-covenant terms; the flesh truly _cannot_ take the land. Caleb says "_the LORD_... he will bring us in" — new-covenant terms; God is well able. **Both reports are true to their own covenant.** This is why Hebrews named _unbelief_ as the thing that barred them (Heb 3:19): they tried the promise by the flesh, found it impossible, and called God's promise a lie.

[ILL] Two men look through the same prison bars. One sees mud; the other sees stars. The giants were real, the walls were high — but God was bigger than all of it. What you fix your eyes on determines what you believe is possible.

- **the giants in your character are real**
  - anger — real. lust — real. pride — real. fear — real.
  - but the question is never "Are the giants real?" It is "Is God able?" — and Romans 4:21 already answered: He is "able also to perform."
  - [→] The majority report is often the unbelief report. Faithfulness may be unpopular. The ten spies were preaching Satan's old charge — _the law cannot be kept, the giants cannot be conquered_ — as a reconnaissance briefing. Caleb refuted it with faith.

[→] If God is able and the land is His to give, why does He not change us instantly? Why a forty-year campaign instead of a single afternoon?

## The "Little by Little" Principle — Progressive, Not Instantaneous

**Doctrine first: sanctification is progressive and Spirit-wrought. Then the conquest shows us why.**

- **The plain doctrine: change is by beholding, gradually, by the Spirit — 2 Corinthians 3:18**
  - "But we all, with open face beholding as in a glass the glory of the Lord, are changed into the same image from glory to glory, even as by the Spirit of the Lord" (2 Cor 3:18)
  - [→] "From glory to glory" — stage upon stage, not all at once. "By the Spirit" — His work, not your strain. This is the engine; the conquest is the picture of it.
  - "But the path of the just is as the shining light, that shineth more and more unto the perfect day" (Prov 4:18) — more and more, not all at once.

- **The conquest shows the same method — Deuteronomy 7:22**
  - "And the LORD thy God will put out those nations before thee **by little and little**: thou mayest not consume them at once, lest the beasts of the field increase upon thee" (Deut 7:22)
  - "By little and little I will drive them out from before thee, until thou be increased, and inherit the land" (Ex 23:30)
  - [→] God Himself explains _why_ gradual: "lest the beasts of the field increase upon thee." Ground taken faster than it can be occupied turns wild. Each victory builds the strength — "until thou be increased" — for the next. Sanctification is the same: God removes enemies at the pace your growth can hold.

[DYK🔎] "By little and little" (Hebrew _me'at me'at_) appears precisely in the conquest promises (Ex 23:30; Deut 7:22), tied to God's deliberate method of giving the land. Progressive, intentional, paced. The same God who would not give Canaan in a day will not hand you a finished character in a day — and for the same loving reason.

- **The danger the slow conquest guards against — the empty swept house — Matthew 12:43-45**
  - "When the unclean spirit is gone out of a man, he walketh through dry places, seeking rest, and findeth none" (Matt 12:43)
  - he returns and finds the house "empty, swept, and garnished" (Matt 12:44)
  - "Then goeth he, and taketh with himself seven other spirits more wicked than himself... and the last state of that man is worse than the first" (Matt 12:45)
  - [→] The house was _cleaned_ but never _filled_ — and that is exactly the peril of leaving Babylon (Study 23) without entering Canaan. You came out — but what moved in? A swept-out life with no Christ filling it is a vacancy sign for the enemy. This is why God drives the nations out _by little and little_: vacant ground must be occupied as fast as it is cleared.

- **What fills the house? Christ, by His Word taken in — John 4:14; Psalm 1:2-3**
  - the unclean spirit wanders "dry places, seeking rest"; the blessed man is "planted by the rivers of water" (Ps 1:3) — and the difference is that he "meditate[s]" on God's law "day and night" (Ps 1:2).
  - "Thy words were found, and I did eat them; and thy word was unto me the joy and rejoicing of mine heart" (Jer 15:16) — the Word taken in, not merely heard.
  - "But whosoever drinketh of the water that I shall give him shall never thirst; but the water that I shall give him shall be in him a well of water springing up into everlasting life" (John 4:14) — Christ implants an internal, living fountain. Not external information; indwelling transformation. This is "Christ liveth in me" (Gal 2:20) — the house filled.

[→] If the war is gradual and the cleared ground must be occupied by Christ Himself, how do we actually fight from day to day?

## The Warfare Pattern: Joshua's Captain (Joshua 5:13-15)

**The conquest framework now shows us _how to fight_ — and here too the doctrine underneath is plain: we abide, we receive orders, we wield the one offensive weapon God forged. The fight is real; the power is His.**

- **The Commander appears — not "for us," but above us**
  - "When Joshua was by Jericho... behold, there stood a man over against him with his sword drawn in his hand" (Josh 5:13)
  - Joshua asked: "Art thou for us, or for our adversaries?"
  - "And he said, Nay; but as captain of the host of the LORD am I now come" (Josh 5:14)
  - [→] The answer corrects the question. Christ does not enlist in _our_ campaign; we enlist in _His_. "Nay" — He is not "for us" as a hired ally; He is the Captain, and we fall in behind. The land is taken on His orders, by His sword, for His glory.

- **Surrender, then orders**
  - "And Joshua fell on his face to the earth, and did worship, and said unto him, What saith my lord unto his servant?" (Josh 5:14)
  - "Loose thy shoe from off thy foot; for the place whereon thou standest is holy" (Josh 5:15)
  - [→] The right response to the Commander is the new covenant in posture: worship first ("not I, but Christ"), then ask for orders ("what saith my lord"). Joshua withdrew from the army _before_ meeting the Captain — **the war room comes before the battlefield.**

- **How we fight, grounded in doctrine: abide and believe — John 15:4-5; Romans 10:17**
  - "Abide in me, and I in you. As the branch cannot bear fruit of itself, except it abide in the vine; no more can ye, except ye abide in me" (John 15:4)
  - "I am the vine, ye are the branches: He that abideth in me, and I in him, the same bringeth forth much fruit: for without me ye can do nothing" (John 15:5)
  - "So then faith cometh by hearing, and hearing by the word of God" (Rom 10:17)
  - [→] This is the engine of _every_ tactic that follows — prayer, self-examination, the armor. None of it is self-improvement. It is the branch abiding in the Vine and faith fed by the Word. "Without me ye can do nothing" is the old-covenant verdict on self-effort; "abide in me... the same bringeth forth much fruit" is the new.

### Prayer as the War Room

- **daily convening with the Commander** — "Evening, and morning, and at noon, will I pray, and cry aloud: and he shall hear my voice" (Ps 55:17); "My voice shalt thou hear in the morning, O LORD; in the morning will I direct my prayer unto thee, and will look up" (Ps 5:3). Not ritual — reconnaissance and the receiving of orders.
- **reveal my blind spots** — "Search me, O God, and know my heart: try me, and know my thoughts: and see if there be any wicked way in me" (Ps 139:23-24)
- **show me my weak points** — "Who can understand his errors? cleanse thou me from secret faults" (Ps 19:12)
- **give me today's orders** — "Cause me to know the way wherein I should walk; for I lift up my soul unto thee" (Ps 143:8)

### Mapping the Terrain: Self-Examination

- **know your specific sins** — not vague "I'm a sinner," but specific: impatience at a particular hour, pride under correction, a recurring lustful thought. Generals do not fight with vague objectives.
- **identify patterns and triggers** — when do you fall (time, fatigue, mood)? where (place, company)? what precedes it? "Let us search and try our ways, and turn again to the LORD" (Lam 3:40).
- [→] This is not morbid introspection but the new heart cooperating: God reveals (Ps 139:23), you map, He drives the nation out "by little and little."

### The Whole Armor of God (Ephesians 6:10-17)

- "Put on the whole armour of God, that ye may be able to stand against the wiles of the devil" (Eph 6:11)
- "For we wrestle not against flesh and blood, but against principalities, against powers, against the rulers of the darkness of this world, against spiritual wickedness in high places" (Eph 6:12)
- The armor — loins girt with truth, the breastplate of righteousness, feet shod with the gospel of peace, the shield of faith, the helmet of salvation, the sword of the Spirit (Eph 6:14-17). **Every piece is something God supplies; you put it on, He forged it** — the new covenant down to the last buckle.
- Only one piece is offensive: "the sword of the Spirit, which is the word of God" (Eph 6:17). Christ defeated Satan in the wilderness with "It is written" three times and nothing else (Matt 4:4, 7, 10). The sword you hold in Canaan is the same sword — wielded by faith that "cometh by hearing... the word of God" (Rom 10:17).

[→] We know how to fight and with what. But what are we fighting _toward_? What is the target the new covenant is forming?

## The Standard: Christ's Own Character

**The doctrine names the target without ambiguity, and it is a Person, not an abstraction.**

- **The standard is Christ Himself — 1 John 2:6**
  - "He that saith he abideth in him ought himself also so to walk, even as he walked" (1 John 2:6)
  - [→] Not "try to be good" — _walk as He walked._ The standard the new covenant writes on the heart is the very character of Christ (Study 3: the image restored). The conquest is not toward a vague better self; it is toward His likeness.

- **Made concrete in His life**
  - **how He spoke** — "Never man spake like this man" (John 7:46); "A soft answer turneth away wrath" (Prov 15:1).
  - **how He took criticism** — "Who, when he was reviled, reviled not again; when he suffered, he threatened not; but committed himself to him that judgeth righteously" (1 Pet 2:23).
  - **how He treated sinners** — "Neither do I condemn thee: go, and sin no more" (John 8:11). Firm on sin, gentle with the sinner — truth and grace together.

- **The fruit standard — what the Spirit grows, not what the flesh achieves — Galatians 5:22-25**
  - "But the fruit of the Spirit is love, joy, peace, longsuffering, gentleness, goodness, faith, meekness, temperance: against such there is no law" (Gal 5:22-23)
  - "And they that are Christ's have crucified the flesh with the affections and lusts. If we live in the Spirit, let us also walk in the Spirit" (Gal 5:24-25)
  - [STR] Mark the word: **fruit**, not _works_ or _achievements_. A branch does not manufacture grapes by effort; it bears them by abiding (John 15:5). The nine graces are the new covenant made visible — the character of Christ grown in you "by the Spirit," exactly as 2 Cor 3:18 promised. Self-examine by _this_ list: is love growing? joy independent of circumstance? temperance strengthening? That is the report card of the conquest.
  - "Walk in the Spirit, and ye shall not fulfil the lust of the flesh. For the flesh lusteth against the Spirit, and the Spirit against the flesh" (Gal 5:16-17) — the giants are the flesh; the conquest is the Spirit dispossessing them, by little and little.

[→] We know the standard and the engine. Now the honest question the new covenant invites: is this actually happening?

## The Proof Test: Are You in the Fight?

**This is not condemnation — it is the invitation to honesty the new covenant makes safe, because God examines us not to crush but to heal what we are willing to see.**

- **The command — Romans-grade audit, not gentle reflection — 2 Corinthians 13:5**
  - "Examine yourselves, whether ye be in the faith; prove your own selves. Know ye not your own selves, how that Jesus Christ is in you, except ye be reprobates?" (2 Cor 13:5)
  - "prove" = test, put to trial. The test is not "Are you trying hard?" but "Is Christ _in_ you?" — the new covenant indwelling (Gal 2:20).

- **The logic that makes the audit hopeful, not crushing**
  - God promised the work: "A new heart also will I give you... and I will put my spirit within you, and cause you to walk in my statutes" (Eze 36:26-27).
  - God cannot fail: "God is not a man, that he should lie... hath he said, and shall he not do it?" (Num 23:19).
  - so if transformation is _not_ happening, the conclusion is never "God's promise failed" — it is "find the blockage." Is the house being filled (Matt 12:44), or only swept? Look at the **fruit**: "Even so faith, if it hath not works, is dead, being alone" (James 2:17); "by their fruits ye shall know them" (Matt 7:20).

- **The Laodicean danger — the giant you cannot see — Revelation 3:17-20**
  - "Because thou sayest, I am rich, and increased with goods, and have need of nothing; and knowest not that thou art wretched, and miserable, and poor, and blind, and naked" (Rev 3:17)
  - "I counsel thee to buy of me gold tried in the fire... and anoint thine eyes with eyesalve, that thou mayest see" (Rev 3:18)
  - the deadliest enemy is not the giant in plain sight but the self-deception that says "I have need of nothing." Yet even here the new covenant holds: "Behold, I stand at the door, and knock: if any man hear my voice, and open the door, I will come in to him" (Rev 3:20).
  - [→] He does not break in. He knocks. The conquest is His to win, but He will never override your will — and that is the whole of your part: open the door, take up the sword, show up to the war room. Are you still sewing fig leaves, or are you in Canaan with sword drawn?

## The Synthesis: Surrendered Warfare

- **not passive, not self-effort — the third thing**
  - passivity says: "God will do it; I'll wait."
  - self-effort says: "I must do it; God will help."
  - the new covenant says: "God will do it through me as I cooperate" — "work out... for it is God which worketh in you" (Phil 2:12-13).

- **the sword is in your hand; the victory is His**
  - "Fight the good fight of faith" (1 Tim 6:12) — you must fight.
  - "Thanks be to God, which giveth us the victory through our Lord Jesus Christ" (1 Cor 15:57) — He gives the victory.
  - "I can do all things through Christ which strengtheneth me" (Phil 4:13) — the strength is borrowed, the doing is real.
  - you cannot win without Him; He will not win without your consent. That is surrendered warfare.

[TANGENT] The daily burnt offering at the sanctuary — morning and evening — pictured this continual consecration, and the fire never went out: "The fire shall ever be burning upon the altar; it shall never go out" (Lev 6:13). Continuous surrender, continuous supply. The Spirit's fire on the new-covenant heart burns by the same rule — fed every morning and evening, never let to die.

---

## Appeal

- There are only two covenants, and you live under one of them today. The old says "_I_ will do it" and builds fig leaves and golden calves. The new says "_God_ will do it in me" and writes His law on the heart. Which is your religion?
- Daily surrender is the most _active_ thing you will ever do. It costs attention, intention, discipline. It is not drifting — it is deliberate. But the power is never yours: "for it is God which worketh in you" (Phil 2:13).
- The Commander is faithful: "God is faithful, who will not suffer you to be tempted above that ye are able; but will with the temptation also make a way to escape" (1 Cor 10:13). He has never lost a battle. He has never broken a promise — "hath he said, and shall he not do it?" (Num 23:19).
- Every giant conquered in your character is evidence entered in heaven's courtroom. Charge B said the law cannot be kept; every habit broken, every appetite surrendered, every sin forsaken — by His power, not yours — refutes it. Charge A said they only obey for reward; but the conquest costs everything and promises nothing in this life but God Himself, so those who conquer Canaan do it for love.
- What is your Canaan? What are your giants? Name them. Map them. Bring them to the Commander.
- The question is not "Can God conquer?" — He is "able also to perform" (Rom 4:21). The question is: will you fall on your face, take up the sword, and show up to the war room tomorrow morning?

**Central Verse:** "But this shall be the covenant that I will make with the house of Israel; After those days, saith the LORD, I will put my law in their inward parts, and write it in their hearts; and will be their God, and they shall be my people." (Jeremiah 31:33)

**[→ Study 27]** The new covenant writes the law on a living heart — but the conquest only matters to the living. One enemy remains that no sword of self-effort can touch: death, and the lie that the dead are not really dead. The same serpent who whispered "we will do" at Eden also whispered "ye shall not surely die" — and that second lie becomes Satan's masterpiece in the final crisis. Study 27 arms the traveler with the truth about the state of the dead, the one bulwark against the last great deception.
