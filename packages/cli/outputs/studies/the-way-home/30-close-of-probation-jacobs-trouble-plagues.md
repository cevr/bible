# Study 30: The Close of Probation, Jacob's Trouble, and the Seven Last Plagues

**There comes an hour when the investigative judgment ends, the great High Priest rises from His seat, and the door of mercy shuts — not because God grew weary of pleading, but because every case has been decided. Then the seven last plagues fall, this time unmingled with mercy, on those who chose the mark; and God's sealed people pass through the time of Jacob's trouble — sheltered, though not exempted, their verdict already in. This is the darkest hour earth has ever known. And it is the hour the whole cosmic trial has been building toward.**

## Michael Stands Up — Probation Closes

**The first thing to settle is WHEN probation closes and WHAT closes it — and Scripture marks the exact moment with a single act: a Prince who has been seated rises to His feet.**

- **"At that time shall Michael stand up"**
  - "And at that time shall Michael stand up, the great prince which standeth for the children of thy people: and there shall be a time of trouble, such as never was since there was a nation even to that same time: and at that time thy people shall be delivered, every one that shall be found written in the book" (Dan 12:1)
  - Michael — "who is like God" — is the prince who "standeth FOR" His people: He stands in their place, pleading. Scripture identifies Him: the archangel whose voice raises the dead is Christ's own (1 Thess 4:16; John 5:28-29), and that archangel is named Michael (Jude 9). The One who _standeth for_ the saints is Christ their Mediator.
  - [STR] Note the verb change. Michael has been _standing for_ His people — interceding. Now He stands _up_. He rises from the work of mediation. The sitting High Priest of Hebrews 8:1 — "set on the right hand of the throne" — gets up. The case is closed.

- **Why this is the close of probation, proved from the text itself**
  - The same verse that says Michael stands up says "thy people shall be delivered, every one that shall be found written in the book." The book is already written. The names are already in. No case is still pending — deliverance now simply _executes_ a verdict already reached.
  - The Day of Atonement type fixes the same moment: when the high priest finished in the Most Holy Place and came out, the work of cleansing was done for that year — atonement made, the camp's cases settled (Lev 16:30,33). Christ our High Priest (Heb 8:1-2) finishing His Most Holy Place ministry is Michael standing up.
  - [→] Probation does not close on God's whim. It closes when the investigative judgment — opened in Daniel 7:9-10, dated to 1844 in Studies 14 and 18 — reaches its last case and renders its last verdict. The court rises because the court is finished.

- **The decree that fixes every destiny forever**
  - "He that is unjust, let him be unjust still: and he which is filthy, let him be filthy still: and he that is righteous, let him be righteous still: and he that is holy, let him be holy still" (Rev 22:11)
  - Four sentences, each ending in _still_. Character is frozen as it stands. No more changing sides. No more pleading. The unjust will not become just, and — wonderfully — the righteous will not fall away. The record is sealed.
  - [→] This is not God arbitrarily slamming a door on people still reaching for Him. He shuts it because there is no one left reaching who has not been answered. Every "filthy" one has refused the cleansing; every "holy" one has received it. Probation closes on the far side of the last free choice, not before it.

- **The proof that mercy's work has ceased — the temple no one can enter**
  - "And I saw another sign in heaven, great and marvellous, seven angels having the seven last plagues; for in them is filled up the wrath of God" (Rev 15:1)
  - "And after that I looked, and, behold, the temple of the tabernacle of the testimony in heaven was opened... And the temple was filled with smoke from the glory of God, and from his power; and no man was able to enter into the temple, till the seven plagues of the seven angels were fulfilled" (Rev 15:5,8)
  - Here is the literal, textual proof that intercession has ended: _no man was able to enter into the temple._ The sanctuary where the High Priest pleaded the blood is now closed to all comers, "till the seven plagues... were fulfilled." With the temple shut, there is no mediation going on inside it. The smoke of God's unveiled glory fills the place no sinner can stand before.
  - [STR] Lay the three texts side by side and the doctrine stands on Scripture alone: **Michael stands up** (Dan 12:1) → **every character is fixed** (Rev 22:11) → **the temple is shut and the plagues begin** (Rev 15:8; 16:1). Probation has closed. You can verify it with an open Bible and never need a single human authority.

[Q] **"Standing without a mediator — doesn't that mean God's people are abandoned, left to face the end on their own merit?"**
→ No. Read the texts carefully and the fear dissolves. Michael ceases the _work of intercession_ (Dan 12:1, He stands up) because that work is _finished_ — the cases are decided, the people are sealed (Rev 7:3). He does not cease to be their Savior, their Friend, or their coming King. Hebrews says He "ever liveth to make intercession" (Heb 7:25) — He lives on; what ends is the courtroom phase, not His love. A surgeon who finishes the operation steps back from the table — not because he abandoned the patient, but because the surgery succeeded. The saints do not then stand on their own merit; they stand because the righteousness of Christ, no longer merely imputed from outside, has by the Spirit been _reproduced within_ them — the law written in the inward parts (Jer 31:33). The Mediator's work is _complete_, not _absent_. This is the plain force of Daniel 12:1 and Revelation 22:11; the doctrine needs no other support than these.

> A devotional echo, _after_ the Bible has settled it: those who live through this hour, Ellen White wrote, "are to stand in the sight of a holy God without a mediator" (GC 425) — and elsewhere, that "when the character of Christ shall be perfectly reproduced in His people, then He will come" (COL 69.1). These confirm; they do not prove. The proof is Daniel 12:1 + Revelation 22:11 + Revelation 15:8, and any reader can check it.

## The Seven Last Plagues — Wrath Without Mercy Mingled

**With the temple shut and probation closed, the seven last plagues fall. The decisive point is established straight from the text: they are LITERAL, they come AFTER probation, and they target the mark-bearers — not the sealed.**

- **"Filled up the wrath of God" — judgment with no mercy left in it**
  - "Seven angels having the seven last plagues; for in them is filled up the wrath of God" (Rev 15:1)
  - All through history God's judgments have been _tempered_ — "in wrath remember mercy" (Hab 3:2). These are called the _last_ plagues, in which His wrath is "filled up" — full, undiluted, complete. They come precisely _because_ mercy's work is finished (Rev 15:8). The temple is shut; no intercession stands between the impenitent and the consequences they chose.
  - [→] The reason the plagues are unmingled is the reason the temple is shut. Once "no man was able to enter" the place of mediation (Rev 15:8), there is no more mercy mixed into the cup. Justice alone remains.

- **The plagues fall on those who have the mark — this is the sharp, literal point**
  - "And the first went, and poured out his vial upon the earth; and there fell a noisome and grievous sore upon the men which had the mark of the beast, and upon them which worshipped his image" (Rev 16:2)
  - The very first vial names its target with precision: "the men which had the mark of the beast, and... them which worshipped his image." The plagues are not blind catastrophe striking everyone alike. They are _targeted_ judgment on a world that, after the loud cry and the three angels' warning, deliberately took the counterfeit (the mark, established in Studies 21 and 28).
  - The men struck by the fourth and fifth vials "blasphemed the name of God... and repented not" (Rev 16:9,11) — proof that probation is closed: under the plagues no one repents, because the day of repentance is over. The plagues reveal a settled, unchangeable rejection, exactly as Revelation 22:11 fixed it.

- **The first five vials — read the text plainly**
  - "And the second angel poured out his vial upon the sea; and it became as the blood of a dead man: and every living soul died in the sea" (Rev 16:3)
  - "And the third angel poured out his vial upon the rivers and fountains of waters; and they became blood" (Rev 16:4)
  - "And the fourth angel poured out his vial upon the sun; and power was given unto him to scorch men with fire" (Rev 16:8)
  - "And the fifth angel poured out his vial upon the seat of the beast; and his kingdom was full of darkness; and they gnawed their tongues for pain" (Rev 16:10)
  - Sores, blood, scorching heat, darkness on the seat of the beast — judgments answering "Even so, Lord God Almighty, true and righteous are thy judgments" (Rev 16:7). They are deserved: "they have shed the blood of saints and prophets, and thou hast given them blood to drink; for they are worthy" (Rev 16:6).
  - [→] We stop the plague-account here at the fifth vial. The sixth vial dries the "Euphrates" and gathers the world to Armageddon (Rev 16:12-16), and the seventh brings the voice that says "It is done" — but those belong to the _deliverance_, and they wait for Study 31. This study stands in the hour after the temple is shut and before the King appears.

[DYK🔎] These are deliberately the antitype of Egypt's plagues. Several of the seven last plagues echo the plagues of Exodus almost item for item — sores (Ex 9:9-11), waters to blood (Ex 7:20), darkness (Ex 10:22), hail (Ex 9:23). Paul tells us why the parallel is no accident: "Now all these things happened unto them for ensamples... written for our admonition, upon whom the ends of the world are come" (1 Cor 10:11). Egypt was the rehearsal; the final crisis is the performance. And one detail from Egypt is the heart of the next point.

## Goshen Spared — Sheltered, Not Exempted

**The same Bible that says the plagues fall on the mark-bearers shows what becomes of God's people IN that hour. The Exodus type, which Scripture itself authorizes as an "ensample," answers exactly.**

- **God put a division between His people and the world's**
  - "And I will sever in that day the land of Goshen, in which my people dwell, that no swarms of flies shall be there... And I will put a division between my people and thy people" (Ex 8:22-23)
  - "And the LORD shall sever between the cattle of Israel and the cattle of Egypt... and all the cattle of Egypt died: but of the cattle of the children of Israel died not one" (Ex 9:4,6)
  - As the plagues fell on Egypt, Goshen was untouched. The hail beat down Egypt's fields; in Goshen there was no hail (Ex 9:26). God drew a literal line, and the judgments respected it. This is the "ensample" (1 Cor 10:11) of the final plagues: they fall on the mark-bearers (Rev 16:2) and do not touch the sealed.

- **The promise of the secret place — Psalm 91**
  - "He that dwelleth in the secret place of the most High shall abide under the shadow of the Almighty" (Ps 91:1)
  - "Surely he shall deliver thee from the snare of the fowler, and from the noisome pestilence" (Ps 91:3)
  - "A thousand shall fall at thy side, and ten thousand at thy right hand; but it shall not come nigh thee" (Ps 91:7)
  - "There shall no evil befall thee, neither shall any plague come nigh thy dwelling. For he shall give his angels charge over thee, to keep thee in all thy ways" (Ps 91:10-11)
  - Notice the exact word: "neither shall any _plague_ come nigh thy dwelling." The psalm is tailored to this very hour. It is the Goshen promise put to music. The vials of Revelation 16 are aimed at the mark; Psalm 91 is the shield over those who dwell with God.

- **The condition is DWELLING — built before the crisis, not found in it**
  - "He that _dwelleth_ in the secret place" — not he who visits, not he who runs there when the sky turns black. _Dwell._ Abide. Live there. The secret place is a relationship built in the ordinary days so that it holds in the extraordinary one.
  - [→] You cannot move into the shelter during the storm. The ones sheltered when the plagues fall are the ones who were already living in the secret place when the sun was still shining. "Daniel purposed in his heart" (Dan 1:8) before the test came; the three Hebrews knew their answer before the furnace (Dan 3:18). The dwelling is decided now.

- **Sheltered — but not exempted from the trouble**
  - Here is the careful distinction the text demands. Psalm 91 promises _no plague_ will touch the sealed — and that promise holds literally. But it does not promise a life without anguish. Daniel 12:1 says "thy people shall be _delivered_" — delivered _out of_ the trouble, which means they go _through_ it, not _around_ it. Jacob wrestled all night and walked away with the deliverance _and_ a limp (Gen 32:28,31).
  - "I will be with him in trouble" (Ps 91:15) — not "I will keep him out of trouble," but "I will be _in it with him_." The shelter is not the removal of the storm; it is the presence of the One who holds you through it.
  - [→] So two things are true at once, and the text holds both: the plagues themselves — the literal sores, blood, darkness — do not fall on the sealed (Rev 16:2; Ps 91:10). But the _time of trouble_ — the death decree, the terror, the wrestling of soul — they pass straight through. That wrestling has a name, and a story.

[ILL] A child caught in a thunderstorm does not stop hearing the thunder when his father picks him up. The lightning still cracks; the rain still drives. What changes is not the storm — it is the grip. The fear drains away because the arms are real. Psalm 91 is not the promise of a sky without thunder. It is the promise of the Father's grip in the storm.

## The Time of Jacob's Trouble — The Last Assault of the Accuser

**The plagues are the world's portion. The saints have their own ordeal, and Scripture gives it a precise name and a precise pattern. This is not a vague "hard time"; the Bible labels it after one specific night in one man's life.**

- **The Bible NAMES it — "the time of Jacob's trouble"**
  - "Alas! for that day is great, so that none is like it: it is even the time of Jacob's trouble; but he shall be saved out of it" (Jer 30:7)
  - Jeremiah does not say "a trouble _like_ Jacob's." He says it _is_ "the time of Jacob's trouble." The prophet himself hangs the label. This is text-authorized typology, not an interpreter's overlay — Scripture takes one night in Genesis 32 and tells us the last crisis will wear its shape. And note the same promise as Daniel 12:1: "he shall be _saved out of it_" — through, not around.

- **The story Jeremiah is pointing to — Genesis 32**
  - Jacob's whole crisis grows out of his past sin. Years before, he had stolen the birthright and the blessing by deception — Esau's own bitter words: "Is not he rightly named Jacob? for he hath supplanted me these two times: he took away my birthright; and, behold, now he hath taken away my blessing" (Gen 27:36). _Jacob_ means supplanter, deceiver. The name was an accusation he carried.
  - Now, returning home, the past comes for him: "he cometh to meet thee, and four hundred men with him. Then Jacob was greatly afraid and distressed" (Gen 32:6-7). He prays in raw terror: "Deliver me, I pray thee, from the hand of my brother... for I fear him, lest he will come and smite me, and the mother with the children" (Gen 32:11).
  - He sends everyone across the brook. He is alone in the dark. And then someone seizes him:
  - "And Jacob was left alone; and there wrestled a man with him until the breaking of the day. And when he saw that he prevailed not against him, he touched the hollow of his thigh; and the hollow of Jacob's thigh was out of joint, as he wrestled with him" (Gen 32:24-25)
  - Wounded — unable to fight by strength — Jacob does the only thing left. He holds on:
  - "I will not let thee go, except thou bless me" (Gen 32:26)
  - "What is thy name? And he said, Jacob" (Gen 32:27) — he confesses who he is. _Supplanter._ He names his own sin to the One who already knows it.
  - "Thy name shall be called no more Jacob, but Israel: for as a prince hast thou power with God and with men, and hast prevailed" (Gen 32:28)
  - "And Jacob called the name of the place Peniel: for I have seen God face to face, and my life is preserved" (Gen 32:30)

- **The pattern applied — what God's people feel in that hour**
  - Their anguish is _not_ doubt about God's existence and _not_ fear that probation might still reverse against them — that question is settled (Rev 22:11). It is the specific terror Jacob felt: that their own past sin disqualifies them; that the accuser is right about them.
  - Satan is "the accuser of our brethren... which accused them before our God day and night" (Rev 12:10). In the time of Jacob's trouble he makes his last argument, dragging every past failure into the light as proof that they were never really God's. Like Jacob, they cannot fight this by strength. They can only hold on: _I will not let thee go._
  - [→] The wrestling is not God testing whether He should save them — Michael already stood up, the book is already written (Dan 12:1). It is Satan's final attempt to make them _feel_ unsaved when in fact they are sealed. The verdict is in; the accuser is contesting an already-decided case. And here is the mercy of it: like Jacob, when they confess their name and refuse to let go, they receive a new one.

- **The Bible's own courtroom picture of this — Joshua the high priest**
  - Scripture gives us this exact scene in vision: "And he shewed me Joshua the high priest standing before the angel of the LORD, and Satan standing at his right hand to resist him" (Zech 3:1)
  - Joshua stands accused, "clothed with filthy garments" (Zech 3:3) — guilty, defenseless, with the accuser pressing his case. And the Lord answers Satan, not Joshua: "The LORD rebuke thee, O Satan... is not this a brand plucked out of the fire?" (Zech 3:2)
  - "Take away the filthy garments from him. And unto him he said, Behold, I have caused thine iniquity to pass from thee, and I will clothe thee with change of raiment" (Zech 3:4)
  - This is the time of Jacob's trouble in one frame: the accused saint, the accusing devil, the filthy garments of a sinful past — and the verdict already rendered in the saint's favor. The accuser is rebuked; the iniquity is _caused to pass_; the filthy garments come off and the clean raiment goes on. The saints do not argue their own case. The Judge has already ruled. They only hold on while the accuser rages.

- **Both of Satan's charges answered — in real time, before the universe**
  - **Charge A: "They only obey for reward"** (Job 1:9-11). By now every reward has been stripped: no buying, no selling (the boycott of the mark), no standing, a death decree hanging over them. Satan said no one serves God once the hedge is removed. And still they trust: "Though he slay me, yet will I trust in him" (Job 13:15). They serve God when there is nothing left to gain — Charge A collapses.
  - **Charge B: "The law cannot be kept"** (the lie of Eden). Through the plagues, under the death decree, with the temple shut and no intercession sounding, they keep the commandments — "Here is the patience of the saints: here are they that keep the commandments of God, and the faith of Jesus" (Rev 14:12). Not by their own strength, but "kept by the power of God" (1 Pet 1:5). The law is kept under the worst conditions earth has ever produced — Charge B collapses.
  - [→] The time of trouble is not bare suffering. It is the final exhibit in the cosmic trial that opened in Study 1. Satan's two charges are not answered by argument; they are answered by a living people who obey when every bribe is gone and keep the law when keeping it could cost their lives. The watching universe sees the verdict demonstrated, not merely declared.

[Q] **"How can sinful people live through that whole time without a Mediator and not fall into sin?"**
→ The same way Jacob prevailed — not by their strength, but by God's keeping. Scripture is explicit: they are "kept by the power of God through faith" (1 Pet 1:5); presented "faultless" by the One "able to keep you from falling" (Jude 24); "in their mouth was found no guile: for they are without fault before the throne of God" (Rev 14:5). This is not sinless perfection achieved by self-effort during the crisis. It is the finished work of sanctification — the law already "written in their hearts" (Jer 31:33) before the door closed — now revealed under pressure. Christ no longer pleads in the temple, but He still _keeps_; what ends is intercession for new transgression, not His sustaining power. They stand the way Jacob stood: crippled, clinging, and kept. "I will not let thee go."

[TANGENT] The time to prepare for the time of trouble is not during it — it is now. "Daniel purposed in his heart" _before_ the king's meat was set before him (Dan 1:8). The three Hebrews settled their answer _before_ the furnace was heated (Dan 3:17-18). Preparation means hiding God's Word so deep in the heart that when the books are closed and the temple shut, the truth is already written within (Jer 31:33); building the habit of trust in small trials so it holds in the great one; and surrendering cherished sins _now_, while the High Priest still ministers and the blood still pleads, rather than clinging to them until the door is shut. The time of trouble does not _build_ character — it _reveals_ it. Whatever is written in the heart when Michael stands up is what stands.

## Appeal

- The hour is coming when Michael stands up, the temple is filled with smoke, and no man can enter. Daniel said so. John saw it. It is not a metaphor — it is the close of probation, and it is real.
- When it comes, the seven last plagues will fall — and they fall on those who chose the mark. The first vial names them by name (Rev 16:2). The line between Goshen and Egypt will be drawn one last time, and it will hold.
- But the same Bible that aims the plagues at the mark-bearers spreads Psalm 91 over the sealed: "neither shall any plague come nigh thy dwelling." Not a wish — a covenant promise to those who _dwell_.
- And it warns honestly: you will not be exempt from the _trouble_. Jacob wrestled all night and limped away delivered. God's people will pass _through_ the darkest hour earth has known — not around it.
- In that night the accuser will throw your whole past in your face and call it proof you were never His. He did it to Joshua the high priest. He will do it to you. And the answer will not be your argument — it will be the Judge's already-rendered verdict: "Take away the filthy garments from him... I have caused thine iniquity to pass from thee."
- So do the one thing Jacob did when he could no longer fight: confess your name, and refuse to let go. "I will not let thee go, except thou bless me."
- The verdict is decided _now_, not then. The character is written _now_, not then. The dwelling place is built _now_, not then. Settle it while the door is open. Hide the Word in your heart while the temple is still unsealed.
- When Michael stands up, what is written in your heart is what will stand. Will it be the truth — already there, already yours?
