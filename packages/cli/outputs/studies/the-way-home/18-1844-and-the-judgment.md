## First, From the Bible: What Sanctuary Is Cleansed?

**The doctrine of 1844 does not rest on any man's authority. It rests on four texts that interpret one another — Daniel 8:14, Leviticus 16, Daniel 7:9-14, and Hebrews 8-9 — exactly the method of Study 0: line upon line, Scripture its own expositor, never broken against itself. We let the Bible say what 1844 means before we let anyone confirm it.**

- **The prophecy fixes an event, not just a date**
  - "And he said unto me, Unto two thousand and three hundred days; then shall the sanctuary be cleansed" (Dan 8:14)
  - Study 14 proved the arithmetic: 2300 years from 457 BC, terminating in 1844. That work is done.
  - But the verse does not merely mark time — it names a deed: "the sanctuary be cleansed." The whole weight of this study is on that one clause.
  - [STR] The text itself ties a DATE to a DEED. Find what cleansing the Bible means, and you have found what happened in 1844.

- **Which sanctuary? The text forces the answer — the heavenly one**
  - The earthly sanctuary was destroyed in AD 70, seventeen centuries before 1844; there was no earthly temple left to cleanse.
  - Hebrews points to the original it copied: "We have such an high priest, who is set on the right hand of the throne of the Majesty in the heavens; A minister of the sanctuary, and of the true tabernacle, which the Lord pitched, and not man" (Heb 8:1-2)
  - The earthly was only "the example and shadow of heavenly things... See, saith he, that thou make all things according to the pattern shewed to thee in the mount" (Heb 8:5)
  - So there are two sanctuaries: a shadow on earth (gone), and "the true tabernacle, which the Lord pitched, and not man" — and it is the heavenly one Daniel 8:14 has in view.
  - [→] The sanctuary to be cleansed in 1844 is not on earth. It is the real one in heaven, where Christ ministers now.

- **Hebrews even tells us the heavenly things themselves need cleansing**
  - "It was therefore necessary that the patterns of things in the heavens should be purified with these; but the heavenly things themselves with better sacrifices than these" (Heb 9:23)
  - The earthly was "purified" by the blood of animals; "the heavenly things themselves" by Christ's "better" blood. Hebrews 9:23 says in plain words what Daniel 8:14 predicted: a cleansing of the heavenly sanctuary.
  - [STR] Daniel predicts it (8:14); Hebrews confirms it is heavenly and necessary (8:1-2; 9:23). Two writers, one doctrine, no man's authority in between.

[Q] **"How can a sanctuary in heaven be 'unclean' or need 'cleansing'?"**
→ Read Leviticus 16 and the answer is plain: the earthly sanctuary became "unclean" not by dirt but by recorded SIN — "because of the uncleanness of the children of Israel, and because of their transgressions in all their sins" (Lev 16:16). Throughout the year confessed sins were, in figure, transferred into the sanctuary; once a year the record was dealt with and the sanctuary "cleansed." The heavenly sanctuary holds the record of confessed sin (the books, below). To cleanse it is to bring every recorded case to its final disposition — the judgment.

## What "Cleansing" Means: Leviticus 16 Is the Day of Atonement

**Scripture defines its own symbols (1 Cor 2:13). The one annual rite the Bible calls a "cleansing" of the sanctuary is the Day of Atonement, Leviticus 16. So the antitype of Daniel 8:14 is the antitypical Day of Atonement. This is typology of the legitimate kind — a literal rite the text itself ordains as the figure of Christ's literal heavenly ministry (Heb 9), not a meaning imported from outside.**

- **The Day of Atonement is the only "cleansing of the sanctuary" in the Law**
  - "For on that day shall the priest make an atonement for you, to cleanse you, that ye may be clean from all your sins before the LORD" (Lev 16:30)
  - "And he shall make an atonement for the holy sanctuary, and he shall make an atonement for the tabernacle of the congregation, and for the altar... and for all the people of the congregation" (Lev 16:33)
  - The very vocabulary of Daniel 8:14 — "the sanctuary be cleansed" — is the vocabulary of Leviticus 16. The Bible defines the term for us.
  - [→] When the Author uses His own word, you do not guess at a hidden meaning — you find where He used it before. He used it here.

- **Once a year, and only once, into the Most Holy Place**
  - "Speak unto Aaron thy brother, that he come not at all times into the holy place within the vail before the mercy seat... that he die not" (Lev 16:2)
  - "But into the second went the high priest alone once every year, not without blood" (Heb 9:7)
  - Every other day the priest ministered in the first apartment — the daily, continual service (Heb 9:6). On ONE day a year he passed the veil into the second apartment, the Most Holy Place.
  - [STR] Two ministries, two apartments, two phases: a DAILY work in the Holy Place, and a YEARLY work in the Most Holy. This is the track laid back in Study 5 — and it is the track Christ walks.

- **Hebrews makes the two apartments Christ's own two phases of ministry**
  - The earthly priest's two-room pattern was "a figure for the time then present" (Heb 9:9), and Christ "by his own blood he entered in once into the holy place, having obtained eternal redemption for us" (Heb 9:12)
  - From His ascension, Christ ministered the antitype of the DAILY — continual intercession in the first apartment: "he ever liveth to make intercession for them" (Heb 7:25)
  - At the end of the 2300 days, He enters the antitype of the YEARLY — the Most Holy Place, the cleansing, the Day of Atonement.
  - [→] 1844 is not Christ BEGINNING to minister. It is Christ MOVING from one phase of an already-running ministry into the second — exactly as Aaron, after a year of daily service, finally passed the veil on the tenth day of the seventh month.

[DYK🔎] The Hebrew word translated "cleansed" in Daniel 8:14 is _tsadaq_ / _nitsdaq_ — to be made right, justified, vindicated. The Day-of-Atonement cleansing of Leviticus 16 is the Hebrew picture behind it. Daniel was not promised that a building would be scrubbed; he was promised that the sanctuary — and all it represents — would at last be set right. That is why this study is about a verdict, not a mop.

## The Day of Atonement Was a Day of JUDGMENT

**Before we ever reach Daniel 7, Leviticus itself tells us the Day of Atonement was a day of judgment for Israel — a verdict day, separating the genuine from the merely professing. The judgment doctrine is born in the Law, not imported into it.**

- **A verdict was rendered: who is truly in covenant, who only appears to be**
  - "For whatsoever soul it be that shall not be afflicted in that same day, he shall be cut off from among his people" (Lev 23:29)
  - It was not optional and not casual. On this one day every Israelite's standing was settled: those who "afflicted their souls" (repented, submitted) remained; those who did not were "cut off."
  - "Ye shall afflict your souls, and do no work at all" (Lev 16:29) — the whole nation came under examination at once.
  - [→] The Day of Atonement was never mere cleansing. It was the day the covenant was tested and a line was drawn through the congregation. That is a judgment.

- **The two goats: the record dealt with, then traced to its author**
  - Two goats stood at the door: one "for the LORD," slain, its blood carried into the Most Holy; one "for the scapegoat" (Azazel), kept alive (Lev 16:7-10).
  - The Lord's goat — its blood cleanses: "by his own blood he entered in once into the holy place, having obtained eternal redemption for us" (Heb 9:12). This is Christ; the atoning blood is His.
  - The live goat — after the cleansing is finished, the accumulated sins are confessed over it and it bears them away: "Aaron shall lay both his hands upon the head of the live goat, and confess over him all the iniquities of the children of Israel... and shall send him away by the hand of a fit man into the wilderness... And the goat shall bear upon him all their iniquities unto a land not inhabited" (Lev 16:21-22)
  - [STR] Mark the ORDER carefully. The scapegoat bears sin away only AFTER the sanctuary is already cleansed by blood: "when he hath made an end of reconciling the holy place... he shall bring the live goat" (Lev 16:20). The scapegoat provides no atonement — it is not slain, its blood cleanses nothing. It receives final accountability for sins already pardoned and removed by the Lord's goat.
  - [→] The Day of Atonement does not end with forgiveness alone. It ends with sin traced back to its origin and responsibility placed where it belongs — on the one who introduced it. The accuser who stood in Job's courtroom is, in the antitype, the one who at last carries the weight of all he authored.

[Q] **"Doesn't the scapegoat (Azazel) being 'sent away bearing sins' make SATAN a sin-bearer like Christ?"**
→ No — and the text guards against exactly that confusion. The Lord's goat is slain and its blood does the atoning (Lev 16:15-19; Heb 9:12). The scapegoat is never slain, its blood never touches the sanctuary, and it is handled only AFTER "he hath made an end of reconciling the holy place" (Lev 16:20). It bears no penalty FOR the people; it bears the ultimate accountability OF the one who instigated the sin. Christ is the sin-bearer who saves; the scapegoat is the originator who is charged. To make Azazel a co-savior is to break the text against Hebrews 9:12; to make him the originator who finally answers for the rebellion fits both Leviticus and the whole cosmic-trial arc.

## Daniel 7 Shows the Same Judgment — and Dates It Before the Second Coming

**Daniel 8:14 said a sanctuary would be cleansed. Daniel 7 — the parallel vision — shows us the scene: a court convened in heaven, books opened, a verdict rendered, and it is placed in the prophetic sequence BEFORE Christ receives His kingdom and returns. This is where the doctrine that the judgment is PRE-ADVENT and INVESTIGATIVE rests — on the order of the verses, not on anyone's say-so.**

- **The court convened — a real heavenly courtroom**
  - "I beheld till the thrones were cast down, and the Ancient of days did sit... his throne was like the fiery flame... A fiery stream issued and came forth from before him: thousand thousands ministered unto him, and ten thousand times ten thousand stood before him: the judgment was set, and the books were opened" (Dan 7:9-10)
  - A throne placed, a Judge seated, an innumerable host attending, books opened, a session convened. Every mark of a court.
  - [→] "The judgment was set, and the books were opened." A judgment that opens BOOKS is investigative by definition — it examines a record. This is the antitypical Day of Atonement seen from the courtroom side.

- **The order of the verses fixes the time: judgment FIRST, then the kingdom**
  - The books are opened in 7:9-10. Only AFTER that, in 7:13-14, does the Son of man receive His kingdom:
  - "I saw in the night visions, and, behold, one like the Son of man came with the clouds of heaven, and came to the Ancient of days... And there was given him dominion, and glory, and a kingdom" (Dan 7:13-14)
  - Note WHERE He comes in verse 13 — "to the Ancient of days," not to the earth. This is a coming UP to the throne to receive the kingdom, not the coming DOWN to earth of the Second Advent. The session of verses 9-10 precedes it.
  - "But the judgment shall sit, and they shall take away his dominion, to consume and to destroy it unto the end" (Dan 7:26) — the court sits, and only then is sentence executed.
  - [STR] The sequence is fixed by the text: court convenes and books open (9-10) → kingdom given to the Son and the saints (13-14, 22, 27) → dominion of the beast destroyed (26). The cases are reviewed BEFORE the kingdom is awarded. That is a pre-advent judgment, proved by the order of Daniel's own words.

- **The New Testament seals the order: the reward is already decided when He comes**
  - "And, behold, I come quickly; and my reward is with me, to give every man according as his work shall be" (Rev 22:12)
  - At the Second Coming Christ brings the reward WITH Him, already apportioned "according as his work shall be." If the reward is in His hand when He arrives, the deciding was done before He left.
  - "And as it is appointed unto men once to die, but after this the judgment" (Heb 9:27); "we are made a spectacle unto the world, and to angels, and to men" (1 Cor 4:9)
  - [→] Reward already determined at His coming (Rev 22:12) + books opened before the kingdom is given (Dan 7:9-14) = a judgment that must finish BEFORE the Advent. The pre-advent investigative judgment is the only reading that lets every text stand together (John 10:35).

[DYK🔎] The Millerites expected Jesus to return to EARTH in 1844 (Dan 7:13). They had the right date and the right text — but read "came to the Ancient of days" as if it were "came to the earth." Daniel 7:13 says the opposite: the Son of man came UP to the Ancient of days to receive the kingdom and open the judgment. The date was exact. The event was a heavenly one, not an earthly one. The disappointment (Study 16) was a misread direction, not a miscalculated number.

## For the Saints, the Verdict Is FOR You — Not Against You

**The same Daniel 7 that opens the books also names the verdict, and it is mercy: the judgment is rendered IN FAVOR of God's people. Read with Romans and John, the believer learns to meet the judgment hour not with terror but with assurance grounded in his Advocate.**

- **The verdict is given TO the saints, FOR the saints**
  - "Until the Ancient of days came, and judgment was given to the saints of the most High; and the time came that the saints possessed the kingdom" (Dan 7:22)
  - The judgment is not a weapon against God's people — it is the verdict that vindicates them and hands them the kingdom. The court that opens against the persecuting horn (7:26) finds FOR the persecuted saints.
  - [→] Satan wants you to dread the judgment. Daniel says the judgment is given TO you. It is where the long accusation is finally overturned in your favor.

- **No condemnation — and that is not the same as no judgment**
  - "There is therefore now no condemnation to them which are in Christ Jesus" (Rom 8:1)
  - "He that heareth my word, and believeth on him that sent me, hath everlasting life, and shall not come into condemnation; but is passed from death unto life" (John 5:24)
  - Read carefully: these say no CONDEMNATION, not no judgment. The believer's case still comes up — but the verdict is settled by his standing "in Christ Jesus," not by a flawless record of his own.
  - [STR] Judgment and condemnation are not the same word. Everyone is judged (Eccl 12:14); the one "in Christ" is not condemned (Rom 8:1). The distinction is the whole comfort of the doctrine.

- **Why no terror: a living High Priest stands in the court as your Advocate**
  - "Wherefore he is able also to save them to the uttermost that come unto God by him, seeing he ever liveth to make intercession for them" (Heb 7:25)
  - "If any man sin, we have an advocate with the Father, Jesus Christ the righteous" (1 John 2:1)
  - "Let us therefore come boldly unto the throne of grace, that we may obtain mercy, and find grace to help in time of need" (Heb 4:16)
  - The same High Priest who cleanses the sanctuary is the Advocate who pleads your case in it. The Judge is your Father; the Advocate is your Brother; the evidence is a life He Himself renewed.
  - [→] You do not enter this court alone or unrepresented. "Having therefore, brethren, boldness to enter into the holiest by the blood of Jesus" (Heb 10:19). Boldness — not because you are innocent, but because your Advocate has secured the verdict.

## The Books — Real Evidence, an Open Record

**The judgment opens BOOKS (Dan 7:10). Scripture names them. They are not God refreshing His memory — He needs no record. They are the evidence laid open before the watching universe, so the verdict can be SEEN to be just.**

- **The Bible names the books that are opened**
  - "The judgment was set, and the books were opened" (Dan 7:10)
  - The book of life — the names of the professed: "whose names are in the book of life" (Phil 4:3); "I will not blot out his name out of the book of life" (Rev 3:5)
  - The book of remembrance — the deeds recorded: "a book of remembrance was written before him for them that feared the LORD" (Mal 3:16)
  - Every secret thing: "For God shall bring every work into judgment, with every secret thing, whether it be good, or whether it be evil" (Eccl 12:14)
  - [→] The record exists. The judgment is the moment that record is opened and read — names matched to lives, profession tested against reality.

- **The investigation is for the UNIVERSE to see, not for God to learn**
  - God already knows every heart; He does not open books to discover anything. So who is the audience?
  - "To the intent that now unto the principalities and powers in heavenly places might be known by the church the manifold wisdom of God" (Eph 3:10)
  - "For we are made a spectacle unto the world, and to angels, and to men" (1 Cor 4:9)
  - [STR] The books are opened for the SAME audience that watched Job — "thousand thousands... and ten thousand times ten thousand stood before him" (Dan 7:10). The judgment is God submitting His decisions to open scrutiny because He has nothing to hide.

[→] Tie it back to Study 1: God answers the accusation not by force but by open evidence. "Let God be true, but every man a liar; as it is written, That thou mightest be justified in thy sayings, and mightest overcome when thou art judged" (Rom 3:4). God Himself consents to be judged in the open. The investigative judgment is that consent enacted — the books on the table, the universe reading, the verdict visible.

## The Same Courtroom — From Job to 1844

**This is the narrative frame of the whole series, and it is an ILLUSTRATION of the doctrine just proved, not its foundation. The doctrine stands on Daniel 7:9-14, Leviticus 16, and Revelation 22:12. The Job parallel shows us the PATTERN the Bible uses, so the cosmic shape of 1844 makes sense.**

- **Study 1 opened with two charges, and Job is the Bible's own staged courtroom**
  - Charge A: "Doth Job fear God for nought?" — loyalty is bought (Job 1:9)
  - Charge B: the law cannot be kept (Gen 3:1; Rom 8:7)
  - "Now there was a day when the sons of God came to present themselves before the LORD, and Satan came also among them" (Job 1:6)
  - A heavenly council. An accuser. An accused. A challenge over MOTIVE. This is the same shape Daniel 7 shows on a cosmic scale.

- **God answered the charge not by argument but by display**
  - Job was never told of the council or the accusation. His faithfulness was therefore uncontaminated by performance — "Though he slay me, yet will I trust in him" (Job 13:15)
  - That trust, under pressure and without explanation, answered the charge no sermon could.
  - And the court found FOR Job: "ye have not spoken of me the thing that is right, as my servant Job hath... for him will I accept" (Job 42:7-8). The accused was vindicated; the accusers were the ones condemned.
  - [→] God answers accusations by demonstration, not declaration. This is the principle that governs the investigative judgment — and it is why a judgment is needed at all even though God already knows.

- **1844 runs on the identical principle**
  - Same accuser: "the accuser of our brethren is cast down, which accused them before our God day and night" (Rev 12:10)
  - Same method: open evidence before a cosmic audience (Eph 3:10; 1 Cor 4:9)
  - Same outcome for the faithful: the accused vindicated, the accuser exposed (Dan 7:22 ; Lev 16:21-22)
  - [→] The investigative judgment is not God investigating you. It is God vindicating you before the universe — the same way He vindicated Job.

[Q] **"Why does God need a judgment if He already knows everything?"**
→ He does not need it for Himself. He holds it for the same reason He permitted Job's trial: the accusation is about MOTIVE, and motive cannot be refuted by explanation — only demonstrated. Job did not know he was watched, so his trust proved itself genuine. The judgment lays every redeemed life open before the universe so the unfallen worlds can read for themselves that God's mercy is just and His people's loyalty is real. God submits His decisions to open scrutiny (Rom 3:4) because He has nothing to hide.

[ILL] Job's courtroom had one-way glass — the defendant could not see the packed gallery, so his responses were real. The investigative judgment is the same courtroom on a cosmic scale. God does not rig the evidence and He does not coach the witness. He opens the books and lets the universe read them.

> "The Lord desires through His people to answer Satan's charges by showing the results of obedience to right principles." (COL 296.4)

That sentence does not PROVE the doctrine — Daniel 7, Leviticus 16, and Job 1 already did. It simply confirms, in a later voice, what the Bible established: the judgment is God's open answer to the accuser.
