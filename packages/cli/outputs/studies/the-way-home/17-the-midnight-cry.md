# Study 17: The Midnight Cry — The Chart, the Tarrying, the Seventh-Month

**Habakkuk 2 framed the whole experience before it happened: "write the vision, make it plain upon tables" gave the chart; "though it tarry, wait for it" gave the tarrying; and "the just shall live by his faith" gave the test. Christ's own parable of the ten virgins then maps the road exactly — lamps, slumber, a midnight cry — and the autumn Day-of-Atonement type fixes the very year and day the cry pointed to: the tenth day of the seventh month, October 22, 1844. The time was right; only the event was misread.**

## The Vision Made Plain Upon Tables

**Study 16 left the traveler with Daniel's book open and the 2300 days terminating in 1844 — but a date on a page is not yet a movement, and a movement of believers must be carried through a delay. Long before any of it happened, Habakkuk wrote the script: a vision to be written plainly, a tarrying to be endured, and a faith to live by. Begin where God Himself began — with the order He gave the prophet.**

- **God commanded the prophet to take his stand and watch for the answer**
  - "I will stand upon my watch, and set me upon the tower, and will watch to see what he will say unto me, and what I shall answer when I am reproved" (Hab 2:1)
  - The prophet does not invent the vision; he climbs the tower and waits for God to speak. The posture is watching, not guessing — the same posture the whole movement would take.
  - [→] This is the Bereans' nobility put into a single verse: take your stand on the word and watch to see what He will say. Everything that follows is God's answer, not a man's scheme.

- **The first thing God said was: write it, and make it plain enough to run with**
  - "And the LORD answered me, and said, Write the vision, and make it plain upon tables, that he may run that readeth it" (Hab 2:2)
  - A "table" is a writing-board, a chart. God's own instruction is to put the vision on a board in letters so plain that a man can read it at a glance and run with the message.
  - [STR] Mark the command precisely: the vision is to be _written_, _made plain_, _on tables_, so the reader can _run_. That is not a private mystical experience. It is a published chart for a public movement.

[DYK🔎] When the Advent believers, reading Daniel 8 and 9, drew up a large chart in 1842 laying out the four kingdoms, the 1260 days, the 2300 days, and the date they terminated — and printed it on cloth boards to hang in meeting-houses so a reader could "run" with it — they were not inventing a method. They were obeying Habakkuk 2:2 to the letter. The Bible asked for a chart; the movement produced one. (The documenting of _which_ chart, and the men who drew it, is in the "Document from History" section below — but the command itself is pure Scripture.)

- **The vision is for an appointed time, and it cannot lie**
  - "For the vision is yet for an appointed time, but at the end it shall speak, and not lie: though it tarry, wait for it; because it will surely come, it will not tarry" (Hab 2:3)
  - Two truths sit in one verse. (1) The vision has an _appointed time_ — God set a terminus; it is not open-ended. (2) The vision "shall not lie" — the prophetic word itself is never the thing that fails.
  - [→] Read it slowly: "it shall speak, and not lie." When the disappointment came, the temptation was to say the prophecy lied. Habakkuk forbids that conclusion in advance. The vision does not lie. If there is a failure, it is in the _reader_, never in the word.

- **And built right into the appointed time is a tarrying**
  - "though it tarry, wait for it; because it will surely come, it will not tarry" (Hab 2:3)
  - God did not hide the delay; He _wrote it into the vision_. "Though it tarry" is not an accident or a defeat — it is a clause of the prophecy. The wait was foretold, and the command attached to it is one word: _wait_.
  - [STR] So the tarrying was not the vision breaking down. The tarrying _was part of the vision_. A believer who expected no delay had simply not read verse 3 to the end.

- **The faith that carries a man across the tarrying**
  - "Behold, his soul which is lifted up is not upright in him: but the just shall live by his faith" (Hab 2:4)
  - This is the verse Paul builds the gospel on (Rom 1:17; Gal 3:11; Heb 10:38). Here it sits in its first home: the man who waits out the appointed time, when the proud man gives up, "shall live by his faith."
  - [→] Habakkuk gave the movement everything before it arrived: a chart (v.2), an appointed terminus (v.3), a foretold delay (v.3), and the faith to survive it (v.4). The Way Home is mapped one chapter ahead of the travelers.

[Q] **"Isn't applying Habakkuk to the 1844 movement a stretch — a verse pressed into a frame it was never about?"**
→ Watch the next study's own text answer it. The New Testament tells us _which_ vision is for an appointed time and must be "waited for": "yet a little while, and he that shall come will come, and will not tarry. Now the just shall live by faith" (Heb 10:37-38) — Paul quotes Habakkuk 2:3-4 and applies it directly to the _coming of the Lord_ and the waiting saints. We are not importing the frame. Hebrews already set Habakkuk 2 on the very subject — a foretold coming, a foretold tarrying, a faith to live by. The 1844 experience is simply that script being lived out, line for line.

## The Ten Virgins — Christ's Map of the Same Road

**Habakkuk gave the chart, the tarrying, and the faith. Christ gave the same three in a story — and added the one element Habakkuk did not name: a cry at midnight. The parable of the ten virgins is not a vague moral about being ready in general; in its plain prophetic sense it is a map of this exact movement. Lay the parable beside the history and every clause lands.**

- **The story opens with a going-forth to meet the bridegroom**
  - "Then shall the kingdom of heaven be likened unto ten virgins, which took their lamps, and went forth to meet the bridegroom" (Matt 25:1)
  - The virgins do not stay home; they _go forth_ — a movement of people who leave where they were to meet a coming Bridegroom. That is precisely what the Advent reformation was: a body of believers across many churches going out to meet a returning Christ.
  - The lamp is the Bible's own symbol for the word: "Thy word is a lamp unto my feet, and a light unto my path" (Ps 119:105); "we have also a more sure word of prophecy... as unto a light that shineth in a dark place" (2 Pet 1:19). The virgins go forth carrying the open word.

- **All ten slumber and sleep while the bridegroom tarries**
  - "While the bridegroom tarried, they all slumbered and slept" (Matt 25:5)
  - There it is again — the tarrying, the same word from Habakkuk 2:3, now in a story. And notice: the delay is in the parable _on purpose_. Christ wrote a tarrying into His own picture of the movement, exactly as God wrote it into Habakkuk's vision.
  - [→] _All ten_ slept — wise and foolish alike. The slumber is not the dividing line; it is the shared experience of the whole company during the delay. The dividing line comes later, and it is about the oil.

- **At midnight — the darkest, most discouraged hour — the cry is made**
  - "And at midnight there was a cry made, Behold, the bridegroom cometh; go ye out to meet him" (Matt 25:6)
  - The cry does not come at dawn when hope is high. It comes at _midnight_ — after the tarrying, in the discouragement, when the first expectation had passed and the sleep was deepest. Out of that darkness a renewed, definite proclamation breaks: _He is coming — go out to meet Him._
  - [STR] This is the title of the study and the heart of the parable: the _midnight cry_. It is a second, sharper message that revives the slumbering movement _after_ a real delay — not the first announcement, but the awakening one. History calls it the seventh-month movement; Christ called it the cry at midnight.

- **The cry sends them to trim their lamps — back to the word**
  - "Then all those virgins arose, and trimmed their lamps" (Matt 25:7)
  - The response to the cry is not a new emotion but a return to the lamp — the believers went back to the prophetic word and the sanctuary types and _re-examined the reckoning_. The midnight cry drove the movement deeper into Scripture, not away from it.

- **The dividing line is oil, and the door, when the bridegroom comes**
  - "And while they went to buy, the bridegroom came; and they that were ready went in with him to the marriage: and the door was shut" (Matt 25:10)
  - Here the parable touches the very event of 1844, and we must read it carefully, because the next study turns on it. The bridegroom _came_ — but the text does not say he came _to this earth_. He came, and "they that were ready _went in with him to the marriage_." The coming in this parable is a coming to a _marriage_, into which the ready ones enter with Him.
  - [→] Hold that distinction. The disappointment of 1844 was born of expecting the bridegroom to come _here_. The parable says the bridegroom comes _to a marriage_, and the ready go _in_. The next clause of prophecy will tell us where that marriage is.

[DYK🔎] Revelation supplies the marriage the virgins go in to: "the marriage of the Lamb is come, and his wife hath made herself ready... Blessed are they which are called unto the marriage supper of the Lamb" (Rev 19:7,9). And Daniel shows where the Bridegroom _went_ in 1844 — not down to earth, but up to the Ancient of Days: "one like the Son of man came with the clouds of heaven, and came to the Ancient of days, and they brought him near before him. And there was given him dominion, and glory, and a kingdom" (Dan 7:13-14). The bridegroom's "coming" in Matthew 25:10 is His coming _to the Father to receive the kingdom_ — the same event Daniel saw. The virgins were not wrong that He came; they were wrong about _where_.

## The Time Right, the Event Misread — The Disappointment Resolved

**This is the hinge of the whole study, and it answers the deepest wound of 1844. The movement did not fail because the date was wrong. The date was right. What failed was the expectation of _what would happen_ on that date. Habakkuk already told them the vision "shall not lie" (Hab 2:3) — so the error had to be in the reading, never in the word. Scripture resolves the disappointment by showing the right event.**

- **They expected the Bridegroom to come to earth; He came to the Ancient of Days**
  - "I saw in the night visions, and, behold, one like the Son of man came with the clouds of heaven, and came to the Ancient of days, and they brought him near before him" (Dan 7:13)
  - "And there was given him dominion, and glory, and a kingdom, that all people, nations, and languages, should serve him" (Dan 7:14)
  - The believers read "the bridegroom cometh" as _the second advent_. But Daniel 7 shows a different coming on the prophetic calendar: the Son of man coming _to the Father_, in heaven, to receive a kingdom. That is the coming the date pointed to — a heavenly event, invisible to earth.
  - [STR] So the cloud lifts. Christ did exactly what the time-prophecy required; He moved — but in heaven, to begin the closing work of His ministry, not to the earth. The disciples on the road to Emmaus made the identical mistake at the first advent: "we trusted that it had been he which should have redeemed Israel" (Luke 24:21) — right Messiah, right week, wrong expectation of _what He would do that week_. The disappointment of 1844 is the same shape, and is resolved the same way.

- **The cleansing of the sanctuary IS that coming — the same event from another angle**
  - "Unto two thousand and three hundred days; then shall the sanctuary be cleansed" (Dan 8:14)
  - Daniel 8 says the 2300 days end at a _cleansing of the sanctuary_. Daniel 7 says they end with the Son of man coming to the Ancient of Days to begin His reign. These are not two events but one, seen from two windows — the priestly window (a cleansing) and the throne-room window (a coming to receive the kingdom). Hebrews confirms the place: "Christ is not entered into the holy places made with hands... but into heaven itself, now to appear in the presence of God for us" (Heb 9:24).
  - [→] The next study (18) opens this cleansing fully. Here, simply settle the heart: the 2300 days landed exactly where Daniel said — on a heavenly work — and the believers' only error was looking down when they should have looked up.

[Q] **"If the date was right, why were they allowed to suffer the disappointment at all?"**
→ Because the disappointment was _written into the prophecy_, like the tarrying. Habakkuk said "though it tarry, wait for it" (Hab 2:3); Christ wrote a slumber and a midnight crisis into the parable (Matt 25:5-6); Revelation 10 wrote the bitterness into the experience itself — "it shall make thy belly bitter, but it shall be in thy mouth sweet as honey" (Rev 10:9-10, Study 16). The bitterness was a _foretold step on the road_, not a derailment. God let His people learn, in the only way that teaches it, that the failure was in their expectation, never in His word — and then He commissioned them onward: "Thou must prophesy again before many peoples, and nations, and tongues, and kings" (Rev 10:11).

## The Autumn Type Fixes the Day — October 22

**Now to the most striking proof of all, and the legitimate kind of typology: a literal type the text itself appoints, fixing a literal date. The spring feasts met their antitypes to the very day at the first advent. The autumn feast — the Day of Atonement on the tenth day of the seventh month — is the type of the sanctuary's cleansing (Dan 8:14). If the spring types met their day, the autumn type must meet its day too. That carries the reckoning from 1843 to the precise day in 1844: the tenth of the seventh month.**

- **God fixed the cleansing to one exact day of the year — forever**
  - "And this shall be a statute for ever unto you: that in the seventh month, on the tenth day of the month, ye shall afflict your souls, and do no work at all" (Lev 16:29)
  - "For on that day shall the priest make an atonement for you, to cleanse you, that ye may be clean from all your sins before the LORD" (Lev 16:30)
  - The cleansing of the sanctuary was not done at any time the priest chose. God nailed it to a _date_ — the tenth day of the seventh month — and called it a statute _for ever_. The cleansing of Daniel 8:14 is the antitype of _this_ service (Lev 16; Heb 9:23), so its date is governed by _this_ calendar.
  - "Also on the tenth day of this seventh month there shall be a day of atonement... and ye shall afflict your souls" (Lev 23:27); "It shall be unto you a sabbath of rest, and ye shall afflict your souls: in the ninth day of the month at even, from even unto even, shall ye celebrate your sabbath" (Lev 23:32).

- **The spring types met their antitypes on the very day — so the autumn type must too**
  - The first three feasts of the sacred year (spring) were fulfilled by Christ _exactly on their appointed days_, not approximately:
    - Passover, the fourteenth day of the first month (Lev 23:5): "Christ our passover is sacrificed for us" (1 Cor 5:7) — He died as the Passover lamb, on Passover.
    - Wavesheaf of firstfruits, "the morrow after the sabbath" (Lev 23:10-11): "now is Christ risen from the dead, and become the firstfruits of them that slept" (1 Cor 15:20) — He rose as the firstfruits, on the firstfruits day.
    - Pentecost, fifty days later (Lev 23:15-16): "when the day of Pentecost was fully come, they were all with one accord in one place" (Acts 2:1) — the Spirit fell on Pentecost, "when the day... was fully come."
  - [STR] Three spring types, three antitypes, each landing on the _very day_ the type appointed — because "when the fulness of the time was come, God sent forth his Son" (Gal 4:4): God keeps His own calendar to the day.
  - [→] Here is the inescapable logic. If the _spring_ types met their antitypes precisely on the appointed days, then the _autumn_ type — the Day of Atonement, the cleansing of the sanctuary — meets _its_ antitype precisely on its appointed day: the tenth day of the seventh month. That is not numerology; it is reading the same calendar God already proved He keeps.

- **The jubilee trumpet — the very type of a proclamation on that day**
  - "Then shalt thou cause the trumpet of the jubile to sound on the tenth day of the seventh month, in the day of atonement shall ye make the trumpet sound throughout all your land" (Lev 25:9)
  - On the Day of Atonement, God commanded a _trumpet sounded through all the land_ — a loud, public proclamation announcing the cleansing-day. The midnight cry of the seventh-month movement was the antitype of that trumpet: a proclamation, sounded everywhere, fixing the very day of the antitypical atonement.
  - [→] So the type does not only fix the _date_ of the cleansing; it even foretells a _cry going through the land_ to announce it. The seventh-month movement is written into the Levitical calendar itself.

- **Why the day was October 22 — the 2300 days reach the autumn**
  - The 2300 days end "at the time of the end" (Dan 8:17) and reach to "the cleansing" (Dan 8:14). The whole period is reckoned by the year-day key — "each day for a year" (Num 14:34) — from the decree of Ezra 7 (Study 14). The believers first reckoned the terminus to 1843; the corrected reckoning landed in the _autumn_ of 1844 because the starting decree itself took effect in the _autumn_ of 457 BC.
  - The text shows the decree's journey began and arrived in stages across the year: "upon the first day of the first month began he to go up from Babylon, and on the first day of the fifth month came he to Jerusalem" (Ezra 7:9) — Ezra reached Jerusalem in the fifth month, and the decree's full provisions went into effect that autumn. A period that _began_ in autumn must _end_ in autumn.
  - [STR] So the 2300 years, begun in the autumn of 457 BC, terminate in the autumn of 1844 — and the autumn type fixes the exact day within that autumn: the tenth of the seventh month. The reckoning was corrected _by chronology and by the type_, not by anyone's authority — the believers simply read the calendar more carefully and let Scripture set the day.

[DYK🔎] The correction from "1843" to "the seventh month of 1844" was not a retreat — it was the chart obeying its own data. The original chart was already "made plain upon tables" (Hab 2:2); the trimming of the lamps (Matt 25:7) was the movement re-examining the reckoning and finding that the autumn-to-autumn span of the 2300 years pushed the terminus into the seventh month. The same Bible-method that drew the chart corrected the chart. (Dates and the men involved are documented below.)

## "Can the Time Be Known?" — Answering the Objection in Advance

**The whole project of fixing a date runs straight into a famous text: "of that day and hour knoweth no man." If that verse forbids ever knowing any prophetic time, the midnight cry was presumption. So settle it from the Bible: that verse speaks of the _second advent_, which remains undated; it never forbids understanding the _prophetic periods_ God Himself gave to be understood.**

- **The "day and hour" of Matthew 24:36 is the SECOND ADVENT — still undated to this day**
  - "But of that day and hour knoweth no man, no, not the angels of heaven, but my Father only" (Matt 24:36)
  - This is the day of Christ's _return to earth_ — the literal second coming. No man knows it, and the Advent believers never claimed to. October 22 was never offered as the date of the second advent; it was the date the 2300-day prophecy ran out, the beginning of the heavenly cleansing (Dan 8:14; 7:13-14).
  - [→] The believers' error, in fact, was _forgetting_ this distinction — they treated the prophetic terminus as if it were the undated "day and hour." Matthew 24:36 does not condemn the date they had; it explains the disappointment they suffered. The prophetic clock can be read; the day of the advent itself cannot.

- **The same chapter commands us to KNOW when the time is near**
  - "Now learn a parable of the fig tree; When his branch is yet tender, and putteth forth leaves, ye know that summer is nigh" (Matt 24:32)
  - "So likewise ye, when ye shall see all these things, know that it is near, even at the doors" (Matt 24:33)
  - In the very same sermon, Christ commands His people to _read the signs and KNOW_ — "know that it is near." He cannot, two breaths later, forbid all knowledge of time. Matthew 24:36 hides the precise day of the advent; Matthew 24:33 commands the reading of the prophetic time that shows it "even at the doors." Scripture does not break against itself (John 10:35).

- **God will do nothing without first revealing it through prophecy**
  - "Surely the Lord GOD will do nothing, but he revealeth his secret unto his servants the prophets" (Amos 3:7)
  - A God who reveals everything beforehand did not hand His church a 2300-day prophecy in order to keep its terminus hidden. The very existence of dated prophecy — 70 weeks, 1260 days, 1290, 1335, 2300 — is proof that prophetic _time_ is meant to be understood.

- **And the understanding is reserved for the waiting, purified people**
  - "Many shall be purified, and made white, and tried; but the wicked shall do wickedly: and none of the wicked shall understand; but the wise shall understand" (Dan 12:10)
  - Daniel was told that at "the time of the end" his sealed book would be opened and "the wise shall understand." The understanding of the time-prophecies is _promised_ to the end-time believers — the very company that gave the midnight cry. To say the time cannot be known is to contradict Daniel's own promise that it would be.

[Q] **"But they set a date and were disappointed — doesn't that prove date-setting is wrong?"**
→ It proves the _event_ was misread, not that the _time_ was unknowable. Read the two failures side by side. The faithful servant watches and reckons (Matt 25:6-7; 24:33). The _evil_ servant says the opposite: "My lord delayeth his coming" (Matt 24:48) — he does not err by expecting Christ too soon; he errs by deciding Christ tarries indefinitely, and falls to eating and drinking with the drunken (Matt 24:49-51). Scripture's warning is not against watching for a near coming; it is against giving up the watch. The midnight-cry believers were the watching virgins, not the scoffing servant. Their date was the right terminus of a real prophecy; only their expectation of the event needed correcting — and Scripture corrected it (Dan 7:13-14; 8:14).

## What the Whole Experience Proves

**Step back and see the shape of it. Every element of the 1844 experience — chart, tarrying, midnight cry, disappointment, and the right event in the wrong place — was written into the prophetic word centuries in advance. A movement cannot script its own foretelling. The precision is itself the proof.**

- **God wrote the chart-command, the delay, and the faith ahead of time** — Habakkuk 2:2-4. The believers did not invent the method; they obeyed a verse.
- **God wrote the midnight cry into Christ's own parable** — Matthew 25:1-13. A renewed proclamation _after_ a tarrying, sending the people back to the lamp.
- **God wrote the exact day into the Levitical calendar** — the tenth of the seventh month (Lev 16:29; 23:27), proven by the spring types meeting their days (1 Cor 5:7; 15:20; Acts 2:1).
- **God wrote the right event into Daniel** — a coming to the Ancient of Days, a cleansing of the sanctuary (Dan 7:13-14; 8:14) — so the disappointment is resolved, not denied.
- [STR] A movement that fulfills, to the clause, a script written across Habakkuk, Leviticus, Daniel, and Matthew is not a human miscalculation that stumbled into a date. It is God carrying a people through a foretold valley exactly as He said He would. The error of 1844 was an honest, _foretold_ error about _where_; the timing was the Lord's own.

## Appeal

- God did not leave His people to cross the tarrying alone. He gave them a chart to read by, a tower to stand on, a cry at midnight, and a faith to live by — and He gave it all _before_ the road was walked.
- The bitterest hour of their faith — the dark midnight after the first hope passed — was the very hour He had appointed for the cry. He does His deepest work at midnight.
- If you are in a tarrying of your own — a promise that seems to delay, a hope that seems to fail — Habakkuk's word is your word: "though it tarry, wait for it; because it will surely come, it will not tarry" (Hab 2:3). The vision does not lie. The failure, when there is one, is never in His word.
- "Let your loins be girded about, and your lights burning; and ye yourselves like unto men that wait for their lord, when he will return from the wedding" (Luke 12:35-36). The Bridegroom _has_ gone in to the marriage above. The cry has been made. The only question is whether your lamp has oil.
- Will you trim your lamp and go out to meet Him?

## Document from History — The Chart, the Tarrying, and October 22

_(Per the historicist method: the Bible proves the meaning; history records the fulfillment. These dates and persons are documented from the record — they do not establish the doctrine, they confirm that it happened on schedule.)_

- **The 1843 chart.** In 1842 Charles Fitch and Apollos Hale prepared a large prophetic chart laying out Daniel 2, 7, 8, and 9 — the four kingdoms, the 1260 days, and the 2300 days terminating about 1843 — printed on cloth boards to hang in meeting-houses. It was the literal obedience of "write the vision, and make it plain upon tables, that he may run that readeth it" (Hab 2:2).
- **The tarrying time.** When the first reckoning (ending in the Jewish year 1843, which ran to the spring of 1844) passed without the advent, the movement entered the "tarrying time" of the spring and summer of 1844 — the slumber of Matthew 25:5, the delay of Habakkuk 2:3.
- **Snow's seventh-month movement — the Midnight Cry.** In the summer of 1844 Samuel S. Snow pressed the point that, because the autumn type of the Day of Atonement (Lev 16:29; 23:27) governs the cleansing of Daniel 8:14, and because the 2300 years began in the autumn of 457 BC, the period must terminate in the _autumn_ of 1844 — on the tenth day of the seventh month. His message, published as _The True Midnight Cry_ (August 1844), revived the slumbering movement; this seventh-month proclamation is the historical answer to "at midnight there was a cry made" (Matt 25:6).
- **October 22, 1844.** The tenth day of the seventh month, reckoned by the Karaite (rather than Rabbinic) Jewish calendar, fell that year on October 22. This became the day to which the corrected reckoning of the 2300 days pointed — the antitypical Day of Atonement, the beginning of the cleansing of the heavenly sanctuary (its meaning unfolded in Study 18).
- **The corrected chart.** After the disappointment, the reckoning that had located the terminus in the autumn of 1844 (autumn-to-autumn, 457 BC to 1844) was vindicated; a corrected chart (1850) reflected the autumn terminus and the heavenly nature of the event.

[DYK🔎] The seventh-month believers may be quoted as a confirming echo _after_ the Bible has fixed the day. Samuel Snow's own published reasoning ran exactly along the typological line proven above: because the spring feasts met their antitypes in the spring of the crucifixion year, the autumn feast — the Day of Atonement on the tenth of the seventh month — must meet its antitype in the autumn, on its appointed day. He did not prove the date by his own authority; he proved it from Leviticus and Daniel, and history then recorded that the cry went through the land precisely as the jubilee trumpet of Leviticus 25:9 had foreshadowed.
