# Study 16: The Little Book — The Bittersweet of 1844

**The cold math of the timeline becomes a lived crisis of faith. In Revelation 10 a mighty angel comes down with Daniel's little book now OPEN, plants one foot on the sea and one on the land, lifts his hand to heaven, and swears that "there should be time no longer" — no prophetic period runs past the great terminus. The book he gives the prophet to eat is sweet as honey in the mouth and bitter in the belly: the blessed hope that thrilled the heart, and the disappointment that crushed it. And here is the wonder — God wrote BOTH the sweetness and the bitterness into the prophecy beforehand. The tarrying was foretold; the disappointment was a foretold step on the Way Home. And the chapter does not end in the dark: "Thou must prophesy again."**

## Where We Stand on the Way Home

**Study 14 walked the 2300 days from the decree of 457 BC to the autumn of 1844, with three of its four dates already signed by history at the first advent. Study 15 showed every other clock — the daily, the 1290, the 1335, the 2520 — striking the same hour. The arithmetic is done. But a date on a chart is one thing; living through it is another. Revelation 10 is the chapter where the timeline stops being numbers and becomes a man's experience — sweet, then bitter, then sent out again. Before we ask what the cleansing of the sanctuary MEANS (Study 18), we must walk through what it COST the people who first reached this date.**

- **John was shown this scene standing exactly where the timeline leaves us**
  - Revelation 10 falls between the sixth trumpet (the woe that closed on August 11, 1840 — Study 13) and the seventh (Rev 11:14-15). It is the interlude at the very hinge of history where the prophetic clocks run out.
  - "The second woe is past; [and], behold, the third woe cometh quickly" (Rev 11:14) — Rev 10 is the breath drawn between them.
  - [→] The chapter is positioned at the END of the long prophetic periods. That position is the first clue to what the angel's oath means.

## The Mighty Angel With the Open Book

**Watch the figure John sees. Every detail is loaded, and every detail the Bible itself unlocks. This is no minor messenger — he is robed in the symbols of Deity, and he holds a book we have met before.**

- **A mighty angel clothed with the glory of God**
  - "And I saw another mighty angel come down from heaven, clothed with a cloud: and a rainbow [was] upon his head, and his face [was] as it were the sun, and his feet as pillars of fire" (Rev 10:1)
  - Every emblem is the glory of God Himself. The cloud is His chariot — "Behold, he cometh with clouds" (Rev 1:7); "coming in the clouds of heaven with power and great glory" (Matt 24:30).
  - The rainbow is the token of the throne and of the covenant — "[there was] a rainbow round about the throne" (Rev 4:3); "as the appearance of the bow that is in the cloud... so was the appearance of the likeness of the glory of the LORD" (Eze 1:28).
  - The face "as it were the sun" is the face of Christ transfigured and glorified (compare Rev 1:16). The feet "as pillars of fire" recall the pillar that led Israel home.
  - [STR] Compare spiritual with spiritual (1 Cor 2:13): the cloud, the rainbow, the sun-bright face, the fiery pillar — these are the Bible's own wardrobe for the LORD. This is a heavenly messenger carrying a message of the highest authority, clothed in the glory of God Himself.

- **In his hand — a LITTLE BOOK, and it is OPEN**
  - "And he had in his hand a little book open: and he set his right foot upon the sea, and [his] left [foot] on the earth" (Rev 10:2)
  - One foot on the sea, one on the land — the message is for the WHOLE world, every continent, every shore. A worldwide proclamation is coming.
  - But the key word is "open." A book that is open was once a book that was SHUT. Which book in all Scripture was sealed shut, then unsealed at the very end of time?

[STR] **Only one book in the Bible was commanded sealed and reserved for the time of the end — Daniel's.** This is the master key to Revelation 10. Set the two scenes side by side and they interpret each other.

- **Daniel's book was SEALED — explicitly, and to a fixed deadline**
  - "But thou, O Daniel, shut up the words, and seal the book, [even] to the time of the end: many shall run to and fro, and knowledge shall be increased" (Dan 12:4)
  - "And he said, Go thy way, Daniel: for the words [are] closed up and sealed till the time of the end" (Dan 12:9)
  - And specifically the time-vision — the 2300 days: "shut thou up the vision; for it [shall be] for many days" (Dan 8:26).
  - Even Daniel himself did not understand it: "I was astonished at the vision, but none understood [it]" (Dan 8:27).

- **The little book OPEN in Revelation 10 is Daniel's book UNSEALED**
  - Daniel's was the ONLY book sealed to "the time of the end." Revelation 10 shows a little book OPEN in the hand of the angel. Scripture gives no other account anywhere of Daniel's book being opened. By the Bible's own line-upon-line method (Isa 28:10), the open book of Revelation 10 can only be the sealed book of Daniel, now loosed.
  - [→] Daniel was told the words were "closed up and sealed till the time of the end." Revelation 10 shows them OPEN. Therefore Revelation 10 is set IN the time of the end — exactly when the long prophetic periods are running out.

[DYK🔎] The angel told Daniel WHEN the seal would break: "at the time of the end... knowledge shall be increased" and "the wise shall understand" (Dan 12:4,10). The book was not sealed because God wanted it hidden forever — He sealed it on a timer. When the 2300-day clock approached its end, the seal would lift and a generation would suddenly UNDERSTAND what Daniel could not. The opening of the book and the rise of a worldwide study of prophecy are the SAME event, foretold to fall together.

[Q] **"Couldn't the 'little book' just be a different book — Revelation itself, or something John invented?"**
→ Test it by Scripture, not by preference. (1) The angel makes the book the ground of his OATH about prophetic TIME (Rev 10:6) — and Daniel's is the time-prophecy book, the one with the 1260, the 2300, the 1290, the 1335. (2) It is called "open," which only makes sense of a book once SHUT — and Daniel's is the only book in Scripture commanded shut "till the time of the end" (Dan 12:4,9). (3) The chapter ends "thou must prophesy again... before many peoples, and nations" (Rev 10:11) — a RENEWED preaching of a message already given, which fits a reopened book exactly. Three independent textual hooks all land on Daniel. The Book interprets itself.

## The Oath: "There Should Be Time No Longer"

**Now the angel does something solemn. He lifts his hand to heaven and SWEARS an oath — and the content of the oath is the most misread line in the chapter. Read it in the plain literal sense, in its own context, and it is not mysterious at all.**

- **The angel lifts his hand and swears by the Creator**
  - "And the angel which I saw stand upon the sea and upon the earth lifted up his hand to heaven, And sware by him that liveth for ever and ever, who created heaven, and the things that therein are, and the earth, and the things that therein are, and the sea, and the things which are therein, that there should be time no longer" (Rev 10:5-6)
  - This is the posture of a sacred oath (compare Dan 12:7, where the man clothed in linen lifts BOTH hands to heaven and swears, sealing the 1260-year period). The same gesture, the same kind of subject — prophetic time.

- **"Time no longer" = no more PROPHETIC TIME — the prophetic periods end here**
  - The whole context is the time-prophecy book just opened. The angel, standing where the long periods run out, swears that no further prophetic period stretches beyond this point. The clocks of Daniel — having run to their terminus — strike for the last time. After this, no datable prophetic period remains.
  - This is NOT a denial of literal time continuing (the very next verse speaks of "the DAYS of the voice of the seventh angel" — Rev 10:7 — so ordinary time obviously goes on). It is a declaration that PROPHETIC time has expired.
  - [STR] Read it as the context demands: the longest time-prophecy (the 2300 days) is the last one, and at its close the angel swears there is "time no longer" — no more prophetic clock to follow. The math of Studies 14-15 has reached the wall.

[→] This single line is God's own GUARD against date-setting. Once the 2300 days expire, the angel swears under oath that NO prophetic period runs past it. So anyone who, after 1844, sets a new date for the second coming from a "prophetic period" is breaking this oath — there is no such period left. "It is not for you to know the times or the seasons" (Acts 1:7) becomes true again precisely because the last measured period has run out. The oath shuts the door on every later date-setting scheme.

- **The seventh angel's sounding completes "the mystery of God"**
  - "But in the days of the voice of the seventh angel, when he shall begin to sound, the mystery of God should be finished, as he hath declared to his servants the prophets" (Rev 10:7)
  - The "mystery of God" is the gospel itself (Rom 16:25; Col 1:26-27) — the plan of salvation. Its finishing is now in view. Beyond the last prophetic period lies the closing work of the gospel.
  - [→] So the angel's oath does not say "the end is now"; it says "the measured road is run; the closing work begins." That is the door Study 18 walks through.

[DYK🔎] Notice the seven thunders in verses 3-4. The angel "cried with a loud voice, as [when] a lion roareth: and when he had cried, seven thunders uttered their voices. And when the seven thunders had uttered their voices, I was about to write: and I heard a voice from heaven saying unto me, Seal up those things which the seven thunders uttered, and write them not" (Rev 10:3-4). One thing in this very chapter was deliberately LEFT sealed. Mark the lesson: a book is unsealed (Daniel) and a portion is RE-sealed (the thunders). God opens what we need and withholds what we don't — which is itself a rebuke to every speculator who would mine the unsealed thunders for a new date. The oath against further prophetic time and the sealing of the thunders teach the same restraint.

## The Book Eaten: Sweet in the Mouth, Bitter in the Belly

**Here the prophecy turns from a vision John WATCHES to an experience John LIVES. He is told to take the book and EAT it — and the taste is the heart of this whole study. This is the Bible's own figure for taking a message inside you, and Scripture has used it before.**

- **The command: take the book and EAT it**
  - "And the voice which I heard from heaven spake unto me again, and said, Go [and] take the little book which is open in the hand of the angel which standeth upon the sea and upon the earth" (Rev 10:8)
  - "And I went unto the angel, and said unto him, Give me the little book. And he said unto me, Take [it], and eat it up; and it shall make thy belly bitter, but it shall be in thy mouth sweet as honey" (Rev 10:9)
  - "And I took the little book out of the angel's hand, and ate it up; and it was in my mouth sweet as honey: and as soon as I had eaten it, my belly was bitter" (Rev 10:10)

[STR] **"Eating the book" is not a strange new image — it is a Scripture-given figure the prophets used before, and the Bible defines it for us.** This is typology, not spiritualizing: the text itself authorizes the meaning by giving us the prior pattern.

- **Ezekiel ate the same kind of book — and it tasted the same way**
  - "open thy mouth, and eat that I give thee. And when I looked, behold, an hand [was] sent unto me; and, lo, a roll of a book [was] therein" (Eze 2:8-9)
  - "Son of man, eat that thou findest; eat this roll, and go speak unto the house of Israel" (Eze 3:1)
  - "Then did I eat [it]; and it was in my mouth as honey for sweetness" (Eze 3:3)
  - And note — that roll was "written... lamentations, and mourning, and woe" (Eze 2:10): sweet to receive, sorrowful in its burden. The same pattern Revelation 10 names: sweet to taste, bitter to digest.

- **Jeremiah said it plainly: receiving God's word is eating it, and it is joy**
  - "Thy words were found, and I did eat them; and thy word was unto me the joy and rejoicing of mine heart: for I am called by thy name, O LORD God of hosts" (Jer 15:16)
  - [→] So the Bible has FIXED the figure: to "eat" a book is to receive its message into yourself, to make it your own. There is nothing mystical here — Jeremiah and Ezekiel hand us the dictionary, and John uses their word.

- **Now read the taste — and read it as God WROTE it: sweet THEN bitter, in that order**
  - **Sweet in the mouth** = the message, when first received, is pure joy. To open Daniel's book and find that the King is coming, the long night is nearly over, the blessed hope is at hand — "Looking for that blessed hope, and the glorious appearing of the great God and our Saviour Jesus Christ" (Titus 2:13) — that is honey on the tongue.
  - **Bitter in the belly** = but as the message is digested — as the appointed time arrives and is LIVED — it turns to grief. The hoped-for event does not come as expected. The sweetness of the proclamation gives way to the bitterness of disappointment.
  - [STR] Mark the ORDER carefully, because it is the whole point: the angel said it would be sweet FIRST, bitter AFTER. The disappointment was not an accident that ruined a good message; it was written INTO the message in advance. God foretold the bitterness before anyone tasted it.

[Q] **"If the message led to disappointment, wasn't the message simply WRONG?"**
→ This is the decisive question, and Revelation 10 answers it before it was ever asked. If the bitterness had been the sign of error, the angel would never have COMMANDED the eating, nor foretold the bitter taste as part of a TRUE message. But he did. The bitterness was promised by inspiration as the second half of a genuine experience. So the disappointment proves the message was on schedule — it does NOT prove the message was false. (Where the mistake actually lay, the next study shows: the TIME was right; the EVENT was misread.) A foretold bitterness cannot be the fingerprint of a wrong message — it is the fingerprint of THIS message, exactly as written.

## The Tarrying Was Written In: Habakkuk's Tower

**Lest anyone think the delay and the disappointment caught God off guard, look back to a prophet centuries earlier. Habakkuk 2 is the standing answer to "but it didn't happen when we thought." God built the waiting — and the apparent delay — right into the vision He gave to be made plain.**

- **God told Habakkuk: write the vision plainly, for it runs on an appointed time**
  - "I will stand upon my watch, and set me upon the tower, and will watch to see what he will say unto me" (Hab 2:1)
  - "And the LORD answered me, and said, Write the vision, and make [it] plain upon tables, that he may run that readeth it" (Hab 2:2)
  - "For the vision [is] yet for an appointed time, but at the end it shall speak, and not lie: though it tarry, wait for it; because it will surely come, it will not tarry" (Hab 2:3)

- **Read what God packed into that one verse (Hab 2:3)**
  - "the vision is yet for an APPOINTED time" — there IS a fixed schedule; the time can be known.
  - "though it TARRY, wait for it" — God HIMSELF said there would be a tarrying, a season where it seems delayed. He commanded patience THROUGH the delay.
  - "it will surely come, it will not tarry" — and yet it is NOT truly late; from heaven's side it arrives exactly on time. The "tarrying" is felt by the watcher, not caused by the clock.
  - [STR] So the experience of Revelation 10 was written by Habakkuk: a vision made plain, an appointed time, a felt delay that tests the watchers, and a sure arrival. The disappointment was not a derailment — it was the "tarrying" God Himself had foretold and commanded His people to wait through.

- **And the verse that anchors the waiting one: live by faith THROUGH it**
  - "Behold, his soul [which] is lifted up is not upright in him: but the just shall live by his faith" (Hab 2:4)
  - The tarrying is precisely where "the just shall live by his faith." When the sweet turns bitter, faith — not sight, not feeling — carries the watcher across. The disappointment is the proving ground of faith, not its refutation.
  - [→] Habakkuk 2 and Revelation 10 are one prophecy in two halves: Habakkuk gives the FRAME (appointed time, tarrying, sure arrival, live by faith); Revelation 10 gives the TASTE (sweet, then bitter). Compare spiritual with spiritual and the disappointment stands explained centuries in advance.

## The Disciples Tasted It First: The Road to Emmaus

**There is a prior case in Scripture where the time was right, the hope was sweet, and the event was so misread that it shattered the believers — and Christ Himself called them not liars but slow learners. The Bible hands us this parallel; the bitter belly of Revelation 10 had a rehearsal at the cross.**

- **The disciples' hope was sweet — and the cross made it bitter**
  - "But we trusted that it had been he which should have redeemed Israel: and beside all this, to day is the third day since these things were done" (Luke 24:21)
  - "We TRUSTED" — past tense, the sweetness curdled. They had the right Messiah, the right prophecies, even the right week. But they expected an earthly throne and got a cross, and the disappointment crushed them.
  - [ILL] Two men walking to Emmaus, faces downcast, rehearsing a hope that had died three days ago. That is the picture of the bitter belly — sincere believers, sound in their texts, undone because they had misread WHAT would happen, not WHEN.

- **Christ's verdict: the fault was not in the prophecies but in reading too little of them**
  - "O fools, and slow of heart to believe ALL that the prophets have spoken: Ought not Christ to have suffered these things, and to enter into his glory?" (Luke 24:25-26)
  - "And beginning at Moses and all the prophets, he expounded unto them in all the scriptures the things concerning himself" (Luke 24:27)
  - [→] Notice: Jesus did NOT say "your prophecies were false." He said "you believed too FEW of them." The cure for a misread disappointment is not to throw away the prophecies but to read MORE of them — line upon line — until the misread piece falls into place. That is exactly the recovery the next study traces: the sanctuary truth that the disappointed believers had not yet read.

[STR] **This is text-warranted typology, not invention.** The Emmaus road is the Bible's own picture of a true people, with true prophecies, suffering a real disappointment from a misread EVENT — and being restored by a fuller reading of the very Scriptures that disappointed them. The bitter belly of Revelation 10 follows the same shape: right time, misread event, recovery by deeper Bible study.

## "Thou Must Prophesy Again": The Sweet Returns

**Revelation 10 does not end in the dark. The bitter belly is verse 10; verse 11 is a COMMISSION. The disappointment is not the last word — it is the doorway to a renewed, worldwide proclamation. The Way Home does not stop at the disappointment; it passes through it.**

- **The command after the bitterness: prophesy AGAIN**
  - "And he said unto me, Thou must prophesy again before many peoples, and nations, and tongues, and kings" (Rev 10:11)
  - "AGAIN" — the same message that was eaten, now re-proclaimed, but wider: not to a region but to "many peoples, and nations, and tongues, and kings." The disappointment did not end the work; it ENLARGED it.
  - [→] The bitter step was not the grave of the movement but its commissioning. After 1844 the message goes out further than before. This is the hinge into the three angels' messages of Revelation 14.

- **The renewed message IS the first angel of Revelation 14**
  - "And I saw another angel fly in the midst of heaven, having the everlasting gospel to preach unto them that dwell on the earth, and to every nation, and kindred, and tongue, and people, Saying with a loud voice, Fear God, and give glory to him; for the hour of his judgment is come; and worship him that made heaven, and earth, and the sea, and the fountains of waters" (Rev 14:6-7)
  - Compare spiritual with spiritual (1 Cor 2:13): Revelation 10's angel stands on sea and land (worldwide) and swears by Him "who created heaven... and the earth... and the sea"; Revelation 14's angel flies to "every nation, and kindred, and tongue" and calls men to "worship him that made heaven, and earth, and the sea." Same scope, same Creator-appeal, same hour-of-judgment timing. The "prophesy again" of Revelation 10:11 is the everlasting-gospel proclamation of Revelation 14:6-7.
  - [STR] So the chain is: book opened → message eaten (sweet) → time fulfilled, event misread (bitter) → COMMISSIONED AGAIN, worldwide. The disappointment is the BIRTH-PANG of the three angels' messages, not their refutation. (Those messages are Study 19.)

[DYK🔎] "The hour of his judgment IS come" (Rev 14:7) is present tense — a message that could only be true AFTER a specific date had arrived. That is why the renewed proclamation could not begin until the prophetic clock ran out (Rev 10:6). The bitter disappointment and the new authority to announce "the hour is come" are joined at the hip: only when the last period closed could the judgment-hour message begin. The bitterness and the new commission are two sides of the same fulfilled date.

[Q] **"Why would a good God put His people through a disappointment at all? Why not spare them?"**
→ Because the disappointment did a work nothing else could. It SIFTED. "Many shall be purified, and made white, and tried" (Dan 12:10) — the trying is named in the same sealed book the angel opened. It separated those who followed a feeling from those who followed the Word; when the feeling failed, only those anchored in Scripture remained to "prophesy again." And it FORCED a deeper reading — the disappointed were driven back to the sanctuary texts they had skipped (just as Emmaus drove the disciples back to "all the prophets"). God did not waste the bitterness; He used it to refine a people and to uncover the truth they had not yet seen. The bitter belly produced the worldwide voice of verse 11.

## Document From History — How the Sweet-Then-Bitter Fell Out

**[The Bible has now proved the doctrine: Daniel's book opens at the time of the end, the prophetic periods terminate, the message is sweet-then-bitter by God's own foretelling, and a renewed worldwide proclamation follows. The MEANING is settled from Scripture alone. What follows is the historicist fulfillment lane only — the record of how this prediction fell out on schedule. The meaning above does not rest on these dates; the dates merely show the prediction came true.]**

- **The sweet: the worldwide Advent awakening, 1840-1844**
  - As the 2300-day reckoning came to its close (Study 14), a study of Daniel's once-sealed prophecies broke out across continents — exactly the "knowledge shall be increased... the wise shall understand" of Daniel 12:4,10. The message that the King was at hand spread on every shore the angel's feet had touched. This was the honey in the mouth: a people thrilled with the blessed hope, the sweetest season the church had known since Pentecost.
  - [DYK🔎] The on-ramp to this awakening was the publicly-verified August 11, 1840 fulfillment of the sixth trumpet (Study 13) — Josiah Litch's date, published two years in advance, falling out to the day. That receipt, signed by history, sent multitudes back to Daniel's reopened book trusting the same year-day method that reckoned the 2300 days. The opening of the book and the rise of the movement fell together, as Daniel 12 foretold.

- **The appointed time and the tarrying (Hab 2:3 fulfilled)**
  - The movement first looked to "about the year 1843" and then, as the chronology was refined, to the autumn of 1844. Between the passing of the early expectation and the seventh-month conviction came a season of waiting and seeming delay — the felt "tarrying" of Habakkuk 2:3. (The chart, the tarrying, and the corrected October 22 reckoning are the subject of Study 17.) The delay matched the prophecy: a vision for "an appointed time," with a tarrying to be waited through by faith.

- **The bitter: the Great Disappointment, autumn 1844**
  - When the expected day passed and the visible second coming did not occur, the believers were plunged into a grief the New Testament had described in advance — the bitter belly of Revelation 10:10. Like the disciples on the Emmaus road, they had the right time and a misread event. The sweetness of the proclamation turned, as the angel had sworn it would, to bitterness in the digesting.
  - [→] The disappointment fell out in the precise ORDER God had written it: sweet first (the awakening), bitter after (the autumn of 1844). The very thing skeptics point to as proof the movement was false is the thing inspiration had named beforehand as proof it was THIS movement.

- **The recovery: the sanctuary "key which unlocked the mystery"**
  - Out of the bitterness came the deeper reading — exactly the Emmaus pattern of going back to "all the prophets." The disappointed students returned to the very text they had under-read: "unto two thousand and three hundred days; then shall the sanctuary be cleansed" (Dan 8:14). They had assumed the "sanctuary" was the earth and its "cleansing" was the burning at the second coming. A fuller study of Daniel, Leviticus 16, and Hebrews 8-9 showed the sanctuary cleansed in 1844 was the HEAVENLY one, and the event of that date a heavenly one (Heb 8:1-2) — not the visible advent. The time had been right; only the event was misread. This recovered sanctuary truth was the key that unlocked the whole disappointment — and it became the "prophesy again" of Revelation 10:11. (Its full proof is Study 18.)
  - [STR] So history ran the prophecy's whole arc: book opened (the awakening) → sweet (the blessed hope proclaimed) → appointed time and tarrying (Hab 2:3) → bitter (autumn 1844) → prophesy again (the renewed worldwide message that became the three angels). Every step the Bible had written beforehand, history then signed.

## Appeal

- Notice the tenderness of God in this chapter: He did not let His people walk into the bitterness blind. He wrote the sweetness AND the bitterness into the prophecy beforehand — so that when the disappointment came, those who knew their Bibles could say, "this, too, He foretold."
- A disappointment that God PREDICTED cannot be a disappointment that God CAUSED by error. The bitter belly was the foretold second half of a true message — not the wreckage of a false one.
- The cure for a misread disappointment is never to throw away the prophecies; it is to read MORE of them. "O fools, and slow of heart to believe ALL that the prophets have spoken." The disappointed were not sent away — they were sent back to the Book, and the Book opened wider than before.
- Have you ever tasted the sweet of a hope, and then the bitter of its apparent failure — and concluded that God had abandoned you, or that His word had failed? Revelation 10 says: stand upon your watch like Habakkuk; the vision is for an appointed time; though it tarry, wait for it. The just shall live by his faith THROUGH the bitterness, not around it.
- The bitter belly was not the end of the Way Home — it was a doorway. "Thou must prophesy again." God turns the disappointment into a commission. Will you let Him do that with yours — let the sweetness return, not as a feeling recovered, but as a deeper truth uncovered and a wider work given?
