# Study 0: Hath God Said? — How to Read This Book

**Before we can establish a single doctrine, we must settle one prior question: how do we read this Book at all? The serpent's first words to humanity were not a denial but a method — "Yea, hath God said?" — the casting of a haze over the plain word so that what God said plainly could be made to mean anything. Every error that follows in this series begins where interpretation is surrendered; every truth begins where we take God at His word.**

## Prep Reading

- Genesis 3:1-5
- 2 Peter 1:19-21
- Isaiah 28:9-10
- 1 Corinthians 2:13
- 2 Timothy 3:15-17
- Isaiah 8:20

---

## The First Shot Was Fired at the Word

**As a prequel, this study stands before Study 1: the cosmic trial is fought over God's character, and the very first shot fired on earth was aimed not at God's throne but at His word — "hath God said?" — so before we trace the controversy we must decide whose reading of the Book we will trust.**

- **The serpent's opening move is a question about interpretation — not a denial**
  - "Now the serpent was more subtil than any beast of the field which the LORD God had made. And he said unto the woman, Yea, hath God said, Ye shall not eat of every tree of the garden?" (Gen 3:1)
  - He does not begin "There is no God." He does not begin "God lied." He begins by unsettling the plain word — did God _really_ say that? did He mean it _that_ way?
  - [STR] The fall is, first of all, a hermeneutical event. The whole catastrophe turns on _how a sentence is read_.

- **Eve's reply already drifts — the word is handled loosely**
  - "But of the fruit of the tree which is in the midst of the garden, God hath said, Ye shall not eat of it, neither shall ye touch it, lest ye die" (Gen 3:3)
  - God never said "neither shall ye touch it." She has added to the word — a small thing, but the grip has already loosened.
  - [→] Once the word is held loosely, the next push topples it.

- **Only after the word is loosened does the open contradiction come**
  - "And the serpent said unto the woman, Ye shall not surely die" (Gen 3:4)
  - "For God doth know that in the day ye eat thereof, then your eyes shall be opened, and ye shall be as gods, knowing good and evil" (Gen 3:5)
  - Notice the bait: a _hidden, higher_ meaning behind the plain command. The plain sense ("ye shall die") is for the simple; the initiated will find a deeper secret ("your eyes shall be opened").
  - [→] The lie always offers a deeper meaning behind the plain word. The serpent does not hand you a different Bible; he hands you a different way of reading it.

[STR] **The serpent's play, in four moves:** (1) _question_ the plain word — "hath God said?" (2) _loosen_ it — "neither shall ye touch it." (3) _reverse_ it — "ye shall not surely die." (4) _offer a higher hidden meaning_ — "your eyes shall be opened, as gods." Every false system since runs this same play. Learn it here and you will see it everywhere in this series.

## How the Bible Says It Must Be Read

**The remedy is not a clever counter-method invented by men. It is the way the Book asks to be read — Scripture testifying about its own interpretation. Watch the Author claim the meaning back.**

- **The Author keeps the meaning — not the private mind of the reader**
  - "Knowing this first, that no prophecy of the scripture is of any private interpretation" (2 Pet 1:20)
  - "For the prophecy came not in old time by the will of man: but holy men of God spake as they were moved by the Holy Ghost" (2 Pet 1:21)
  - The same Spirit who _gave_ the word must _interpret_ it. The word was not loosed by the will of man; it may not be loosed by the will of man either.
  - [→] This is the exact opposite of the serpent. He says the meaning is yours to supply; Peter says the meaning belongs to the One who spoke it.

[DYK🔎] The Greek behind "private interpretation" in 2 Peter 1:20 is _idias epiluseōs_ — literally "one's _own_ un-loosing, one's own releasing." _Idios_ = one's own, private; _epilusis_ = a loosening, a setting free. The very word warns against the reader supplying his _own_ solution. The Author holds the key; you do not get to invent one.

- **The word is sure and light-giving — not a riddle needing a mystic key**
  - "We have also a more sure word of prophecy; whereunto ye do well that ye take heed, as unto a light that shineth in a dark place" (2 Pet 1:19)
  - A lamp does not hide the room; it shows it. The word is _more sure_, not more obscure.

- **Doctrine is built by gathering ALL Scripture says — line upon line**
  - "Whom shall he teach knowledge? and whom shall he make to understand doctrine?" (Isa 28:9)
  - "For precept must be upon precept, precept upon precept; line upon line, line upon line; here a little, and there a little" (Isa 28:10)
  - You do not build a doctrine on one verse pried loose; you gather every passage on the subject and let them agree.

- **Scripture is its own dictionary — it defines its own symbols**
  - "Which things also we speak, not in the words which man's wisdom teacheth, but which the Holy Ghost teacheth; comparing spiritual things with spiritual" (1 Cor 2:13)
  - Where a figure is used, the Bible itself supplies the meaning of the figure. You do not import a meaning; you compare Scripture with Scripture until the Book explains itself.

- **The unity of Scripture is the test — it cannot be broken against itself**
  - "If he called them gods, unto whom the word of God came, and the scripture cannot be broken" (John 10:35)
  - Any reading that breaks one text against another is, by that very fact, the wrong reading.

- **The Scriptures are sufficient and clear — given by inspiration**
  - "And that from a child thou hast known the holy scriptures, which are able to make thee wise unto salvation through faith which is in Christ Jesus" (2 Tim 3:15)
  - "All scripture is given by inspiration of God, and is profitable for doctrine, for reproof, for correction, for instruction in righteousness" (2 Tim 3:16)
  - "That the man of God may be perfect, throughly furnished unto all good works" (2 Tim 3:17)

- **Add nothing; take nothing away — to supply a hidden meaning is to add to the word**
  - "Every word of God is pure: he is a shield unto them that put their trust in him" (Prov 30:5)
  - "Add thou not unto his words, lest he reprove thee, and thou be found a liar" (Prov 30:6)
  - "If any man shall add unto these things, God shall add unto him the plagues that are written in this book" (Rev 22:18)
  - "And if any man shall take away from the words of the book of this prophecy, God shall take away his part out of the book of life" (Rev 22:19)
  - [→] To read a secret meaning _into_ the words that the words do not carry is to ADD to the word. The curse falls on the method, not just on a stray phrase.

- **The Bible's own rule, gathered into one:**
  - Scripture is its own expositor; take the plain sense unless the text itself marks a figure
  - Let the Author — the Spirit — interpret, never the private mind
  - Gather every passage before you conclude; let them agree
  - Never break Scripture against itself
  - Add nothing; take nothing away

[DYK🔎] This is exactly the method William Miller recovered — but it is the BIBLE'S method, not a man's invention. He stopped arguing with the Book and let it read itself, then wrote out the rules with the proof-texts printed underneath each one. The next section lays them out and shows how every rule is proven by the very Scriptures he cited.

> "He submitted himself to the Bible as the word of God, the only sufficient, infallible rule. He saw that it must be its own interpreter. He dared not attempt to explain Scripture to sustain a preconceived theory or doctrine, but held it his duty to learn what is its direct and obvious teaching." (GC 173.2, on Zwingli)

## The Rules, and How the Bible Proves Each One

**When a man sat down with nothing but the Book and a determination to let it explain itself, the same rules surfaced — because they were never his rules. William Miller wrote out fourteen, but he did a thing most rule-makers never do: under each rule he printed the Scriptures that PROVE it. The rule is only the summary; the verse beneath it is the authority. Read them and watch — every rule is the Book describing how it asks to be read. We do not keep these rules because Miller taught them; we keep them because the Bible teaches them, and Miller merely gathered them.**

- **Rule 1 — Every word must have its proper bearing on the subject.**
  - _Proof:_ "Till heaven and earth pass, one jot or one tittle shall in no wise pass from the law, till all be fulfilled" (Matt 5:18) — not one stroke is idle; every word is weighed.

- **Rule 2 — All Scripture is necessary, and may be understood by diligent study.**
  - _Proof:_ "All scripture is given by inspiration of God, and is profitable for doctrine... that the man of God may be perfect, throughly furnished" (2 Tim 3:16-17) — the whole is given, the whole is profitable, the whole may be understood.

- **Rule 3 — Nothing in Scripture will be hid from those who ask in faith.**
  - _Proof:_ "If any of you lack wisdom, let him ask of God... and it shall be given him. But let him ask in faith, nothing wavering" (Jas 1:5-6); "The secret things belong unto the LORD our God: but those things which are revealed belong unto us" (Deut 29:29).

- **Rule 4 — To understand doctrine, bring ALL the Scriptures together on the subject; if your theory has no contradiction, you cannot be in error.**
  - _Proof:_ "For precept must be upon precept... line upon line; here a little, and there a little" (Isa 28:10); "beginning at Moses and all the prophets, he expounded... in all the scriptures the things concerning himself" (Luke 24:27).

- **Rule 5 — Scripture must be its own expositor, since it is a rule of itself.**
  - _Proof:_ "comparing spiritual things with spiritual" (1 Cor 2:13); "The law of the LORD is perfect... the testimony of the LORD is sure, making wise the simple" (Ps 19:7). If a teacher's guess or creed becomes the rule, then his wisdom — not the Bible — is your authority.
  - [→] This is the keystone rule, and it is pure Scripture: the Book defines its own terms. Lose it, and every other rule collapses.

- **Rules 6-9 — Prophecy is given in visions, figures, and parables, and the same things are revealed again and again; combine them all into one.**
  - _Proof:_ "I have also spoken by the prophets, and I have multiplied visions, and used similitudes" (Hos 12:10); "All these things spake Jesus unto the multitude in parables" (Matt 13:34); Pharaoh's doubled dream (Gen 41:1-32) and Daniel's repeated outlines (Dan 2; 7) are the same future told twice — so they are read together, not against each other.

- **Rule 10 — Figures always carry a figurative meaning, and the Bible itself supplies the key.**
  - _Proof:_ the Book defines its own symbols — waters = peoples ("The waters which thou sawest... are peoples, and multitudes, and nations," Rev 17:15); a lamp = the Word ("Thy word is a lamp unto my feet," Ps 119:105); beasts = kingdoms (Dan 7:17); a day = a year ("I have appointed thee each day for a year," Eze 4:6).
  - [DYK🔎] Notice rule 10 does not invent these meanings — it _cites_ them. Every symbol Miller lists is one the Bible decodes for you elsewhere. The interpreter never supplies the meaning; he finds where the Book already gave it (rule 12: "trace your figurative word through your Bible, and where you find it explained, put it on your figure").

- **Rule 11 — How to know when a word is figurative: if the plain sense makes good sense and does no violence to nature, take it literally; if not, figuratively.**
  - _Proof:_ a sun-clothed woman or a seven-headed beast (Rev 12:1-2; 17:3) cannot be literal without violence to nature — so the text itself signals the figure. **Literal unless the text forces a figure** — the plain sense is the default, never despised.

- **Rule 13 — To know you have the right historical fulfilment: if EVERY word of the prophecy is literally fulfilled, the history is true; if one word lacks fulfilment, look for another event.**
  - _Proof:_ "For God takes care that history and prophecy doth agree" — "they trusted in thee, and were not confounded" (Ps 22:5); "Israel shall be saved... ye shall not be ashamed nor confounded world without end" (Isa 45:17). This is the test the whole prophetic chain in this series will pass — prophecy spec laid against documented history, word for word.

- **Rule 14 — The most important rule: you must have faith — a faith willing to surrender the dearest thing on earth rather than disbelieve God's word.**
  - _Proof:_ "But without faith it is impossible to please him" (Heb 11:6); "He that... numbers the hairs of our head" will "guard the translation of his own word" for those who trust Him — the plowman without Hebrew or Greek may yet be kept from erring far from the truth.

[Q] **"Isn't this just imposing a man's system on the Bible?"**
→ It is the opposite. A man imposing a system gives you his rule and asks you to trust _him_. Miller gave you the rule and then printed the verse underneath, inviting you to check him against the Book — and to throw out any rule the Book will not support. Test rule 5 by 1 Corinthians 2:13. Test rule 10 by Revelation 17:15 and Ezekiel 4:6. Test rule 4 by Isaiah 28:10. Every rule answers to Scripture; that is the difference between a method drawn _from_ the word and a meaning forced _onto_ it. The rules are not above the Bible — they are the Bible, summarized.

[STR] **The whole of it in one line:** these are not Miller's rules — they are the Bible's, written down by a man who refused to argue with the Book. Take the plain sense; let Scripture define its own figures; gather every passage; lay prophecy against history word for word; and believe God. Every doctrine in this series is built with these tools, and every tool is forged from a text.

## The Counterfeit Named — Origen and the "Spiritual" Method

**There is a rival method, and it has a name and a pedigree. It is the open door through which every error in this series will walk. To know it, trace it to its fountainhead.**

- **The pagan-platonic ROOT: Ammonius Saccas at Alexandria**
  - His "grand object... to bring all sects and religions into harmony" required him "particularly by allegorical interpretations to remove all impediments out of his way" (GEP 386.1)
  - The system underneath was "AEgyptiaco-Platonic philosophy" — pantheism, the eternity of the world, the soul, demons (GEP 386.2-3) — pagan doctrine "embellished" with "forms of expression borrowed from the sacred Scriptures" (GEP 386.3)
  - [→] Mark it: the _content_ was heathen; the _vocabulary_ was Bible. Allegory was the tool that let one pour pagan wine into Scripture's cup.

- **The seam: two meanings, the husk and the kernel**
  - These philosophers "believed the language of Scripture to contain two meanings; the one obvious, and corresponding with the direct import of the words; the other recondite, and concealed under the words, like a nut by the shell. The former they neglected, as of little value" (GEP 386.4)
  - The plain sense becomes the shell to be cracked and thrown away; the "real" meaning is whatever the interpreter finds hidden inside.

- **Clement → Origen: the method multiplied "from his own wild imagination"**
  - "Origen imbibed all the allegorical and mystifying processes of both Ammonius and Clement, and multiplied upon them from his own wild imagination. He was not content with finding two meanings in the Scriptures as those before him, but took the secondary sense, the hidden meaning, and added to it four additional meanings of his own" (GEP 387.2)
  - The result: "in every passage of Scripture there are at least three meanings, and there may be any number from three to six" (GEP 387.2)

- **Origen's actual system: the literal sense is the "baser" sense — to be despised**
  - "Man is composed of three parts... The Scriptures resemble man, and therefore have a threefold sense: (a) a literal sense which corresponds to the body; (b) a moral sense corresponding to the soul; and (c) a mystical sense which corresponds to the mind" (GEP 388.1)
  - "As the body is the baser part of man, so the literal is the baser sense of Scripture; and as the body often betrays good men into sin, so the literal sense of Scripture often leads into error" (GEP 388.1)
  - The literal "was to be despised and separated as far as possible from the hidden sense, and counted of the least possible worth" (GEP 388.2)

- **Origen's own damning words**
  - "The source of many evils lies in adhering to the carnal or external part of Scripture. Those who do so will not attain to the kingdom of God. Let us therefore seek after the spirit and substantial fruit of the word, which are hidden and mysterious" (GEP 388.3, quoting Origen)
  - And the logical next step: "The Scriptures are of little use to those who understand them as they are written" (GEP 388.5, quoting Origen)
  - [→] Read that last line slowly. It is the exact inversion of "a light that shineth in a dark place." Origen has turned the lamp into a locked box.

- **The same Alexandrian school — and where it led**
  - "In that dismal mixture of downright heathenism, and the profession and forms of Christianity in the philosophical schools of Ammonius Saccas, Clement, and Origen, in Alexandria" (ECE 95.2)
  - "With such a system as this for a basis, it is logical enough that the Catholic church should forbid the common people to read the Scriptures. For Origen is one of the chiefest Fathers of the Catholic Church" (GEP 388.5)
  - [→] If the plain word is worthless and only the initiated can find the secret, then of course the common people must not read it for themselves. The allegorical method and the locked Bible are the same door, swinging.

## The Fruits Thereof — Why So Many Competing Interpretations

**This answers the modern complaint: why is there so much contention over "what the Bible means"? Why so many denominations, so many readings? The division is not in the Book. It is in the method men bring to the Book.**

- **Despise the plain sense, and there is no longer any control on meaning**
  - Once a "hidden meaning" is admitted behind the words, every interpreter supplies his own secret key — and Scripture can be made to mean anything
  - The allegorical method is the _mother_ of doctrinal confusion: it spiritualized away the literal second advent, the literal resurrection, the literal kingdom, the plain commandments
  - Miller named the symptom exactly: explain by fancy, "and by this means there will be as many different explanations as there are ingenious men. But I dare not trifle thus with the word of God" (MWV2 233.3)
  - [→] By the literal / Scripture-its-own-expositor method the Bible is one harmonious whole. By the allegorical / private-meaning method it becomes a wax nose — bent to whatever face the interpreter wants.

- **The line between illustration and interpretation**
  - A figure the text itself marks (a parable Christ Himself explains) _teaches_ the plain truth. A hidden meaning the interpreter imports _replaces_ it.
  - Miller's rule: figures "must be explained in the same way... by the subject and Bible" (MWV1 21.7); Christ "has given us rules by which to explain parables, by explaining some himself" (MWV2 233.3) — the Author marks His own figures.

- **A legitimate allegory used as ILLUSTRATION vs. allegory used as INTERPRETATION**
  - "In a loathsome dungeon... John Bunyan breathed the very atmosphere of heaven; and there he wrote his wonderful allegory of the pilgrim's journey from the land of destruction to the celestial city" (GC 252.2)
  - Bunyan's _Pilgrim's Progress_ is allegory honestly labeled — a story written to picture a plain truth. Origen's allegory is the opposite: a plain truth dissolved to find a hidden one.
  - [→] An illustration carries the truth _toward_ you. An "interpretation" of this kind carries the truth _away_ from you and leaves its own invention in its place. The first serves the word; the second supplants it.

[Q] **"So who decides which reading is right?"**
→ Not the cleverest interpreter — the Author. The whole question collapses into the one Peter already settled: "no prophecy of the scripture is of any private interpretation" (2 Pet 1:20). The man with three to six hidden meanings can never be answered, because he answers to nothing outside his own imagination. The man who lets Scripture explain Scripture _can_ be checked — by Scripture. One method is accountable to the Book; the other is accountable only to itself. That is why the contention never ends on the allegorical side, and why it _can_ end on the literal side.

[DYK🔎] Peter warned of the same wresting in his own day: "in which are some things hard to be understood, which they that are unlearned and unstable _wrest_, as they do also the other scriptures, unto their own destruction" (2 Pet 3:16). The word is "wrest" — to twist, to torture into a shape. The competing-interpretations problem is not new and not mysterious. It is the old twisting, done by men who first despised the plain sense.

## The Return — The Sure Word

**The remedy is as old as the Bereans and as plain as a lamp. Take God at His plain word; let the Spirit who wrote it interpret it by itself; add nothing. This is the foundation on which the whole Way Home series stands.**

- **Search the word for yourself — daily, to verify**
  - "These were more noble than those in Thessalonica, in that they received the word with all readiness of mind, and searched the scriptures daily, whether those things were so" (Acts 17:11)
  - The Bereans did not crack the husk for a secret; they checked the plain word against the plain word. That is nobility of mind.
  - "Search the scriptures; for in them ye think ye have eternal life: and they are they which testify of me" (John 5:39)

- **Take heed to the more sure word — the lamp, not the riddle**
  - "We have also a more sure word of prophecy... as unto a light that shineth in a dark place" (2 Pet 1:19)
  - The word is the light _by which_ you walk this series — not a darkness needing a mystic to light it.

- **The final test: the law and the testimony**
  - "To the law and to the testimony: if they speak not according to this word, it is because there is no light in them" (Isa 8:20)
  - Every teacher, every reading, every "deeper meaning" in the studies to come is to be measured here. Not "is it ingenious?" but "does it speak according to this word?"

> "The Bible is its own expositor. One passage will prove to be a key that will unlock other passages, and in this way light will be shed upon the hidden meaning of the word. By comparing different texts treating on the same subject, viewing their bearing on every side, the true meaning of the Scriptures will be made evident." (CE 85.1)

## Appeal

- The first lie ever told was not "there is no God." It was "hath God said?" — a haze cast over the plain word
- Two methods stand in the field, and they are irreconcilable: take God at His plain word and let Scripture explain itself — or despise the plain word and supply a secret meaning of your own
- The first guards the sheep. The second is the open door to every error in this series
- Settle this one question first, and the rest of the series will hold. Surrender it, and nothing after it can stand.
- Will you take God at His word?
