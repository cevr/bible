# 6. Daniel 9 — The Seventy Weeks Unlock the 2300 Days (The Keystone)

> _This is the keystone of the whole historicist arch. In Daniel 8 the angel Gabriel
> explained ram, he-goat, and little horn, but left Daniel fainting and the 2300 days
> unexplained ("none understood it"). In chapter 9 Gabriel returns to "give skill and
> understanding" and finishes the unfinished vision. Seventy weeks — 490 years — are
> "cut off" (determined) from the 2300, dating from the decree to restore Jerusalem
> (Artaxerxes, 457 B.C.). Those 490 years measure off the Messiah to the day: 69 weeks
> (483 yrs) to His anointing at baptism (A.D. 27); cut off in the midst of the last week
> (A.D. 31 crucifixion); the 70 weeks closing A.D. 34 when the gospel turns to the
> Gentiles. Subtract 490 from 2300 and 1810 years remain past A.D. 34 — reaching the
> autumn of 1844. Locate the seventy weeks and the 2300 days are surely located.
> (DAR 192, 206-207; GC 326-328, 410.)_

### Gabriel returns to finish the unexplained vision of Daniel 8

- **Daniel 8:14** — "Unto two thousand and three hundred days; then shall the sanctuary be cleansed." The longest time-prophecy in Scripture — but in chapter 8 it is left without a starting date.

  > "The angel had been sent to Daniel for the express purpose of explaining to him the point which he had failed to understand in the vision of the eighth chapter, the statement relative to time — 'unto two thousand and three hundred days; then shall the sanctuary be cleansed.'" — GC 326.

  > "This must therefore have been the point which he did not understand; and as without this the other portions of the vision were of no avail, he could well say, while the application of this period was left in obscurity, that he did not understand the vision." — DAR 191.

- **Daniel 8:26-27** — The vision of the evening and morning "is true," yet shut up "for many days"; Daniel "fainted, and was sick," and "none understood it." The 2300-day number was given but its application was withheld — the loose end chapter 9 ties.

  > "Why did not Gabriel at this time fully carry out his instructions, and cause Daniel to understand the vision? — Because Daniel had received all that he could then bear. Further instruction is therefore deferred to a future time." — DAR 182.

  > "He could endure no more, and the angel left him for a time. Daniel 'fainted, and was sick certain days.' 'And I was astonished at the vision,' he says, 'but none understood it.'" — GC 325-326.

- **Daniel 9:1-2** — Daniel, "in the first year of Darius," understood "by books" Jeremiah's seventy years of captivity were ending — and so prayed for his people, mistaking those literal seventy years for the indignation.

  > "Daniel thought seventy years' captivity in Babylon was the indignation. Gabriel told him, not so. But 'seventy sevens are determined upon thy people and upon thy holy city, to finish the transgression and to make an end of sins.'" — PREX1 130.

- **Daniel 9:21** — "Even the man Gabriel, whom I had seen in the vision at the beginning" — the very same angel of chapter 8 — was "caused to fly swiftly" to Daniel "about the time of the evening oblation." The same messenger comes back to finish the same vision.

  > "If this view of the subject is correct, we should naturally expect, when the angel completed his explanation of the vision, that he would commence with the very point which had been omitted; namely, the time. And this we find to be true in fact." — DAR 191.

  > "That commission must be fulfilled. In obedience to it, the angel, some time afterward, returned to Daniel, saying: 'I am now come forth to give thee skill and understanding;' 'therefore understand the matter, and consider the vision.'" — GC 325.

- **Daniel 9:22-23** — Gabriel: "I am now come forth to give thee skill and understanding... therefore understand the matter, and consider the vision." He sends Daniel back to "the vision" — the only vision left unexplained, that of the 2300 days.

  > "There was one important point in the vision of chapter 8 which had been left unexplained, namely, that relating to time — the period of the 2300 days; therefore the angel, in resuming his explanation, dwells chiefly upon the subject of time." — GC 325.

### "Seventy weeks are cut off" — the seventy weeks are part of the 2300 days

- **Daniel 9:24** — "Seventy weeks are determined upon thy people and upon thy holy city" — 70 weeks of years = 490 years, to finish transgression, make an end of sins, make reconciliation, bring in everlasting righteousness, seal up vision and prophecy, and anoint the most Holy.

  > "After citing Daniel's attention back to the former vision in the most direct and emphatic manner, and assuring him that he had now come forth to give him understanding in the matter, he commences upon the very point there omitted, and says, 'Seventy weeks are determined upon thy people and upon thy holy city.'" — DAR 191.

  > "After bidding Daniel 'understand the matter, and consider the vision,' the very first words of the angel are: 'Seventy weeks are determined upon thy people and upon thy Holy City.' The word here translated 'determined' literally signifies 'cut off.'" — GC 326.

- **The Hebrew "determined" = "cut off."** The word _nehhtak_ (Dan. 9:24) "properly" means "to cut off" — Gesenius, Stockius, the Septuagint, Theodotion, and the Vulgate all carry the sense of cutting. The 490 years are cut off — but from what?

  > "This word Gesenius, in his Hebrew Lexicon, defines as follows: 'Properly, to cut off; tropically, to divide; and so to determine, to decree.'… Thus Chaldaic and Rabbinical authority, and that of the earliest versions, the Septuagint and Vulgate, give the single signification of cutting off, to this verb." — DAR 192.

- **There is only one period to cut from.** "There is no period from which the seventy weeks could be cut off but the 2300 days of the previous vision." The 70 weeks are the first 490 years of the 2300; therefore the two periods begin together.

  > "The language cannot be intelligibly referred to anything else;… there is no period from which the seventy weeks could be cut off but the 2300 days of the previous vision.… Cut off from what? — The 2300 days, most assuredly." — DAR 192.

  > "As the 2300 days was the only period of time mentioned in chapter 8, it must be the period from which the seventy weeks were cut off; the seventy weeks must therefore be a part of the 2300 days, and the two periods must begin together." — GC 326.

- **Numbers 14:34** — "each day for a year" — the wilderness sentence, God's own rule that a prophetic day stands for a literal year.

  > "The explanation of these prophetic periods is based on what is called the 'year-day principle;' that is, making each day stand for a year, according to the Scriptural rule for the application of symbolic time. Ezekiel 4:6; Numbers 14:34." — DAR 197.

- **Ezekiel 4:6** — "I have appointed thee each day for a year." The second witness to the year-day principle that turns 70 weeks (490 days) into 490 years and 2300 days into 2300 years.

  > "What is more conclusive than all else is the fact that the prophecies have actually been fulfilled on this principle, — a demonstration of its correctness from which there is no appeal." — DAR 197.

### The starting date: the decree to restore and build Jerusalem, 457 B.C.

- **Daniel 9:25** — Reckon "from the going forth of the commandment to restore and to build Jerusalem unto the Messiah the Prince" — seven weeks and threescore and two weeks (69 weeks); "the street shall be built again, and the wall, even in troublous times." The decree, not the captivity's end, is the anchor.

  > "The angel now gives to Daniel the event which is to mark the commencement of the seventy weeks. They were to date from the going forth of the commandment to restore and build Jerusalem.… If, now, we can find a date which will harmonize with all these events, we have, beyond a doubt, the true application." — DAR 196.

  > "The seventy weeks were declared by the angel to date from the going forth of the commandment to restore and build Jerusalem. If the date of this commandment could be found, then the starting point for the great period of the 2300 days would be ascertained." — GC 326.

- **Four candidate decrees — only one fits.** Cyrus (B.C. 536, Ezra 1) and Darius (B.C. 519, Ezra 6) concerned only the temple; Nehemiah's grant (B.C. 444, Neh. 2) was merely verbal and too late. Only Artaxerxes' decree to Ezra (B.C. 457, Ezra 7) restored the full civil and ecclesiastical state.

  > "There are but four events which can be taken as answering to the commandment to restore and build Jerusalem.… these decrees had reference principally to the restoration of the temple and the temple-worship of the Jews, and not to the restoration of their civil state and polity, all of which must be included in the expression, 'To restore and to build Jerusalem.'" — DAR 197-200.

- **Ezra 7:11-26** — The full decree of Artaxerxes to Ezra: silver and gold, temple service, and power to "set magistrates and judges... whether it be unto death, or to banishment" — a restoration of the whole Jewish polity, the only grant ample enough to "restore and build Jerusalem."

  > "It empowered him to ordain laws, set magistrates and judges, and execute punishment even unto death; in other words, to restore the Jewish state, civil and ecclesiastical, according to the law of God and the ancient customs of that people. Inspiration has seen fit to preserve this decree; and a full and accurate copy of it is given in the seventh chapter of the book of Ezra." — DAR 198.

  > "In the seventh chapter of Ezra the decree is found. Verses 12-26. In its completest form it was issued by Artaxerxes, king of Persia, 457 B.C." — GC 326.

- **Ezra 6:14** — The Jews built "according to the commandment of Cyrus, and Darius, and Artaxerxes" — three kings, but "the commandment" (singular). The three decrees are reckoned as one whole, perfected and "gone forth" only in the grant to Ezra.

  > "The decrees of these three kings are spoken of as one, — 'the commandment'… showing that they are all reckoned as a unit, the different decrees being but the successive steps by which the work was accomplished. And this decree could not be said to have 'gone forth,' as intended by the prophecy, till the last permission which the prophecy required was embodied in the decree." — DAR 206.

  > "These three kings, in originating, reaffirming, and completing the decree, brought it to the perfection required by the prophecy to mark the beginning of the 2300 years." — GC 326-327.

- **Nehemiah 2:1-8** — Nehemiah's request "in the twentieth year of Artaxerxes" (B.C. 444) was only spoken permission with travel-letters; he found Jerusalem's work already underway (Neh. 2:16) under Ezra's decree, finishing in 52 days — far short of the prophecy's seven weeks (49 years) for the building. Hence not the starting point.

  > "Permission was granted him, but we have no evidence that it was anything more than verbal.… When he arrived at Jerusalem, he found rulers and priests, nobles and people, already engaged in the work of building Jerusalem.… These were, of course, acting under the decree given to Ezra thirteen years before. And finally, Nehemiah, having arrived at Jerusalem, finished the work he came to accomplish, in fifty-two days." — DAR 199-200.

- **The 457 B.C. date is fixed by astronomy.** The canon of Ptolemy, "demonstrated by the concurrent agreement of more than twenty eclipses," places the seventh of Artaxerxes at B.C. 457 — a date that cannot be moved without unsettling all chronology.

  > "This canon places the seventh year of Artaxerxes in the year B.C. 457; and the accuracy of this canon is demonstrated by the concurrent agreement of more than twenty eclipses. This date we cannot change from B.C. 457, without first demonstrating the inaccuracy of Ptolemy's canon." — DAR 205.

### The 69 weeks (483 years): to Messiah the Prince — A.D. 27 anointing

- **Daniel 9:25** — "unto the Messiah the Prince shall be seven weeks, and threescore and two weeks" — 69 weeks = 483 years. From the autumn of 457 B.C., 483 years reach to the autumn of A.D. 27.

  > "Sixty-nine weeks, or 483 years, were to extend to Messiah the Prince. Dating from B.C. 457, they end in A.D. 27." — DAR 201.

  > "The decree of Artaxerxes went into effect in the autumn of 457 B.C. From this date, 483 years extend to the autumn of A.D. 27.… At that time this prophecy was fulfilled." — GC 327.

- **"Messiah" means "the Anointed One."** At A.D. 27 Christ was anointed — not crowned with oil but with the Spirit, at His baptism, exactly where the 483 years end.

  > "The word 'Messiah' signifies 'the Anointed One.' In the autumn of A.D. 27 Christ was baptized by John and received the anointing of the Spirit." — GC 327.

- **Matthew 3:16-17** — At His baptism "the Spirit of God" descended "like a dove" and the Father's voice declared, "This is my beloved Son." The visible anointing of the Messiah the Prince.

  > "What event then occurred? Luke thus informs us: 'Now when all the people were baptized,… the Holy Ghost descended in a bodily shape like a dove upon him, and a voice came from heaven, which said, Thou art my beloved Son; in thee I am well pleased.' Luke 3:21, 22; margin, A.D. 27." — DAR 201.

- **Acts 10:38** — "God anointed Jesus of Nazareth with the Holy Ghost and with power" — Peter naming the baptism as the anointing that made Him Messiah (the Anointed).

  > "The apostle Peter testifies that 'God anointed Jesus of Nazareth with the Holy Ghost and with power.' Acts 10:38." — GC 327.

- **Luke 4:18-19** — "The Spirit of the Lord is upon me, because he hath anointed me to preach the gospel... to preach the acceptable year of the Lord." Christ Himself claims the anointing the 69 weeks foretold.

  > "And the Saviour Himself declared: 'The Spirit of the Lord is upon Me, because He hath anointed Me to preach the gospel to the poor.' Luke 4:18." — GC 327.

- **Mark 1:14-15** — Straightway after His baptism Jesus came "preaching the gospel of the kingdom of God... The time is fulfilled." The only prophetic time then closing was the 69 weeks to Messiah the Prince — and the Messiah announces its fulfilment with His own lips.

  > "After this, Jesus came 'preaching the gospel of the kingdom of God, and saying, The time is fulfilled.' Mark 1:14, 15.… no prophetic period can be found then terminating, except the sixty-nine weeks of the prophecy of Daniel.… The Messiah had now come; and with his own lips he announced the termination of that period which was to be marked by his manifestation." — DAR 201-202.

  > "After His baptism He went into Galilee, 'preaching the gospel of the kingdom of God, and saying, The time is fulfilled.' Mark 1:14, 15." — GC 327.

- **Luke 3:1** — "In the fifteenth year of the reign of Tiberius Caesar" John's ministry — and soon Christ's baptism — is dated; the 15th of Tiberius corresponds to A.D. 27, the very year the 483 years expire. (The seeming clash with "about thirty years" is resolved by the four-year error in the Dionysian era — Christ was born about B.C. 4.)

  > "The answer to this question is found in the fact that Christ was born between three and four years before the beginning of the Christian era.… His birth is carried back to the latter part of U.C. 749, a little over three years before A.D. 1. Christ was therefore thirty years of age in A.D. 27." — DAR 201.

- **Galatians 4:4** — "When the fulness of the time was come, God sent forth his Son" — the "fulness of the time" is no vague phrase but the precise terminus of the prophetic 69 weeks.

  > "The decree of Artaxerxes went into effect in the autumn of 457 B.C. From this date, 483 years extend to the autumn of A.D. 27.… At that time this prophecy was fulfilled." — GC 327.

### The midst of the week: Messiah cut off — A.D. 31 crucifixion

- **Daniel 9:26** — "And after threescore and two weeks shall Messiah be cut off, but not for himself" — the Anointed One slain, His death substitutionary ("not for himself"), and the city and sanctuary doomed to "the prince that shall come" (Rome).

  > "After threescore and two weeks, according to the record, the Messiah was to be cut off. It is the same as if it had read: And after threescore and two weeks, in the midst of the seventieth week, shall Messiah be cut off, and cause the sacrifice and oblation to cease.… the crucifixion is definitely located in the middle of the seventieth week." — DAR 202-203.

  > "The prophet in the last part of verse 27 speaks of the soon-following destruction of Jerusalem by the Roman power; and finally of the destruction of that power itself, called in the margin 'the desolator.'" — DAR 211.

- **Daniel 9:27** — "He shall confirm the covenant with many for one week" (the last seven years, A.D. 27-34); "and in the midst of the week he shall cause the sacrifice and the oblation to cease." The middle of the 70th week — three and a half years after A.D. 27 — falls in the spring of A.D. 31.

  > "The Messiah was to confirm the covenant with many for one week. This would be the last week of the seventy, or the last seven years of the 490. In the midst of the week, the prophecy informs us, he should cause the sacrifice and oblation to cease. These Jewish ordinances, pointing to the death of Christ, could cease only at the cross." — DAR 202-203.

  > "'In the midst of the week He shall cause the sacrifice and the oblation to cease.' In A.D. 31, three and a half years after His baptism, our Lord was crucified." — GC 327-328.

- **Matthew 27:50-51** — When Jesus "yielded up the ghost... the veil of the temple was rent in twain from the top to the bottom." Heaven itself caused the sacrifice and oblation to cease at the cross; type met antitype, the shadow-service ended.

  > "These Jewish ordinances, pointing to the death of Christ, could cease only at the cross; and there they did virtually come to an end, though the outward observance was kept up till the destruction of Jerusalem, A.D. 70." — DAR 202.

  > "With the great sacrifice offered upon Calvary, ended that system of offerings which for four thousand years had pointed forward to the Lamb of God. Type had met antitype, and all the sacrifices and oblations of the ceremonial system were there to cease." — GC 328.

- **A.D. 31 fixed by His four Passovers.** Christ kept four Passovers (John 2:13; 5:1; 6:4; 13:1), giving a ministry of three and a half years from autumn A.D. 27 to spring A.D. 31 — "exactly... the midst of the seventieth week." Thirteen ancient authorities locate the crucifixion in the spring of A.D. 31.

  > "As he began his ministry in the autumn of A.D. 27, his first Passover would occur the following spring, A.D. 28; his second, A.D. 29; his third, A.D. 30; and his fourth and last, A.D. 31. This gives us three years and a half for his public ministry, and corresponds exactly to the prophecy that he should be cut off in the midst, or middle, of the seventieth week.… Here, then, are thirteen credible authorities locating the crucifixion of Christ in the spring of A.D. 31." — DAR 203-204.

### The 70 weeks close: A.D. 34 — the gospel turns to the Gentiles

- **Daniel 9:24** — The 70 weeks were "determined upon thy people" — allotted to the Jews as the special people of God; when the 490 years run out, that special standing ends. Going forward from the cross (A.D. 31) three and a half years reaches the autumn of A.D. 34.

  > "Going from the crucifixion forward three and a half years, we are brought to the autumn of A.D. 34, as the grand terminating point of the whole period of the seventy weeks.… these are just the events which one would expect to take place when that specified period which was cut off for the Jews, and allotted to them as a peculiar people, should fully expire." — DAR 204-205.

  > "The seventy weeks, or 490 years, especially allotted to the Jews, ended, as we have seen, in A.D. 34.… Then the message of salvation, no longer restricted to the chosen people, was given to the world." — GC 328.

- **Acts 1:8** — "Ye shall be witnesses unto me both in Jerusalem... and unto the uttermost part of the earth." The widening of the gospel beyond the Jews waits on the close of their seventy weeks.

  > "Then the message of salvation, no longer restricted to the chosen people, was given to the world. The disciples, forced by persecution to flee from Jerusalem, 'went everywhere preaching the word.'" — GC 328.

- **Acts 3:25** — "Ye are the children of the prophets, and of the covenant" — within the 70th week (A.D. 27-34) the offer of the covenant goes "to you first," to the Jews; only at A.D. 34 does it pass to all nations.

  > "During this time, extending from A.D. 27 to A.D. 34, Christ, at first in person and afterward by His disciples, extended the gospel invitation especially to the Jews. As the apostles went forth with the good tidings of the kingdom, the Saviour's direction was: 'Go not into the way of the Gentiles… but go rather to the lost sheep of the house of Israel.'" — GC 327-328.

- **The martyrdom of Stephen marks A.D. 34.** The Sanhedrin "sealed its rejection of the gospel by the martyrdom of Stephen and the persecution of the followers of Christ" (Acts 9:1-18); the scattered disciples "went everywhere preaching," and the apostles turned to the Gentiles — exactly the event due when the Jews' allotted period expired.

  > "This date is marked by the martyrdom of Stephen, the formal rejection of the gospel of Christ by the Jewish Sanhedrin in the persecution of his disciples, and the turning of the apostles to the Gentiles. Acts 9:1-18." — DAR 204-205.

  > "Through the action of the Jewish Sanhedrin, the nation sealed its rejection of the gospel by the martyrdom of Stephen and the persecution of the followers of Christ.… The disciples, forced by persecution to flee from Jerusalem, 'went everywhere preaching the word.'" — GC 328.

### The keystone arithmetic: 490 from 2300 → 1844

| Event                                             | Reference                 | Date                 |
| ------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------- | -------------------- |
| Decree to Ezra — "going forth of the commandment" | Ezra 7; Dan. 9:25         | autumn 457 B.C.      |
| 7 weeks (49 yrs): street & wall rebuilt           | Dan. 9:25                 | B.C. 408             |
| 69 weeks (483 yrs): Messiah anointed at baptism   | Dan. 9:25; Mark 1:15      | autumn A.D. 27       |
| Midst of 70th week: Messiah cut off (crucified)   | Dan. 9:26-27; Matt. 27:51 | spring A.D. 31       |
| 70 weeks (490 yrs) end: gospel to the Gentiles    | Dan. 9:24; Acts 9         | autumn A.D. 34       |
| Remainder of the 2300: 2300 − 490 = 1810 yrs      | Dan. 8:14                 | A.D. 34 + 1810       |
| 2300 days end: "the sanctuary shall be cleansed"  | Dan. 8:14                 | autumn **A.D. 1844** |

- **2300 − 490 = 1810.** "The seventy weeks are but the first 490 years of the 2300. Take 490 from 2300, and there remain 1810." The 490 ended in the autumn of A.D. 34.

  > "The seventy weeks are but the first 490 years of the 2300. Take 490 from 2300, and there remain 1810. The 490, as we have seen, ended in the autumn of A.D. 34." — DAR 206-207.

  > "The seventy weeks — 490 days — having been cut off from the 2300, there were 1810 days remaining. After the end of 490 days, the 1810 days were still to be fulfilled." — GC 328.

- **A.D. 34 + 1810 = autumn 1844.** "If to this date we now add the remaining 1810 years... we have the autumn of A.D. 1844. Thus speedily and surely do we find the termination of the 2300 days, when once the seventy weeks have been located."

  > "If to this date we now add the remaining 1810 years, we shall have the termination of the whole period. Thus, to A.D. 34, autumn, add 1810, and we have the autumn of A.D. 1844. Thus speedily and surely do we find the termination of the 2300 days, when once the seventy weeks have been located." — DAR 206-207.

  > "From A.D. 34, 1810 years extend to 1844. Consequently the 2300 days of Daniel 8:14 terminate in 1844. At the expiration of this great prophetic period, upon the testimony of the angel of God, 'the sanctuary shall be cleansed.'" — GC 328.

  > "The first 490 years of the 2300 having then ended, 1810 years would remain. From A.D. 34, 1810 years extend to 1844. 'Then,' said the angel, 'shall the sanctuary be cleansed.'" — GC 410.

- **The same chain in The Great Controversy.** "The seventy weeks — 490 days — having been cut off from the 2300, there were 1810 days remaining... From A.D. 34, 1810 years extend to 1844. Consequently the 2300 days of Daniel 8:14 terminate in 1844."

  > "The seventy weeks — 490 days — having been cut off from the 2300, there were 1810 days remaining.… From A.D. 34, 1810 years extend to 1844. Consequently the 2300 days of Daniel 8:14 terminate in 1844." — GC 328.

  > "The first 490 years of the 2300 having then ended, 1810 years would remain. From A.D. 34, 1810 years extend to 1844.… All the preceding specifications of the prophecy had been unquestionably fulfilled at the time appointed." — GC 410.

- **Miller's witness (1840s).** "The seventy weeks were evidently fulfilled in the year A.D. 33, beginning 457 years B.C., at the going forth of the commandment to Ezra to restore the law and the people to Jerusalem" — Gabriel was "so particular in defining the beginning and the end" precisely because definite time was meant.

  > "The seventy weeks were evidently fulfilled in the year A.D. 33, beginning 457 years B.C., at the going forth of the commandment to Ezra to restore the law and the people to Jerusalem.… Why should the man Gabriel be so particular in defining the beginning and the end of the seventy weeks, if indefinite time only is meant?" — MWV1 43.

- **Litch's witness.** Daniel's prayer over Jeremiah's seventy years is "the true connecting link between the vision of the ram and goat of the 8th chapter and the prophecy of seventy weeks"; Gabriel corrects Daniel — not seventy years of captivity, but seventy sevens "cut off" for the people.

  > "This prayer of Daniel is the true connecting link between the vision of the ram and goat of the 8th chapter and the prophecy of seventy weeks, or the seventy sevens.… Seventy weeks, or sevens, are determined, or cut off, for them to accomplish the national 'transgression, and make an end of sins.'" — PREX1 130.

- **Why it must be the autumn.** It takes 457 full years B.C. and 1843 full years A.D. to make 2300; since Ezra reached Jerusalem only in the fifth month and the work began in the seventh (autumn) of 457, the whole period runs to the seventh month — autumn, Jewish time — of 1844.

  > "It takes 457 full years before Christ, and 1843 full years after, to make 2300.… the period is to be dated, not from the starting of Ezra from Babylon, but from the actual commencement of the work at Jerusalem; which it is not probable could be earlier than the seventh month (autumn) of 457.… The whole period would therefore extend to the seventh month, autumn, Jewish time, of 1844." — DAR 207.

- **The whole arch stands or falls here.** As the pioneers said: "If the connection between the seventy weeks of Daniel 9 and the 2300 days of Daniel 8 does not exist, the whole system is shaken to its foundation; if it does exist, as we suppose, the system must stand." Every specification of Daniel 9 was "unquestionably fulfilled at the time appointed" — fixing 457 B.C. and, with it, 1844 beyond reasonable doubt.

  > "'If the connection between the seventy weeks of Daniel 9 and the 2300 days of Daniel 8 does not exist, the whole system is shaken to its foundation; if it does exist, as we suppose, the system must stand.' — Harmony of the Prophetic Chronology, p. 33." — DAR 195.

  > "All the preceding specifications of the prophecy had been unquestionably fulfilled at the time appointed.… To deny that the days ended at that time was to involve the whole question in confusion, and to renounce positions which had been established by unmistakable fulfillments of prophecy." — GC 410.
