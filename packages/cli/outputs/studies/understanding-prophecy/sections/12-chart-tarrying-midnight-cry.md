# 12. The 1843 & 1850 Charts, the Tarrying Time, and the True Midnight Cry

> _Habakkuk 2 is the hub of the whole Advent experience: "Write the vision, make it plain upon tables" was fulfilled in the prophetic chart of Fitch and Hale (1842-43) and its corrected successor (1850) — the vision made plain on tables, afterward endorsed as "directed by the hand of the Lord." "Though it tarry, wait for it" foretold the tarrying time that followed the spring-1844 disappointment; and the parable of the ten virgins maps the first cry, the slumber, and the TRUE midnight cry — Samuel Snow's seventh-month movement, "Behold, the bridegroom cometh," fixing the tenth day of the seventh month, Oct. 22, 1844, as the cleansing of the sanctuary. The disappointment was resolved when it was seen that the Bridegroom came, not to earth, but to the Ancient of Days (Dan. 7:13) to receive His kingdom. (GC 392-408, 426-432; EW 74, 232-238; TRMC, Aug. 22, 1844.)_

### The vision made plain upon tables — the prophetic chart

- **Habakkuk 2:1** — "I will stand upon my watch, and set me upon the tower, and will watch to see what he will say unto me." The watchman on the tower is the believer who studies prophecy and waits for its word; Bates titled his tract on this very pathway "Second Advent Way Marks and High Heaps," urging the people to "stand continually upon the watch tower in the day time."

  > "Set thee up waymarks, make thee high heaps; set thy heart towards the highway, even the way that thou wentest." Jeremiah 31:21. — BP2 45.

  > "But, bless the Lord, there are some that are fully determined to correct their dead reckoning, by watching every opportunity for an observation of the Sun of Righteousness, and by faith cling fast to all the promises, doing as the Prophet has shown them - 'Stand continually upon the watch tower in the day time, and set in their ward every night,' watching for all the terrestrial and celestial land marks and heaps in their pathway…" — BP2 46.

  > "Among these prophecies was that of Habakkuk 2:1-4: 'I will stand upon my watch, and set me upon the tower, and will watch to see what He will say unto me…'" — GC 392.

- **Habakkuk 2:2** — "Write the vision, and make it plain upon tables, that he may run that readeth it." As early as 1842 this command suggested to Charles Fitch the preparation of a prophetic chart to illustrate Daniel and the Revelation; its publication was regarded as a literal fulfillment of Habakkuk, the vision "made plain upon tables."

  > "As early as 1842 the direction given in this prophecy to 'write the vision, and make it plain upon tables, that he may run that readeth it,' had suggested to Charles Fitch the preparation of a prophetic chart to illustrate the visions of Daniel and the Revelation. The publication of this chart was regarded as a fulfillment of the command given by Habakkuk." — GC 392.

- **Habakkuk 2:2** — The chart of 1843 (Fitch & Hale) was afterward declared inspired in its providential use: "I have seen that the 1843 chart was directed by the hand of the Lord, and that it should not be altered; that the figures were as He wanted them; that His hand was over and hid a mistake in some of the figures, so that none could see it, until His hand was removed." The corrected 1850 chart then displayed the figures rightly.

  > "I have seen that the 1843 chart was directed by the hand of the Lord, and that it should not be altered; that the figures were as He wanted them; that His hand was over and hid a mistake in some of the figures, so that none could see it, until His hand was removed." — EW 74.

- **Daniel 8:14** — "Unto two thousand and three hundred days; then shall the sanctuary be cleansed." This was the central line of the chart — the great prophetic period whose terminus the tables were "made plain" to proclaim.

  > "Then I saw in relation to the 'daily' (Daniel 8:12) that the word 'sacrifice' was supplied by man's wisdom, and does not belong to the text, and that the Lord gave the correct view of it to those who gave the judgment hour cry." — EW 74.

  > "That which led to this movement was the discovery that the decree of Artaxerxes for the restoration of Jerusalem, which formed the starting point for the period of the 2300 days, went into effect in the autumn of the year 457 B.C.… Reckoning from the autumn of 457, the 2300 years terminate in the autumn of 1844." — GC 398-399.

### The day-for-a-year rule the chart was built upon

- **Numbers 14:34** — "Each day for a year, shall ye bear your iniquities" — the inspired key that a prophetic day stands for a literal year, the rule by which the 2300 days were reckoned as 2300 years on the chart.

  > "Following his rule of making Scripture its own interpreter, Miller learned that a day in symbolic prophecy represents a year (Numbers 14:34; Ezekiel 4:6); he saw that the period of 2300 prophetic days, or literal years, would extend far beyond the close of the Jewish dispensation…" — GC 324.

  > "Seventy weeks, then, or 490 days of the 2300, were cut off upon, or allotted to, Jerusalem and the Jews… if these seventy weeks are fulfilled as weeks of years, then the 2300 days, of which these are a part, are so many years." — DAR 193.

- **Ezekiel 4:6** — "I have appointed thee each day for a year" — the second witness to the year-day principle, doubly establishing the measure of the prophetic periods.

  > "Following his rule of making Scripture its own interpreter, Miller learned that a day in symbolic prophecy represents a year (Numbers 14:34; Ezekiel 4:6)…" — GC 324.

  > "By the events given to transpire in the seventy weeks, the prophecy is tested… if these seventy weeks are fulfilled as weeks of years, then the 2300 days, of which these are a part, are so many years. Thus the events of the seventy weeks furnish a key to the whole vision." — DAR 193.

### Though it tarry, wait for it — the tarrying time

- **Habakkuk 2:3** — "For the vision is yet for an appointed time... though it tarry, wait for it; because it will surely come, it will not tarry." No one noticed before the disappointment that an apparent delay — a tarrying time — stood in the same prophecy; after the spring of 1844 this scripture became very significant.

  > "No one, however, then noticed that an apparent delay in the accomplishment of the vision—a tarrying time—is presented in the same prophecy. After the disappointment, this scripture appeared very significant: 'The vision is yet for an appointed time…though it tarry, wait for it; because it will surely come, it will not tarry.'" — GC 392.

- **Habakkuk 2:3** — The disappointed but faithful ones, searching again, "discovered a tarrying time — 'Though it tarry, wait for it.' In their love for Christ's immediate coming, they had overlooked the tarrying of the vision, which was calculated to manifest the true waiting ones." The hand of the Lord was now removed from the figures, and the periods were seen to reach to 1844, not 1843.

  > "The hand of the Lord was removed from the figures, and the mistake was explained. They saw that the prophetic periods reached to 1844… Light from the Word of God shone upon their position, and they discovered a tarrying time—'Though it [the vision] tarry, wait for it.' In their love for Christ's immediate coming, they had overlooked the tarrying of the vision, which was calculated to manifest the true waiting ones." — EW 236.

- **Habakkuk 2:4** — "The just shall live by his faith." The believers held fast through the dark interval by faith, not by sight, exactly as the prophecy that foretold the tarrying also prescribed the remedy.

  > "After the disappointment, this scripture appeared very significant: 'The vision is yet for an appointed time…though it tarry, wait for it; because it will surely come, it will not tarry.... The just shall live by his faith.'" — GC 392.

- **Hebrews 10:36-37** — "Ye have need of patience... For yet a little while, and he that shall come will come, and will not tarry." The apostle's own echo of Habakkuk — patience through delay, then certain coming — strengthened the waiting Advent band.

  > "The waiting ones rejoiced, believing that He who knows the end from the beginning had looked down through the ages and, foreseeing their disappointment, had given them words of courage and hope. Had it not been for such portions of Scripture, admonishing them to wait with patience and to hold fast their confidence in God's word, their faith would have failed in that trying hour." — GC 392-393.

- **Hebrews 10:38** — "Now the just shall live by faith: but if any man draw back, my soul shall have no pleasure in him." The tarrying separated those who lived by faith from those who drew back when the Lord did not come at the expected hour.

  > "And now to take up again the heavy burden of life's cares and perplexities, and to endure the taunts and sneers of a scoffing world, was a terrible trial of faith and patience." — GC 404.

### The two cries — the parable of the ten virgins

- **Matthew 25:1** — "Then shall the kingdom of heaven be likened unto ten virgins, which took their lamps, and went forth to meet the bridegroom." The lamps are the Bible; the going forth is the widespread reformation under the first angel's message proclaiming Christ's soon coming.

  > "The coming of Christ, as announced by the first angel's message, was understood to be represented by the coming of the bridegroom. The widespread reformation under the proclamation of His soon coming, answered to the going forth of the virgins… All had taken their lamps, the Bible, and by its light had gone forth to meet the Bridegroom." — GC 393-394.

- **Matthew 25:2-4** — The wise took oil in their vessels; the foolish took none. The wise had the grace of the Spirit and a personal faith that disappointment could not overthrow; the foolish had only a theory of the truth and good emotions.

  > "The latter class had received the grace of God, the regenerating, enlightening power of the Holy Spirit… These had a personal experience, a faith in God and in His word, which could not be overthrown by disappointment and delay. Others 'took their lamps, and took no oil with them.'… satisfied with the flickering light of good emotions, without a thorough understanding of the truth or a genuine work of grace in the heart." — GC 394.

- **Matthew 25:5** — "While the bridegroom tarried, they all slumbered and slept." The tarrying of the bridegroom is the passing of the spring-1844 time of expectation — the disappointment and seeming delay — during which all, wise and foolish alike, slumbered.

  > "'While the bridegroom tarried, they all slumbered and slept.' By the tarrying of the bridegroom is represented the passing of the time when the Lord was expected, the disappointment, and the seeming delay… 'They all slumbered and slept;' one class in unconcern and abandonment of their faith, the other class patiently waiting till clearer light should be given." — GC 394.

- **Matthew 25:6** — "And at midnight there was a cry made, Behold, the bridegroom cometh; go ye out to meet him." In the summer of 1844, midway between the spring expectation and the autumn fulfilment, the message was proclaimed in the very words of Scripture — this is the TRUE midnight cry, the seventh-month movement.

  > "In the summer of 1844, midway between the time when it had been first thought that the 2300 days would end, and the autumn of the same year, to which it was afterward found that they extended, the message was proclaimed in the very words of Scripture: 'Behold, the Bridegroom cometh!'" — GC 398.

  > "This was the midnight cry, which was to give power to the second angel's message. Angels were sent from heaven to arouse the discouraged saints and prepare them for the great work before them… Angels were sent to the humble, devoted ones, and constrained them to raise the cry, 'Behold, the Bridegroom cometh; go ye out to meet Him!'" — EW 238.

- **Matthew 25:7** — "Then all those virgins arose, and trimmed their lamps." The midnight cry roused the whole waiting body; like a tidal wave it swept the land, fanaticism vanishing before it "like early frost before the rising sun."

  > "Like a tidal wave the movement swept over the land. From city to city, from village to village, and into remote country places it went, until the waiting people of God were fully aroused. Fanaticism disappeared before this proclamation like early frost before the rising sun." — GC 400.

- **Matthew 25:10** — "They that were ready went in with him to the marriage: and the door was shut." Those who, by the testimony of Scripture, followed Christ by faith into the most holy place "went in" to the marriage; this took place in heaven while the saints were yet on earth.

  > "At the appointed time the Bridegroom came, not to the earth, as the people expected, but to the Ancient of Days in heaven, to the marriage… 'They that were ready went in with Him to the marriage: and the door was shut.' They were not to be present in person at the marriage; for it takes place in heaven, while they are upon the earth." — GC 427.

- **Matthew 25:13** — "Watch therefore, for ye know neither the day nor the hour wherein the Son of man cometh." The watching command frames the whole parable — and the disappointment proved that the date of the event, not the spiritual watch, was what God had appointed to test His people.

  > "Chapter 25 opens with the words: 'Then shall the kingdom of heaven be likened unto ten virgins.' Here is brought to view the church living in the last days… In this parable their experience is illustrated by the incidents of an Eastern marriage." — GC 393.

  > "Miller and his associates fulfilled prophecy and gave a message which Inspiration had foretold should be given to the world, but which they could not have given had they fully understood the prophecies pointing out their disappointment… The first and second angels' messages were given at the right time and accomplished the work which God designed to accomplish by them." — GC 405.

### The seventh-month movement — Snow's argument from the types

- **Leviticus 23:27** — "On the tenth day of this seventh month there shall be a day of atonement... ye shall afflict your souls." Snow reasoned from the Mosaic types that, as the spring feasts (Passover, firstfruits, Pentecost) had met their antitype to the very day at Christ's first advent, so the autumn type — the Day of Atonement on the tenth of the seventh month — must meet its antitype in the cleansing of the sanctuary that very year.

  > "Thus we see that those types that pointed to events connected with our Lord's first coming, were fulfilled exactly at the time of their observance. And every one who is not willingly blind must see, and feel too, that those which remain unfulfilled, will be fulfilled with an equally strict regard to time… Those types which were to be observed in the 7th month, have never yet had their fulfilment in the antitype." — TRMC, Aug. 22, 1844, p. 4.

  > "Arguments drawn from the Old Testament types also pointed to the autumn as the time when the event represented by the 'cleansing of the sanctuary' must take place. This was made very clear as attention was given to the manner in which the types relating to the first advent of Christ had been fulfilled." — GC 399.

- **Leviticus 16:29-30** — "In the seventh month, on the tenth day... ye shall afflict your souls... for on that day shall the priest make an atonement for you, to cleanse you." The high priest entered the most holy place once a year on this day to cleanse the sanctuary — the exact type of the antitypical cleansing of Daniel 8:14, which Snow placed on the tenth day of the seventh month, 1844.

  > "Another type is given in Leviticus 23:26-32, that is, the day of atonement or reconciliation, on the tenth day of the 7th month, in which the high priest went into the most holy place of the tabernacle, presenting the blood of the victim before the mercy-seat, after which on the same day be came out and blessed the waiting congregation of Israel… As this was on the tenth day of the 7th month, so on that day Jesus will certainly come, because not a single point of the law is to fail." — TRMC, Aug. 22, 1844, p. 4.

  > "Under the Mosaic system the cleansing of the sanctuary, or the great Day of Atonement, occurred on the tenth day of the seventh Jewish month (Leviticus 16:29-34), when the high priest, having made an atonement for all Israel, and thus removed their sins from the sanctuary, came forth and blessed the people." — GC 399.

  > "In the examination of the sanctuary, on chapter 8:14, we saw that a time came when the earthly sanctuary gave place to the heavenly, and the priestly ministration was transferred to that… The last event, therefore, of the seventy weeks, here brought to view, is the anointing of the heavenly tabernacle, or the opening of the ministration there." — DAR 193.

- **Leviticus 16:33-34** — "He shall make an atonement for the holy sanctuary... And this shall be an everlasting statute... to make an atonement for the children of Israel for all their sins once a year." The yearly, fixed-date character of the type fixed the antitype's date: "the tenth day of the seventh Jewish month... which in the year 1844 fell upon the twenty-second of October."

  > "The tenth day of the seventh month, the great Day of Atonement, the time of the cleansing of the sanctuary, which in the year 1844 fell upon the twenty-second of October, was regarded as the time of the Lord's coming. This was in harmony with the proofs already presented that the 2300 days would terminate in the autumn, and the conclusion seemed irresistible." — GC 399.

- **Leviticus 23:29** — "Whatsoever soul it be that shall not be afflicted in that same day, he shall be cut off from among his people." The solemnity of afflicting the soul on that day underscored, for the movement, the searching, heart-humbling character of the autumn cry.

  > "There was little ecstatic joy, but rather deep searching of heart, confession of sin, and forsaking of the world. A preparation to meet the Lord was the burden of agonizing spirits. There was persevering prayer and unreserved consecration to God." — GC 400.

  > "A mighty work was accomplished by the midnight cry. The message was heart-searching, leading the believers to seek a living experience for themselves. They knew that they could not lean upon one another." — EW 238.

- **Leviticus 25:9** — "Then shalt thou cause the trumpet of the jubile to sound on the tenth day of the seventh month, in the day of atonement." Snow joined the jubilee trumpet to the Day of Atonement: as the jubilee proclaimed liberty and redemption to all the land on the tenth of the seventh month, so the antitypical jubilee would bring "the glorious deliverance of the people of God."

  > "In Leviticus 25:8-13, 23, 24, we find that on the tenth day of the seventh month, in the fiftieth year, the jubilee trumpet was always to be blown, and redemption granted to all the land. Let any man read carefully the connection of this subject, and he must surely see that this is a most striking type of the glorious deliverance of the people of God…" — TRMC, Aug. 22, 1844, p. 4.

### The seventh-month date — Oct. 22, 1844

- **Daniel 8:14** — "Unto two thousand and three hundred days; then shall the sanctuary be cleansed." The discovery that the decree of Artaxerxes (the starting point) went into effect in the autumn of 457 B.C. — not at the year's beginning — moved the terminus of the 2300 years to the autumn of 1844, harmonizing prophecy with the type.

  > "That which led to this movement was the discovery that the decree of Artaxerxes for the restoration of Jerusalem, which formed the starting point for the period of the 2300 days, went into effect in the autumn of the year 457 B.C., and not at the beginning of the year, as had been formerly believed. Reckoning from the autumn of 457, the 2300 years terminate in the autumn of 1844." — GC 398.

- **Leviticus 23:32** — "In the ninth day of the month at even... from even unto even, shall ye celebrate your sabbath." The Day of Atonement was reckoned by the actual Karaite (not Rabbinic) new moon, which placed the tenth day of the seventh month in 1844 on October 22 — the precise date of the true midnight cry.

  > "The tenth day of the seventh month, the great Day of Atonement, the time of the cleansing of the sanctuary, which in the year 1844 fell upon the twenty-second of October, was regarded as the time of the Lord's coming." — GC 399-400.

- **Hebrews 10:37** — "He that shall come will come, and will not tarry." After the long uncertainty, the seventh-month cry came with the force of a definite, scripturally-fixed point of time, and "the conclusion seemed irresistible."

  > "This was in harmony with the proofs already presented that the 2300 days would terminate in the autumn, and the conclusion seemed irresistible." — GC 399-400.

### Why the time CAN be known — answering "no man knoweth"

- **Amos 3:7** — "Surely the Lord GOD will do nothing, but he revealeth his secret unto his servants the prophets." God hides nothing of His great purposes from His waiting people; the prophetic periods were given to be understood, not concealed.

  > "'The secret things,' says Moses, 'belong unto the Lord our God: but those things which are revealed belong unto us and to our children forever;' and the Lord declares by the prophet Amos, that He 'will do nothing, but He revealeth His secret unto His servants the prophets.'… The students of God's word may, then, confidently expect to find the most stupendous event to take place in human history clearly pointed out in the Scriptures of truth." — GC 324.

  > "The Old Testament contains the testimony of the Father concerning his Son, and concerning the time of both his first and second comings. Therefore the time is to be understood." — TRMC, p. 1.

- **Matthew 24:36** — "Of that day and hour knoweth no man, no, not the angels... but my Father only." Snow showed that since this declares the same of the Son as of angels and men, it cannot mean the time is forever unknowable; rather, "none but God the Father maketh known" the time — and through His word He does make it known.

  > "But if it prove this, it likewise proves that the Son of God himself is never to know the time: for the passage declares precisely the same concerning him, that it does concerning angels and men… So in the passage first quoted, it is declared that none but God the Father maketh known the day and hour, that is, the definite time of the second coming of his Son. And this necessarily implies that God makes the time known." — TRMC, Aug. 22, 1844, p. 1.

- **Daniel 12:10** — "None of the wicked shall understand; but the wise shall understand." The wise were promised understanding of the very prophetic time the careless could not see — the warrant of the whole movement.

  > "See Daniel 12:10, 'Many shall be purified, and made white, and tried; but the wicked shall do wickedly; and none of the wicked shall understand; but the wise shall understand.'… It is by the teaching of his word, as we are led therein by the Holy Spirit, that we are to understand the time of the coming of our Glorious King." — TRMC, p. 1.

### The disappointment resolved — the Bridegroom came to the Ancient of Days

- **Daniel 7:13** — "One like the Son of man came with the clouds of heaven, and came to the Ancient of days, and they brought him near before him." This coming — to the Ancient of Days in heaven, NOT to the earth — is the same event as the bridegroom going in to the marriage in the parable. The disappointment lay not in the date but in mistaking the event.

  > "The coming of Christ as our high priest to the most holy place, for the cleansing of the sanctuary, brought to view in Daniel 8:14; the coming of the Son of man to the Ancient of Days, as presented in Daniel 7:13… are descriptions of the same event; and this is also represented by the coming of the bridegroom to the marriage, described by Christ in the parable of the ten virgins, of Matthew 25." — GC 426.

- **Daniel 7:14** — "There was given him dominion, and glory, and a kingdom... his dominion is an everlasting dominion." The marriage of Matthew 25 is Christ's reception of His kingdom from the Ancient of Days; "the bridegroom came... not to the earth, as the people expected, but to the Ancient of Days in heaven, to the marriage, the reception of His kingdom."

  > "The marriage represents the reception by Christ of His kingdom… Christ, as stated by the prophet Daniel, will receive from the Ancient of Days in heaven, 'dominion, and glory, and a kingdom;' He will receive the New Jerusalem, the capital of His kingdom, 'prepared as a bride adorned for her husband.'" — GC 426-427.

- **Daniel 8:14** — The true event of Oct. 22, 1844, was the beginning of the cleansing of the heavenly sanctuary — Christ's entrance into the most holy place — which Daniel 8:14, Daniel 7:13, and Matthew 25 all describe as one. The TIME was right; only the EVENT had been misread.

  > "The coming of Christ as our high priest to the most holy place, for the cleansing of the sanctuary, brought to view in Daniel 8:14; the coming of the Son of man to the Ancient of Days, as presented in Daniel 7:13… are descriptions of the same event; and this is also represented by the coming of the bridegroom to the marriage… of Matthew 25." — GC 426.

  > "Now the important point in this type is the completion of the reconciliation at the coming of the high priest out of the holy place. The high priest was a type of Jesus our High Priest; the most holy place a type of heaven itself…" — TRMC, Aug. 22, 1844, p. 4.

- **Habakkuk 2:20** — "But the LORD is in his holy temple: let all the earth keep silence before him." The answer to the disappointment was that the Lord had entered the holy temple above to begin His closing ministry — exactly the prophet's own conclusion to the vision of the appointed time.

  > "At the appointed time the Bridegroom came, not to the earth, as the people expected, but to the Ancient of Days in heaven, to the marriage, the reception of His kingdom… But they are to understand His work, and to follow Him by faith as He goes in before God." — GC 427.

### The everlasting gospel that drove the chart and the cry

- **Revelation 14:6** — "I saw another angel fly in the midst of heaven, having the everlasting gospel to preach unto them that dwell on the earth." The chart, the tarrying, and the midnight cry were all the outworking of this first angel's worldwide proclamation.

  > "I saw that God was in the proclamation of the time in 1843. It was His design to arouse the people and bring them to a testing point, where they should decide for or against the truth… Ministers laid aside their sectarian views and feelings and united in proclaiming the coming of Jesus. Wherever the message was given, it moved the people." — EW 232.

  > "It is a pleasing feature of the prophetic word that the people of God are never brought into positions of trial and difficulty, and there abandoned… but it takes them through to the end, and shows the issue in every conflict." — DAR 582.

- **Revelation 14:7** — "Fear God, and give glory to him; for the hour of his judgment is come." Miller and the pioneers "sounded far and near the cry, 'Fear God, and give glory to Him; for the hour of His judgment is come'" — the judgment-hour message that the cleansing of the sanctuary, begun Oct. 22, 1844, fulfilled.

  > "He ceased not to preach the everlasting gospel to crowds wherever he was invited, sounding far and near the cry, 'Fear God, and give glory to Him; for the hour of His judgment is come.'" — EW 232.

  > "A great religious awakening under the proclamation of Christ's soon coming is foretold in the prophecy of the first angel's message of Revelation 14… 'With a loud voice' he proclaims the message: 'Fear God, and give glory to Him; for the hour of His judgment is come.'" — GC 355.
