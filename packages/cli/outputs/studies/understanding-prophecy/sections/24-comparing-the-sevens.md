# 24. Comparing the Sevens — Churches, Seals, Trumpets, Plagues

> _The book of Revelation runs four "sevens" over the very same stretch of
> history, each viewing it from a different watch-tower. The seven_ **churches**
> _give the church's inward, religious state; the seven_ **seals** _her outward
> providential fortunes; the seven_ **trumpets** _the political and warlike
> judgments on the nations among whom she dwells; and the seven_ **plagues**
> _the final, unmingled wrath poured out on the wicked AFTER probation has
> forever closed. Three of the lines run together through the gospel
> dispensation; the fourth begins only when mercy has ceased to plead. (DAR
> 345, 402, 452, 638-642; GC 627-629.)_

### The four lines at a glance

| Series             | Standpoint           | Subject viewed                     | Reaches                                    | Key text          |
| ------------------ | -------------------- | ---------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ | ----------------- |
| **Seven Churches** | Religious            | Spiritual state of the church      | Ephesus (apostolic) → Laodicea (last days) | Rev. 1:11, 20     |
| **Seven Seals**    | Providential         | Outward fortunes of the church     | First century → second coming              | Rev. 6:1-2, 12-14 |
| **Seven Trumpets** | Political / military | Wars and judgments on the nations  | Rome's fall → 1840 → end                   | Rev. 8:2; 9:15    |
| **Seven Plagues**  | Final wrath          | The wicked, after probation closes | Wholly future, "without mixture"           | Rev. 15:1; 16:1-2 |

### Series 1 — The Seven CHURCHES: the religious standpoint

- **Revelation 1:11** — "What thou seest, write in a book, and send it unto the seven churches... Ephesus... Smyrna... Pergamos... Thyatira... Sardis... Philadelphia... Laodicea." Seven literal Asian congregations chosen because their names and conditions sketch seven successive states of the whole church to the end.

  > "Having, in the first chapter, mapped out the subject by a general reference to the seven churches, represented by the seven candlesticks, and to the ministry of the churches, represented by the seven stars, John now takes up each church particularly, and writes the message designed for it, addressing the epistle in every case to the angel, or pastors, of the church." — DAR 345.1.

  > "Under this emblematical representation of the seven churches of Asia, the Holy Spirit has delineated seven different states of the Christian church, which would appear in succession, extending to the coming of our Lord and the consummation of all things; that this is given in descriptions taken from the names, states, and conditions of these churches…" — DAR 346.3.

- **Revelation 1:20** — "The seven stars are the angels of the seven churches: and the seven candlesticks... are the seven churches." The candlestick is the church; the star (angel) is her ministry — Scripture interprets its own symbols.

  > "Having, in the first chapter, mapped out the subject by a general reference to the seven churches, represented by the seven candlesticks, and to the ministry of the churches, represented by the seven stars, John now takes up each church particularly…" — DAR 345.1.

  > "The angel of a church must denote a messenger, or minister, of that church; and as these churches each cover a period of time, the angel of each church must denote the ministry, or all the true ministers of Christ during the period covered by that church." — DAR 347.2.

- **Revelation 2:1-5** — Ephesus, "the apostolic age," had received the doctrine of Christ in its purity, yet "left thy first love." This is the church's _inner_ condition, not her outward battles.

  > "The first church named is Ephesus. According to the application here made, this would cover the first, or apostolic age of the church… Those early Christians had received the doctrine of Christ in its purity. They enjoyed the benefits and blessings of the gifts of the Holy Spirit. They were noted for works, labor, and patience." — DAR 347.1.

  > "The Cause of Complaint. — 'I have somewhat against thee,' says Christ, 'because thou hast left thy first love.' … The charge here is not that of falling from grace, nor that love is extinguished, but diminished. No zeal, no suffering can atone for the want of first love." — DAR 347.3.

- **Revelation 1:19** — "Write the things which thou hast seen, and the things which are, and the things which shall be hereafter." The threefold division that frames the whole prophecy as successive periods, not a single moment.

  > "Under this emblematical representation of the seven churches of Asia, the Holy Spirit has delineated seven different states of the Christian church, which would appear in succession, extending to the coming of our Lord and the consummation of all things." — DAR 346.3.

> **Bullet — the Churches' standpoint.** The seven churches give the spiritual,
> religious history of the church — her love, zeal, doctrine, and decline —
> from apostolic Ephesus to lukewarm Laodicea. Many expositors (Vitringa,
> Matthew Henry) held that the Spirit here "delineated seven different states
> of the Christian church, which would appear in succession, extending to the
> coming of our Lord." _(DAR 346.3; 347.1.)_

### Series 2 — The Seven SEALS: the providential standpoint

- **Revelation 6:1-2** — The Lamb opens the first seal; "behold a white horse... he went forth conquering, and to conquer." The white horse is "a fit emblem of the triumphs of the gospel in the first century," its whiteness the purity of faith — the church's outward fortunes, not her trumpet-wars.

  > "VERSE 1. And I saw when the Lamb opened one of the seals… And I saw, and behold a white horse; and he that sat on him had a bow; and a crown was given unto him: and he went forth conquering, and to conquer." — DAR 402.1.

  > "The first symbol, a white horse, and the rider who bears a bow, and to whom a crown is given, and who goes forth conquering and to conquer, is a fit emblem of the triumphs of the gospel in the first century of this dispensation. The whiteness of the horse denotes the purity of faith in that age." — DAR 403.1.

- **Revelation 6:8** — The pale horse: "his name that sat on him was Death... to kill with sword, and with hunger, and with death." The fortunes of the church darken through the centuries of decline and persecution.

  > "The rider on this horse is named Death; and Hell (ᾅδης, the grave) follows with him. The mortality is so great during this period that it would seem as if 'the pale nations of the dead' had come upon earth… It must refer to the time in which the papacy bore its unrebuked, unrestrained, and persecuting rule, commencing about A. D. 538." — DAR 408.2.

- **Revelation 6:9-11** — The fifth seal: souls "under the altar... slain for the word of God... How long, O Lord, dost thou not judge and avenge our blood?" The era of papal martyrdom; white robes given, and a command to "rest yet for a little season" — the church's persecuted providential lot.

  > "The seals denote events of a religious character, and contain the history of the church from the opening of the Christian era to the coming of Christ." — DAR 402.2.

  > "Then their praises were sung, their virtues admired, their fortitude applauded, their names honored, and their memories cherished. White robes were thus given unto every one of them." — DAR 413.

- **Revelation 6:12-14** — The sixth seal: "a great earthquake; and the sun became black... the moon became as blood... the stars of heaven fell." The Lisbon earthquake (1755), the Dark Day (1780), the falling stars (1833) — public providences marking the approach of the end. We "stand between the 13th and 14th verses."

  > "VERSE 1. And I saw when the Lamb opened one of the seals… And I saw, and behold a white horse; and he that sat on him had a bow." — DAR 402.1.

  > "From looking at the past, and beholding the word of God fulfilled, we are now called to look at events in the future, which are no less sure to come. Here is our position, unmistakably defined. We stand between the 13th and 14th verses of this chapter." — DAR 432.2.

- **Revelation 8:1** — The seventh seal: "silence in heaven about the space of half an hour." When heaven empties at Christ's coming; "the world's dream of carnal security" is broken. The seals run from the first century to the second advent.

  > "Isaiah (24: 19, 20) says: 'The earth is utterly broken down, the earth is clean dissolved… The earth shall reel to and fro like a drunkard, and shall be removed like a cottage; and the transgression thereof shall be heavy upon it; and it shall fall, and not rise again.'" — DAR 432.4.

  > "Then will the world's dream of carnal security be effectually broken. Kings, who, intoxicated with their own earthly authority, have never dreamed of a higher power than themselves, now realize that there is One who reigns King of kings." — DAR 433.1.

> **Bullet — the Seals' standpoint.** The seven seals embrace the outward,
> providential history of the same church — her conquests, her decline, her
> martyrdoms, and the signs preceding her Lord — "reaching down to the close of
> probationary time." Where the churches show the heart, the seals show the
> fortunes. _(DAR 402.2; 403.1.)_

### Series 3 — The Seven TRUMPETS: the political standpoint

- **Revelation 8:2** — "I saw the seven angels which stood before God; and to them were given seven trumpets." A new and distinct series. "In the seals we have had the history of the church... In the seven trumpets... we have the principal political and warlike events which were to transpire during the same time."

  > "VERSE 2. And I saw the seven angels which stood before God; and to them were given seven trumpets." — DAR 453.2.

  > "This verse introduces a new and distinct series of events. In the seals we have had the history of the church during what is called the gospel dispensation. In the seven trumpets, now introduced, we have the principal political and warlike events which were to transpire during the same time." — DAR 453.3.

- **Revelation 8:7** — The first trumpet: "hail and fire mingled with blood... the third part of trees was burnt up." The Gothic invasions that broke Western Rome — judgments on the _nations_, not on the church's spiritual state.

  > "'Hail and fire, mingled with blood,' were cast upon the earth. The terrible effects of this Gothic invasion are represented as 'hail,' from the fact of the northern origin of the invaders; 'fire,' from the destruction by flame of both city and country; and 'blood,' from the terrible slaughter of the citizens of the empire…" — DAR 455.6.

- **Revelation 9:1, 5, 10** — The fifth trumpet (first woe): a fallen star, the bottomless pit, and power "to hurt men five months." The 150 years (a prophetic five months) of Ottoman tormenting — day-for-a-year (Num. 14:34; Eze. 4:6).

  > "'Five months,' thirty days to a month, give us one hundred and fifty days; and these days, being symbolic, signify one hundred and fifty years. Commencing July 27, 1299, the one hundred and fifty years reach to 1449. During that whole period the Turks were engaged in an almost perpetual warfare with the Greek empire, but yet without conquering it." — DAR 479.5.

- **Revelation 9:15** — The sixth trumpet: "the four angels were loosed, which were prepared for an hour, and a day, and a month, and a year, for to slay the third part of men." The four sultanies on the Euphrates (Aleppo, Iconium, Damascus, Bagdad) loosed to overthrow the Eastern empire.

  > "When the sixth angel sounded, it was commanded to take off the restraints which had been imposed on the nation, by which they were restricted to the work of tormenting men, and their commission was enlarged so as to permit them to slay the third part of men." — DAR 480.2.

  > "The Four Angels. — These were the four principal sultanies of which the Ottoman empire was composed, located in the country watered by the great river Euphrates. These sultanies were situated at Aleppo, Iconium, Damascus, and Bagdad. Previously they had been restrained; but God commanded, and they were loosed." — DAR 480.3.

- **Revelation 9:15 (the time computed)** — An hour + day + month + year = 391 years and 15 days. Beginning July 27, 1449 (when Constantine reigned only by the Turk's permission), the period "would end Aug. 11, 1840."

  > "The four angels were loosed for an hour, a day, a month, and a year, to slay the third part of men. This period amounts to three hundred ninety-one years and fifteen days, during which Ottoman supremacy was to exist in Constantinople." — DAR 481.1.

  > "Commencing when the one hundred and fifty years ended, July 27, 1449, the period would end Aug. 11, 1840. Judging from the manner of the commencement of the Ottoman supremacy, that it was by a voluntary acknowledgment on the part of the Greek emperor that he reigned only by permission of the Turkish sultan…" — DAR 485.1.

- **Revelation 9:18-21** — "By these three was the third part of men killed, by the fire, and by the smoke, and by the brimstone." The gunpowder and cannon by which Constantinople fell (1453) and Ottoman supremacy was finally broken — "yet repented not."

  > "These verses express the deadly effect of the new mode of warfare introduced. It was by means of these agents, — gunpowder, firearms, and cannon, — that Constantinople was finally overcome, and given into the hands of the Turks." — DAR 484.2.

  > "The events that transpired under the sixth trumpet constituted the second woe; yet these judgments led to no improvement in the manners and morals of men. Those who escaped them learned nothing by their manifestation in the earth." — DAR 487.3.

> **The 1840 test (sixth trumpet).** Josiah Litch, "in 1838, two years before
> the predicted event," published from Revelation 9 that Ottoman independence
> would depart in "A.D. 1840, sometime in the month of August." On the
> **eleventh day of August, 1840**, the sultan's ultimatum passed into foreign
> hands — "the matter would be forever beyond the control" of the Turk — and
> Mohammedan independence in Constantinople departed on the very day. "The
> exact accomplishment... gave a mighty impetus to the great Advent movement."
> _(DAR 485.2; 486.2; 487.1; GC 334.4.)_

> **Bullet — the Trumpets' standpoint.** The seven trumpets give the political
> and military judgments on the nations among whom the church dwells — Rome's
> fall under the four winds of barbarian invasion, the Saracen and Ottoman
> woes, ending in the verifiable date of August 11, 1840. They are God's
> scourges on apostate Christendom, not the spiritual record of the churches.
> _(DAR 453.3; 487.4.)_

### Series 4 — The Seven PLAGUES: the final, unmingled wrath

- **Revelation 15:1** — "Seven angels having the seven last plagues; for in them is filled up the wrath of God." Called the **last** plagues because in them God's wrath is "filled up" — "a manifestation of Heaven's unmingled wrath... for the last generation of the wicked. The work of mercy is then forever past."

  > "This chapter introduces the seven last plagues, a manifestation of Heaven's unmingled wrath, and the fullness of its measure, for the last generation of the wicked. The work of mercy is then forever past." — DAR 638.1.

  > "This chapter gives a description of the seven vials of the unmingled wrath of God, and the effects that follow as they are poured upon the earth." — DAR 641.1.

- **Revelation 15:8** — "The temple was filled with smoke from the glory of God... and no man was able to enter into the temple, till the seven plagues... were fulfilled." No mediation during the plagues — the door of the sanctuary is shut; probation is over before the first vial falls.

  > "This chapter gives a description of the seven vials of the unmingled wrath of God, and the effects that follow as they are poured upon the earth." — DAR 641.

  > "When Christ ceases His intercession in the sanctuary, the unmingled wrath threatened against those who worship the beast and his image and receive his mark (Revelation 14:9, 10), will be poured out." — GC 627.3.

- **Revelation 16:1-2** — "Go your ways, and pour out the vials of the wrath of God upon the earth. And the first... poured out his vial... and there fell a noisome and grievous sore upon the men which had the mark of the beast." The plague falls on the very class warned by the third angel — proof it follows the close of his work.

  > "The Chronology of the Plagues. — The description of this plague clearly reveals at once their chronology; for it is poured out upon those who have the mark of the beast, and who worship his image, — the identical work against which the third angel warns us. This is conclusive proof that these judgments are not poured out till after this angel closes his work." — DAR 641.3.

- **Revelation 14:9-10** — "If any man worship the beast... The same shall drink of the wine of the wrath of God, which is poured out **without mixture** into the cup of his indignation." The plagues are this very wine — unmixed with the mercy that has tempered every prior judgment.

  > "These plagues are the wine of God's wrath without mixture, threatened by the third angel… Such language cannot be applied to any judgments visited upon the earth while Christ pleads between his Father and our fallen race; hence we must locate them in the future, when probation shall have closed." — DAR 642.3.

  > "When Christ ceases His intercession in the sanctuary, the unmingled wrath threatened against those who worship the beast and his image and receive his mark (Revelation 14:9, 10), will be poured out." — GC 627.3.

- **Revelation 16:10-12** — The fifth vial fills the beast's kingdom "with darkness," the sixth dries up "the great river Euphrates" — yet men "blasphemed... and repented not." Because mercy has withdrawn, the plagues harden rather than convert.

  > "The Sixth Plague. — What is the great River Euphrates, upon which this vial is poured out? … One [view] is that it is a symbol of the nation occupying the territory through which that river flows. The latter opinion is preferable… [for] it should be noticed that the drying up takes place to prepare the way of the kings of the East; that is, regular military organizations." — DAR 641-650.

- **Revelation 16:17** — The seventh vial poured "into the air; and there came a great voice out of the temple of heaven, from the throne, saying, It is done." The wrath finished; the voice of God shaking earth and heaven (cf. Rev. 6:14).

  > "From Hebrews 12:25-27, Joel 3:16, Jeremiah 25:30-33, Revelation 16:17, we learn that it is the voice of God, as he speaks in terrible majesty from his throne in heaven, that causes this fearful commotion in earth and sky… and not only the earth will shake, but the heavens also." — DAR 432.3.

- **Revelation 22:11** — "He that is unjust, let him be unjust still... and he that is holy, let him be holy still." The decree that fixes every destiny — the close of probation — spoken just before the plagues; after it, no man's character can change and mercy no longer pleads.

  > "When the third angel's message closes, mercy no longer pleads for the guilty inhabitants of the earth… Then Jesus ceases His intercession in the sanctuary above… 'He that is unjust, let him be unjust still:… and he that is holy, let him be holy still.' … Every case has been decided for life or death." — GC 613.2.

  > "When Christ ceases His intercession in the sanctuary, the unmingled wrath threatened against those who worship the beast and his image and receive his mark… will be poured out." — GC 627.3.

> **Bullet — the Plagues' standpoint.** The seven last plagues stand alone:
> they fall only on the wicked, only on those who have the mark of the beast,
> and only **after probation has closed** — "when Christ ceases His
> intercession in the sanctuary." "All the judgments upon men, prior to the
> close of probation, have been mingled with mercy... but in the final
> judgment, wrath is poured out unmixed with mercy." This is what makes the
> plagues unique among the four sevens. _(GC 627.3; 628.2; DAR 642.3.)_

### Why they are FOUR lines, not one

- **Revelation 4:1** — "Come up hither, and I will shew thee things which must be hereafter." Each new vision (1:11 churches, 6:1 seals, 8:2 trumpets, 15:1 plagues) restarts the prophet's view, sweeping the same ground from a fresh standpoint rather than continuing where the last left off.

  > "The expression 'after this' does not denote that what is recorded in chapter 4 and onward was to take place after the fulfillment of everything recorded in the three preceding chapters, but only that after he had seen and heard what is there recorded, he had the new view which he now introduces." — DAR 384.

  > "This verse introduces a new and distinct series of events. In the seals we have had the history of the church during what is called the gospel dispensation. In the seven trumpets, now introduced, we have the principal political and warlike events which were to transpire during the same time." — DAR 453.3.

- **Revelation 11:18** — "The nations were angry, and thy wrath is come, and the time of the dead, that they should be judged." The seventh trumpet, like the seventh seal and seventh plague, lands at one and the same terminus — the judgment and reward — proving the lines are parallel, not consecutive.

  > "'And Thy Wrath Is Come.' — The wrath of God for the present generation is filled up in the seven last plagues (chapter 15:1), which consequently must here be referred to, and which are soon to be poured out upon the earth." — DAR 506.3.

- **Daniel 12:1; Revelation 22:11** — "Michael shall stand up" the moment the third angel's message closes and "mercy no longer pleads"; the unjust are sealed unjust, the holy sealed holy. This single hinge separates the first three sevens (running _through_ probation) from the fourth (poured out _after_ it).

  > "When the third angel's message closes, mercy no longer pleads for the guilty inhabitants of the earth… He lifts His hands and with a loud voice says, 'It is done;'… 'He that is unjust, let him be unjust still:… and he that is holy, let him be holy still.'" — GC 613.2.

  > "When Christ ceases His intercession in the sanctuary, the unmingled wrath threatened against those who worship the beast and his image and receive his mark (Revelation 14:9, 10), will be poured out." — GC 627.3.

> **Summary distinguishing all four.** Churches = the church's _religious_ life
> (her spiritual state), Seals = her _providential_ life (her outward
> fortunes), Trumpets = the _political_ judgments on the nations around her —
> all three running together through the gospel dispensation; while Plagues =
> the _final wrath_ on the wicked, alone among the four because it falls only
> after probation closes, "without mixture" of mercy, on those who bear the
> mark of the beast. _(DAR 453.3; 641.3; 642.3; GC 627.3-628.2.)_
