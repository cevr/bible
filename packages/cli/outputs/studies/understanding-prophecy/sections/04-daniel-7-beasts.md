# 4. Daniel 7 — The Four Beasts, the Little Horn, and the Judgment

> _Daniel's night-vision repeats the image of chapter 2 at higher resolution: four
> wild beasts rise from the wind-tossed sea — lion (Babylon), bear (Medo-Persia),
> leopard (Greece), and a dreadful nameless beast (Rome) with ten horns. Among them
> a little horn rises, plucks up three, blasphemes the Most High, wears out the
> saints, thinks to change times and laws, and reigns 1260 years (538-1798). Then
> the scene shifts upward: the Ancient of Days takes the throne of judgment, the
> books are opened, and one like the Son of man is brought before Him to receive the
> kingdom. The same Papacy is met again in Daniel 8, 2 Thessalonians 2, and
> Revelation 13. (DAR 113-144; GC 439, 446.)_

### The four winds, the great sea, the four beasts

- **Daniel 7:2-3** — "The four winds of the heaven strove upon the great sea... and four great beasts came up from the sea, diverse one from another." Winds = strife and war (Jer. 49:36-37); the sea = "peoples, and multitudes, and nations, and tongues" (Rev. 17:15). Out of the political turmoil of nations the four world-empires arise.

  > "Winds, in symbolic language, denote strife, political commotion, and war.… That winds denote strife and war is further evident from a consideration of the vision itself; for as the result of the striving of the winds, kingdoms arise and fall; and these events are accomplished through political strife. The Bible definition of sea, or waters, when used as a symbol, is, peoples, and nations, and tongues." — DAR 114.

- **Daniel 7:17** — "These great beasts, which are four, are four kings, which shall arise out of the earth." The angel's own key: each beast is a kingdom — the same four metals of Daniel 2 under living symbols.

  > "These beast are therefore symbols of four great kingdoms… The first beast of this vision must therefore denote the same as the head of gold of the great image, namely, the kingdom of Babylon, and the other beasts the succeeding kingdoms shown by that image." — DAR 114.

- **Daniel 7:18** — "But the saints of the most High shall take the kingdom... for ever, even for ever and ever." The vision's goal is not another earthly empire but the everlasting kingdom of the saints — the fifth kingdom of Daniel 2's stone.

  > "(3) The time that the saints possess the kingdom; that is, the time of their entrance upon the possession of the new earth.… the territory so long misruled by the wicked powers of earth, the enemies of God's people, will be taken by the righteous, to be held by them forever and ever." — DAR 125.

### Beast one — the lion: Babylon

- **Daniel 7:4** — "The first was like a lion, and had eagle's wings... the wings thereof were plucked... and a man's heart was given to it." The lion with eagle's wings = Babylon, the head of gold; the wings denote the rapidity of Nebuchadnezzar's conquests, and their plucking and the "man's heart" picture the kingdom's decline and Nebuchadnezzar's humbling (Dan. 4).

  > "At first the lion had eagle's wings, denoting the rapidity with which Babylon extended its conquests under Nebuchadnezzar.… The boldness and spirit of the lion were gone. A man's heart, weak, timorous, and faint, had taken its place. Such was emphatically the case with the nation during the closing years of its history, when it had become enfeebled and effeminate through wealth and luxury." — DAR 115.1.

### Beast two — the bear: Medo-Persia

- **Daniel 7:5** — "Another beast, a second, like to a bear, and it raised up itself on one side, and it had three ribs in the mouth of it... Arise, devour much flesh." The bear = Medo-Persia; it raised itself "on one side" because the Persian division came up last but rose higher — the same fact as the higher horn of the ram (Dan. 8:3). The three ribs = Babylon, Lydia, and Egypt, ground down by this power (B.C. 538-331).

  > "The bear raised itself up on one side. This kingdom was composed of two nationalities, the Medes and the Persians.… and this was fulfilled by the Persian division of the kingdom, which came up last, but attained the higher eminence.… The three ribs perhaps signify the three provinces of Babylon, Lydia, and Egypt, which were especially ground down and oppressed by this power." — DAR 115.3.

### Beast three — the leopard: Greece

- **Daniel 7:6** — "Another, like a leopard, which had upon the back of it four wings of a fowl; the beast had also four heads; and dominion was given to it." The four-winged leopard = Grecia; the wings denote the lightning speed of Alexander's marches, and the four heads the fourfold division of his empire among his generals after his death — exactly fulfilled and no more, for "the prophecy permitted no more."

  > "The third kingdom, Grecia, is represented by this symbol.… Two wings, the number the lion had, were not sufficient, it must have four; this would denote unparalleled celerity of movement, which we find to be historically true of the Grecian kingdom. The conquests of Grecia under Alexander have no parallel in historic annals for suddenness and rapidity." — DAR 116.2.

  > "As Alexander left no available successor, why did not the huge empire break up info countless petty fragments? Why into just four parts, and no more? — Because the prophecy had said that there should be four. The leopard had four heads, the rough goat four horns, the kingdom was to have four divisions; and thus it was." — DAR 117.4.

### Beast four — dreadful and terrible: Rome, with ten horns

- **Daniel 7:7** — "A fourth beast, dreadful and terrible, and strong exceedingly; and it had great iron teeth: it devoured and brake in pieces... and it had ten horns." No beast in nature could symbolize it; the iron teeth answer to the iron legs of Daniel 2. This is Rome — diverse from all before it, treading down the whole earth.

  > "Inspiration finds no beast in nature which it can make even the basis of a symbol to represent the power here illustrated.… This beast, of course, corresponds to the fourth division of the great image — the legs of iron.… It devoured as with iron teeth, and brake in pieces; and it ground the nations into the very dust beneath its brazen feet." — DAR 118.1-2.

  > "The two legs of iron symbolize Rome, not merely during its closing years, but from the very beginning of its existence as a nation.… No such division into two great parts is denoted by the other symbols under which Rome is represented in the book of Daniel; namely, the great and terrible beast of Daniel 7, and the little horn of chapter 8." — DAR 64.

- **Daniel 7:23** — "The fourth beast shall be the fourth kingdom upon earth... shall devour the whole earth, and shall tread it down, and break it in pieces." The angel names it plainly as the fourth and last of the world-empires — Rome.

  > "Thus he said, The fourth beast shall be the fourth kingdom upon earth, which shall be diverse from all kingdoms, and shall devour the whole earth, and shall tread it down, and break it in pieces." — DAR 126.

- **Daniel 7:24** — "The ten horns out of this kingdom are ten kings that shall arise." The ten horns = the ten kingdoms into which Western Rome was broken between A.D. 351 and 483 (the same as the ten toes of Dan. 2): Alemanni, Franks, Burgundians, Suevi, Vandals, Visigoths, Anglo-Saxons, Heruli, Lombards, and Ostrogoths.

  > "The rise of the ten horns, or the division of Rome into ten kingdoms, between the years A. D. 351 and 483, has already been noticed.… As these horns denote kingdoms, the little horn must denote a kingdom also, but not of the same nature, because it was diverse from the others." — DAR 125.2.

  > "It had ten horns, which are explained in verse 24 to be ten kings, or kingdoms, which should arise out of this empire.… These divisions have ever since been spoken of as the ten kingdoms of the Roman empire. A. D. 351-483." — DAR 118.2.

- **Daniel 2:40** — "The fourth kingdom shall be strong as iron... shall it break in pieces and bruise." The cross-link: the iron of the image and the iron-toothed beast are one and the same Rome.

  > "But it may be asked, Why not suppose the two legs to denote division as well as the toes?… we do find, we think, good reason for supposing that the toes denote division, as here claimed." — DAR 64.

### The little horn — the Papacy

- **Daniel 7:8** — "Among them another little horn, before whom there were three of the first horns plucked up by the roots: and... in this horn were eyes like the eyes of man, and a mouth speaking great things." The little horn rises among the ten — a power within Rome, not outside it: the Papacy. The eyes = the shrewdness and foresight of the papal hierarchy; the mouth speaking great things = the arrogant claims of the bishops of Rome.

  > "A little horn (at first little, but afterward more stout than its fellows) thrust itself up among them.… Three kingdoms were plucked up before it. This little horn, as we shall have occasion to notice more fully hereafter, was the papacy.… And 'in this horn were eyes like the eyes of a man, and a mouth speaking great things,' — the eyes, a fit emblem of the shrewdness, penetration, cunning, and foresight of the papal hierarchy; and the mouth speaking great things, a fit symbol of the arrogant claims of the bishops of Rome." — DAR 118.3-119.1.

- **Daniel 7:20** — "That horn... whose look was more stout than his fellows." Though "little" at first, it grew until it overtopped the kingdoms around it — a power both ecclesiastical and political, diverse from the secular horns.

  > "A little horn (at first little, but afterward more stout than its fellows) thrust itself up among them. It was not content quietly to find a place of its own, and fill it; it must thrust aside some of the others, and usurp their places." — DAR 118.4.

  > "As these horns denote kingdoms, the little horn must denote a kingdom also, but not of the same nature, because it was diverse from the others. They were political kingdoms.… the spiritual kingdom of the papacy." — DAR 125.2.

- **Daniel 7:24 (b)** — "He shall be diverse from the first, and he shall subdue three kings." The three horns plucked up to make room for the Papacy were the three Arian powers that stood in its way: the **Heruli (A.D. 493), the Vandals (534), and the Ostrogoths (538).**

  > "The position is here confidently taken that the three powers, or horns, plucked up before the papacy, were the Heruli, the Vandals, and the Ostrogoths; and this position rests upon the following statements of historians." — DAR 129.2.

  > "From the historical testimony above cited, we think it clearly established that the three horns plucked up were the powers named; viz., the Heruli in A. D. 493, the Vandals in 534, and the Ostrogoths in 538." — DAR 136.2.

- **Daniel 7:8** (the three plucked) — "before whom there were three of the first horns plucked up by the roots." The clearing of the Ostrogoths from Rome in 538 left the bishop of Rome supreme — the very year the 1260 years begin.

  > "From the historical testimony above cited, we think it clearly established that the three horns plucked up were the powers named; viz., the Heruli in A. D. 493, the Vandals in 534, and the Ostrogoths in 538." — DAR 136.2.

  > "But this edict could not go into effect until the Arian Ostrogoths, the last of the three horns that were plucked up to make room for the papacy, were driven from Rome; and this was not accomplished, as already shown, till A. D. 538.… hence from this latter year we are to reckon, as this was the earliest point where the saints were in reality in the hand of this power." — DAR 141.1.

### The marks of the little horn — blasphemy, persecution, changing the law

- **Daniel 7:25** — "He shall speak great words against the most High, and shall wear out the saints of the most High, and think to change times and laws: and they shall be given into his hand until a time and times and the dividing of time." Four marks: (1) blasphemy against God, (2) persecution of the saints, (3) presuming to change God's times and law, (4) a reign of 1260 years.

  > "'He shall speak great words against the Most High.' Has the papacy done this? Look at a few of the pope's self-accepted titles: 'Vicegerent of the Son of God,' 'Our Lord God, the Pope,' 'Another God upon earth,' 'King of the world,' 'King of kings and Lord of lords.'" — DAR 136.3.

  > "In corroboration of these facts, fifty million martyrs — this is the lowest computation made by any historian — will rise up in the judgment as witnesses against her bloody work." — DAR 140.1.

  > "the times and laws in question were such as this power should only think to change, but not be able to change. They are the laws of the same Being to whom the saints belong who are worn out by this power; namely, the laws of the Most High.… it has taken hold of the fourth commandment, torn from its place the Sabbath of Jehovah, the only memorial of the great God ever given to man, and erected in its place a rival institution to serve another purpose." — DAR 140.3.

  > "The ordinary Jewish year, which must be used as the basis of reckoning, contained three hundred and sixty days. Three years and a half contained twelve hundred and sixty days. As each day stands for a year, we have twelve hundred and sixty years for the continuation of the supremacy of this horn." — DAR 141.1.

- **Daniel 7:21** — "The same horn made war with the saints, and prevailed against them." The persecuting power: fifty million martyrs — the lowest historian's estimate — answer that this war was indeed waged, from the Waldenses to the Inquisition.

  > "Daniel beheld this horn making war upon the saints. Has such a war been waged by the papacy? Fifty million martyrs, with a voice like the sound of many waters, answer, Yes. Witness the cruel persecutions of the Waldenses, the Albigenses, and Protestants in general, by the papal power." — DAR 125.3.

  > "In corroboration of these facts, fifty million martyrs — this is the lowest computation made by any historian — will rise up in the judgment as witnesses against her bloody work." — DAR 140.1.

- **Daniel 7:25 (c)** — "think to change times and laws." Not human laws (which it could change), but the laws of the Most High, which it could only _think_ to change: it expunged the second commandment, divided the tenth, and tore the Sabbath of Jehovah from the fourth commandment, erecting Sunday in its place.

  > "And has the papacy attempted this? — Yes, even this. It has, in its catechisms, expunged the second commandment of the decalogue to make way for its adoration of images. It has divided the tenth commandment to make up the number ten. And, more audacious than all! it has taken hold of the fourth commandment, torn from its place the Sabbath of Jehovah… and erected in its place a rival institution." — DAR 140.3.

  > "The papacy has attempted to change the law of God. The second commandment, forbidding image worship, has been dropped from the law, and the fourth commandment has been so changed as to authorize the observance of the first instead of the seventh day as the Sabbath.… The change in the fourth commandment exactly fulfills the prophecy. For this the only authority claimed is that of the church. Here the papal power openly sets itself above God." — GC 446.1-2.

- **2 Thessalonians 2:3-4** — "That man of sin... the son of perdition; who opposeth and exalteth himself above all that is called God... so that he as God sitteth in the temple of God." Paul's "man of sin" is the same little horn — a power within the professed church, exalting itself above God.

  > "Says Daniel, of the little horn, the papacy: 'He shall think to change times and the law.'… And Paul styled the same power the 'man of sin,' who was to exalt himself above God. One prophecy is a complement of the other. Only by changing God's law could the papacy exalt itself above God." — GC 446.1.

- **2 Thessalonians 2:7-8** — "The mystery of iniquity doth already work: only he who now letteth will let, until he be taken out of the way." The "let" (hindrance) was pagan imperial Rome; when it was "taken out of the way," the man of sin was revealed — and at last the Lord "shall consume" him "with the brightness of his coming."

  > "This period, as stated in preceding chapters, began with the supremacy of the papacy, A.D. 538, and terminated in 1798. At that time the pope was made captive by the French army, the papal power received its deadly wound, and the prediction was fulfilled, 'He that leadeth into captivity shall go into captivity.'" — GC 439.

- **Daniel 8:9** — "Out of one of them came forth a little horn, which waxed exceeding great, toward the south, and toward the east, and toward the pleasant land." The little horn of Daniel 8, rising after Greece's four divisions and growing exceeding great, is the same Rome/Papacy that "magnified himself even to the prince of the host" (8:11).

  > "as the prophecies which remain concern times posterior to the Chaldean monarchy, and principally relate to the church and people of God generally, they are written in the Hebrew language… The vision he speaks of as the one 'which appeared unto him at the first,' is doubtless the vision of the seventh chapter, which he had in the first year of Belshazzar." — DAR 145.

- **Revelation 13:1-2** — "A beast rise up out of the sea, having seven heads and ten horns... like unto a leopard, and his feet were as the feet of a bear, and his mouth as the mouth of a lion: and the dragon gave him his power, and his seat, and great authority." John's beast combines all of Daniel's beasts in one — the Papacy, heir to the power, seat, and authority of pagan Rome.

  > "In chapter 13 (verses 1-10) is described another beast, 'like unto a leopard,' to which the dragon gave 'his power, and his seat, and great authority.' This symbol, as most Protestants have believed, represents the papacy, which succeeded to the power and seat and authority once held by the ancient Roman empire." — GC 439.1.

- **Revelation 13:5-7** — "There was given unto him a mouth speaking great things and blasphemies; and power... to continue forty and two months... And it was given unto him to make war with the saints, and to overcome them." The same blasphemy, the same war on the saints, the same prophetic period as Daniel 7:25.

  > "Of the leopardlike beast it is declared: 'There was given unto him a mouth speaking great things and blasphemies.… And it was given unto him to make war with the saints, and to overcome them.' This prophecy, which is nearly identical with the description of the little horn of Daniel 7, unquestionably points to the papacy." — GC 439.1.

  > "The forty and two months are the same as the 'time and times and the dividing of time,' three years and a half, or 1260 days, of Daniel 7—the time during which the papal power was to oppress God's people. This period… began with the supremacy of the papacy, A.D. 538, and terminated in 1798." — GC 439.2.

### The 1260 years — 538 to 1798

- **Daniel 7:25 (d)** — "a time and times and the dividing of time" = 3½ prophetic years = 1260 prophetic days.

  > "A time, as we have seen from chapter 4:23, is one year; two times, the least that could be denoted by the plural, two years, and the dividing of time, or half a time… half a year.… We thus have three years and a half for the continuance of this power." — DAR 141.1.

- **Numbers 14:34** — "each day for a year, shall ye bear your iniquities." The day-year principle, stated plainly.

  > "The rule given us in the Bible is, that when a day is used as a symbol, it stands for a year. Ezekiel 4:6; Numbers 14:34." — DAR 141.1.

- **Ezekiel 4:6** — "I have appointed thee each day for a year." The second witness to the day-for-a-year rule of prophetic time.

  > "We must now consider that we are in the midst of symbolic prophecy; hence in this measurement the time is not literal, but symbolic also.… The rule given us in the Bible is, that when a day is used as a symbol, it stands for a year. Ezekiel 4:6; Numbers 14:34." — DAR 141.1.

- **Revelation 12:6** — "the woman fled into the wilderness... that they should feed her there a thousand two hundred and threescore days." The same 1260 of the church in the wilderness under papal supremacy.

  > "Three years and a half contained twelve hundred and sixty days. As each day stands for a year, we have twelve hundred and sixty years for the continuation of the supremacy of this horn." — DAR 141.

- **Revelation 12:14** — "a time, and times, and half a time" — the identical phrase as Daniel 7:25, fixing the three expressions (1260 days = 42 months = a time, times, and half a time) as one period.

  > "The Hebrew, or rather the Chaldaic, word for time in the text before us, is עִדָּן iddân, which Gesenius defines thus: 'Time. Spec, in prophetic language for a year. Daniel 7:25… for a year, also two years, and half a year; i. e., for three years and a half.'" — DAR 141.1.

- **Revelation 11:2-3** — "forty and two months... a thousand two hundred and threescore days." Forty-two months of thirty days = 1260 days — Scripture's own arithmetic equating the periods.

  > "The ordinary Jewish year, which must be used as the basis of reckoning, contained three hundred and sixty days. Three years and a half contained twelve hundred and sixty days. As each day stands for a year, we have twelve hundred and sixty years for the continuation of the supremacy of this horn." — DAR 141.1.

- **Daniel 7:25 fulfilled** — Justinian's edict (533) made the bishop of Rome head of all the churches, but took effect only when the Ostrogoths were driven from Rome in **538**. Counting forward: **538 + 1260 = 1798**, when Berthier with a French army entered Rome, proclaimed a republic, and took the pope prisoner — the deadly wound.

  > "The edict of the emperor Justinian, dated A. D. 533, made the bishop of Rome the head of all the churches. But this edict could not go into effect until the Arian Ostrogoths… were driven from Rome; and this was not accomplished… till A. D. 538.… For 538 + 1260 = 1798; and in the year 1798, Berthier, with a French army, entered Rome, proclaimed a republic, took the pope prisoner, and for a time abolished the papacy." — DAR 141.1.

  > "At that time the pope was made captive by the French army, the papal power received its deadly wound, and the prediction was fulfilled, 'He that leadeth into captivity shall go into captivity.'" — GC 439.2.

### The judgment scene — the Ancient of Days and the books

- **Daniel 7:9-10** — "The thrones were cast down [set up], and the Ancient of days did sit, whose garment was white as snow... the judgment was set, and the books were opened." The Ancient of Days = God the Father, a personal Being, taking the throne of judgment; "thrones were cast down" should read _set up_. The opened books bring the investigative judgment to view.

  > "By an unfortunate translation in verse 9, a wrong idea is almost sure to be conveyed. The words cast down are from a word which in the original signifies just the opposite, namely, to set up.… The thrones are not the thrones of earthly kingdoms, which are to be thrown down at the last day, but thrones of judgment, which are to be 'placed,' or set up, in the court of God on high just before the end." — DAR 119.4.

  > "The 'Ancient of days,' God the Father, takes the throne of judgment. Mark the description of his person. Those who believe in the impersonality of God are obliged to admit that he is here described as a personal being.… The books are opened, and the cases of all come up for examination before that great tribunal." — DAR 120.1.

- **Daniel 7:10 (b)** — "thousand thousands ministered unto him, and ten thousand times ten thousand stood before him." The heavenly court convenes — a scene that, by Daniel 8:14, is "even now transpiring in the sanctuary above."

  > "It will appear from the testimony of chapter 8:14, that this solemn work is even now transpiring in the sanctuary above." — DAR 121.1.

- **Daniel 8:14** — "Unto two thousand and three hundred days; then shall the sanctuary be cleansed." The judgment of Daniel 7 is the cleansing of the heavenly sanctuary, which opened in 1844 at the close of the 2300 days.

  > "It will appear from the testimony of chapter 8:14, that this solemn work is even now transpiring in the sanctuary above." — DAR 121.1.

- **Daniel 7:11-12** — "I beheld even till the beast was slain, and his body destroyed, and given to the burning flame. As concerning the rest of the beasts, they had their dominion taken away; yet their lives were prolonged for a season." The fourth beast persists, blaspheming, until destroyed by fire at the end — unlike the earlier beasts, whose peoples lived on after their dominion passed.

  > "The fourth terrible beast continues without change of character, and the little horn continues to utter its blasphemies… till the beast is given to the burning flame; and this is not its conversion, but its destruction.… The life of the fourth beast is not prolonged after its dominion is gone, as were the lives of the preceding beasts.… Its career ends in the lake of fire, and it has no existence beyond." — DAR 121.2-5.

- **Daniel 7:13-14** — "One like the Son of man came with the clouds of heaven, and came to the Ancient of days, and they brought him near before him. And there was given him dominion, and glory, and a kingdom." This is **not** the second advent to earth, for it is a coming _to_ the Ancient of Days in the heavenly temple. The Son of man receives His kingdom _before_ His return — at the close of His priestly work, the marriage of the Lamb.

  > "The scene here described is not the second advent of Christ to this earth, unless the Ancient of days is on this earth; for it is a coming to the Ancient of days.… The Son of man receives his kingdom before his return to this earth.… He receives the kingdom at the close of his priestly work in the sanctuary." — DAR 122.2-3.

- **Matthew 22:11-13** — "the king came in to see the guests" — the inspection of the wedding company for the wedding garment pictures the investigative judgment that examines every professed believer's record.

  > "As the last thing before the marriage, the King comes in to see the guests, to ascertain if all are properly arrayed in the wedding garment… But this question of fitness for the kingdom can be determined only by the investigative Judgment of the sanctuary." — DAR 599.1.

- **Revelation 14:7** — "Fear God, and give glory to him; for the hour of his judgment is come." The first angel's message announces that the very judgment of Daniel 7:9-10 has begun — the message of 1844.

  > "This period… began with the supremacy of the papacy, A.D. 538, and terminated in 1798." — GC 439.

  > "It will appear from the testimony of chapter 8:14, that this solemn work is even now transpiring in the sanctuary above." — DAR 121.1.

- **Acts 1:11** — "this same Jesus... shall so come in like manner as ye have seen him go." The coming of Daniel 7:13 (to the Father) and the coming of Acts 1:11 (to the earth) are distinct events — the first inaugurates the judgment, the second executes it.

  > "The Son of man receives his kingdom before his return to this earth. (See Luke 19:10-12 and onward.) This is a scene, therefore, which transpires in the heavenly temple… He receives the kingdom at the close of his priestly work in the sanctuary." — DAR 122.3.

### The verdict — the saints possess the kingdom

- **Daniel 7:22** — "Until the Ancient of days came, and judgment was given to the saints of the most High; and the time came that the saints possessed the kingdom." Three events in order: the Ancient of Days comes to judgment, the verdict is rendered in the saints' favor, and they receive the everlasting kingdom.

  > "In verse 22 three consecutive events seem to be brought to view.… (1) The coming of the Ancient of days… (2) The judgment that is given to the saints… (3) The time that the saints possess the kingdom; that is, the time of their entrance upon the possession of the new earth." — DAR 125.4.

- **Daniel 7:26** — "But the judgment shall sit, and they shall take away his dominion, to consume and to destroy it unto the end." The same judgment that vindicates the saints strips the little horn of its dominion and consumes it forever.

  > "The fourth terrible beast continues without change of character, and the little horn continues to utter its blasphemies… till the beast is given to the burning flame; and this is not its conversion, but its destruction.… Its career ends in the lake of fire, and it has no existence beyond." — DAR 121.2-5.

- **Daniel 7:27** — "the kingdom... under the whole heaven, shall be given to the people of the saints of the most High, whose kingdom is an everlasting kingdom, and all dominions shall serve and obey him." The vision closes where Daniel 2 closed — with the eternal kingdom of God filling the earth, after every beast and horn has passed.

  > "(3) The time that the saints possess the kingdom; that is, the time of their entrance upon the possession of the new earth. Then the last vestige of the curse, of sin, and of sinners, root and branch, will have been wiped away, and the territory so long misruled by the wicked powers of earth… will be taken by the righteous, to be held by them forever and ever." — DAR 125.
