# 4. Daniel 7 — The Four Beasts, the Little Horn, and the Judgment

> _Daniel's night-vision repeats the image of chapter 2 at higher resolution: four
> wild beasts rise from the wind-tossed sea — lion (Babylon), bear (Medo-Persia),
> leopard (Greece), and a dreadful nameless beast (Rome) with ten horns. Among them
> a little horn rises, plucks up three, blasphemes the Most High, wears out the
> saints, thinks to change times and laws, and reigns 1260 years (538-1798). Then
> the scene shifts upward: the Ancient of Days takes the throne of judgment, the
> books are opened, and one like the Son of man is brought before Him to receive the
> kingdom. The same Papacy is met again in Daniel 8, 2 Thessalonians 2, and
> Revelation 13. (DAR 113-144; GC 439, 446.)_

### The four winds, the great sea, the four beasts

- **Daniel 7:2-3** — "The four winds of the heaven strove upon the great sea... and four great beasts came up from the sea, diverse one from another." Winds = strife and war (Jer. 49:36-37); the sea = "peoples, and multitudes, and nations, and tongues" (Rev. 17:15). Out of the political turmoil of nations the four world-empires arise. _(DAR 113-114.)_
- **Daniel 7:17** — "These great beasts, which are four, are four kings, which shall arise out of the earth." The angel's own key: each beast is a kingdom — the same four metals of Daniel 2 under living symbols. _(DAR 113-114.)_
- **Daniel 7:18** — "But the saints of the most High shall take the kingdom... for ever, even for ever and ever." The vision's goal is not another earthly empire but the everlasting kingdom of the saints — the fifth kingdom of Daniel 2's stone. _(DAR 125.)_

### Beast one — the lion: Babylon

- **Daniel 7:4** — "The first was like a lion, and had eagle's wings... the wings thereof were plucked... and a man's heart was given to it." The lion with eagle's wings = Babylon, the head of gold; the wings denote the rapidity of Nebuchadnezzar's conquests, and their plucking and the "man's heart" picture the kingdom's decline and Nebuchadnezzar's humbling (Dan. 4). _(DAR 115.1.)_

### Beast two — the bear: Medo-Persia

- **Daniel 7:5** — "Another beast, a second, like to a bear, and it raised up itself on one side, and it had three ribs in the mouth of it... Arise, devour much flesh." The bear = Medo-Persia; it raised itself "on one side" because the Persian division came up last but rose higher — the same fact as the higher horn of the ram (Dan. 8:3). The three ribs = Babylon, Lydia, and Egypt, ground down by this power (B.C. 538-331). _(DAR 115.3.)_

### Beast three — the leopard: Greece

- **Daniel 7:6** — "Another, like a leopard, which had upon the back of it four wings of a fowl; the beast had also four heads; and dominion was given to it." The four-winged leopard = Grecia; the wings denote the lightning speed of Alexander's marches, and the four heads the fourfold division of his empire among his generals after his death — exactly fulfilled and no more, for "the prophecy permitted no more." _(DAR 116-117.)_

### Beast four — dreadful and terrible: Rome, with ten horns

- **Daniel 7:7** — "A fourth beast, dreadful and terrible, and strong exceedingly; and it had great iron teeth: it devoured and brake in pieces... and it had ten horns." No beast in nature could symbolize it; the iron teeth answer to the iron legs of Daniel 2. This is Rome — diverse from all before it, treading down the whole earth. _(DAR 117.5-118.1; DAR 64.)_
- **Daniel 7:23** — "The fourth beast shall be the fourth kingdom upon earth... shall devour the whole earth, and shall tread it down, and break it in pieces." The angel names it plainly as the fourth and last of the world-empires — Rome. _(DAR 126.)_
- **Daniel 7:24** — "The ten horns out of this kingdom are ten kings that shall arise." The ten horns = the ten kingdoms into which Western Rome was broken between A.D. 351 and 483 (the same as the ten toes of Dan. 2): Alemanni, Franks, Burgundians, Suevi, Vandals, Visigoths, Anglo-Saxons, Heruli, Lombards, and Ostrogoths. _(DAR 125.2; DAR 126.)_
- **Daniel 2:40** — "The fourth kingdom shall be strong as iron... shall it break in pieces and bruise." The cross-link: the iron of the image and the iron-toothed beast are one and the same Rome. _(DAR 64.)_

### The little horn — the Papacy

- **Daniel 7:8** — "Among them another little horn, before whom there were three of the first horns plucked up by the roots: and... in this horn were eyes like the eyes of man, and a mouth speaking great things." The little horn rises among the ten — a power within Rome, not outside it: the Papacy. The eyes = the shrewdness and foresight of the papal hierarchy; the mouth speaking great things = the arrogant claims of the bishops of Rome. _(DAR 118.3-119.1.)_
- **Daniel 7:20** — "That horn... whose look was more stout than his fellows." Though "little" at first, it grew until it overtopped the kingdoms around it — a power both ecclesiastical and political, diverse from the secular horns. _(DAR 118.4; DAR 126.3.)_
- **Daniel 7:24 (b)** — "He shall be diverse from the first, and he shall subdue three kings." The three horns plucked up to make room for the Papacy were the three Arian powers that stood in its way: the **Heruli (A.D. 493), the Vandals (534), and the Ostrogoths (538).** _(DAR 129.2; DAR 136.2.)_
- **Daniel 7:8** (the three plucked) — "before whom there were three of the first horns plucked up by the roots." The clearing of the Ostrogoths from Rome in 538 left the bishop of Rome supreme — the very year the 1260 years begin. _(DAR 136.2; DAR 141.1.)_

### The marks of the little horn — blasphemy, persecution, changing the law

- **Daniel 7:25** — "He shall speak great words against the most High, and shall wear out the saints of the most High, and think to change times and laws: and they shall be given into his hand until a time and times and the dividing of time." Four marks: (1) blasphemy against God, (2) persecution of the saints, (3) presuming to change God's times and law, (4) a reign of 1260 years. _(DAR 138-141.)_
- **Daniel 7:21** — "The same horn made war with the saints, and prevailed against them." The persecuting power: fifty million martyrs — the lowest historian's estimate — answer that this war was indeed waged, from the Waldenses to the Inquisition. _(DAR 125.3; DAR 140.1.)_
- **Daniel 7:25 (c)** — "think to change times and laws." Not human laws (which it could change), but the laws of the Most High, which it could only _think_ to change: it expunged the second commandment, divided the tenth, and tore the Sabbath of Jehovah from the fourth commandment, erecting Sunday in its place. _(DAR 140.3; GC 446.1-2.)_
- **2 Thessalonians 2:3-4** — "That man of sin... the son of perdition; who opposeth and exalteth himself above all that is called God... so that he as God sitteth in the temple of God." Paul's "man of sin" is the same little horn — a power within the professed church, exalting itself above God. _(GC 446.1.)_
- **2 Thessalonians 2:7-8** — "The mystery of iniquity doth already work: only he who now letteth will let, until he be taken out of the way." The "let" (hindrance) was pagan imperial Rome; when it was "taken out of the way," the man of sin was revealed — and at last the Lord "shall consume" him "with the brightness of his coming." _(GC 439.)_
- **Daniel 8:9** — "Out of one of them came forth a little horn, which waxed exceeding great, toward the south, and toward the east, and toward the pleasant land." The little horn of Daniel 8, rising after Greece's four divisions and growing exceeding great, is the same Rome/Papacy that "magnified himself even to the prince of the host" (8:11). _(DAR 145+.)_
- **Revelation 13:1-2** — "A beast rise up out of the sea, having seven heads and ten horns... like unto a leopard, and his feet were as the feet of a bear, and his mouth as the mouth of a lion: and the dragon gave him his power, and his seat, and great authority." John's beast combines all of Daniel's beasts in one — the Papacy, heir to the power, seat, and authority of pagan Rome. _(GC 439.1.)_
- **Revelation 13:5-7** — "There was given unto him a mouth speaking great things and blasphemies; and power... to continue forty and two months... And it was given unto him to make war with the saints, and to overcome them." The same blasphemy, the same war on the saints, the same prophetic period as Daniel 7:25. _(GC 439.1-2.)_

### The 1260 years — 538 to 1798

- **Daniel 7:25 (d)** — "a time and times and the dividing of time" = 3½ prophetic years = 1260 prophetic days. _(DAR 141.1.)_
- **Numbers 14:34** — "each day for a year, shall ye bear your iniquities." The day-year principle, stated plainly. _(DAR 141.1.)_
- **Ezekiel 4:6** — "I have appointed thee each day for a year." The second witness to the day-for-a-year rule of prophetic time. _(DAR 141.1.)_
- **Revelation 12:6** — "the woman fled into the wilderness... that they should feed her there a thousand two hundred and threescore days." The same 1260 of the church in the wilderness under papal supremacy. _(DAR 141.)_
- **Revelation 12:14** — "a time, and times, and half a time" — the identical phrase as Daniel 7:25, fixing the three expressions (1260 days = 42 months = a time, times, and half a time) as one period. _(DAR 141.1.)_
- **Revelation 11:2-3** — "forty and two months... a thousand two hundred and threescore days." Forty-two months of thirty days = 1260 days — Scripture's own arithmetic equating the periods. _(DAR 141.1.)_
- **Daniel 7:25 fulfilled** — Justinian's edict (533) made the bishop of Rome head of all the churches, but took effect only when the Ostrogoths were driven from Rome in **538**. Counting forward: **538 + 1260 = 1798**, when Berthier with a French army entered Rome, proclaimed a republic, and took the pope prisoner — the deadly wound. _(DAR 141.1; GC 439.2.)_

### The judgment scene — the Ancient of Days and the books

- **Daniel 7:9-10** — "The thrones were cast down [set up], and the Ancient of days did sit, whose garment was white as snow... the judgment was set, and the books were opened." The Ancient of Days = God the Father, a personal Being, taking the throne of judgment; "thrones were cast down" should read _set up_. The opened books bring the investigative judgment to view. _(DAR 119.2-4; DAR 120.1.)_
- **Daniel 7:10 (b)** — "thousand thousands ministered unto him, and ten thousand times ten thousand stood before him." The heavenly court convenes — a scene that, by Daniel 8:14, is "even now transpiring in the sanctuary above." _(DAR 121.1.)_
- **Daniel 8:14** — "Unto two thousand and three hundred days; then shall the sanctuary be cleansed." The judgment of Daniel 7 is the cleansing of the heavenly sanctuary, which opened in 1844 at the close of the 2300 days. _(DAR 121.1.)_
- **Daniel 7:11-12** — "I beheld even till the beast was slain, and his body destroyed, and given to the burning flame. As concerning the rest of the beasts, they had their dominion taken away; yet their lives were prolonged for a season." The fourth beast persists, blaspheming, until destroyed by fire at the end — unlike the earlier beasts, whose peoples lived on after their dominion passed. _(DAR 121.2-5.)_
- **Daniel 7:13-14** — "One like the Son of man came with the clouds of heaven, and came to the Ancient of days, and they brought him near before him. And there was given him dominion, and glory, and a kingdom." This is **not** the second advent to earth, for it is a coming _to_ the Ancient of Days in the heavenly temple. The Son of man receives His kingdom _before_ His return — at the close of His priestly work, the marriage of the Lamb. _(DAR 122.2-3.)_
- **Matthew 22:11-13** — "the king came in to see the guests" — the inspection of the wedding company for the wedding garment pictures the investigative judgment that examines every professed believer's record. _(Parallel; cf. DAR 122.)_
- **Revelation 14:7** — "Fear God, and give glory to him; for the hour of his judgment is come." The first angel's message announces that the very judgment of Daniel 7:9-10 has begun — the message of 1844. _(GC 439; DAR 121.1.)_
- **Acts 1:11** — "this same Jesus... shall so come in like manner as ye have seen him go." The coming of Daniel 7:13 (to the Father) and the coming of Acts 1:11 (to the earth) are distinct events — the first inaugurates the judgment, the second executes it. _(DAR 122.3.)_

### The verdict — the saints possess the kingdom

- **Daniel 7:22** — "Until the Ancient of days came, and judgment was given to the saints of the most High; and the time came that the saints possessed the kingdom." Three events in order: the Ancient of Days comes to judgment, the verdict is rendered in the saints' favor, and they receive the everlasting kingdom. _(DAR 125.4.)_
- **Daniel 7:26** — "But the judgment shall sit, and they shall take away his dominion, to consume and to destroy it unto the end." The same judgment that vindicates the saints strips the little horn of its dominion and consumes it forever. _(DAR 121.2-5.)_
- **Daniel 7:27** — "the kingdom... under the whole heaven, shall be given to the people of the saints of the most High, whose kingdom is an everlasting kingdom, and all dominions shall serve and obey him." The vision closes where Daniel 2 closed — with the eternal kingdom of God filling the earth, after every beast and horn has passed. _(DAR 125.)_
