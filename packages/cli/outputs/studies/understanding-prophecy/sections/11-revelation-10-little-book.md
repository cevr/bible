# 11. Revelation 10 — The Little Book and the Bittersweet Disappointment

> _The mighty angel of Revelation 10 comes down with a little book OPEN in his hand — the prophecy of Daniel, sealed "to the time of the end" (Dan. 12:4, 9) and now at last unsealed. Setting one foot on the sea and one on the land, he lifts his hand and swears that "time shall be no longer" — that no prophetic period reaches beyond the close of the 2300 days in 1844. The book is honey-sweet in the mouth (the blessed hope of Christ's near coming) but bitter in the belly (the great disappointment when, at the appointed time, the Lord did not come). To the disappointed the angel says, "Thou must prophesy again" — the renewed proclamation of the third angel that follows. (DAR 488-496; GC 374, 404, 423; SSP 187-190.)_

### The mighty angel with the OPEN book (Daniel unsealed)

- **Revelation 10:1-2** — "Another mighty angel come down from heaven, clothed with a cloud: and a rainbow upon his head, and his face as the sun... and he had in his hand a little book OPEN." A book once shut is now spread open at the time of the end — the symbol of the prophecy of Daniel made plain.

  > "And I saw another mighty angel come down from heaven, clothed with a cloud: and a rainbow was upon his head, and his face was as it were the sun, and his feet as pillars of fire. 2. And he had in his hand a little book open: and he set his right foot upon the sea, and his left foot on the earth." — DAR 488.1.

  > "There is no book spoken of as closed up and sealed except the book of Daniel's prophecy; and there is no account of the opening of that book, unless it be here in the 10th of Revelation. … Nothing more could be required … to prove that the little book which the angel had in his hand open, was the book of the prophecy of Daniel." — DAR 488.4.

- **Daniel 12:4** — "Shut up the words, and seal the book, even to the time of the end: many shall run to and fro, and knowledge shall be increased." Daniel's book was sealed only UNTIL the time of the end; therefore at the time of the end it must be opened — and there is no record of its opening save here in Revelation 10.

  > "Since this book was closed up only till the time of the end, it follows that at the time of the end the book would be opened; and as its closing was mentioned in prophecy, it would be but reasonable to expect that in the predictions of events to take place at the time of the end, the opening of this book would also be mentioned." — DAR 488.4.

  > "There the time of the end commenced, and the book was opened. And since that time, many have run to and fro, and knowledge on these prophetic subjects has marvelously increased." — DAR 489.1.

- **Daniel 12:9** — "Go thy way, Daniel: for the words are closed up and sealed till the time of the end." The same sealed book, the same appointed unsealing — the little book in the angel's hand is Daniel's prophecy laid open.

  > "There is no book spoken of as closed up and sealed except the book of Daniel's prophecy; and there is no account of the opening of that book, unless it be here in the 10th of Revelation." — DAR 488.4.

  > "That we are in the time of the end, when the book of this prophecy should no longer be sealed, but be open and understood, is shown by Revelation 10:1, 2, where a mighty angel is seen to come down from heaven with a little book in his hand open." — DAR 309.4.

- **Daniel 8:26** — "Shut thou up the vision; for it shall be for many days." The 2300-day vision was likewise sealed for "many days," to be understood only when the long period drew near.

  > "We read in Daniel of a book which was closed up and sealed to a certain time… Since this book was closed up only till the time of the end, it follows that at the time of the end the book would be opened." — DAR 488.4.

- **Daniel 8:17** — "At the time of the end shall be the vision." The vision belongs to the time of the end; only then would the book be opened and the chronology of the angel's message be understood.

  > "That we are in the time of the end, when the book of this prophecy should no longer be sealed, but be open and understood, is shown by Revelation 10:1, 2, where a mighty angel is seen to come down from heaven with a little book in his hand open." — DAR 309.4.

### The angel's posture, voice, and the sealed thunders

- **Revelation 10:2-3** — He set his right foot on the sea and his left on the earth, and "cried with a loud voice, as when a lion roareth." A world-wide proclamation — sea and land — uttered with the loud voice of a great message.

  > "The position of this angel, one foot upon the sea and the other on the land, denotes the wide extent of his proclamation by sea and by land. … he has one foot upon the sea, from which we may infer that his message would cross the ocean, and extend to the various nations and divisions of the globe." — DAR 490.1.

- **Revelation 10:3-4** — "Seven thunders uttered their voices... Seal up those things which the seven thunders uttered, and write them not." What the thunders spoke John was forbidden to write; we leave them where John left them — sealed, unwritten, and to us unknown.

  > "It would be vain to speculate to any great length upon the seven thunders, in hope of gaining a definite knowledge of what they uttered. We must acquiesce in the directions given to John concerning them, and leave them where he left them, sealed up, unwritten, and consequently to us unknown." — DAR 491.2.

### "Time shall be no longer" — no prophetic period beyond 1844

- **Revelation 10:5-6** — The angel "lifted up his hand to heaven, and sware by him that liveth for ever and ever... that there should be time no longer." Not that time itself should cease — the very next verse speaks of days yet to come — but that no more PROPHETIC TIME should be measured; the longest prophetic period reaches only to 1844.

  > "It must therefore mean prophetic time; for there is no other to which it can refer. Prophetic time shall be no more … but no prophetic period should extend beyond this message; those that reach to the latest point would all close there." — DAR 492.2.

- **Revelation 10:7** — "But in the days of the voice of the seventh angel... the mystery of God should be finished, as he hath declared to his servants the prophets." The seventh trumpet begins to sound when prophetic time is at an end (1844); the "mystery of God" is the gospel, to be finished in these closing days.

  > "In the days when he shall begin to sound, the mystery of God shall be finished. Not in the day when he shall begin to sound … but in the early years of his sounding, the mystery of God shall be finished." — DAR 493.2.

  > "It is the same, then, as if the angel had declared, In the days of the voice of the seventh angel, when he shall begin to sound, the gospel shall be finished. … It will be finished when the number of God's people is made up, mercy ceases to be offered, and probation closes." — DAR 494.1.

  > "The seventh trumpet began to sound when prophetic time was at an end, that is, in 1844. The mystery of God is the Gospel of Jesus Christ; the sacrifice of the Lamb of God." — SSP 187.1.

- **Daniel 8:14** — "Unto two thousand and three hundred days; then shall the sanctuary be cleansed." This longest of the prophetic lines, the 2300 years, terminating in the autumn of 1844, is the very "time" beyond which no prophetic period extends.

  > "The close of the prophetic period was 1844, so that the 'quickly' would be the time between 1840 and 1844, and the seventh trumpet began to sound when prophetic time was at an end, that is, in 1844." — SSP 187.1.

  > "It was ascertained that the decree was given near the close of the year 457 B. C., and therefore the prophetic period must reach to the fall of the year 1844." — CET 50.1.

- **Numbers 14:34** — "Each day for a year." The day-for-a-year rule (with Eze. 4:6) is the key that turns the 2300 days into 2300 years, reaching from 457 B.C. to A.D. 1844.

  > "The language cannot be intelligibly referred to anything else; for the word here rendered determined signifies 'cut off;' and there is no period from which the seventy weeks could be cut off but the 2300 days of the previous vision." — DAR 192.1.

  > "It was not at first perceived that if the decree did not go forth at the beginning of the year 457 B. C., the 2300 years would not be completed at the close of 1843. But it was ascertained that the decree was given near the close of the year 457 B. C., and therefore the prophetic period must reach to the fall of the year 1844." — CET 50.1.

- **Amos 3:7** — "Surely the Lord GOD will do nothing, but he revealeth his secret unto his servants the prophets." The mystery to be finished is the same the prophets foretold — God hides nothing from His servants.

  > "Here God's purpose to gather together all in Christ is called the 'mystery' of his will. This is accomplished through the gospel. … Here the gospel is declared plainly to be a mystery." — DAR 493.4.

  > "In view of these testimonies, few will be disposed to deny that the mystery of God is the gospel." — DAR 494.1.

### One angel, one message — Revelation 10 = the first angel of Revelation 14

- **Revelation 14:6-7** — "Another angel... having the everlasting gospel to preach... Saying with a loud voice, Fear God, and give glory to him; for the hour of his judgment is come." Both angels have a special message, both cry with a loud voice, both proclaim time and judgment — the angel of Revelation 10 and the first angel of Revelation 14 are the same advent proclamation.

  > "The points of identity between them are easily seen: (1) They both have a special message to proclaim; (2) they both utter their proclamation with a loud voice; (3) they both use similar language, referring to the great Creator … and (4) they both proclaim time, one swearing that time should be no more, and the other proclaiming that the hour of God's Judgment has come." — DAR 490.1.

  > "The sixth and seventh verses of the tenth chapter of Revelation are parallel with the sixth and seventh verses of the fourteenth chapter." — SSP 187.2.

- **Revelation 11:18** — "The time of the dead, that they should be judged... reward unto thy servants the prophets, and to the saints." The seventh trumpet brings in the judgment hour announced by the angel — the same investigative work begun in 1844.

  > "From the events to take place under the sounding of the seventh trumpet, its commencement may be located with sufficient definiteness at the close of the prophetic periods in 1844. Not many years from that date, then, the mystery of God is to be finished." — DAR 493.3.

### Eat the book — sweet in the mouth (the type of the prophets)

- **Revelation 10:8-9** — "Go and take the little book which is open... Take it, and eat it up; and it shall make thy belly bitter, but it shall be in thy mouth sweet as honey." John, standing as the representative of the church, is bidden to eat — to study the open prophecy until its meaning is comprehended.

  > "In verse 8, John himself is brought in to act a part as a representative of the church … This could most appropriately be set forth in the person of some member of the human family; hence John is himself called upon to act a part in this symbolic representation." — DAR 495.2.

  > "John, symbolizing God's people, was bidden to take it out of the angel's hand. … he said, 'Take it, and eat it up.' Eating the Word of God implies a careful study until the meaning is fully comprehended." — SSP 188.1.

- **Ezekiel 2:8-10** — "Open thy mouth, and eat that I give thee... a roll of a book... written within and without... lamentations, and mourning, and woe." The prophet's parallel: he too must eat a book before he proclaims.

  > "He said unto me, Take it, and eat it up… Eating the Word of God implies a careful study until the meaning is fully comprehended. Jesus often used the figure in a spiritual sense, referring to His body and the 'bread of life.'" — SSP 188.1.

- **Ezekiel 3:1-3** — "Eat this roll, and go speak... and it was in my mouth as honey for sweetness." Ezekiel ate the same sweet roll before his commission — the exact figure renewed in Revelation 10.

  > "He said unto me, Take it, and eat it up; and it shall make thy belly bitter, but it shall be in thy mouth sweet as honey. … Eating the Word of God implies a careful study until the meaning is fully comprehended." — SSP 188.1.

- **Jeremiah 15:16** — "Thy words were found, and I did eat them; and thy word was unto me the joy and rejoicing of mine heart." The honey-like sweetness of the advent truth was Jeremiah's joy in eating God's words — the same experience set under the same figure.

  > "There are not a few now living who have in their own experience met a striking fulfillment of these verses, in the joy with which they received the message of Christ's immediate second coming, the honey-like sweetness of the precious truths then brought out. … (See the same facts brought to view under a similar figure in Jeremiah 15:16-18.)" — DAR 495.3.

  > "Thy words were found, and I did eat them; and thy word was unto me the Joy and rejoicing of mine heart: for I am called by thy name, O Lord God of hosts. Jeremiah 15:16." — SSP 188.1.

### Bitter in the belly — the great disappointment of 1844

- **Revelation 10:10** — "I took the little book... and ate it up; and it was in my mouth sweet as honey: and as soon as I had eaten it, my belly was bitter." The blessed hope was honey to the taste; the passing of the appointed time turned it to wormwood — the disappointment, not the truth, was bitter.

  > "What had been so like honey to their taste, suddenly became like wormwood and gall. But those who had patience to endure … soon learned that the mistake was only in the event, not in the time." — DAR 495.3.

  > "The autumn of 1844 came and went, and the intensity of the disappointment was beyond description. No earthly inducement ever seemed so sweet as the message of His coming; no disappointment was ever so bitter as that experienced by the believers in the second coming of Christ." — SSP 189.1.

- **Habakkuk 2:3** — "The vision is yet for an appointed time, but at the end it shall speak, and not lie: though it tarry, wait for it; because it will surely come, it will not tarry." The "tarrying time" was foreseen; when the spring of 1844 passed, it was found the vision had not failed — the decree of 457 B.C. carried the period to the autumn.

  > "Therefore the vision of time did not tarry, though it had seemed to do so. We learned to rest upon the language of the prophet: 'The vision is yet for an appointed time, but at the end it shall speak, and not lie: though it tarry, wait for it; because it will surely come, it will not tarry.' Habakkuk 2:3." — CET 50.1.

  > "Those who had been disappointed were not long left in darkness; for in searching the prophetic periods with earnest prayer the error was discovered, and the tracing of the prophetic pencil down through the tarrying time." — CET 50.3.

  > "We are placed in a position which God foresaw his children would be placed in at the end of the vision; and for which he made provision, by the prophet Habakkuk … 'For the vision is yet for an appointed time, but at the end [of the prophetic periods] it shall speak and not lie: though it tarry [beyond their apparent termination], wait for it.'" — GSAM 153.2.

- **Habakkuk 2:1** — "I will stand upon my watch, and set me upon the tower, and will watch to see what he will say... when I am reproved." God provided beforehand for the disappointed watchers through Habakkuk's tower — patient waiting through the apparent delay.

  > "We are placed in a position which God foresaw his children would be placed in at the end of the vision; and for which he made provision, by the prophet Habakkuk, when he says, 'I will stand upon my watch, and set me upon the tower, and will watch to see what he will say unto me, and what I shall answer when I am reproved.'" — GSAM 153.2.

- **Daniel 8:27** — "I Daniel fainted, and was sick certain days... and I was astonished at the vision, but none understood it." Even the prophet was sickened and dismayed at the vision he could not yet comprehend — a foretaste of the bitterness of those who ate the book in 1844.

  > "What had been so like honey to their taste, suddenly became like wormwood and gall. But those who had patience to endure, so to speak, the digesting process, soon learned that the mistake was only in the event, not in the time." — DAR 495.3.

- **Luke 24:21** — "We trusted that it had been he which should have redeemed Israel." The disciples' bitter disappointment at the cross was the very type of 1844 — "We looked for Him to save us, but He has not come."

  > "Yet this disappointment was not so great as was that experienced by the disciples at the time of Christ's first advent. … The disciples were accomplishing the purpose of God; yet they were doomed to a bitter disappointment." — GC 404.2.

  > "The disciples, weeping at the tomb over a crucified Saviour, seemed to drain the cup of bitterness, but a potion no less galling was drunk by the disciples in 1844. 'We thought that it was He that would save Israel,' was echoed eighteen hundred years later in the words, 'We looked for Him to save us, but He has not come.'" — SSP 189.1.

- **Acts 1:6-7** — "Lord, wilt thou at this time restore again the kingdom to Israel?... It is not for you to know the times or the seasons." As the first disciples mistook the EVENT while God's purpose stood, so the advent believers mistook the event of 1844, not the time.

  > "When Jesus rode triumphantly into Jerusalem, His followers believed that He was about to ascend the throne of David and deliver Israel from her oppressors. … Their expectations had not been realized in a single particular, and their hopes died with Jesus." — GC 404.2.

  > "Those who had patience to endure, so to speak, the digesting process, soon learned that the mistake was only in the event, not in the time, and that what the angel had given them was not unto death, but to their nourishment and support." — DAR 495.3.

### The sanctuary — the key that explained the bitterness

- **Daniel 8:14** — "Then shall the sanctuary be cleansed." When the cloud of disappointment was pierced, the truth of the heavenly sanctuary was discovered: the event of 1844 was not the second advent but the opening of the investigative judgment — Christ's entrance within the veil.

  > "The subject of the sanctuary was the key which unlocked the mystery of the disappointment of 1844. It opened to view a complete system of truth, connected and harmonious, showing that God's hand had directed the great advent movement." — GC 423.1.

  > "When the cloud was pierced by eyes which searched for Christ, the truth in regard to the heavenly sanctuary was discovered. … In 1844 the antitypical work of the day of atonement was begun in the heavenly sanctuary. That is, Christ at that time passed within the veil … The investigative judgment was opened." — SSP 187.2-188.

- **Revelation 11:18** — "The time of the dead, that they should be judged." The sanctuary truth showed that the hour of judgment, not the end of the world, had come — the mistake was in the event, never in the time.

  > "As the disciples of Jesus after the terrible night of their anguish and disappointment were 'glad when they saw the Lord,' so did those now rejoice who had looked in faith for His second coming. … Now in the holy of holies they again beheld Him, their compassionate High Priest, soon to appear as their king and deliverer." — GC 423.1.

  > "From the events to take place under the sounding of the seventh trumpet, its commencement may be located with sufficient definiteness at the close of the prophetic periods in 1844." — DAR 493.3.

### "Thou must prophesy again" — the post-disappointment commission

- **Revelation 10:11** — "And he said unto me, Thou must prophesy again before many peoples, and nations, and tongues, and kings." After the first and second messages ceased as leading proclamations, a renewed message was foretold — the third angel's message, now in process of fulfilment.

  > "Another message is to go forth after the time when the first and second messages, as leading proclamations, ceased. In other words, we have here a prophecy of the third angel's message, now, as we believe, in process of fulfilment. … it is to go before 'many peoples, and nations, and tongues, and kings.'" — DAR 496.2.

  > "Many expected that those who passed through the disappointment, would forever sink out of sight, but the angel said, 'Thou must prophesy again, before many peoples, and nations, and tongues, and kings.' This foretells the third message of Revelation 14:9-12, which will go to all the world, increasing as it goes, until it swells into a loud cry." — SSP 190.1.

- **Revelation 14:6-7** — "The everlasting gospel... to every nation, and kindred, and tongue, and people." The renewed proclamation goes again to the same world-wide audience — peoples, nations, tongues, kings — swelling at last into the loud cry.

  > "Many peoples, the nations of earth, representatives from every tongue, rich and poor, even kings on their thrones, will hear this last message of mercy which goes to the earth in the beginning of the sounding of the seventh trumpet. … what is begun on earth, mingles with the song of the redeemed on the other shore." — SSP 190.2.

- **Habakkuk 2:3** — "It will surely come, it will not tarry." The vision that seemed to tarry will yet speak and not lie; the disappointed are commanded to wait and to prophesy again, for the promise stands sure.

  > "For the vision is yet for an appointed time, but at the end [of the prophetic periods] it shall speak and not lie: though it tarry [beyond their apparent termination], wait for it; because it will surely come, [in the fullness of the prophetic times, beyond which] it will not tarry.' Habakkuk 2:1-3." — GSAM 153.2.

  > "For yet a little while, and he that shall come will come, and will not tarry. Now the just shall live by faith; but if any man draw back, my soul shall have no pleasure in him." — GSAM 153.3.

  > "Those who had been disappointed were not long left in darkness; for in searching the prophetic periods with earnest prayer the error was discovered, and the tracing of the prophetic pencil down through the tarrying time." — CET 50.3.
