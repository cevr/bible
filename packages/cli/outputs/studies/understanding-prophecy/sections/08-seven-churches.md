# 8. The Seven Churches of Revelation 1-3

> _The messages to the seven churches of Asia are not seven local letters merely, but a continuous prophecy: seven successive periods of the Christian church, running from apostolic Ephesus to the judgment-hour Laodicea. Each name, in its very meaning, fits the spiritual condition of its age; together they span the whole gospel dispensation, the number seven denoting fulness and completeness. This is no novel view — Bishop Newton, More, and Vitringa held it before us. (DAR 328-329, 345-347; GC 381-382.)_

### The seal of the seven: stars and candlesticks defined

- **Revelation 1:11** — "What thou seest, write in a book, and send it unto the seven churches which are in Asia; unto Ephesus, and unto Smyrna, and unto Pergamos, and unto Thyatira, and unto Sardis, and unto Philadelphia, and unto Laodicea." The seven are named in fixed order; that order is the order of history, apostolic to advent.

  > "Some reasons why the seven churches, or more properly the messages to them, should be regarded as prophetic, having their application to seven distinct periods covering the Christian age, have been given in the … remarks on chapter 1:4. It may here be added that this view is neither new nor local." — DAR 345.3.

- **Revelation 1:20** — "The seven stars are the angels of the seven churches: and the seven candlesticks which thou sawest are the seven churches." Scripture defines its own symbols: the lampstand is the church; the star is its messenger.

  > "The seven stars which the Son of man held in his right hand (verse 20), are declared to be the angels of the seven churches. The angels of the churches, doubtless all will agree, are the ministers of the churches." — DAR 328.3.

  > "John, looking into the Christian dispensation, saw only seven candlesticks, representing seven churches, in the midst of which stood the Son of man.… The whole gospel church in seven divisions, or periods, must be symbolized by them; and hence the seven churches must be applied in the same manner." — DAR 329.1.

- **Revelation 1:20** (the stars) — The seven stars held in Christ's right hand "are the angels of the seven churches," and an angel of a church is its minister; held in His right hand, they denote the upholding, guidance, and protection given to all true ministers of the whole gospel age.

  > "Their being held in the right hand of the Son of man denotes the upholding power, guidance, and protection vouchsafed to them.… May not, rather, all the true ministers of the whole gospel age derive from this representation the consolation of knowing that they are upheld and guided by the right hand of the great Head of the church?" — DAR 328.3.

- **Revelation 1:20** (the candlesticks) — John saw only seven lampstands, "and seven is a number denoting fulness and completeness"; so the whole gospel church, in seven divisions or periods, is symbolized — the Son of man standing in the midst to take cognizance of all His churches through the dispensation.

  > "The position of the Son of man in their midst must denote his presence with them, his watchcare over them, and his searching scrutiny of all their works.… Seven, as used in the Scriptures, is a number denoting fulness and completeness.… The whole gospel church in seven divisions, or periods, must be symbolized by them." — DAR 329.1.

- **Revelation 1:11; Revelation 1:20** (why prophetic) — "Many contend... that the seven epistles are prophetical of so many successive periods, or states, of the church, from the beginning to the conclusion of all" — the view is "neither new nor local."

  > "This view is neither new nor local. Benson quotes Bishop Newton as saying, 'Many contend, and among them such learned men as More and Vitringa, that the seven epistles are prophetical of so many successive periods, or states, of the church, from the beginning to the conclusion of all.'" — DAR 345.3.

### 1. Ephesus — apostolic purity (A.D. 31-100)

- **Revelation 2:1** — "These things saith he that holdeth the seven stars in his right hand, who walketh in the midst of the seven golden candlesticks." Christ opens to the first church as the One who upholds the ministry and watches over all the lampstands.

  > "The angel of a church must denote a messenger, or minister, of that church; and as these churches each cover a period of time, the angel of each church must denote the ministry, or all the true ministers of Christ during the period covered by that church." — DAR 347.2.

- **Revelation 2:2-3** — "I know thy works, and thy labour, and thy patience... thou hast tried them which say they are apostles, and are not, and hast found them liars." The early Christians had the doctrine of Christ in purity, the gifts of the Spirit, and tried and exposed false apostles. The name Ephesus, "desirable," well describes the church in its first state.

  > "The definition of the word Ephesus, is desirable, which may well be taken as a good descriptive term of the character and condition of the church in its first state. Those early Christians had received the doctrine of Christ in its purity. They enjoyed the benefits and blessings of the gifts of the Holy Spirit.… They tried false apostles, searched out their true characters, and found them liars." — DAR 347.1.

- **Revelation 2:4** — "Nevertheless I have somewhat against thee, because thou hast left thy first love." Even the apostolic age cooled toward its close; the decline begins.

  > "'I have somewhat against thee,' says Christ, 'because thou hast left thy first love.'… 'The charge here is not that of falling from grace, nor that love is extinguished, but diminished. No zeal, no suffering can atone for the want of first love.'" — DAR 347.3-348.

- **Revelation 2:5** — "Repent... or else I will come unto thee quickly, and will remove thy candlestick out of his place." The threat to remove the lampstand confirms the candlestick is the church-period itself.
- **Revelation 2:6-7** — "Thou hatest the deeds of the Nicolaitans, which I also hate.... To him that overcometh will I give to eat of the tree of life." Reward to the overcomer of every age is Eden restored.

  > "The first church named is Ephesus. According to the application here made, this would cover the first, or apostolic age of the church.… In faithfulness to the pure principles taught by Christ, they could not bear those that were evil." — DAR 347.1.

### 2. Smyrna — the persecuted church (A.D. 100-313)

- **Revelation 2:8** — "These things saith the first and the last, which was dead, and is alive." The risen Christ presents Himself to a dying, persecuted people as the One who Himself conquered death.

  > "Smyrna signifies myrrh, fit appellation for the church of God while passing through the fiery furnace of persecution, and proving herself a 'sweet-smelling savor' unto him." — DAR 354.1.

- **Revelation 2:9** — "I know thy works, and tribulation, and poverty, (but thou art rich)." Smyrna signifies "myrrh," a fit name for the church passing through the fiery furnace of persecution and proving herself a "sweet-smelling savor" unto God.

  > "Smyrna signifies myrrh, fit appellation for the church of God while passing through the fiery furnace of persecution, and proving herself a 'sweet-smelling savor' unto him. But we soon reach the days of Constantine, when the church presents a new phase." — DAR 354.1.

- **Revelation 2:10** — "Ye shall have tribulation ten days: be thou faithful unto death, and I will give thee a crown of life." The "ten days" are prophetic: ten years (day-for-a-year), fulfilled in the last and bloodiest of the ten persecutions, under Diocletian, A.D. 302-312.

  > "As this message is prophetic, the time mentioned in it must also be regarded as prophetic, and would denote ten years. And it is a noticeable fact that the last and most bloody of the ten persecutions continued just ten years, under Diocletian, from A. D. 302 to A. D. 312." — DAR 352.3-353.

- **Revelation 2:11** — "He that overcometh shall not be hurt of the second death." To the martyr church, the promise that the worst the persecutor can do reaches only the first death.

### 3. Pergamos — worldly exaltation (A.D. 313-538)

- **Revelation 2:12** — "These things saith he which hath the sharp sword with two edges." To a church tempted to compromise, Christ comes as the One whose word divides truth from error.

  > "We now reach a period when influences began to work through which errors and evils were likely to creep into the church." — DAR 354.4.

- **Revelation 2:13** — "I know thy works, and where thou dwellest, even where Satan's seat is." Pergamos signifies "height, elevation" — the period from Constantine's professed conversion (A.D. 323) to the establishment of the papacy (A.D. 538), when the church was exalted into worldly favor.

  > "The word Pergamos signifies hight, elevation. The period covered by this church may be located from the days of Constantine, or perhaps, rather, from his professed conversion to Christianity, A. D. 323, to the establishment of the papacy, A. D. 538." — DAR 354.5.

- **Revelation 2:14-15** — "Them that hold the doctrine of Balaam... so hast thou also them that hold the doctrine of the Nicolaitans." Worldly policy, pride, and popularity bred false doctrine — the working of the "mystery of iniquity" that ripened into the papal man of sin.

  > "It was a period in which the true servants of God had to struggle against a spirit of worldly policy, pride, and popularity among the professed followers of Christ, and against the virulent workings of the mystery of iniquity, which finally resulted in the full development of the papal man of sin." — DAR 354.5.

- **Revelation 2:16-17** — "Repent; or else I will come unto thee quickly, and will fight against them with the sword of my mouth.... To him that overcometh will I give to eat of the hidden manna." Christ's remedy is the sword of His mouth — His word — against corrupting doctrine.

### 4. Thyatira — papal supremacy and the long tribulation (538-1798)

- **Revelation 2:18** — "These things saith the Son of God, who hath his eyes like unto a flame of fire, and his feet are like fine brass." Searching eyes for the longest and darkest period of the church.

  > "Thyatira signifies 'sweet savor of labor,' or 'sacrifice of contrition.' This would well describe the state of the church of Jesus Christ during the long period of papal triumph and persecution." — DAR 359.1.

- **Revelation 2:19** — "I know thy works, and charity, and service, and faith, and thy patience... and the last to be more than the first." Thyatira ("sweet savor of labor," "sacrifice of contrition") is the one church commended for spiritual _improvement_ — believers grew in grace amid the great tribulation such as never was.

  > "This age of such dreadful tribulation upon the church as never was … improved the religious condition of believers.… There had been an improvement in their condition, a growth in grace.… This church is the only one that is commended for an improvement in spiritual things." — DAR 359.1.

- **Revelation 2:20-23** — "Thou sufferest that woman Jezebel, which calleth herself a prophetess, to teach and to seduce my servants." Jezebel pictures the apostate, persecuting power tolerated within Christendom during the 1260 years of papal triumph (538-1798).

  > "As in the preceding church Antipas denoted, not an individual, but a class of persons, so, doubtless, Jezebel is here to be understood in the same sense." — DAR 359.2.

- **Revelation 2:24-25** — "Unto the rest in Thyatira... which have not known the depths of Satan... that which ye have already hold fast till I come." A faithful remnant is addressed through the whole supremacy; "till I come" carries this church across the centuries.
- **Revelation 2:26-28** — "He that overcometh, and keepeth my works unto the end, to him will I give power over the nations.... And I will give him the morning star." The overcomers of the dark ages are promised dominion and Christ Himself, the Morning Star.

### 5. Sardis — the reformed churches grown formal (1798-1833)

- **Revelation 3:1** — "These things saith he that hath the seven Spirits of God, and the seven stars; I know thy works, that thou hast a name that thou livest, and art dead." Sardis ("that which remains," "song of joy") is the reformed churches — having a name to live, but sunk into formality.

  > "Sardis signifies 'prince or song of joy,' or 'that which remains.' We then have before us, as constituting this church, the reformed churches.… The great fault found with this church is that it has a name to live, but is dead." — DAR 363.3-363.4.

- **Revelation 3:2-3** — "Be watchful, and strengthen the things which remain, that are ready to die.... If therefore thou shalt not watch, I will come on thee as a thief." A call to the Protestant bodies, from the era after the papal deadly wound (1798) to the great Advent awakening, to revive before probation closes.

  > "If the dates of the preceding churches have been correctly fixed, the period covered by the church of Sardis must commence about the year 1798.… [it constitutes] the reformed churches, from the date above named to the great movement which marked another era in the history of the people of God." — DAR 363.2-363.3.

- **Revelation 3:4-5** — "Thou hast a few names even in Sardis which have not defiled their garments; and they shall walk with me in white... I will not blot out his name out of the book of life." A faithful few remain undefiled; their names stand in the book of life unblotted.
- **Revelation 3:6** — "He that hath an ear, let him hear what the Spirit saith unto the churches." The same closing summons binds every period into one prophetic series.

### 6. Philadelphia — the Advent movement, the open door (1833-1844)

- **Revelation 3:7** — "These things saith he that is holy, he that is true, he that hath the key of David, he that openeth, and no man shutteth; and shutteth, and no man openeth." Christ holds the key — the opening of the most holy place of the heavenly sanctuary in 1844.

  > "A similar opening and shutting, or change of ministration, must be accomplished by Christ when the time comes for the cleansing of the heavenly sanctuary. And the time did come for this service to commence at the close of the 2300 days, in 1844.… the opening being the opening of his ministration in the most holy place, and the shutting, its cessation in the first apartment." — DAR 367.2.

- **Revelation 3:8** — "I have set before thee an open door, and no man can shut it: for thou hast a little strength, and hast kept my word." Philadelphia signifies "brotherly love," the position and spirit of those who received the Advent message up to the autumn of 1844, who laid aside party names and selfishness in one consecrated movement.

  > "The word Philadelphia signifies brotherly love, and expresses the position and spirit of those who received the Advent message up to the autumn of 1844. As they came out of the sectarian churches, they left party names and party feelings behind.… Selfishness and covetousness were laid aside, and a spirit of consecration and sacrifice was cherished." — DAR 366.3.

- **Revelation 3:9-10** — "I will make them of the synagogue of Satan... to come and worship before thy feet.... I also will keep thee from the hour of temptation, which shall come upon all the world." A promise of vindication and of preservation through the trial coming on the whole earth.

  > "Verse 9 probably applies to those who do not keep pace with the advancing light of truth, and who oppose those that do. Such shall yet be made to feel and confess that God loves those, who … continue to advance in the knowledge of his truth." — DAR 368.1.

  > "Those who now live in patient, faithful obedience to the commandments of God and the faith of Jesus, will be kept in the hour of temptation and peril just before us." — DAR 368.2.

- **Revelation 3:11-12** — "Behold, I come quickly: hold that fast which thou hast.... Him that overcometh will I make a pillar in the temple of my God." To the Advent believers, the imminent coming and a fixed, permanent place in God's temple — "he shall go no more out."

### 7. Laodicea — the judgment-hour church (1844-advent)

- **Revelation 3:14** — "These things saith the Amen, the faithful and true witness, the beginning of the creation of God." The last church meets Christ as the faithful and true Witness — fitting, for this is the period of the investigative Judgment.

  > "This is, then, the final message to the churches ere the close of probation. And though the description of their condition which he gives to the indifferent Laodiceans is fearful and startling, nevertheless it cannot be denied; for the Witness is 'faithful and true.'" — DAR 371.2.

- **Revelation 3:15-16** — "Thou art neither cold nor hot.... because thou art lukewarm... I will spue thee out of my mouth." Laodicea signifies "the judging of the people," or "a just people"; it is the last stage of the church, under the third angel's message, while the great day of atonement is transpiring.

  > "Laodicea signifies the judging of the people, or, according to Cruden, a just people.… It is the last stage of the church. It consequently applies to believers under the third message … while the great day of atonement is transpiring, and the investigative Judgment is going forward upon the house of God." — DAR 371.1.

- **Revelation 3:17** — "Thou sayest, I am rich, and increased with goods, and have need of nothing; and knowest not that thou art wretched, and miserable, and poor, and blind, and naked." The self-deceived, self-satisfied condition of the church living in the judgment hour.

  > "Such the Laodiceans think is their condition. They are not hypocrites, because they 'know not' that they are poor, miserable, blind, and naked." — DAR 374.1.

- **Revelation 3:18** — "I counsel thee to buy of me gold tried in the fire... and white raiment... and anoint thine eyes with eyesalve, that thou mayest see." The threefold remedy: tried faith and love, Christ's righteousness, and spiritual discernment.

  > "Buy of me, says the true Witness, gold tried in the fire, that thou mayest be rich, and white raiment, that thou mayest be clothed, and anoint thine eyes with eye-salve, that thou mayest see. This shows at once to the deceived Laodiceans the objects they lack, and the extent of their destitution." — DAR 374.2.

- **Revelation 3:19-20** — "As many as I love, I rebuke and chasten: be zealous therefore, and repent. Behold, I stand at the door, and knock." Even the lukewarm are loved; Christ still pleads at the door of the last church.

  > "The Token of Love. — This, strange as it may seem, is chastisement. 'As many as I love, I rebuke and chasten.' If we are without chastisement, we are not sons." — DAR 378.1.

  > "'Behold, I stand at the door, and knock.' Why does he? Not because he is without home elsewhere.… But he goes round from door to door in Laodicea. He stands at each, and knocks, because he came to seek and to save that which is lost." — DAR 378.3.

- **Revelation 3:21-22** — "To him that overcometh will I grant to sit with me in my throne.... He that hath an ear, let him hear what the Spirit saith unto the churches." The series closes as it ran — the overcomer enthroned with Christ, and the Spirit's call sounding to every age at once.

### The church as a woman — symbol confirmed

- **Revelation 1:20 with Revelation 12; Revelation 17** — A woman is the Bible symbol of a church: "a virtuous woman representing a pure church, a vile woman an apostate church." The lampstand-church of Revelation 1-3 is the same pure woman who, fallen, becomes Babylon.

  > "In Revelation 17 Babylon is represented as a woman—a figure which is used in the Bible as the symbol of a church, a virtuous woman representing a pure church, a vile woman an apostate church." — GC 381.1.

- **2 Corinthians 11:2** — "I have espoused you to one husband, that I may present you as a chaste virgin to Christ." The marriage figure binds Christ to His church through all seven periods; her unfaithfulness is spiritual adultery.

  > "In the Bible the sacred and enduring character of the relation that exists between Christ and His church is represented by the union of marriage.… And Paul employs the same figure in the New Testament when he says: 'I have espoused you to one husband, that I may present you as a chaste virgin to Christ.'" — GC 381.2.

  > "The unfaithfulness of the church to Christ in permitting her confidence and affection to be turned from Him, and allowing the love of worldly things to occupy the soul, is likened to the violation of the marriage vow." — GC 381.3-382.
