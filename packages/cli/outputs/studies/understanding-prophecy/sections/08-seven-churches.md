# 8. The Seven Churches of Revelation 1-3

> _The messages to the seven churches of Asia are not seven local letters merely, but a continuous prophecy: seven successive periods of the Christian church, running from apostolic Ephesus to the judgment-hour Laodicea. Each name, in its very meaning, fits the spiritual condition of its age; together they span the whole gospel dispensation, the number seven denoting fulness and completeness. This is no novel view — Bishop Newton, More, and Vitringa held it before us. (DAR 328-329, 345-347; GC 381-382.)_

### The seal of the seven: stars and candlesticks defined

- **Revelation 1:11** — "What thou seest, write in a book, and send it unto the seven churches which are in Asia; unto Ephesus, and unto Smyrna, and unto Pergamos, and unto Thyatira, and unto Sardis, and unto Philadelphia, and unto Laodicea." The seven are named in fixed order; that order is the order of history, apostolic to advent. _(DAR 345.3-347.1.)_
- **Revelation 1:20** — "The seven stars are the angels of the seven churches: and the seven candlesticks which thou sawest are the seven churches." Scripture defines its own symbols: the lampstand is the church; the star is its messenger. _(DAR 328.3-329.1.)_
- **Revelation 1:20** (the stars) — The seven stars held in Christ's right hand "are the angels of the seven churches," and an angel of a church is its minister; held in His right hand, they denote the upholding, guidance, and protection given to all true ministers of the whole gospel age. _(DAR 328.3.)_
- **Revelation 1:20** (the candlesticks) — John saw only seven lampstands, "and seven is a number denoting fulness and completeness"; so the whole gospel church, in seven divisions or periods, is symbolized — the Son of man standing in the midst to take cognizance of all His churches through the dispensation. _(DAR 329.1.)_
- **Revelation 1:11; Revelation 1:20** (why prophetic) — "Many contend... that the seven epistles are prophetical of so many successive periods, or states, of the church, from the beginning to the conclusion of all" — the view is "neither new nor local." _(DAR 345.3.)_

### 1. Ephesus — apostolic purity (A.D. 31-100)

- **Revelation 2:1** — "These things saith he that holdeth the seven stars in his right hand, who walketh in the midst of the seven golden candlesticks." Christ opens to the first church as the One who upholds the ministry and watches over all the lampstands. _(DAR 347.2.)_
- **Revelation 2:2-3** — "I know thy works, and thy labour, and thy patience... thou hast tried them which say they are apostles, and are not, and hast found them liars." The early Christians had the doctrine of Christ in purity, the gifts of the Spirit, and tried and exposed false apostles. The name Ephesus, "desirable," well describes the church in its first state. _(DAR 347.1.)_
- **Revelation 2:4** — "Nevertheless I have somewhat against thee, because thou hast left thy first love." Even the apostolic age cooled toward its close; the decline begins. _(DAR 347.1; 348.)_
- **Revelation 2:5** — "Repent... or else I will come unto thee quickly, and will remove thy candlestick out of his place." The threat to remove the lampstand confirms the candlestick is the church-period itself.
- **Revelation 2:6-7** — "Thou hatest the deeds of the Nicolaitans, which I also hate.... To him that overcometh will I give to eat of the tree of life." Reward to the overcomer of every age is Eden restored. _(DAR 347.)_

### 2. Smyrna — the persecuted church (A.D. 100-313)

- **Revelation 2:8** — "These things saith the first and the last, which was dead, and is alive." The risen Christ presents Himself to a dying, persecuted people as the One who Himself conquered death. _(DAR 354.1.)_
- **Revelation 2:9** — "I know thy works, and tribulation, and poverty, (but thou art rich)." Smyrna signifies "myrrh," a fit name for the church passing through the fiery furnace of persecution and proving herself a "sweet-smelling savor" unto God. _(DAR 354.1.)_
- **Revelation 2:10** — "Ye shall have tribulation ten days: be thou faithful unto death, and I will give thee a crown of life." The "ten days" are prophetic: ten years (day-for-a-year), fulfilled in the last and bloodiest of the ten persecutions, under Diocletian, A.D. 302-312. _(DAR 352.3-353; Num. 14:34; Eze. 4:6.)_
- **Revelation 2:11** — "He that overcometh shall not be hurt of the second death." To the martyr church, the promise that the worst the persecutor can do reaches only the first death.

### 3. Pergamos — worldly exaltation (A.D. 313-538)

- **Revelation 2:12** — "These things saith he which hath the sharp sword with two edges." To a church tempted to compromise, Christ comes as the One whose word divides truth from error. _(DAR 354.5.)_
- **Revelation 2:13** — "I know thy works, and where thou dwellest, even where Satan's seat is." Pergamos signifies "height, elevation" — the period from Constantine's professed conversion (A.D. 323) to the establishment of the papacy (A.D. 538), when the church was exalted into worldly favor. _(DAR 354.5.)_
- **Revelation 2:14-15** — "Them that hold the doctrine of Balaam... so hast thou also them that hold the doctrine of the Nicolaitans." Worldly policy, pride, and popularity bred false doctrine — the working of the "mystery of iniquity" that ripened into the papal man of sin. _(DAR 354.5.)_
- **Revelation 2:16-17** — "Repent; or else I will come unto thee quickly, and will fight against them with the sword of my mouth.... To him that overcometh will I give to eat of the hidden manna." Christ's remedy is the sword of His mouth — His word — against corrupting doctrine.

### 4. Thyatira — papal supremacy and the long tribulation (538-1798)

- **Revelation 2:18** — "These things saith the Son of God, who hath his eyes like unto a flame of fire, and his feet are like fine brass." Searching eyes for the longest and darkest period of the church. _(DAR 359.1.)_
- **Revelation 2:19** — "I know thy works, and charity, and service, and faith, and thy patience... and the last to be more than the first." Thyatira ("sweet savor of labor," "sacrifice of contrition") is the one church commended for spiritual _improvement_ — believers grew in grace amid the great tribulation such as never was. _(DAR 359.1; Matt. 24:21.)_
- **Revelation 2:20-23** — "Thou sufferest that woman Jezebel, which calleth herself a prophetess, to teach and to seduce my servants." Jezebel pictures the apostate, persecuting power tolerated within Christendom during the 1260 years of papal triumph (538-1798). _(DAR 359.1.)_
- **Revelation 2:24-25** — "Unto the rest in Thyatira... which have not known the depths of Satan... that which ye have already hold fast till I come." A faithful remnant is addressed through the whole supremacy; "till I come" carries this church across the centuries.
- **Revelation 2:26-28** — "He that overcometh, and keepeth my works unto the end, to him will I give power over the nations.... And I will give him the morning star." The overcomers of the dark ages are promised dominion and Christ Himself, the Morning Star.

### 5. Sardis — the reformed churches grown formal (1798-1833)

- **Revelation 3:1** — "These things saith he that hath the seven Spirits of God, and the seven stars; I know thy works, that thou hast a name that thou livest, and art dead." Sardis ("that which remains," "song of joy") is the reformed churches — having a name to live, but sunk into formality. _(DAR 363.1-363.3.)_
- **Revelation 3:2-3** — "Be watchful, and strengthen the things which remain, that are ready to die.... If therefore thou shalt not watch, I will come on thee as a thief." A call to the Protestant bodies, from the era after the papal deadly wound (1798) to the great Advent awakening, to revive before probation closes. _(DAR 363.3.)_
- **Revelation 3:4-5** — "Thou hast a few names even in Sardis which have not defiled their garments; and they shall walk with me in white... I will not blot out his name out of the book of life." A faithful few remain undefiled; their names stand in the book of life unblotted.
- **Revelation 3:6** — "He that hath an ear, let him hear what the Spirit saith unto the churches." The same closing summons binds every period into one prophetic series.

### 6. Philadelphia — the Advent movement, the open door (1833-1844)

- **Revelation 3:7** — "These things saith he that is holy, he that is true, he that hath the key of David, he that openeth, and no man shutteth; and shutteth, and no man openeth." Christ holds the key — the opening of the most holy place of the heavenly sanctuary in 1844. _(DAR 366.3; 368.)_
- **Revelation 3:8** — "I have set before thee an open door, and no man can shut it: for thou hast a little strength, and hast kept my word." Philadelphia signifies "brotherly love," the position and spirit of those who received the Advent message up to the autumn of 1844, who laid aside party names and selfishness in one consecrated movement. _(DAR 366.3.)_
- **Revelation 3:9-10** — "I will make them of the synagogue of Satan... to come and worship before thy feet.... I also will keep thee from the hour of temptation, which shall come upon all the world." A promise of vindication and of preservation through the trial coming on the whole earth. _(DAR 366.3-367.)_
- **Revelation 3:11-12** — "Behold, I come quickly: hold that fast which thou hast.... Him that overcometh will I make a pillar in the temple of my God." To the Advent believers, the imminent coming and a fixed, permanent place in God's temple — "he shall go no more out."

### 7. Laodicea — the judgment-hour church (1844-advent)

- **Revelation 3:14** — "These things saith the Amen, the faithful and true witness, the beginning of the creation of God." The last church meets Christ as the faithful and true Witness — fitting, for this is the period of the investigative Judgment. _(DAR 371.1.)_
- **Revelation 3:15-16** — "Thou art neither cold nor hot.... because thou art lukewarm... I will spue thee out of my mouth." Laodicea signifies "the judging of the people," or "a just people"; it is the last stage of the church, under the third angel's message, while the great day of atonement is transpiring. _(DAR 371.1; Rev. 14:9-14.)_
- **Revelation 3:17** — "Thou sayest, I am rich, and increased with goods, and have need of nothing; and knowest not that thou art wretched, and miserable, and poor, and blind, and naked." The self-deceived, self-satisfied condition of the church living in the judgment hour. _(DAR 371.)_
- **Revelation 3:18** — "I counsel thee to buy of me gold tried in the fire... and white raiment... and anoint thine eyes with eyesalve, that thou mayest see." The threefold remedy: tried faith and love, Christ's righteousness, and spiritual discernment. _(DAR 371-372.)_
- **Revelation 3:19-20** — "As many as I love, I rebuke and chasten: be zealous therefore, and repent. Behold, I stand at the door, and knock." Even the lukewarm are loved; Christ still pleads at the door of the last church. _(DAR 372.)_
- **Revelation 3:21-22** — "To him that overcometh will I grant to sit with me in my throne.... He that hath an ear, let him hear what the Spirit saith unto the churches." The series closes as it ran — the overcomer enthroned with Christ, and the Spirit's call sounding to every age at once.

### The church as a woman — symbol confirmed

- **Revelation 1:20 with Revelation 12; Revelation 17** — A woman is the Bible symbol of a church: "a virtuous woman representing a pure church, a vile woman an apostate church." The lampstand-church of Revelation 1-3 is the same pure woman who, fallen, becomes Babylon. _(GC 381.1.)_
- **2 Corinthians 11:2** — "I have espoused you to one husband, that I may present you as a chaste virgin to Christ." The marriage figure binds Christ to His church through all seven periods; her unfaithfulness is spiritual adultery. _(GC 381.2-382.)_
