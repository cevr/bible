# 21. The Law and the Sabbath in Prophecy

> _The last great controversy is fought over the law of God, and over its heart, the Sabbath. When the temple in heaven is opened, the ark — the receptacle of the ten commandments — is seen (Rev. 11:19), and the law is shown unchanged. The dragon makes war on the remnant who "keep the commandments of God" (Rev. 12:17; 14:12); the little horn "thought to change times and laws" (Dan. 7:25) — and the only law it dared touch was the fourth commandment, tearing out the Sabbath and erecting Sunday in its place. The Sabbath is the seal of God and the memorial of creation, restored in the first angel's call to "worship him that made heaven, and earth" (Rev. 14:7; Isa. 58). The commandment-keepers stand at the close of probation. (DAR 140-141, 627-628; GC 433-454, 640; EW 32-33.)_

### The ark in the heavenly temple holds the law

- **Revelation 11:19** — "The temple of God was opened in heaven, and there was seen in his temple the ark of his testament." The ark, by definition, is the receptacle of the law; the heavenly sanctuary contains the great original of which Sinai's tables were a copy. This opening marks the beginning of the judgment, when attention is drawn back to God's law.

  > "Therefore the announcement that the temple of God was opened in heaven and the ark of His testament was seen points to the opening of the most holy place of the heavenly sanctuary in 1844 as Christ entered there to perform the closing work of the atonement." — GC 433.1-2.

- **Hebrews 9:4** — The earthly ark held "the tables of the covenant" — the ten commandments. The ark's value was solely as the place of the law; so the opening of the heavenly ark sets the Decalogue before the church.

  > "The ark in the tabernacle on earth contained the two tables of stone, upon which were inscribed the precepts of the law of God. The ark was merely a receptacle for the tables of the law, and the presence of these divine precepts gave to it its value and sacredness." — GC 433.2.

- **Hebrews 8:5** — The earthly priests "serve unto the example and shadow of heavenly things." The pattern was shown Moses in the mount; the law in the heavenly ark is "the great original," its earthly transcript unerring.

  > "The law of God in the sanctuary in heaven is the great original, of which the precepts inscribed upon the tables of stone and recorded by Moses in the Pentateuch were an unerring transcript… Not one command has been annulled; not a jot or tittle has been changed." — GC 434.1.

- **Revelation 15:5** — "The temple of the tabernacle of the testimony in heaven was opened." The sanctuary is called the place of "the testimony" — another name for the law of God — confirming the law stands at the center of heaven's worship.

  > "Within the holy of holies, in the sanctuary in heaven, the divine law is sacredly enshrined—the law that was spoken by God Himself amid the thunders of Sinai and written with His own finger on the tables of stone." — GC 433.2.

- **Early Writings (EW 32-33)** — In vision the ark was seen, the tables opened, "the ten commandments written... with the finger of God," and "the fourth, the Sabbath commandment, shone above them all... a halo of glory was all around it." The Sabbath was not nailed to the cross; "the pope had changed it... for he was to change times and laws."

  > "Jesus opened them, and I saw the ten commandments written on them with the finger of God… But the fourth, the Sabbath commandment, shone above them all; for the Sabbath was set apart to be kept in honor of God's holy name… I saw that the Sabbath commandment was not nailed to the cross… But the pope had changed it from the seventh to the first day of the week; for he was to change times and laws." — EW 32.3.

### The controversy is over the commandments

- **Revelation 12:17** — "The dragon was wroth with the woman, and went to make war with the remnant of her seed, which keep the commandments of God, and have the testimony of Jesus Christ." The last church is marked by commandment-keeping; this is the very point of Satan's final assault.

  > "And the dragon was wroth with the woman, and went to make war with the remnant of her seed, which keep the commandments of God, and have the testimony of Jesus Christ." — DAR 516.3.

- **Revelation 14:12** — "Here is the patience of the saints: here are they that keep the commandments of God, and the faith of Jesus." The fruit of the threefold message is a commandment-keeping company — distinguished, of necessity, by the seventh-day Sabbath.

  > "It brings out a company of whom it can be said, 'Here are they that keep the commandments of God and the faith of Jesus.'… those who receive the message are rendered peculiar by their practice in reference to the commandments of God." — DAR 627.3.

  > "These truths, as presented in Revelation 14 in connection with 'the everlasting gospel,' will distinguish the church of Christ at the time of His appearing. For as the result of the threefold message it is announced: 'Here are they that keep the commandments of God, and the faith of Jesus.'" — GC 453.3.

- **Revelation 22:14** — "Blessed are they that do his commandments, that they may have right to the tree of life, and may enter in through the gates into the city." Obedience to the commandments is the condition of entrance to the city of God — the law's authority reaches to the very end.

  > "It brings out a company of whom it can be said, 'Here are they that keep the commandments of God and the faith of Jesus.' In the very heart of Christendom this work is done; and those who receive the message are rendered peculiar by their practice in reference to the commandments of God." — DAR 627.3.

- **Ecclesiastes 12:13** — "Fear God, and keep his commandments: for this is the whole duty of man." The whole of human duty is bound up in the commandments; the controversy turns on whether men will obey or transgress them.

  > "While conflicting doctrines and theories abound, the law of God is the one unerring rule by which all opinions, doctrines, and theories are to be tested. Says the prophet: 'If they speak not according to this word, it is because there is no light in them.'" — GC 452.2.

- **John 14:15** — "If ye love me, keep my commandments." Christ makes obedience the test of love; the remnant's commandment-keeping is no legalism but loyalty to the Lawgiver.

  > "These truths, as presented in Revelation 14 in connection with 'the everlasting gospel,' will distinguish the church of Christ at the time of His appearing… 'Here are they that keep the commandments of God, and the faith of Jesus.'" — GC 453.3.

### The little horn thought to change the law — and touched the Sabbath

- **Daniel 7:25** — The little horn "shall... think to change times and laws." These are not human laws (which it could change at will) but the laws of the Most High — which it could only _think_ to change, not truly change. The papacy expunged the second commandment, divided the tenth, and tore the Sabbath from the fourth, erecting Sunday in its place.

  > "It has, in its catechisms, expunged the second commandment of the decalogue to make way for its adoration of images. It has divided the tenth commandment to make up the number ten. And, more audacious than all! it has taken hold of the fourth commandment, torn from its place the Sabbath of Jehovah, the only memorial of the great God ever given to man, and erected in its place a rival institution to serve another purpose." — DAR 140.3.

- **Daniel 7:8** — The little horn rose "among" the ten horns with "a mouth speaking great things." Identified as the papacy (538-1798), this is the power that presumed to alter the Decalogue — the "great words against the most High."

  > "Pagan Rome could slay the infants, but spare the mothers; but papal Rome slew both mothers and infants together. No age, no sex, no condition in life, was exempt from her relentless rage… And shall 'think to change times and laws.'… the times and laws in question were such as this power should only think to change, but not be able to change. They are… the laws of the Most High." — DAR 140.2-3.

- **Daniel 7:25 (with 2 Thess. 2)** — Daniel's little horn and Paul's "man of sin" are complements: "Only by changing God's law could the papacy exalt itself above God." The change of the Sabbath is precisely how that self-exaltation was accomplished.

  > "Says Daniel, of the little horn, the papacy: 'He shall think to change times and the law.' Daniel 7:25, R.V. And Paul styled the same power the 'man of sin,' who was to exalt himself above God. One prophecy is a complement of the other. Only by changing God's law could the papacy exalt itself above God." — GC 446.1.

- **Isaiah 24:5** — "They have transgressed the laws, changed the ordinance, broken the everlasting covenant." Scripture foretells a transgression that _changes_ an ordinance — fulfilled in the substitution of Sunday for the Sabbath of the everlasting covenant.

  > "The papacy has attempted to change the law of God… the fourth commandment has been so changed as to authorize the observance of the first instead of the seventh day as the Sabbath… An intentional, deliberate change is presented: 'He shall think to change the times and the law.' The change in the fourth commandment exactly fulfills the prophecy." — GC 446.2.

- **The Catholic catechism** — Compared with the Bible, the catechisms drop the second commandment, split the tenth, and rewrite the fourth to enjoin Sunday — Rome openly claiming the change was "made by the church," not by God.

  > "If now the reader will compare the ten commandments as found in Roman Catholic catechisms with those commandments as found in the Bible… the second commandment is left out, that the tenth is divided into two… and that the fourth commandment (called the third in their enumeration) is made to enjoin the observance of Sunday as the Sabbath… They expressly claim a change here, and also that the change has been made by the church." — DAR 554.1-2.

### The law of God is perpetual and unchangeable

- **Matthew 5:17-18** — "Think not that I am come to destroy the law... Till heaven and earth pass, one jot or one tittle shall in no wise pass from the law." Christ did not abolish but established the law; its smallest stroke stands while heaven and earth endure.

  > "They saw, as never before, the force of the Saviour's words: 'Till heaven and earth pass, one jot or one tittle shall in no wise pass from the law.' Matthew 5:18. The law of God, being a revelation of His will, a transcript of His character, must forever endure, 'as a faithful witness in heaven.'" — GC 434.1.

- **Psalm 111:7-8** — "All his commandments are sure. They stand fast for ever and ever." The commandments are not temporary ordinances but eternal verities — incapable of being changed by any earthly power.

  > "Not one command has been annulled; not a jot or tittle has been changed. Says the psalmist: 'Forever, O Lord, Thy word is settled in heaven.' 'All His commandments are sure. They stand fast for ever and ever.' Psalm 119:89; 111:7, 8." — GC 434.1.

- **Psalm 89:34** — "My covenant will I not break, nor alter the thing that is gone out of my lips." God Himself will not alter His word; the claim that He transferred the Sabbath's sanctity to another day contradicts His own declaration.

  > "And I saw that if God had changed the Sabbath from the seventh to the first day, He would have changed the writing of the Sabbath commandment… But I saw that it read the same as when written on the tables of stone by the finger of God, and delivered to Moses on Sinai." — EW 33.1.

- **Psalm 119:142** — "Thy law is the truth." The law is identified with truth itself; to change it is to corrupt the truth — the very work charged to the little horn.

  > "The law of God, being a revelation of His will, a transcript of His character, must forever endure, 'as a faithful witness in heaven.' Not one command has been annulled; not a jot or tittle has been changed." — GC 434.1.

- **James 2:10-12** — "Whosoever shall keep the whole law, and yet offend in one point, he is guilty of all." The Decalogue is an indivisible unity; to break the fourth commandment is to break the whole — so men "shall be judged by the law of liberty."

  > "I saw that the Sabbath commandment was not nailed to the cross. If it was, the other nine commandments were; and we are at liberty to break them all, as well as to break the fourth." — EW 32.3.

- **Romans 3:31** — "Do we then make void the law through faith? God forbid: yea, we establish the law." The gospel does not annul the law but upholds it; faith and obedience stand together. _(Waggoner / Jones, the law and the gospel.)_

- **Romans 7:7** — "I had not known sin, but by the law." The law remains the standard that defines sin; were it abolished, sin itself would be undefinable.

  > "The first step in reconciliation to God is the conviction of sin. 'Sin is the transgression of the law.' 'By the law is the knowledge of sin.' 1 John 3:4; Romans 3:20. In order to see his guilt, the sinner must test his character by God's great standard of righteousness." — GC 467-468.

### The Sabbath: memorial of creation and seal of God

- **Genesis 2:1-3** — "God blessed the seventh day, and sanctified it: because that in it he had rested from all his work." Instituted at creation, before sin and before Israel, the Sabbath is the world's first memorial and belongs to all mankind.

  > "Hallowed by the Creator's rest and blessing, the Sabbath was kept by Adam in his innocence in holy Eden; by Adam, fallen yet repentant, when he was driven from his happy estate. It was kept by all the patriarchs, from Abel to righteous Noah, to Abraham, to Jacob." — GC 453.1.

- **Exodus 20:8-11** — "Remember the sabbath day... the seventh day is the sabbath of the LORD thy God... For in six days the LORD made heaven and earth... and rested the seventh day." The fourth commandment alone names and titles the Lawgiver as Creator — making it the seal of His law.

  > "In the very bosom of the Decalogue is the fourth commandment, as it was first proclaimed: 'Remember the Sabbath day, to keep it holy… for in six days the Lord made heaven and earth, the sea, and all that in them is, and rested the seventh day: wherefore the Lord blessed the Sabbath day, and hallowed it.' Exodus 20:8-11." — GC 434.2.

  > "The seal of God's law is found in the fourth commandment. This only, of all the ten, brings to view both the name and the title of the Lawgiver. It declares Him to be the Creator of the heavens and the earth, and thus shows His claim to reverence and worship above all others." — GC 452.1.

- **Mark 2:27-28** — "The sabbath was made for man... therefore the Son of man is Lord also of the sabbath." Made for man at creation, the Sabbath is Christ's own institution; He is its Lord, not its abolisher.

  > "But the Bible points to the seventh day, and not to the first, as the Lord's day. Said Christ: 'The Son of man is Lord also of the Sabbath.' The fourth commandment declares: 'The seventh day is the Sabbath of the Lord.'" — GC 446.3.

- **Luke 23:56** — The women "rested the sabbath day according to the commandment." Even across the crucifixion the Sabbath stood unchanged — Christ resting in the tomb on the seventh day, His followers honoring it "according to the commandment."

  > "But I saw that it read the same as when written on the tables of stone by the finger of God, and delivered to Moses on Sinai. 'But the seventh day is the Sabbath of the Lord thy God.'" — EW 33.1.

- **Ezekiel 20:12** — "I gave them my sabbaths, to be a sign between me and them, that they might know that I am the LORD that sanctify them." The Sabbath is God's appointed sign of sanctification — a sign that identifies His people.

  > "'Moreover, also I gave them my Sabbaths, to be a sign between me and them, that they might know that I am the Lord that sanctify them.'" — THB 44.1.

- **Ezekiel 20:20** — "Hallow my sabbaths; and they shall be a sign between me and you, that ye may know that I am the LORD your God." Repeated for emphasis: the Sabbath is the token by which God's worshipers know Him from all others.

  > "'I am the Lord your God: walk in my statutes, and keep my judgments, and do them; and hollow my Sabbaths, and they shall be a sign between me and you, that ye may know that I am the Lord your God.'… The Sabbath is a sign between God and his people; hence it seals his law as genuine." — THB 44.1-2.

- **Isaiah 8:16** — "Bind up the testimony, seal the law among my disciples." The seal of God's law is found in the fourth commandment — the only precept bearing the name, title, and dominion of the Lawgiver, the Creator.

  > "'Bind up the testimony, seal the law among My disciples.' Isaiah 8:16. The seal of God's law is found in the fourth commandment. This only, of all the ten, brings to view both the name and the title of the Lawgiver… Aside from this precept, there is nothing in the Decalogue to show by whose authority the law is given." — GC 452.1.

- **Sign and seal are one** — "Sign and seal are synonymous terms" (Rom. 4:11). The Sabbath being the sign between God and His people, it seals His law as genuine; "take this fourth commandment from the ten, and the seal of the living God is gone."

  > "Sign and seal are synonymous terms. See Romans 4:11… The Sabbath is a sign between God and his people; hence it seals his law as genuine… Take this fourth commandment from the ten, and the seal of the living God is gone, and the knowledge of their author is taken from us." — THB 44.2.

### The Sabbath restored in the last message — the final test

- **Revelation 14:7** — "Worship him that made heaven, and earth, and the sea, and the fountains of waters." The first angel's call to worship the Creator is in the very language of the fourth commandment — a summons back to the Sabbath as the judgment hour strikes.

  > "In Revelation 14, men are called upon to worship the Creator; and the prophecy brings to view a class that, as the result of the threefold message, are keeping the commandments of God. One of these commandments points directly to God as the Creator… It follows that the message which commands men to worship God and keep His commandments will especially call upon them to keep the fourth commandment." — GC 437-438.

  > "And worship him that made heaven, and earth, and the sea, and the fountains of waters… Here is the patience of the saints: here are they that keep the commandments of God, and the faith of Jesus." — DAR 585.3.

- **Isaiah 58:12-14** — "Thou shalt be called, The repairer of the breach... if thou turn away thy foot from the sabbath... and call the sabbath a delight." A last-day people repair the breach made in God's law by restoring the trodden-down Sabbath — the very work of the third angel's message.

  > "The breach was made in the law of God when the Sabbath was changed by the Roman power. But the time has come for that divine institution to be restored. The breach is to be repaired and the foundation of many generations to be raised up." — GC 452.4-453.

- **Ezekiel 22:26** — "They have... hid their eyes from my sabbaths, and I am profaned among them." The priests' sin was profaning the holy and hiding their eyes from the Sabbath; the last message exposes and reverses that apostasy. _(Cf. Isa. 58:13.)_

- **Isaiah 66:22-23** — "As the new heavens and the new earth... so shall your seed and your name remain. And it shall come to pass... from one sabbath to another, shall all flesh come to worship before me." The Sabbath outlasts this world and is kept in the earth made new — the final proof of its perpetuity.

  > "Hallowed by the Creator's rest and blessing, the Sabbath was kept by Adam in his innocence in holy Eden… It was kept by all the patriarchs, from Abel to righteous Noah, to Abraham, to Jacob." — GC 453.1.

- **The line of demarcation** — The third angel's message brings its adherents to the seventh day, the only mark that distinguishes them; "no plainer line of demarcation could be drawn between two classes" — Sabbath-keepers and Sunday-keepers.

  > "No plainer line of demarcation could be drawn between two classes. The time which one class regard as sacred, and devote to religious uses, the other look upon as only secular, and devote to ordinary labor… The message brings its adherents to the seventh day; for in this way only are they made peculiar." — DAR 627.3-628.1.

- **Two seals, two marks** — God's seal is His Sabbath; the beast's mark is its rival sabbath, Sunday. "Christendom will at last be divided into just two classes" — those sealed with the seal of the living God and those who receive the mark.

  > "Thus God has a seal, or mark, which is his Sabbath. The beast also has a seal, or mark, which is his Sabbath… Christendom will at last be divided into just two classes; to wit, those who are sealed with the seal of the living God, that is, have his mark, or keep his Sabbath; and those who are sealed with the seal of the beast." — DAR 628.2.

- **The seal of the living God** — "Too late they see that the Sabbath of the fourth commandment is the seal of the living God." In the final crisis the Sabbath becomes the great test of loyalty, distinguishing the worshipers of God from the worshipers of the beast.

  > "Too late they see that the Sabbath of the fourth commandment is the seal of the living God. Too late they see the true nature of their spurious sabbath and the sandy foundation upon which they have been building. They find that they have been fighting against God." — GC 640.1.

  > "Christendom will at last be divided into just two classes; to wit, those who are sealed with the seal of the living God, that is, have his mark, or keep his Sabbath; and those who are sealed with the seal of the beast, that is, have his mark, or keep his Sabbath." — DAR 627-628.

- **Worship Christ, keep His commandments** — "If thou wilt enter into life, keep the commandments" (Matt. 19:17). The Lord who is worshiped as Creator in the first message is the Lord who points men to obedience as the way of life — the law and the gospel meeting in the Sabbath.

  > "These truths, as presented in Revelation 14 in connection with 'the everlasting gospel,' will distinguish the church of Christ at the time of His appearing… 'Here are they that keep the commandments of God, and the faith of Jesus.' And this message is the last to be given before the coming of the Lord." — GC 453.3.
