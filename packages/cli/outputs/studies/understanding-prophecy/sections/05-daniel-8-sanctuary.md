# 5. Daniel 8 — The Ram, the He-Goat, the Little Horn, and the Sanctuary

> _Daniel 8 retraces the same march of empires as chapters 2 and 7, but begins one
> kingdom later — dropping Babylon (already falling) and opening with Medo-Persia. Here
> the angel Gabriel names the powers outright: the ram is Media and Persia, the he-goat
> is Grecia, its great horn the first king. The "little horn" that follows is Rome in both
> its phases, pagan and papal — never Antiochus — and "the daily" together with "the
> transgression of desolation" fills the long 2300 days that reach to the cleansing of the
> sanctuary in 1844. The angel's own interpretation fixes the meaning beyond dispute.
> (DAR 145-182; TTHDS 1-2; GC 328.)_

### The setting and the day-for-a-year key

- **Daniel 8:1-2** — In Belshazzar's third year Daniel sees himself "at Shushan in the palace... by the river of Ulai." Shushan was the metropolis of Elam, then held by Babylon, where the king had a royal palace; soon it passed to the Medes and Persians — the very empire about to be unveiled.

  > "Shushan, as we learn from Prideaux, was the metropolis of the province of Elam. This was then in the hands of the Babylonians, and there the king of Babylon had a royal palace. Daniel, as minister of state, and employed about the king's business, was accordingly in that place. … Abradates, viceroy or prince of Shushan, revolted to Cyrus, and the province was joined to the Medes and Persians." — DAR 146.

- **Numbers 14:34** — "each day for a year" — the wilderness sentence makes a day stand for a year, the rule by which the 2300 days are read as 2300 years.

  > "In advance of any argument on the nature and application of these days, the position may be safely taken that they reach to the cleansing of the heavenly sanctuary, for the earthly was to be cleansed each year; and we make the prophet utter nonsense, if we understand him as saying that at the end of 2300 days … an event should take place which was to occur regularly every year." — DAR 178.

  > "Thus far every specification of the prophecies is strikingly fulfilled, and the beginning of the seventy weeks is fixed beyond question at 457 B.C., and their expiration in A.D. 34. From this data there is no difficulty in finding the termination of the 2300 days." — GC 328.

- **Ezekiel 4:6** — "I have appointed thee each day for a year" — the second witness establishing the year-day principle for all the time-prophecies of Daniel and Revelation.

  > "Respecting the 2300 days … The declaration respecting them is a part of a revelation which is given for the instruction of the people of God, and is consequently to be understood." — DAR 178.

### The ram — Medo-Persia (named by the angel)

- **Daniel 8:3-4** — A ram with two unequal horns, "the higher came up last," pushing westward, northward, and southward, doing "according to his will." The two horns are the two nationalities of the empire; the higher-and-later horn is Persia, which rose after Media and overshadowed it.

  > "The two horns represented the two nationalities of which the empire consisted. The higher came up last. This represented the Persian element, which, from being at first simply an ally of the Medes, came to be the leading division of the empire." — DAR 146.

  > "The distinguishing feature of the Persian empire, the union of the two nationalities which composed it, is represented by the two horns of the ram." — DAR 180.

- **Daniel 8:20** — "The ram which thou sawest having two horns are the kings of Media and Persia." The angel names it plainly; no conjecture is permitted. As the disciples said, "now speakest thou plainly, and speakest no proverb."

  > "As the disciples said to the Lord, so may we here say of the angel who spake to Daniel, 'Lo, now speakest thou plainly, and speakest no proverb.' This is an explanation of the vision in language as plain as need be given." — DAR 180.

### The he-goat — Grecia, and its notable horn (Alexander)

- **Daniel 8:5** — "an he goat came from the west... and touched not the ground: and the goat had a notable horn between his eyes." Grecia lay west of Persia; the goat that scarcely touches the ground pictures the marvelous swiftness of the Macedonian conquest — the same speed shown by the four wings of the leopard in chapter 7.

  > "The goat came from the west. Grecia lay west of Persia. … He 'touched not the ground.' Such was the marvelous celerity of his movements that he did not seem to touch the ground, but to fly from point to point with the swiftness of the wind; the same feature is brought to view by the four wings of the leopard in the vision of chapter 7." — DAR 148.

- **Daniel 7:6** — The third beast, "like a leopard, which had upon the back of it four wings of a fowl... and four heads" — the parallel symbol of Greece: swift (four wings) and four-fold after Alexander (four heads), confirming the goat.

  > "He 'touched not the ground.' Such was the marvelous celerity of his movements that he did not seem to touch the ground, but to fly from point to point with the swiftness of the wind; the same feature is brought to view by the four wings of the leopard in the vision of chapter 7." — DAR 148.

- **Daniel 8:6-7** — The goat runs at the ram "in the fury of his power," breaks his two horns, casts him down, and stamps upon him — the overthrow of Persia by Alexander at the Granicus, Issus, and Arbela (B.C. 331), with none to deliver.

  > "Alexander first vanquished the generals of Darius at the River Granicus in Phrygia; he next attacked and totally routed Darius at the passes of Issus in Cilicia, and afterward on the plains of Arbela in Syria. This last battle occurred B. C. 331, and marked the conclusion of the Persian empire." — DAR 148.

  > "The two horns were broken, and the ram was cast to the ground and stamped upon. Persia was subdued, the country ravaged, its armies cut to pieces and scattered … Thus the ram had no power to stand before the goat, and there was none that could deliver him out of his hand." — DAR 149.

- **Daniel 8:21** — "the rough goat is the king of Grecia: and the great horn that is between his eyes is the first king." That first king was Alexander the Great.

  > "The notable horn between his eyes. This is explained in verse 21 to be the first king of the Macedonian empire. This king was Alexander the Great." — DAR 148.

  > "Grecia attained its greatest glory as a unit under the leadership of Alexander the Great, a general as famous as the world has ever seen. … the one notable horn symbolized Alexander the Great." — DAR 180.

- **Daniel 2:39** — "another third kingdom of brass, which shall bear rule over all the earth" — the same Grecia of the image, here the goat that swept "on the face of the whole earth."

  > "The only natural inference would be that the little horn, the power which in this vision succeeds Grecia as an 'exceeding great' power, is also Rome. … in both those prophetic delineations we have found that the power which succeeded Grecia as the fourth great power, was Rome." — DAR 153.

### The four horns — the division of Greece

- **Daniel 8:8** — "the great horn was broken; and for it came up four notable ones toward the four winds of heaven." Alexander fell in the prime of his strength; his empire shattered and consolidated into four divisions.

  > "But Daniel saw it broken in the very prime of its strength and the hight of its power, when every beholder would have exclaimed, Surely, the kingdom is established, and nothing can overthrow it." — DAR 149.

  > "the family of Alexander being then extinct, the chief commanders of the army … assumed the title of kings. They thereupon fell to leaguing and warring with one another to such a degree that within the short space of fifteen years from Alexander's death, the number was reduced to … four — just the number specified in the prophecy." — DAR 150.

- **Daniel 8:22** — "four kingdoms shall stand up out of the nation, but not in his power." The fourfold division — none equal to the original kingdom; the angel's own commentary on the four horns.

  > "Upon his death, the kingdom fell into fragments, but almost immediately consolidated into four grand divisions … These divisions did not stand in his power. None of them possessed the strength of the original kingdom." — DAR 180-181.

### The little horn — Rome, not Antiochus

- **Daniel 8:9** — "out of one of them came forth a little horn, which waxed exceeding great, toward the south, and toward the east, and toward the pleasant land." This power "exceeding great" must surpass both Persia ("great") and Grecia ("very great"); Antiochus Epiphanes — one of twenty-six Syrian kings, who paid tribute to Rome and was evacuated from Egypt at Rome's command — cannot fit. The power that exceeds them all is Rome.

  > "Antiochus, then, was simply one of the twenty-six kings who constituted the Syrian horn of the goat. He was, for the time being, that horn. Hence he could not be at the same time a separate and independent power, or another and remarkable horn, as the little horn was." — DAR 151.

  > "The little horn, in comparison with the powers that preceded it, was exceeding great. Persia is simply called great … Grecia, being more extensive still, is called very great. Now the little horn, which waxed exceeding great, must surpass them both. How absurd, then, to apply this to Antiochus, who was obliged to abandon Egypt at the dictation of the Romans, to whom he paid enormous sums of money as tribute." — DAR 152.

- **Daniel 2:40** — "the fourth kingdom shall be strong as iron... shall it break in pieces and bruise." The same field as chapters 2 and 7: the fourth world-power after Grecia is Rome — therefore the horn after Grecia is Rome.

  > "The field of vision here is substantially the same as that covered by Nebuchadnezzar's image of chapter 2, and Daniel's vision of chapter 7. And in both those prophetic delineations we have found that the power which succeeded Grecia as the fourth great power, was Rome." — DAR 153.

- **Daniel 8:9 (south/east/pleasant land)** — Rome waxed great toward the south (Egypt a province B.C. 30), the east (Syria B.C. 65), and the pleasant land (Judea B.C. 63), eventually destroying Jerusalem and scattering the Jews — point by point fulfilled in Rome.

  > "The little horn waxed great toward the south. … Egypt was made a province of the Roman empire B. C. 30 … The little horn waxed great toward the east. … Rome conquered Syria B. C. 65 … The little horn waxed great toward the pleasant land. … The Romans made it a province of their empire, B. C. 63, and eventually destroyed the city and the temple, and scattered the Jews over the face of the whole earth." — DAR 154.

- **Daniel 8:10** — "it cast down some of the host and of the stars to the ground, and stamped upon them." Rome cast down illustrious persons and Jewish rulers — the same work as the dragon of Revelation 12:4, which is pagan Rome.

  > "The great red dragon (Revelation 12:4) is said to have cast down a third part of the stars of heaven to the ground. The dragon is there interpreted to symbolize pagan Rome, and the stars it cast to the ground were Jewish rulers. Evidently it is the same power and the same work that is here brought to view, which again makes it necessary to apply this growing horn to Rome." — DAR 154.

- **Daniel 8:11** — "he magnified himself even to the prince of the host, and by him the daily was taken away, and the place of his sanctuary was cast down." Rome alone magnified itself against the Prince — crucifying our Lord under its jurisdiction — and pagan Rome was "remodeled into papal Rome," its seat removed from the city of Rome by Constantine (A.D. 330) to Constantinople.

  > "'By him' (the papal form) 'the daily' (the pagan form) 'was taken away.' Pagan Rome was remodeled into papal Rome. And the place of his sanctuary, or worship, the city of Rome, was cast down. The seat of government was removed by Constantine in A. D. 330 to Constantinople." — DAR 154-155.

- **Daniel 8:24-25** — "his power shall be mighty, but not by his own power... he shall destroy the mighty and the holy people... he shall also stand up against the Prince of princes; but he shall be broken without hand." Rome conquered "not by his own power" (through allies and division), destroyed the "holy people" (1,100,000 slain at Jerusalem), stood up against the Prince of princes at the cross, and shall be "broken without hand" — the very phrase that links its end to the stone of chapter 2.

  > "'Mighty, but not by his own power.' The success of the Romans was owing largely to the aid of their allies, and divisions among their enemies … In taking Jerusalem, they slew eleven hundred thousand Jews, and made ninety-seven thousand captives. … And Rome, finally, in the person of one of its governors, stood up against the Prince of princes, by giving sentence of death against Jesus Christ. 'But he shall be broken without hand,' an expression which identifies the destruction of this power with the smiting of the image of chapter 2." — DAR 181-182.

- **Daniel 8:25 (Prince of princes)** — "he shall also stand up against the Prince of princes" — the Prince is Jesus Christ (cf. Dan. 9:25; Acts 3:15; Rev. 1:5); since Antiochus died 164 years before Christ was born, the prophecy cannot mean him — only Rome. Romanists apply it to Antiochus to escape the prophecy; the text forbids it.

  > "The little horn was to stand up against the Prince of princes. The Prince of princes here means, beyond controversy, Jesus Christ. … But Antiochus died one hundred and sixty-four years before our Lord was born. The prophecy cannot, therefore, apply to him … Romanists take that view to avoid the application of the prophecy to themselves." — DAR 153.

### "The daily" and "the transgression of desolation" — paganism and the papacy

- **Daniel 8:12** — "an host was given him against the daily by reason of transgression; and it cast down the truth to the ground; and it practised, and prospered." The barbarian converts became instruments of Rome's religious form; "by reason of transgression" — the working of the mystery of iniquity; the papacy cast the truth to the ground, practiced its deceptions, and prospered, making war on the saints.

  > "The barbarians that subverted the Roman empire … became converts to the Catholic faith, and the instruments of the dethronement of their former religion. … And this was brought about by reason of 'transgression;' that is, by the working of the mystery of iniquity. … And it has 'prospered.' It has made war with the saints, and prevailed against them." — DAR 155.

- **Daniel 8:13** — "How long shall be the vision concerning the daily, and the transgression of desolation, to give both the sanctuary and the host to be trodden under foot?" The word rendered "daily" (Hebrew _tamid_, "continual") occurs 102 times in the Old Testament and means _continual_ — never "sacrifice," which is a supplied word. It denotes a desolating power that endures a long series of years: the "continual" desolation is paganism; the "transgression of desolation" is the papacy. The two phases of opposition fill the whole vision.

  > "The word here rendered daily occurs in the Old Testament, according to the Hebrew Concordance, one hundred and two times, and is, in the great majority of instances, rendered continual or continually. The idea of sacrifice does not attach to the word at all. … By the 'continuance of desolation,' or the perpetual desolation, we must understand that paganism, through all its long history, is meant; and by 'the transgression of desolation' is meant the papacy." — DAR 157.

  > "From a religious point of view, the world has presented only these two phases of opposition against the Lord's work in the earth. … Medo-Persia was pagan; Grecia was pagan; Rome in its first phase was pagan; these all were embraced in the 'daily.' Then comes the papal form, — the 'transgression of desolation.'" — DAR 158.

- **Daniel 8:13 (the cry "How long?")** — the very question of the martyrs through every age (cf. Rev. 6:10). Heaven records both question and answer "which is prima-facie evidence that this is a matter which it was designed that the church should understand."

  > "But another saint asked this one that spake an important question: How long the vision? and both the question and the answer are placed upon record, which is prima-facie evidence that this is a matter which it was designed that the church should understand." — DAR 156.

  > "No wonder the cry has gone up from suffering martyrs, from age to age, How long, O Lord, how long?" — DAR 158.

### The 2300 days — to the cleansing of the sanctuary (1844)

- **Daniel 8:14** — "Unto two thousand and three hundred days; then shall the sanctuary be cleansed." This is the great time-prophecy of the chapter — the "main pillar on which rested the proclamation of time" in the Advent movement. By the year-day rule, 2300 years.

  > "It is well known that the main pillar on which rested the proclamation of time, was the 2300 days, of Daniel 8. In the vision of that chapter, four things were presented to the Prophet: the ram, the he goat, the little horn, and the period of 2300 days." — TTHDS 1-2.

  > "The appointed time was given, Daniel 8:14: 'Unto 2300 days, then shall the sanctuary be cleansed.' Again, he was expressly told when the resurrection of the just would take place, by his own time being measured." — MWV3 95.

- **Daniel 8:14 (which sanctuary?)** — Not the earth (never called the sanctuary), nor Canaan, nor the church, but the heavenly "true tabernacle, which the Lord pitched, and not man" (Heb. 8:1-2; 9:23-24). The earthly sanctuary was cleansed every year; a period of 2300 years must reach the cleansing of the _heavenly_ sanctuary.

  > "Several objects have been claimed by different ones as the sanctuary here mentioned: (1) The earth; (2) The land of Canaan; (3) The church; (4) The sanctuary, the 'true tabernacle, which the Lord pitched, and not man,' which is 'in the heavens,' and of which the Jewish tabernacle was a type, pattern, or figure. Hebrews 8:1, 2; 9:23, 24." — DAR 160.

  > "the position may be safely taken that they reach to the cleansing of the heavenly sanctuary, for the earthly was to be cleansed each year … The heavenly sanctuary is the one in which the decision of all cases is to be rendered." — DAR 178.

- **Daniel 8:13-14 (closing of the vision)** — "the vision of the evening and the morning... is true" (v. 26) — the 2300 evening-mornings; the sanctuary's cleansing answers the martyrs' "How long?" and centers the whole plan of salvation. The sanctuary "occupies the central position... in the great wheel of truth," and is "a key to the interpretation of the most important prophecies."

  > "'The vision of the evening and the morning,' is that of the 2300 days. In view of the long period of oppression, and the calamities which were to come upon his people, Daniel fainted, and was sick certain days. He was astonished at the vision, but did not understand it." — DAR 182.

  > "the sanctuary is a great central object in the plan of salvation. … In the great wheel of truth, the sanctuary occupies this central position. In it, the great truths of revelation find their focal point. … It is a key to the interpretation of the most important prophecies which are having their fulfillment at the present time." — STTHD 10-12.

- **Daniel 8:14 (the terminus)** — Beginning the 2300 years with the decree to restore Jerusalem in B.C. 457 (the 7th of Artaxerxes) and adding 2300 brings the period to A.D. 1844: "the commandment did not go forth until the middle of B.C. 457... which brings us to the 10th of the 7th month, 1844." The seventy weeks (490 years) cut off from the 2300, dated from 457 B.C. and ending A.D. 34, leave 1810 years that reach from 34 to 1844.

  > "If it be asked why the twenty-three hundred days ended on the 10th of the 7th month, 1844, we answer because the commandment did not go forth until the middle of B. C. 457, therefore the first six months of that year must be added to B. C. 1844, which brings us to the 10th of the 7th month, 1844, which two numbers add together, and the result is 2300 years, full and complete." — BP3 132.

  > "The seventy weeks—490 days—having been cut off from the 2300, there were 1810 days remaining. … From A.D. 34, 1810 years extend to 1844. Consequently the 2300 days of Daniel 8:14 terminate in 1844." — GC 328.

### The angel's interpretation — Gabriel commanded to make Daniel understand

- **Daniel 8:15-16** — "Gabriel, make this man to understand the vision." The interpreter is named; the higher voice that commands him is "probably no other than the archangel Michael, or Christ." Gabriel signifies "the mighty one"; he later announced the births of John and the Messiah (Luke 1:11, 26).

  > "The commandment given was, to make this man, Daniel, understand the vision. It was addressed to Gabriel, a name that signifies 'the mighty one.' … the one who here addressed him was evidently higher in rank, and had power to command and control his actions. This was probably no other than the archangel Michael, or Christ." — DAR 178-179.

- **Daniel 8:17-19** — "Understand, O son of man: for at the time of the end shall be the vision... I will make thee know what shall be in the last end of the indignation." The "indignation" is God's controversy with His covenant people, the long period the sanctuary and host are trodden under foot (cf. Eze. 21:25-27).

  > "The indignation must be understood to cover a period of time. … 'Remove the diadem, and take off the crown.... I will overturn, overturn, overturn it: and it shall be no more, until he come whose right it is; and I will give it him.' Ezekiel 21:25-27, 31." — DAR 179-180.

  > "Here is the period of God's indignation against his covenant people; the period during which the sanctuary and host are to be trodden under foot." — DAR 180.

- **Daniel 8:26-27** — "shut thou up the vision; for it shall be for many days... I was astonished at the vision, but none understood it." Gabriel explained the symbols plainly but left one point — _the time_ — unexplained; Daniel was overwhelmed, and the further instruction is deferred.

  > "Respecting the 2300 days, introduced for the first time in verse 14, there are no data in this chapter from which to determine their commencement and close … It is necessary, therefore, for the present, to pass them by." — DAR 159.

  > "Why did not Gabriel at this time fully carry out his instructions, and cause Daniel to understand the vision? — Because Daniel had received all that he could then bear. Further instruction is therefore deferred to a future time." — DAR 182.

- **Daniel 9:21 (the continuation)** — "the man Gabriel, whom I had seen in the vision at the beginning" — the same Gabriel returns in chapter 9 to finish his commission, supplying the one omitted point, _time_, and so unlocking the 2300 days. Thus "the mystery which hangs over these days in this chapter, is dispelled in the next."

  > "They are spoken of in the midst of a prophecy which the angel Gabriel was commanded to make Daniel understand … It will accordingly be found that the mystery which hangs over these days in this chapter, is dispelled in the next." — DAR 159-160.

  > "There was only one point which the Angel had omitted to mention; and that was, Time; hence that was what troubled Daniel … But Gabriel must explain this also; for he had received his commission, 'Make this man to understand the vision;' and he must fulfill it." — TTHDS 1-2.
