# 5. Daniel 8 — The Ram, the He-Goat, the Little Horn, and the Sanctuary

> _Daniel 8 retraces the same march of empires as chapters 2 and 7, but begins one
> kingdom later — dropping Babylon (already falling) and opening with Medo-Persia. Here
> the angel Gabriel names the powers outright: the ram is Media and Persia, the he-goat
> is Grecia, its great horn the first king. The "little horn" that follows is Rome in both
> its phases, pagan and papal — never Antiochus — and "the daily" together with "the
> transgression of desolation" fills the long 2300 days that reach to the cleansing of the
> sanctuary in 1844. The angel's own interpretation fixes the meaning beyond dispute.
> (DAR 145-182; TTHDS 1-2; GC 328.)_

### The setting and the day-for-a-year key

- **Daniel 8:1-2** — In Belshazzar's third year Daniel sees himself "at Shushan in the palace... by the river of Ulai." Shushan was the metropolis of Elam, then held by Babylon, where the king had a royal palace; soon it passed to the Medes and Persians — the very empire about to be unveiled. _(DAR 146.)_
- **Numbers 14:34** — "each day for a year" — the wilderness sentence makes a day stand for a year, the rule by which the 2300 days are read as 2300 years. _(DAR 178; GC 328.)_
- **Ezekiel 4:6** — "I have appointed thee each day for a year" — the second witness establishing the year-day principle for all the time-prophecies of Daniel and Revelation. _(DAR 178.)_

### The ram — Medo-Persia (named by the angel)

- **Daniel 8:3-4** — A ram with two unequal horns, "the higher came up last," pushing westward, northward, and southward, doing "according to his will." The two horns are the two nationalities of the empire; the higher-and-later horn is Persia, which rose after Media and overshadowed it. _(DAR 146; DAR 180.)_
- **Daniel 8:20** — "The ram which thou sawest having two horns are the kings of Media and Persia." The angel names it plainly; no conjecture is permitted. As the disciples said, "now speakest thou plainly, and speakest no proverb." _(DAR 180.)_

### The he-goat — Grecia, and its notable horn (Alexander)

- **Daniel 8:5** — "an he goat came from the west... and touched not the ground: and the goat had a notable horn between his eyes." Grecia lay west of Persia; the goat that scarcely touches the ground pictures the marvelous swiftness of the Macedonian conquest — the same speed shown by the four wings of the leopard in chapter 7. _(DAR 147-148.)_
- **Daniel 7:6** — The third beast, "like a leopard, which had upon the back of it four wings of a fowl... and four heads" — the parallel symbol of Greece: swift (four wings) and four-fold after Alexander (four heads), confirming the goat. _(DAR 148.)_
- **Daniel 8:6-7** — The goat runs at the ram "in the fury of his power," breaks his two horns, casts him down, and stamps upon him — the overthrow of Persia by Alexander at the Granicus, Issus, and Arbela (B.C. 331), with none to deliver. _(DAR 148-149.)_
- **Daniel 8:21** — "the rough goat is the king of Grecia: and the great horn that is between his eyes is the first king." That first king was Alexander the Great. _(DAR 148; DAR 180.)_
- **Daniel 2:39** — "another third kingdom of brass, which shall bear rule over all the earth" — the same Grecia of the image, here the goat that swept "on the face of the whole earth." _(DAR 153.)_

### The four horns — the division of Greece

- **Daniel 8:8** — "the great horn was broken; and for it came up four notable ones toward the four winds of heaven." Alexander fell in the prime of his strength; his empire shattered and consolidated into four divisions. _(DAR 149-150.)_
- **Daniel 8:22** — "four kingdoms shall stand up out of the nation, but not in his power." The fourfold division — none equal to the original kingdom; the angel's own commentary on the four horns. _(DAR 180-181.)_

### The little horn — Rome, not Antiochus

- **Daniel 8:9** — "out of one of them came forth a little horn, which waxed exceeding great, toward the south, and toward the east, and toward the pleasant land." This power "exceeding great" must surpass both Persia ("great") and Grecia ("very great"); Antiochus Epiphanes — one of twenty-six Syrian kings, who paid tribute to Rome and was evacuated from Egypt at Rome's command — cannot fit. The power that exceeds them all is Rome. _(DAR 151-153.)_
- **Daniel 2:40** — "the fourth kingdom shall be strong as iron... shall it break in pieces and bruise." The same field as chapters 2 and 7: the fourth world-power after Grecia is Rome — therefore the horn after Grecia is Rome. _(DAR 153.)_
- **Daniel 8:9 (south/east/pleasant land)** — Rome waxed great toward the south (Egypt a province B.C. 30), the east (Syria B.C. 65), and the pleasant land (Judea B.C. 63), eventually destroying Jerusalem and scattering the Jews — point by point fulfilled in Rome. _(DAR 154.)_
- **Daniel 8:10** — "it cast down some of the host and of the stars to the ground, and stamped upon them." Rome cast down illustrious persons and Jewish rulers — the same work as the dragon of Revelation 12:4, which is pagan Rome. _(DAR 154.)_
- **Daniel 8:11** — "he magnified himself even to the prince of the host, and by him the daily was taken away, and the place of his sanctuary was cast down." Rome alone magnified itself against the Prince — crucifying our Lord under its jurisdiction — and pagan Rome was "remodeled into papal Rome," its seat removed from the city of Rome by Constantine (A.D. 330) to Constantinople. _(DAR 154-155.)_
- **Daniel 8:24-25** — "his power shall be mighty, but not by his own power... he shall destroy the mighty and the holy people... he shall also stand up against the Prince of princes; but he shall be broken without hand." Rome conquered "not by his own power" (through allies and division), destroyed the "holy people" (1,100,000 slain at Jerusalem), stood up against the Prince of princes at the cross, and shall be "broken without hand" — the very phrase that links its end to the stone of chapter 2. _(DAR 181-182.)_
- **Daniel 8:25 (Prince of princes)** — "he shall also stand up against the Prince of princes" — the Prince is Jesus Christ (cf. Dan. 9:25; Acts 3:15; Rev. 1:5); since Antiochus died 164 years before Christ was born, the prophecy cannot mean him — only Rome. Romanists apply it to Antiochus to escape the prophecy; the text forbids it. _(DAR 153.)_

### "The daily" and "the transgression of desolation" — paganism and the papacy

- **Daniel 8:12** — "an host was given him against the daily by reason of transgression; and it cast down the truth to the ground; and it practised, and prospered." The barbarian converts became instruments of Rome's religious form; "by reason of transgression" — the working of the mystery of iniquity; the papacy cast the truth to the ground, practiced its deceptions, and prospered, making war on the saints. _(DAR 155.)_
- **Daniel 8:13** — "How long shall be the vision concerning the daily, and the transgression of desolation, to give both the sanctuary and the host to be trodden under foot?" The word rendered "daily" (Hebrew _tamid_, "continual") occurs 102 times in the Old Testament and means _continual_ — never "sacrifice," which is a supplied word. It denotes a desolating power that endures a long series of years: the "continual" desolation is paganism; the "transgression of desolation" is the papacy. The two phases of opposition fill the whole vision. _(DAR 154-158.)_
- **Daniel 8:13 (the cry "How long?")** — the very question of the martyrs through every age (cf. Rev. 6:10). Heaven records both question and answer "which is prima-facie evidence that this is a matter which it was designed that the church should understand." _(DAR 156-159.)_

### The 2300 days — to the cleansing of the sanctuary (1844)

- **Daniel 8:14** — "Unto two thousand and three hundred days; then shall the sanctuary be cleansed." This is the great time-prophecy of the chapter — the "main pillar on which rested the proclamation of time" in the Advent movement. By the year-day rule, 2300 years. _(TTHDS 1-2; MWV3 95.)_
- **Daniel 8:14 (which sanctuary?)** — Not the earth (never called the sanctuary), nor Canaan, nor the church, but the heavenly "true tabernacle, which the Lord pitched, and not man" (Heb. 8:1-2; 9:23-24). The earthly sanctuary was cleansed every year; a period of 2300 years must reach the cleansing of the _heavenly_ sanctuary. _(DAR 160; DAR 178.)_
- **Daniel 8:13-14 (closing of the vision)** — "the vision of the evening and the morning... is true" (v. 26) — the 2300 evening-mornings; the sanctuary's cleansing answers the martyrs' "How long?" and centers the whole plan of salvation. The sanctuary "occupies the central position... in the great wheel of truth," and is "a key to the interpretation of the most important prophecies." _(DAR 182; STTHD 10-12.)_
- **Daniel 8:14 (the terminus)** — Beginning the 2300 years with the decree to restore Jerusalem in B.C. 457 (the 7th of Artaxerxes) and adding 2300 brings the period to A.D. 1844: "the commandment did not go forth until the middle of B.C. 457... which brings us to the 10th of the 7th month, 1844." The seventy weeks (490 years) cut off from the 2300, dated from 457 B.C. and ending A.D. 34, leave 1810 years that reach from 34 to 1844. _(BP3 132; GC 328.)_

### The angel's interpretation — Gabriel commanded to make Daniel understand

- **Daniel 8:15-16** — "Gabriel, make this man to understand the vision." The interpreter is named; the higher voice that commands him is "probably no other than the archangel Michael, or Christ." Gabriel signifies "the mighty one"; he later announced the births of John and the Messiah (Luke 1:11, 26). _(DAR 178-179.)_
- **Daniel 8:17-19** — "Understand, O son of man: for at the time of the end shall be the vision... I will make thee know what shall be in the last end of the indignation." The "indignation" is God's controversy with His covenant people, the long period the sanctuary and host are trodden under foot (cf. Eze. 21:25-27). _(DAR 179-180.)_
- **Daniel 8:26-27** — "shut thou up the vision; for it shall be for many days... I was astonished at the vision, but none understood it." Gabriel explained the symbols plainly but left one point — _the time_ — unexplained; Daniel was overwhelmed, and the further instruction is deferred. _(DAR 159-160; DAR 182.)_
- **Daniel 9:21 (the continuation)** — "the man Gabriel, whom I had seen in the vision at the beginning" — the same Gabriel returns in chapter 9 to finish his commission, supplying the one omitted point, _time_, and so unlocking the 2300 days. Thus "the mystery which hangs over these days in this chapter, is dispelled in the next." _(DAR 159-160; TTHDS 1-2.)_
