# 22. The Two Covenants — the Everlasting Gospel in Prophecy

> _The two covenants are not two periods of time but two conditions of the heart. The "old" is man's own promise — "all this we will do" — self-confidence that ends in bondage (Hagar, Sinai). The "new" is God's promise, His law written on the heart by faith (Sarah, the Jerusalem above, liberty). One everlasting covenant runs from Eden through Abraham, sealed in the blood of Christ, and this righteousness by faith is the very "everlasting gospel" the first angel proclaims (Rev. 14:6) — the faith by which the 144,000 and the remnant live. (EVCO 6, 83-84; GTI 184; GC 467-478.)_

### One Everlasting Covenant — from Eden, confirmed in Christ

- **Genesis 3:15** — "I will put enmity between thee and the woman, and between thy seed and her seed." The first preaching of the gospel: the moment man sinned and lost his inheritance, restoration was promised through the woman's Seed. The everlasting covenant begins here, not at Sinai.

  > "But God works all things after the counsel of His own will; not one of His purposes will fail; and so as soon as man had sinned and lost his inheritance, a restoration was promised through Christ, in these words: 'And I will put enmity between thee and the woman, and between thy seed and her seed; it shall bruise thy head, and thou shalt bruise his heel.' … In these words the destruction of Satan and all his work was foretold." — EVCO 38.

- **Genesis 17:7** — "I will establish my covenant... for an everlasting covenant, to be a God unto thee, and to thy seed after thee." One covenant, called everlasting, sealed with Abraham — the same one "through the blood of the everlasting covenant" by which men are perfected.

  > "It is the one everlasting covenant, which is so often spoken of in the Bible. It is 'through the blood of the everlasting covenant' that men are made perfect in every good work to do the will of God." — EVCO 83.

- **Hebrews 13:20** — "the blood of the everlasting covenant." There is but one everlasting covenant, and it stands in Christ's blood — the unbroken line that runs through the whole Bible.

  > "One line runs through the entire Bible, God's everlasting covenant. Standing at the Cross, one can see the working of God's eternal purpose, which He purposed 'in Christ before the world began.' The history from Paradise lost till Paradise restored, is set forth as in a panorama." — EVCO 6.

  > "It is the one everlasting covenant, which is so often spoken of in the Bible. It is 'through the blood of the everlasting covenant' that men are made perfect in every good work to do the will of God." — EVCO 83-84.

- **Galatians 3:8** — "the scripture... preached before the gospel unto Abraham, saying, In thee shall all nations be blessed." The covenant to Abraham was the everlasting gospel itself — gospel and covenant are one.

  > "So we see that the making of this everlasting covenant with Abraham was simply the preaching of the everlasting Gospel of the kingdom… The promise to Abraham was a Gospel promise, and nothing else, and the covenant was the everlasting covenant, of which Christ is Mediator." — EVCO 84.

  > "That the covenant and promise of God are one and the same thing, is clearly seen from Galatians 3:17, where it appears that to disannul the covenant would be to make void the promise." — GTI 132.

### The Old Covenant — the people's own promise (self-righteousness, bondage)

- **Galatians 4:22-25** — "These are the two covenants; the one from the mount Sinai, which gendereth to bondage, which is Agar." Hagar, the bondmaid, answers to "Jerusalem which now is, and is in bondage with her children." The old covenant is symbolized by the slave-woman: she can bring forth none but slaves.

  > "What are the two covenants?—The two women, Hagar and Sarah; for we read that Hagar is Mount Sinai, 'which gendereth to bondage.' That is, just as Hagar could not bring forth any other kind of children than slaves, so the law, even the law that God spoke from Sinai, can not beget freemen." — GTI 184.

  > "The difference is just the difference between a freewoman and a slave. Hagar's children, no matter how many she might have had, would have been slaves, while those of Sarah would necessarily be free." — GTI 187.

- **Exodus 19:8** — "All that the LORD hath spoken we will do." The fatal error: the people made themselves the surety. Instead of "let it be done unto us according to Thy will," they assumed the responsibility of working the works of God in their own strength.

  > "The proper response of the people therefore would have been, 'Amen, even so, O Lord, let it be done unto us according to Thy will.' On the contrary they said, 'All that the Lord hath spoken we will do;' … It was the same self-confidence that led their descendants to say to Christ, 'What shall we do, that we might work the works of God?'" — EVCO 332.

- **Romans 10:3** — they "going about to establish their own righteousness, have not submitted themselves unto the righteousness of God." Their promises "were good for nothing" — this self-confidence is the essence of the old covenant.

  > "It is only when men are ignorant of God's righteousness, that they go about to establish their own righteousness, and refuse to submit themselves to the righteousness of God. … Their promises were good for nothing, because they had not the power to fulfill them." — EVCO 332.

- **Hebrews 8:7** — "For if that first covenant had been faultless, then should no place have been sought for the second." The fault was not in God's holy law but in the people who promised in their own strength (v. 8, "finding fault with them").

  > "But it was not by virtue of the covenant that was made at Sinai, that mercy and peace were there. That covenant brought the people nothing, although everything was there for them to enjoy." — EVCO 323.

  > "Both covenants contain promises, and are founded upon them. … The 'new covenant' is better than the one made at Sinai. … It is better, because the promises upon which it is founded are better." — EVCO 326.

- **Galatians 4:24** — "which gendereth to bondage." The law spoken from Sinai, however holy, "can not beget freemen" any more than Hagar could bear a free child — the moment it is leaned on for righteousness, it brings bondage.

  > "Just as Hagar could not bring forth any other kind of children than slaves, so the law, even the law that God spoke from Sinai, can not beget freemen. It can do nothing but hold them in bondage." — GTI 184.

  > "One is the shadow of the other, the result of placing the veil of unbelief before the light of life." — EVCO 344.

### The New Covenant — God's promise, the law written on the heart (liberty)

- **Jeremiah 31:31-33** — "I will put my law in their inward parts, and write it in their hearts; and will be their God, and they shall be my people." The new covenant is not a new law but the old law in a new place — God Himself does the writing.

  > "Its scope is identical with that of the new covenant, in which God says, 'I will put my law in their inward parts, and write it in their hearts; and will be their God, and they shall be my people.'" — EVCO 84.

  > "In the beginning, man was created in the image of God. He was in perfect harmony with the nature and the law of God; the principles of righteousness were written upon his heart. But sin alienated him from his Maker. … His heart must be renewed by divine grace; he must have a new life from above." — GC 467-468.

- **Hebrews 8:8-10** — "I will make a new covenant... I will put my laws into their mind, and write them in their hearts." Both covenants are made "with the house of Israel" and founded upon promises; the new is better because its promises are God's, not man's.

  > "Both covenants are made with 'the house of Israel'… Both covenants contain promises, and are founded upon them. … The 'new covenant' is better than the one made at Sinai. … It is better, because the promises upon which it is founded are better." — EVCO 326.

- **Hebrews 8:6** — Christ is "the mediator of a better covenant, which was established upon better promises." The superiority lies wholly in the Promiser: God cannot fail.

  > "That covenant brought the people nothing, although everything was there for them to enjoy." — EVCO 323.

  > "The 'new covenant' is better than the one made at Sinai. … It is better, because the promises upon which it is founded are better." — EVCO 326.

- **Hebrews 8:12-13** — "I will be merciful to their unrighteousness... their sins... will I remember no more." Forgiveness, not human resolve, is the foundation of the new covenant.

  > "Both covenants contain promises, and are founded upon them. … It is better, because the promises upon which it is founded are better." — EVCO 326.

  > "We may go to Jesus and be cleansed, and stand before the law without shame and remorse. 'There is therefore now no condemnation to them which are in Christ Jesus, who walk not after the flesh, but after the Spirit.'" — GC 477.

- **Hebrews 10:16-17** — "I will put my laws into their hearts... And their sins and iniquities will I remember no more." The same promise restated: heart-obedience plus full pardon.

  > "In the new birth the heart is brought into harmony with God, as it is brought into accord with His law. … The old life of alienation from God has ended; the new life of reconciliation, of faith and love, has begun." — GC 467-468.

- **Ezekiel 36:26-27** — "A new heart also will I give you... And I will put my spirit within you, and cause you to walk in my statutes." The mechanism of the new covenant: God supplies the heart that keeps His law.

  > "But Christ is the only Light of the world, the Light of life; so that the true and living law is found only in Him. It is His life, because it is in His heart, and out of the heart are the issues of life." — EVCO 344.

  > "It is the work of conversion and sanctification to reconcile men to God by bringing them into accord with the principles of His law. … His heart must be renewed by divine grace; he must have a new life from above. This change is the new birth." — GC 467.

- **Galatians 4:26-28** — "Jerusalem which is above is free, which is the mother of us all... we, brethren, as Isaac was, are the children of promise." Sarah, the freewoman, is the new covenant; her children are free, born "by promise."

  > "What are the two covenants?—The two women, Hagar and Sarah… just as Hagar could not bring forth any other kind of children than slaves, so the law, even the law that God spoke from Sinai, can not beget freemen." — GTI 184.

  > "The difference is just the difference between a freewoman and a slave. Hagar's children, no matter how many she might have had, would have been slaves, while those of Sarah would necessarily be free." — GTI 187.

- **2 Corinthians 3:6** — "the letter killeth, but the spirit giveth life." Ministers of "the new testament" deal not in the letter (the old covenant's dead works) but in the life-giving Spirit.

  > "His heart must be renewed by divine grace; he must have a new life from above. … In the new birth the heart is brought into harmony with God, as it is brought into accord with His law." — GC 467-468.

### The Two Covenants Contrasted

|              | Old Covenant (Hagar / Sinai)               | New Covenant (Sarah / Jerusalem above)  |
| ------------ | ------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------- |
| **Promiser** | the people — "all this we will do"         | God — "I will write it in their hearts" |
| **Result**   | bondage, self-righteousness, death         | liberty, righteousness by faith, life   |
| **Child**    | born "after the flesh" — a slave           | born "by promise" — free                |
| **Law**      | leaned on for righteousness → "the letter" | written within by the Spirit → life     |

_(Gal. 4:22-31; EVCO 323-326, 332, 344; GTI 184-187.)_

### Justified by Faith, Not by the Law — Abraham the Pattern

- **Genesis 15:6** — "And he believed in the LORD; and he counted it to him for righteousness." Abraham was justified by faith before circumcision, before Sinai — the pattern for every believer.

  > "The promise to Abraham was a Gospel promise, and nothing else, and the covenant was the everlasting covenant, of which Christ is Mediator." — EVCO 84.

  > "Faith means the reception of the life of Christ into the heart, together with God's righteousness… God has promised to every man who believes him nothing less than the freedom of the world." — WOR 77.

- **Romans 4:3-5** — "to him that worketh not, but believeth on him that justifieth the ungodly, his faith is counted for righteousness." Righteousness is reckoned, not earned — the heart of the new covenant.

  > "The man who thinks that he himself can get righteousness out of the law is in reality trying to substitute his own righteousness for God's righteousness. … 'Where no law is, there is no transgression;' but there is law everywhere, and therefore transgression. All have sinned, so that the inheritance can not be by the law." — WOR 77.

  > "It is not faith that claims the favor of Heaven without complying with the conditions upon which mercy is to be granted… for genuine faith has its foundation in the promises and provisions of the Scriptures." — GC 472.

- **Romans 4:13-16** — "the promise... was not to Abraham, or to his seed, through the law, but through the righteousness of faith... Therefore it is of faith, that it might be by grace; to the end the promise might be sure to all the seed." If the inheritance were by law, faith is void; it is by grace so the promise cannot fail.

  > "'Therefore it is of faith, that it might be by grace; to the end the promise might be sure to all the seed; not to that only which is of the law, but to that also which is of the faith of Abraham, who is the father of us all.'" — WOR 77-78.

  > "The promise to Abraham was a Gospel promise, and nothing else, and the covenant was the everlasting covenant, of which Christ is Mediator. Its scope is identical with that of the new covenant." — EVCO 84.

- **Galatians 3:6-9** — "they which be of faith are blessed with faithful Abraham." Those of faith — Jew or Gentile — are the true children of Abraham and heirs of the same gospel-promise.

  > "God promises us everything that we need, and more than we can ask or think, as a gift. We give Him ourselves, that is, nothing, and He gives us Himself, that is, everything." — GTI 132.

  > "So we see that the making of this everlasting covenant with Abraham was simply the preaching of the everlasting Gospel of the kingdom, and the assuring to him of a part in its blessings." — EVCO 84.

### The Covenant Is in the Seed — Christ, and a Testament in His Blood

- **Galatians 3:16** — "to Abraham and his seed were the promises made... not... seeds, as of many; but as of one... which is Christ." The whole covenant centers in one Seed; all the promises are Yea and Amen in Him.

  > "God's covenants with men can be nothing else than promises to them… God promises us everything that we need, and more than we can ask or think, as a gift." — GTI 132.

  > "The promise to Abraham was a Gospel promise, and nothing else, and the covenant was the everlasting covenant, of which Christ is Mediator." — EVCO 84.

- **Galatians 3:17-18** — "the covenant, that was confirmed before of God in Christ, the law, which was four hundred and thirty years after, cannot disannul." The law given at Sinai could not annul or replace the earlier covenant of promise — proof the everlasting covenant antedates and outranks Sinai.

  > "That the covenant and promise of God are one and the same thing, is clearly seen from Galatians 3:17, where it appears that to disannul the covenant would be to make void the promise." — GTI 132.

  > "It is the one everlasting covenant, which is so often spoken of in the Bible. … The promise to Abraham was a Gospel promise, and nothing else, and the covenant was the everlasting covenant, of which Christ is Mediator." — EVCO 83-84.

- **Genesis 22:18** — "in thy seed shall all the nations of the earth be blessed." The promise reaches every nation through the Seed — the same scope as the everlasting gospel of Revelation 14:6.

  > "The making of this everlasting covenant with Abraham was simply the preaching of the everlasting Gospel of the kingdom… Its scope is identical with that of the new covenant." — EVCO 84.

- **Hebrews 9:15-17** — "he is the mediator of the new testament, that by means of death... they which are called might receive the promise of eternal inheritance... For where a testament is, there must also... be the death of the testator." The new covenant becomes "of force" only by Christ's death — it is sealed in His blood.

  > "It is the one everlasting covenant, which is so often spoken of in the Bible. It is 'through the blood of the everlasting covenant' that men are made perfect in every good work to do the will of God." — EVCO 83-84.

- **Hebrews 9:11-14** — Christ "by his own blood... entered in once into the holy place, having obtained eternal redemption." The blood of the everlasting covenant ministered in the heavenly sanctuary — the gospel foundation under the whole prophetic chain.

  > "It is 'through the blood of the everlasting covenant' that men are made perfect in every good work to do the will of God. … The promise to Abraham was a Gospel promise, and nothing else, and the covenant was the everlasting covenant, of which Christ is Mediator." — EVCO 83-84.

### The Law Established by Faith, Not Made Void

- **Galatians 3:10-13** — "Christ hath redeemed us from the curse of the law, being made a curse for us." The curse falls only on those under the old covenant's "works of the law"; Christ bears it so the blessing of Abraham reaches the Gentiles.

  > "The law worketh wrath: for by the law is the knowledge of sin. … Their making it was simply a promise to make themselves righteous by their own works, and man in himself is 'without strength.'" — GTI 184.

  > "The law reveals to man his sins, but it provides no remedy. While it promises life to the obedient, it declares that death is the portion of the transgressor. The gospel of Christ alone can free him from the condemnation or the defilement of sin." — GC 467-468.

- **Galatians 3:19, 24** — the law "was added because of transgressions, till the seed should come"; it is "our schoolmaster to bring us unto Christ, that we might be justified by faith." The law drives the sinner to the promise — it never was a rival way of salvation.

  > "But Christ is the only Light of the world, the Light of life; so that the true and living law is found only in Him. … Of this, the law on tables was but the shadow, albeit an exact and perfect shadow. It tells us exactly what we shall find in Christ." — EVCO 344.

  > "The first step in reconciliation to God is the conviction of sin. … In order to see his guilt, the sinner must test his character by God's great standard of righteousness. It is a mirror which shows the perfection of a righteous character." — GC 467.

- **Galatians 3:21** — "Is the law then against the promises of God? God forbid." Law and gospel never conflict; the law cannot give life, so life must come by the promise.

  > "There are two laws just as there are two covenants, occupying the same relation to each other that the two covenants do to each other. One is the shadow of the other, the result of placing the veil of unbelief before the light of life." — EVCO 344.

- **Romans 3:31** — "Do we then make void the law through faith? God forbid: yea, we establish the law." Faith does not abolish the law but writes it in the heart — the new covenant keeps the very commandments of God.

  > "Says Paul: 'Do we then make void the law through faith? God forbid: yea, we establish the law.' … In the new birth the heart is brought into harmony with God, as it is brought into accord with His law." — GC 468.

- **Galatians 3:26-29** — "ye are all the children of God by faith... if ye be Christ's, then are ye Abraham's seed, and heirs according to the promise." All barriers fall in Christ; faith makes us heirs of the everlasting covenant.

  > "The two covenants are not matters of time, but of condition. … The difference is just the difference between a freewoman and a slave… while those of Sarah would necessarily be free." — GTI 187.

  > "The promise to Abraham was a Gospel promise, and nothing else, and the covenant was the everlasting covenant, of which Christ is Mediator." — EVCO 84.

### The Everlasting Gospel — the Faith of the Remnant

- **Revelation 14:6** — "another angel... having the everlasting gospel to preach unto them that dwell on the earth." The first angel proclaims the same everlasting gospel preached to Abraham — righteousness by faith in the everlasting covenant — to every nation before the end.

  > "So we see that the making of this everlasting covenant with Abraham was simply the preaching of the everlasting Gospel of the kingdom… Its scope is identical with that of the new covenant." — EVCO 84.

  > "The law reveals to man his sins, but it provides no remedy. … The gospel of Christ alone can free him from the condemnation or the defilement of sin. … It is only as the law of God is restored to its rightful position that there can be a revival of primitive faith and godliness among His professed people." — GC 467-478.

- **Revelation 14:12** — "Here are they that keep the commandments of God, and the faith of Jesus." The remnant unites the law written in the heart (new covenant) with the faith of Jesus — the living proof that faith establishes the law.

  > "Says Paul: 'Do we then make void the law through faith? God forbid: yea, we establish the law.' … When this mighty change has taken place in the sinner, he has passed from death unto life, from sin unto holiness, from transgression and rebellion to obedience and loyalty." — GC 468.

  > "We may go to Jesus and be cleansed, and stand before the law without shame and remorse. 'There is therefore now no condemnation to them which are in Christ Jesus, who walk not after the flesh, but after the Spirit.'" — GC 477.

  > "But Christ is the only Light of the world, the Light of life; so that the true and living law is found only in Him. It is His life, because it is in His heart." — EVCO 344.

- **Hebrews 8:10 with Revelation 22:14** — the law written within (new covenant) produces those who "do his commandments, that they may have right to the tree of life." The covenant promise reaches its consummation in the restored Eden inheritance promised to Abraham.

  > "It is the one everlasting covenant, which is so often spoken of in the Bible. … Mark well that Abraham himself, as well as his seed, was promised the land for an everlasting possession." — EVCO 83-84.

  > "Here are revealed the heights of attainment that we may reach through faith in the promises of our heavenly Father, when we fulfill His requirements. Through the merits of Christ we have access to the throne of Infinite Power." — GC 477.

- **Galatians 4:30-31** — "Cast out the bondwoman and her son... we are not children of the bondwoman, but of the free." The final separation: those who live by their own promise (Babylon's bondage) are cast out; the free children of the promise stand. This is the experience of righteousness by faith that prepares the remnant for translation.

  > "So then the two covenants existed in every essential particular in the days of Abraham. Even so they do to-day; for the Scripture says now as well as then, 'Cast out the bondwoman and her son.' We see then that the two covenants are not matters of time, but of condition." — GTI 187.

  > "It is not faith that claims the favor of Heaven without complying with the conditions upon which mercy is to be granted, it is presumption… Such are the fruits of Bible conversion and sanctification… It is only as the law of God is restored to its rightful position that there can be a revival of primitive faith and godliness among His professed people." — GC 472-478.
