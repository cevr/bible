# 3. Daniel 2 — The Great Image: Kingdoms Throughout Time

> _In the second year of his reign, the heathen monarch of Babylon dreamed a dream that God had Daniel read back to him and interpret: a great metal man whose head of gold, breast of silver, belly of brass, legs of iron, and feet of iron-and-clay outline the whole course of world empire from Babylon to the end. God Himself names the rule of each metal — a metal is a kingdom — and the prophecy closes when a stone "cut out without hands" smites the image on its feet, grinds the kingdoms to chaff, and becomes a mountain that fills the whole earth. That stone is the everlasting kingdom of Christ. This is not a lesson about Nebuchadnezzar's pride; it is the master-outline of history, from gold to glory. (DAR 42-77; PK 547-548; GC 341.)_

### God's own rule of interpretation — a metal is a kingdom

- **Daniel 2:28** — "There is a God in heaven that revealeth secrets, and maketh known to the king Nebuchadnezzar what shall be in the latter days." The dream is no riddle of arrogance but a divine outline of "what shall be" — prophecy, given to the captive's God, not the king's wise men. _(DAR 42-43.)_
- **Daniel 2:31-33** — A great image: head of fine gold, breast and arms of silver, belly and thighs of brass, legs of iron, feet part iron and part clay. Descending metals of descending worth picture the descending course of "earthly pomp and glory." _(DAR 41-42.)_
- **Daniel 2:38** — "Thou art this head of gold." God then says "after thee shall arise another kingdom" — proving each metal is not a man but a successive kingdom; the king is put for his kingdom. _(DAR 52; GEP 15.)_
- **Daniel 2:39-40** — "After thee shall arise another kingdom inferior to thee, and another third kingdom of brass... and the fourth kingdom shall be strong as iron." Four metals, four kingdoms, named by God in order — the interpretation is not left to man. _(DAR 56; MWV2 43.)_
- **Daniel 7:17** — Scripture's own key: "These great beasts, which are four, are four kings" — i.e., kingdoms (cf. Dan. 7:23). The parallel vision proves the symbols of Daniel 2 are kingdoms, not abstractions. _(DAR 62.)_
- **Daniel 7:23** — "The fourth beast shall be the fourth kingdom upon earth." The four beasts of Daniel 7 run exactly parallel to the four metals of Daniel 2, fixing the count and order beyond dispute. _(DAR 62.)_

### The named kingdoms, in order, with metal and date

| Metal                        | Kingdom                     | Dates               | Scripture        |
| ---------------------------- | --------------------------- | ------------------- | ---------------- |
| Head of **gold**             | Babylon                     | 606–538 B.C.        | Dan. 2:38        |
| Breast/arms of **silver**    | Medo-Persia                 | 538–331 B.C.        | Dan. 2:39a; 8:20 |
| Belly/thighs of **brass**    | Greece                      | 331–168 B.C.        | Dan. 2:39b; 8:21 |
| Legs of **iron**             | Imperial Rome               | 168 B.C.–A.D. 476   | Dan. 2:40        |
| Feet/toes of **iron + clay** | Divided Rome (ten kingdoms) | A.D. 351–476 onward | Dan. 2:41-43     |
| **Stone** cut without hands  | Kingdom of God              | At the end          | Dan. 2:44-45     |

- **Daniel 2:38** — **Babylon, the head of gold.** "Thou art this head of gold" — the golden kingdom of a golden age, founded by Nimrod, that took God's people captive and so entered the field of prophecy. _(DAR 43, 46.)_
- **Daniel 2:39a** — **Medo-Persia, the silver breast and arms.** "Another kingdom inferior to thee" — inferior not in power or extent but in wealth and magnificence. The two arms answer to the two-fold Medo-Persian power. _(DAR 52-53.)_
- **Daniel 5:28** — "Thy kingdom is divided, and given to the Medes and Persians." The handwriting on Belshazzar's wall fixes the silver kingdom by name the very night Babylon fell. _(DAR 50.)_
- **Daniel 5:30-31** — "In that night was Belshazzar the king of the Chaldeans slain. And Darius the Median took the kingdom" — the gold passes to the silver, B.C. 538, exactly as the image foretold. _(DAR 50-51.)_
- **Daniel 8:20** — "The ram which thou sawest having two horns are the kings of Media and Persia" — Scripture itself names the second kingdom, confirming the silver. _(DAR 52; GEP 34.)_
- **Daniel 2:39b** — **Greece, the brass.** "A third kingdom of brass, which shall bear rule over all the earth" — fulfilled when Alexander, one to twenty against Persia at Arbela (B.C. 331), became absolute lord of the East. _(DAR 54.)_
- **Daniel 8:21** — "The rough goat is the king of Grecia: and the great horn... is the first king" — God names the brass kingdom and its first king, Alexander. _(DAR 54; GEP 34.)_
- **Daniel 8:22** — "Four kingdoms shall stand up out of the nation, but not in his power" — Alexander's empire divided among his four generals, still the brass kingdom, not yet the iron. _(DAR 56.)_
- **Daniel 2:40** — **Imperial Rome, the iron.** "The fourth kingdom shall be strong as iron... it shall break in pieces and bruise." Rome's iron monarchy successively broke the images of gold, silver, and brass and "filled the world." _(DAR 56-57.)_
- **Luke 2:1** — "There went out a decree from Caesar Augustus, that all the world should be taxed." The iron kingdom is no guess: it is the Rome of the Gospels, ruling the world at Christ's birth. _(DAR 57.)_

### The ten toes — divided Rome that never reunites (the iron-and-clay church-state lesson)

- **Daniel 2:41** — "The kingdom shall be divided; but there shall be in it of the strength of the iron." The iron does not vanish — Rome breaks into pieces that still carry Rome's iron in them. _(DAR 57.)_
- **Daniel 2:42** — "As the toes of the feet were part of iron, and part of clay, so the kingdom shall be partly strong, and partly broken." The ten toes are the ten kingdoms into which Western Rome divided, A.D. 351–476 — the same as the ten horns of Daniel 7. _(DAR 57-58, 62-63.)_
- **Daniel 2:43** — "They shall mingle themselves with the seed of men: but they shall not cleave one to another, even as iron is not mixed with clay." Where war failed, the kings tried marriage and diplomacy to reunite Europe into one empire — and failed. From Canute to Napoleon, every attempt has broken on this word: _they shall not cleave_. _(DAR 67-68.)_
- **Daniel 7:17, 23** — The fourth beast and its ten horns confirm the toes: ten contemporaneous kingdoms, never again consolidated under one universal monarch. No fifth world-empire of man will ever arise. _(DAR 62, 66.)_

### The stone cut without hands — the kingdom of God set up in the days of these kings

- **Daniel 2:34-35** — "A stone was cut out without hands, which smote the image upon his feet... and brake them to pieces... and the stone... became a great mountain, and filled the whole earth." The smiting is violent and immediate; the kingdoms become chaff blown away, "no place... found for them." This is no peaceful spread of the gospel at the first advent — it is the sudden end of human empire. _(DAR 61, 70.)_
- **Daniel 2:44** — "In the days of these kings shall the God of heaven set up a kingdom, which shall never be destroyed... it shall break in pieces and consume all these kingdoms, and it shall stand for ever." The fifth kingdom is set up not at Christ's first advent but after Rome's tenfold division — "in the days of these kings," contemporaneous with the toes still standing. _(DAR 68-70.)_
- **Daniel 2:45** — "The stone was cut out of the mountain without hands... the great God hath made known to the king what shall come to pass... and the dream is certain, and the interpretation thereof sure." "Without hands" — no human agency; the Lord does it by His own divine power. The chain of prophecy is sealed as certain. _(DAR 70.)_
- **Revelation 11:15** — "The kingdoms of this world are become the kingdoms of our Lord, and of his Christ; and he shall reign for ever and ever." Revelation echoes Daniel 2:44 — the stone-kingdom is Christ's everlasting reign that succeeds, not coexists with, the empires of men. _(DAR 68; GC 341.)_
- **Isaiah 28:16** — "Behold, I lay in Zion for a foundation a stone, a tried stone, a precious corner stone, a sure foundation." The stone of Daniel 2 is the same Stone of prophecy — Christ Himself, the sure foundation of His kingdom.
- **Psalm 118:22** — "The stone which the builders refused is become the head stone of the corner." The rejected Stone becomes the Head — Christ, despised at His first coming, supreme at His second.
- **Luke 20:17-18** — "The stone which the builders rejected... Whosoever shall fall upon that stone shall be broken; but on whomsoever it shall fall, it will grind him to powder." Christ applies the Stone to Himself; on the kingdoms of earth it falls and grinds them to chaff, exactly as Daniel 2:35 declares.

### The certainty and the lesson

- **Daniel 2:29-30** — "He that revealeth secrets maketh known to thee what shall come to pass... this secret is not revealed to me for any wisdom that I have." The outline is God's, not Daniel's — therefore "the dream is certain, and the interpretation thereof sure" (v. 45). _(DAR 42.)_
- **Daniel 2:36-37** — "We will tell the interpretation... Thou, O king, art a king of kings: for the God of heaven hath given thee a kingdom." God who gives kingdoms also takes them away; the rise and fall of these empires is His doing, not chance. _(PK 547-548.)_
- **Summary of the chain** — From the head of gold to the stone, four universal empires only — Babylon, Medo-Persia, Greece, Rome — then division, then the kingdom of God; no fifth world-empire of man, and no reunion of the toes. The whole of history laid open in eight verses, "from a given period to the end of this world." _(MWV2 43; LJL 30-31; GEP 15, 34-35; PK 548.)_
