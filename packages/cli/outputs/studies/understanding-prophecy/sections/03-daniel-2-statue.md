# 3. Daniel 2 — The Great Image: Kingdoms Throughout Time

> _In the second year of his reign, the heathen monarch of Babylon dreamed a dream that God had Daniel read back to him and interpret: a great metal man whose head of gold, breast of silver, belly of brass, legs of iron, and feet of iron-and-clay outline the whole course of world empire from Babylon to the end. God Himself names the rule of each metal — a metal is a kingdom — and the prophecy closes when a stone "cut out without hands" smites the image on its feet, grinds the kingdoms to chaff, and becomes a mountain that fills the whole earth. That stone is the everlasting kingdom of Christ. This is not a lesson about Nebuchadnezzar's pride; it is the master-outline of history, from gold to glory. (DAR 42-77; PK 547-548; GC 341.)_

### God's own rule of interpretation — a metal is a kingdom

- **Daniel 2:28** — "There is a God in heaven that revealeth secrets, and maketh known to the king Nebuchadnezzar what shall be in the latter days." The dream is no riddle of arrogance but a divine outline of "what shall be" — prophecy, given to the captive's God, not the king's wise men.

  > "Eight short verses of the inspired record tell the whole story; yet that story embraces the history of this world's pomp and power.... it gives us all the great outlines of earthly kingdoms from that time to this.... The finger of God is here. Let us heed the lesson well." — DAR 42-43.

- **Daniel 2:31-33** — A great image: head of fine gold, breast and arms of silver, belly and thighs of brass, legs of iron, feet part iron and part clay. Descending metals of descending worth picture the descending course of "earthly pomp and glory."

  > "God would show Nebuchadnezzar the utter emptiness and worthlessness of earthly pomp and glory. And how could this be more impressively done than by an image commencing with the most precious of metals, and continually descending to the baser, till we finally have the coarsest and crudest of materials, — iron mingled with the miry clay…?" — DAR 41-42.

- **Daniel 2:38** — "Thou art this head of gold." God then says "after thee shall arise another kingdom" — proving each metal is not a man but a successive kingdom; the king is put for his kingdom.

  > "The use of the word kingdom here, shows that kingdoms, and not particular kings, are represented by the different parts of this image; and hence when it was said to Nebuchadnezzar, 'Thou art this head of gold,' although the personal pronoun was used, the kingdom, not the person of the king, was meant." — DAR 52.

  > "the Lord had said to him that the head of gold of the great image represented the kingdom of Nebuchadnezzar himself; and that after him should arise another kingdom inferior to his, and a third kingdom inferior to this, and yet another, a fourth kingdom…" — GEP 15.

- **Daniel 2:39-40** — "After thee shall arise another kingdom inferior to thee, and another third kingdom of brass... and the fourth kingdom shall be strong as iron." Four metals, four kingdoms, named by God in order — the interpretation is not left to man.

  > "That Babylon, Medo-Persia, and Grecia are represented respectively by the head of gold, the breast and arms of silver, and the sides of brass, is acknowledged by all.... What kingdom did succeed Grecia in the empire of the world?... One kingdom did this, and one only, and that was Rome." — DAR 56.

  > "'The head of Gold' represented the Chaldean kingdom; 'the breast and arms of silver' represented the Medes and Persians; 'the belly and thighs of brass, which were to bear rule over all the earth,' the Grecian.... 'the legs of iron, and the feet part of iron and part of clay,' fitly represent the Roman kingdom." — MWV2 43.

- **Daniel 7:17** — Scripture's own key: "These great beasts, which are four, are four kings" — i.e., kingdoms (cf. Dan. 7:23). The parallel vision proves the symbols of Daniel 2 are kingdoms, not abstractions.

  > "The image of chapter 2 is exactly parallel with the vision of the four beasts of chapter 7. The fourth beast of chapter 7 represents the same as the iron legs of the image.... and they are just as much independent kingdoms as are the beasts themselves; for the beasts are spoken of in precisely the same manner; namely, as 'four kings which should arise.'" — DAR 62.

- **Daniel 7:23** — "The fourth beast shall be the fourth kingdom upon earth." The four beasts of Daniel 7 run exactly parallel to the four metals of Daniel 2, fixing the count and order beyond dispute.

  > "in Daniel's interpretation of the image he uses the words king and kingdom interchangeably.... This shows that at the time the kingdom of God is set up, there will be a plurality of kings existing contemporaneously." — DAR 62.

### The named kingdoms, in order, with metal and date

| Metal                        | Kingdom                     | Dates               | Scripture        |
| ---------------------------- | --------------------------- | ------------------- | ---------------- |
| Head of **gold**             | Babylon                     | 606–538 B.C.        | Dan. 2:38        |
| Breast/arms of **silver**    | Medo-Persia                 | 538–331 B.C.        | Dan. 2:39a; 8:20 |
| Belly/thighs of **brass**    | Greece                      | 331–168 B.C.        | Dan. 2:39b; 8:21 |
| Legs of **iron**             | Imperial Rome               | 168 B.C.–A.D. 476   | Dan. 2:40        |
| Feet/toes of **iron + clay** | Divided Rome (ten kingdoms) | A.D. 351–476 onward | Dan. 2:41-43     |
| **Stone** cut without hands  | Kingdom of God              | At the end          | Dan. 2:44-45     |

- **Daniel 2:38** — **Babylon, the head of gold.** "Thou art this head of gold" — the golden kingdom of a golden age, founded by Nimrod, that took God's people captive and so entered the field of prophecy.

  > "The kingdom of Babylon, which finally developed into the golden head of this great historic image, was founded by Nimrod, the great-grandson of Noah, over two thousand years before Christ." — DAR 43.

  > "The character of this empire is indicated by the nature of the material composing that portion of the image by which it was symbolized — the head of gold. It was the golden kingdom of a golden age." — DAR 46.

- **Daniel 2:39a** — **Medo-Persia, the silver breast and arms.** "Another kingdom inferior to thee" — inferior not in power or extent but in wealth and magnificence. The two arms answer to the two-fold Medo-Persian power.

  > "It was to be inferior to the preceding kingdom. In what respect inferior? Not in power; for it was its conqueror. Not in extent.... But it was inferior in wealth, luxury, and magnificence." — DAR 52-53.

- **Daniel 5:28** — "Thy kingdom is divided, and given to the Medes and Persians." The handwriting on Belshazzar's wall fixes the silver kingdom by name the very night Babylon fell.

  > "Belshazzar soon became aware of the cause of the disturbance, and died vainly fighting for his imperiled life.... 'In that night was Belshazzar the king of the Chaldeans slain. And Darius the Median took the kingdom.'" — DAR 50.

- **Daniel 5:30-31** — "In that night was Belshazzar the king of the Chaldeans slain. And Darius the Median took the kingdom" — the gold passes to the silver, B.C. 538, exactly as the image foretold.

  > "'In that night was Belshazzar the king of the Chaldeans slain. And Darius the Median took the kingdom, being about threescore and two years old.'" — DAR 50.

  > "Thus the first division of the great image was completed. Another kingdom had arisen, as the prophet had declared. The first instalment of the prophetic dream was fulfilled." — DAR 51.

- **Daniel 8:20** — "The ram which thou sawest having two horns are the kings of Media and Persia" — Scripture itself names the second kingdom, confirming the silver.

  > "The succeeding kingdom, Medo-Persia, is the one which answers to the breast and arms of silver of the great image." — DAR 52.

  > "'The ram which thou sawest having two horns are the kings of Media and Persia. And the rough goat is the king of Grecia: and the great horn that is between his eyes is the first king.'" — GEP 34.

- **Daniel 2:39b** — **Greece, the brass.** "A third kingdom of brass, which shall bear rule over all the earth" — fulfilled when Alexander, one to twenty against Persia at Arbela (B.C. 331), became absolute lord of the East.

  > "the deciding point was reached on the field of Arbela, B. C. 331, in which the Grecians, though only one to twenty in number as compared with the Persians, were entirely victorious; and Alexander thenceforth became absolute lord of the Persian empire to the utmost extent that it was ever possessed by any of its own kings." — DAR 54.

- **Daniel 8:21** — "The rough goat is the king of Grecia: and the great horn... is the first king" — God names the brass kingdom and its first king, Alexander.

  > "'And another third kingdom of brass shall bear rule over all the earth,' said the prophet.... Grecia now comes into the field of vision, to be, for a time, the all-absorbing object of attention, as the third of what are called the great universal empires of the earth." — DAR 54.

  > "'And the rough goat is the king of Grecia: and the great horn that is between his eyes is the first king.'" — GEP 34.

- **Daniel 8:22** — "Four kingdoms shall stand up out of the nation, but not in his power" — Alexander's empire divided among his four generals, still the brass kingdom, not yet the iron.

  > "Alexander's successors (his four leading generals) did not conquer his empire, and erect another in its place; they simply divided among themselves the empire which Alexander had conquered, and left ready to their hand." — DAR 56.

- **Daniel 2:40** — **Imperial Rome, the iron.** "The fourth kingdom shall be strong as iron... it shall break in pieces and bruise." Rome's iron monarchy successively broke the images of gold, silver, and brass and "filled the world."

  > "It conquered Grecia; it subdued all things; like iron, it broke in pieces and bruised." — DAR 56.

  > "'the images of gold, or silver, or brass, that might serve to represent the nations or their kings, were successively broken by the iron monarchy of Rome.'" — DAR 56-57.

- **Luke 2:1** — "There went out a decree from Caesar Augustus, that all the world should be taxed." The iron kingdom is no guess: it is the Rome of the Gospels, ruling the world at Christ's birth.

  > "At the opening of the Christian era, this empire took in the whole south of Europe, France, England, the greater part of the Netherlands, Switzerland, and the south of Germany, Hungary, Turkey, and Greece, not to speak of its possessions in Asia and Africa." — DAR 57.

### The ten toes — divided Rome that never reunites (the iron-and-clay church-state lesson)

- **Daniel 2:41** — "The kingdom shall be divided; but there shall be in it of the strength of the iron." The iron does not vanish — Rome breaks into pieces that still carry Rome's iron in them.

  > "Rome, before its division into ten kingdoms, lost that iron tenacity which it possessed to a superlative degree during the first centuries of its career. Luxury… began to corrode and weaken its iron sinews, and thus prepared the way for its subsequent disruption into ten kingdoms." — DAR 57.

- **Daniel 2:42** — "As the toes of the feet were part of iron, and part of clay, so the kingdom shall be partly strong, and partly broken." The ten toes are the ten kingdoms into which Western Rome divided, A.D. 351–476 — the same as the ten horns of Daniel 7.

  > "the kingdom represented by that portion of the image to which the toes belonged, was finally divided into ten parts.... To take the ten toes to represent the ten kingdoms into which Rome was divided seems like such an easy, consistent, and matter-of-course procedure." — DAR 57-58.

  > "This division was accomplished between the years A. D. 351 and A. D. 483.... all historians agree in this, that out of the territory of Western Rome, ten separate kingdoms were ultimately established." — DAR 62-63.

- **Daniel 2:43** — "They shall mingle themselves with the seed of men: but they shall not cleave one to another, even as iron is not mixed with clay." Where war failed, the kings tried marriage and diplomacy to reunite Europe into one empire — and failed. From Canute to Napoleon, every attempt has broken on this word: _they shall not cleave_.

  > "'They shall not cleave one to another, even as iron is not mixed with clay.' And the history of Europe is but a running commentary on the exact fulfillment of these words. From the time of Canute to the present age, it has been the policy of reigning monarchs.... And did he [Napoleon] succeed? — Nay.... The iron would not mingle with clay. The ten kingdoms continue still." — DAR 67-68.

- **Daniel 7:17, 23** — The fourth beast and its ten horns confirm the toes: ten contemporaneous kingdoms, never again consolidated under one universal monarch. No fifth world-empire of man will ever arise.

  > "The ten horns of the beast, of course, correspond very naturally to the ten toes of the image.... The ten horns, beyond controversy, represent the ten kingdoms into which Rome was divided." — DAR 62.

  > "With Rome fell the last of the universal empires belonging to this world in its present state.... When Rome fell, such possibilities forever passed away. Crushed beneath the weight of its own vast proportions, it crumbled to pieces, never to be united again." — DAR 66.

### The stone cut without hands — the kingdom of God set up in the days of these kings

- **Daniel 2:34-35** — "A stone was cut out without hands, which smote the image upon his feet... and brake them to pieces... and the stone... became a great mountain, and filled the whole earth." The smiting is violent and immediate; the kingdoms become chaff blown away, "no place... found for them." This is no peaceful spread of the gospel at the first advent — it is the sudden end of human empire.

  > "When the stone smites the image upon its feet, the image is dashed in pieces. Violence is used. The effect is immediate. The image becomes as chaff.... It is blown off, removed away, as incompatible and unavailable material; and no place is found for it." — DAR 61.

  > "The feet and toes. But these were not developed until four centuries and a half after the crucifixion of Christ. The image was, at the time of the crucifixion, only developed to the thighs… if the kingdom of God was there set up… it smote it upon the thighs, not upon the feet, where the prophecy places the smiting." — DAR 70.

- **Daniel 2:44** — "In the days of these kings shall the God of heaven set up a kingdom, which shall never be destroyed... it shall break in pieces and consume all these kingdoms, and it shall stand for ever." The fifth kingdom is set up not at Christ's first advent but after Rome's tenfold division — "in the days of these kings," contemporaneous with the toes still standing.

  > "The kings, or kingdoms, in the days of which the God of heaven is to set up his kingdom, are evidently those kingdoms which arose out of the Roman empire. Then the kingdom of God here brought to view could not have been set up, as some claim it was, in connection with the first advent of Christ." — DAR 68-70.

- **Daniel 2:45** — "The stone was cut out of the mountain without hands... the great God hath made known to the king what shall come to pass... and the dream is certain, and the interpretation thereof sure." "Without hands" — no human agency; the Lord does it by His own divine power. The chain of prophecy is sealed as certain.

  > "This shows that the smiting is not done by an agent acting for another, not by the church… but it is a work which the Lord does by his own divine power, without any human agency." — DAR 70.

- **Revelation 11:15** — "The kingdoms of this world are become the kingdoms of our Lord, and of his Christ; and he shall reign for ever and ever." Revelation echoes Daniel 2:44 — the stone-kingdom is Christ's everlasting reign that succeeds, not coexists with, the empires of men.

  > "We here reach the climax of this stupendous prophecy.... The kingdom of God! Grand provision for a new and glorious dispensation, in which his people shall find a happy terminus of this world's sad, degenerate, and changing career." — DAR 68.

  > "The study of the Revelation directs the mind to the prophecies of Daniel, and both present most important instruction, given of God to men, concerning events to take place at the close of this world's history." — GC 341.

- **Isaiah 28:16** — "Behold, I lay in Zion for a foundation a stone, a tried stone, a precious corner stone, a sure foundation." The stone of Daniel 2 is the same Stone of prophecy — Christ Himself, the sure foundation of His kingdom.
- **Psalm 118:22** — "The stone which the builders refused is become the head stone of the corner." The rejected Stone becomes the Head — Christ, despised at His first coming, supreme at His second.
- **Luke 20:17-18** — "The stone which the builders rejected... Whosoever shall fall upon that stone shall be broken; but on whomsoever it shall fall, it will grind him to powder." Christ applies the Stone to Himself; on the kingdoms of earth it falls and grinds them to chaff, exactly as Daniel 2:35 declares.

### The certainty and the lesson

- **Daniel 2:29-30** — "He that revealeth secrets maketh known to thee what shall come to pass... this secret is not revealed to me for any wisdom that I have." The outline is God's, not Daniel's — therefore "the dream is certain, and the interpretation thereof sure" (v. 45).

  > "Human wisdom never devised so brief a record which embraced so much. Human language never set forth in so few words, so great a volume of historical truth. The finger of God is here. Let us heed the lesson well." — DAR 42.

- **Daniel 2:36-37** — "We will tell the interpretation... Thou, O king, art a king of kings: for the God of heaven hath given thee a kingdom." God who gives kingdoms also takes them away; the rise and fall of these empires is His doing, not chance.

  > "Honored by men with the responsibilities of state and with the secrets of kingdoms bearing universal sway, Daniel was honored by God as His ambassador, and was given many revelations of the mysteries of ages to come." — PK 547-548.

- **Summary of the chain** — From the head of gold to the stone, four universal empires only — Babylon, Medo-Persia, Greece, Rome — then division, then the kingdom of God; no fifth world-empire of man, and no reunion of the toes. The whole of history laid open in eight verses, "from a given period to the end of this world."

  > "'The head of Gold' represented the Chaldean kingdom; 'the breast and arms of silver' represented the Medes and Persians; 'the belly and thighs of brass'… the Grecian.... 'the legs of iron, and the feet part of iron and part of clay,' fitly represent the Roman kingdom, which still exists, although in a broken state." — MWV2 43.

  > "the prophecies of Daniel were given with the plain design… of setting forth a connected chain of events from a given period to the end of this world, and the setting up of Christ's eternal kingdom of glory." — LJL 30-31.

  > "making, in all, four universal empires from the time of Nebuchadnezzar to the setting up of the kingdom of God in the earth." — GEP 15.

  > "As the lion corresponds to the head of gold of the great image, and so represents Babylon, so the bear of this vision corresponds to the 'other kingdom inferior' to Babylon, represented in the breast and arms of silver." — GEP 34-35.

  > "So perished the Medo-Persian kingdom, and the kingdoms of Grecia and Rome. And so perishes all that has not God for its foundation. Only that which is bound up with His purpose, and expresses His character, can endure." — PK 548.
