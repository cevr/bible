## The Mingling — How the Peculiar People Lost Their Peculiarity

> The peculiar people were not destroyed by persecution but dissolved by alliance: the sons of God saw, chose by sight, and took wives of the daughters of men — and when the two classes became one flesh, the distinction God had put between them was gone, "all flesh had corrupted his way," and the faithful stream shrank to eight souls. The mingling is the standing mechanism of apostasy: the world never rises to the church; the church always descends to the world.

**The two lines before the union — pilgrims and the world-worshiping class — Gen. 2:24; 4:19; Deut. 14:1-2:**

- _Gen. 2:24._ "Therefore shall a man leave his father and his mother, and shall cleave unto his wife: and they shall be one flesh" — Eden's marriage institution: one man, one wife, one flesh. This is the standard against which the ruin of this section is measured; the attack falls not on a human custom but on a gift from before sin.
- _Gen. 4:19._ "And Lamech took unto him two wives: the name of the one was Adah, and the name of the other Zillah" — polygamy begins in Cain's line, the fifth from the fugitive who "went out from the presence of the LORD" (_Gen. 4:16_; see "Cain and Abel — the Two Worships"). Before the mingling, the corruption of marriage is confined to the serpent's seed; the mingling is how it crosses the line.
- _Deut. 14:1-2._ "Ye are the children of the LORD your God... For thou art an holy people unto the LORD thy God, and the LORD hath chosen thee to be a peculiar people unto himself, above all the nations that are upon the earth" — Scripture's own idiom: "children of the LORD" names the professed covenant people, and their calling is PECULIARITY — set apart above the nations. This is the very thing the mingling dissolves.
- _PP 81.1._ White: "thus standing at the head of that great class of men who worship the god of this world.... To the crime of murder, in which Cain had led the way, Lamech, the fifth in descent, added polygamy.... the descendants of Seth... counting themselves 'strangers and pilgrims on the earth'" — the two lines drawn before the union: Cain's, the world-worshiping class with polygamy already in it; Seth's, the pilgrim people.
- _GSAM 40.1._ Loughborough: "Afterward Seth was counted in the line of descent from Adam.... Cain, the real first-born, is not counted in the pedigree.... after the birth of Seth, men began to call themselves by the name of the Lord" — the pioneer witness to the historical fact of two genealogical streams: Seth counted in the covenant pedigree, Cain excluded, the Sethites bearing the Lord's name (see "The Two Lineages — the City and the Name").

**Scripture defines its own phrase — who are the sons of God? — Gen. 6:1-2; Luke 3:38; Matt. 22:30:**

- _Gen. 6:1-2._ "And it came to pass, when men began to multiply on the face of the earth, and daughters were born unto them, That the sons of God saw the daughters of men that they were fair; and they took them wives of all which they chose" — the text of the ruin. Two classes, one act: the sons of God SAW, judged by the sight of the eyes ("fair"), and TOOK "wives of all which they chose" — choice unbounded by God's counsel, and in "all which they chose" the plural liberty of Lamech now practiced in the faithful line.
  - **the sons of God** = the covenant line of Seth, the professed people of God. Scripture defines the phrase in this very genealogy: Adam is "the son of God" (_Luke 3:38_), and the covenant people are "the children of the LORD your God," a "peculiar people" (_Deut. 14:1-2_) — read literally, per Miller's Rule, not angels, "for in the resurrection they neither marry, nor are given in marriage, but are as the angels of God in heaven" (_Matt. 22:30_) (_Gen. 6:2_; _Luke 3:38_; _Deut. 14:1_; _Matt. 22:30_).
  - **the daughters of men** = the Cainite line, the world — the daughters of the "men" who "began to multiply on the face of the earth" (_Gen. 6:1_), the class descended from the man who "went out from the presence of the LORD" (_Gen. 4:16_), into which polygamy had already entered (_Gen. 4:19_) (_Gen. 6:1-2_; _Gen. 4:16_; _Gen. 4:19_).
- _Luke 3:38._ "Which was the son of Enos, which was the son of Seth, which was the son of Adam, which was the son of God" — Scripture's own definition, planted in the antediluvian genealogy itself: "son of God" is covenant language for Adam and his faithful line. The Bible defines its own figures; no angel-mythology is needed or allowed.
- _Matt. 22:30._ "For in the resurrection they neither marry, nor are given in marriage, but are as the angels of God in heaven" — Christ's own testimony closes the door on the angel reading of _Gen. 6:2_: angels do not marry. The widespread "sons of God = angels" interpretation is excluded on Scripture's own terms — the sons of God are men of the covenant line, and the tragedy of Genesis 6 is a CHURCH tragedy, not a monster story.
- _CTr 39.2._ White: "The worshipers of God called themselves the sons of God," and "there were two parties in the world then, and there always will be" — the most direct inspired confirmation of the Sethite reading: the phrase of _Gen. 6:2_ is the self-designation of the worshipers of God, and the two-party division it records is universalized — "there always will be." The Genesis 6 division is the division of this whole study (see "The Enmity — Two Seeds from One Fall").

**The mechanism of ruin — saw, chose, took — Gen. 6:2; PP 81.2; AH 121.3:**

- _Gen. 6:2._ "the sons of God saw the daughters of men that they were fair; and they took them wives of all which they chose" — mark the verbs: SAW (attraction), CHOSE (alliance), TOOK (descent). Not one word of persecution, compulsion, or heresy — the peculiar people were not conquered; they married.
  - **the mingling** = the alliance of the two classes by which the peculiar people lose their peculiarity — the standing mechanism of apostasy. The sons of God chose by sight and took "wives of all which they chose" (_Gen. 6:2_), and the end was that "all flesh had corrupted his way" (_Gen. 6:12_); the abiding law is one-directional — the yoke of light with darkness is forbidden (_2 Cor. 6:14_), "evil communications corrupt good manners" (_1 Cor. 15:33_), "the friendship of the world is enmity with God" (_Jas. 4:4_) — the church descends, the world never rises (_Gen. 6:1-5_; _Gen. 6:12_; _2 Cor. 6:14_; _1 Cor. 15:33_; _Jas. 4:4_).
- _PP 81.2._ White: "For some time the two classes remained separate.... the latter, in order to escape from their contaminating influence, withdrew to the mountains, and there made their home.... But in the lapse of time they ventured, little by little, to mingle with the inhabitants of the valleys.... The children of Seth, attracted by the beauty of the daughters of Cain's descendants, displeased the Lord by intermarrying with them.... they lost their peculiar, holy character" — the load-bearing anchor, and the section's title in her own words: the separation held, the descent came "little by little," the attraction was by beauty, the act was intermarriage, and the loss was exactly "their peculiar, holy character." The same paragraph converts two inferences into cited fact: "the restrictions of the seventh commandment were disregarded, 'and they took them wives of all which they chose'" — Lamech's plural liberty (_Gen. 4:19_) now practiced in the faithful line — and the mingled Sethites went "in the way of Cain" (_Jude 11_; see "Cain and Abel — the Two Worships").
- _CTr 39.2._ White: "The descendants of Seth went up into the mountains and there made themselves homes separate from the sons of Cain.... But after the exhortations and the influence of Enoch were removed from them, they commenced to unite with the descendants of Cain" — the timing: the separation held only while Enoch's voice held; when the living witness was removed, the union began. Distinction is not self-sustaining; it must be kept.
- _AH 121.3._ White: "There is in itself no sin in eating and drinking, or in marrying and giving in marriage. It was lawful to marry in the time of Noah, and it is lawful to marry now.... But in the days of Noah men married without consulting God or seeking His guidance and counsel" — the sin located precisely: not marriage, which is Eden's own institution (_Gen. 2:24_), but marriage by the sight of the eyes without God — "wives of all which they chose." The lawful thing, taken out of God's hand, became the instrument of ruin.

**The result — the distinction dissolves, corruption is total — Gen. 6:5, 11-12; 1 Cor. 15:33:**

- _Gen. 6:5._ "And GOD saw that the wickedness of man was great in the earth, and that every imagination of the thoughts of his heart was only evil continually" — the sons of God "saw" and took; now GOD saw. Once the distinction dissolved, corruption ran without remainder — "every imagination... only evil continually" — thought itself apostate.
- _Gen. 6:11-12._ "The earth also was corrupt before God, and the earth was filled with violence. And God looked upon the earth, and, behold, it was corrupt; for all flesh had corrupted his way upon the earth" — "ALL flesh": the two classes have become one class. The union did not lift the Cainites; it erased the Sethites.
- _1 Cor. 15:33._ "Be not deceived: evil communications corrupt good manners" — the apostle states the one-directional law the antediluvian record demonstrates: corrupt association corrupts good character, never the reverse. "Be not deceived" — the deception is precisely the hope that the mingling will run uphill.
- _AH 460.1._ White: "It was by associating with idolaters and joining in their festivities that the Hebrews were led to transgress God's law.... So now it is by leading the followers of Christ to associate with the ungodly and unite in their amusements that Satan is most successful in alluring them into sin.... God requires of His people now as great a distinction from the world, in customs, habits, and principles, as He required of Israel anciently" — the law confirmed across every age: association allures the faithful downward, and the required distinction is undiminished now.

**The remnant of the unmingled — Gen. 6:8-9; 1 Pet. 3:20:**

- _Gen. 6:8-9._ "But Noah found grace in the eyes of the LORD.... Noah was a just man and perfect in his generations, and Noah walked with God" — against "all flesh," one "but." Noah is "perfect in his generations" — his family line unmingled, the distinction kept — and he "walked with God" as Enoch had. When the class dissolved, the enmity survived in one house.
- _1 Pet. 3:20._ "when once the longsuffering of God waited in the days of Noah, while the ark was a preparing, wherein few, that is, eight souls were saved by water" — the arithmetic of the mingling: the covenant line, once a class filling the mountains, is "few, that is, eight souls." When the church marries the world, it is the church that is lost — the world remains, eight souls come out of it.

**The lesson codified into law — no yoke with darkness — Deut. 7:3-4; 2 Cor. 6:14-17; Jas. 4:4:**

- _Deut. 7:3-4._ "Neither shalt thou make marriages with them; thy daughter thou shalt not give unto his son, nor his daughter shalt thou take unto thy son. For they will turn away thy son from following me... and destroy thee suddenly" — God writes the antediluvian lesson into covenant statute, and gives the reason: "for they will turn away thy son." The reason IS the mechanism of Genesis 6 — Infinite Wisdom legislating against the mingling because its direction never varies (the statute Israel broke; see "Israel Mingles — 'Like All the Nations'").
- _2 Cor. 6:14._ "Be ye not unequally yoked together with unbelievers: for what fellowship hath righteousness with unrighteousness? and what communion hath light with darkness?" — the New Testament form of the same law. The five questions of vv. 14-16 all expect the answer NONE: no fellowship, no communion, no concord, no part, no agreement — the two classes admit no yoke.
- _2 Cor. 6:16-17._ "ye are the temple of the living God... Wherefore come out from among them, and be ye separate, saith the Lord, and touch not the unclean thing; and I will receive you" — the remedy for the mingling is separation, and God's reception is conditioned on it. The "come out" that closes this study is already sounding here (see "Come Out, My People — the Pilgrim Call").
- _Jas. 4:4._ "Ye adulterers and adulteresses, know ye not that the friendship of the world is enmity with God? whosoever therefore will be a friend of the world is the enemy of God" — the Genesis 6 sin transposed to the church and named ADULTERY: friendship with the world is the marriage of the sons of God to the daughters of men in its spiritual form. There is no third class — to befriend the world is to change seeds.
- _CCh 120.3._ White: "The Lord commanded ancient Israel not to intermarry with the idolatrous nations around them: 'Neither shalt thou make marriages with them.'... Infinite Wisdom, foreseeing the result of such unions, declares: 'For they will turn away thy son from following Me.'... In the New Testament are similar prohibitions concerning the marriage of Christians with the ungodly" — Deuteronomy 7, the peculiar-people calling, and the unequal-yoke command bound into one continuous law flowing out of the antediluvian ruin.
- _8MR 108.1._ White: "All combinations with unbelievers that bind as a yoke must be broken. These words apply not only to making a marriage covenant with an unbeliever, but to the making of all unions where the worldly element can have a prevailing influence over believers" — the principle widened beyond marriage: EVERY alliance in which the worldly element can prevail is the Genesis 6 mechanism in another dress.
- _Ev 617.4._ White: "The Lord's injunction, 'Be ye not unequally yoked together with unbelievers,' refers not only to the marriage of Christians with the ungodly, but to all alliances in which the parties are brought into intimate association, and in which there is need of harmony in spirit and action" — the same confirmation: wherever the two classes are brought into intimate association, the unequal-yoke command stands guard.

**The type stamped on the last days — Matt. 24:37-39:**

- _Matt. 24:37-39._ "But as the days of Noe were, so shall also the coming of the Son of man be. For as in the days that were before the flood they were eating and drinking, marrying and giving in marriage, until the day that Noe entered into the ark, And knew not until the flood came, and took them all away" — Christ Himself seals this section as prophecy: "marrying and giving in marriage" — the very engine of the antediluvian apostasy — is named as the sign-condition of the generation that meets Him. The mingling is not ancient history; it is the last-day mechanism, walked in full at "As in the Days of Noah — the Last Mingling."

**DEFINITION — THE MINGLING — HOW THE PECULIAR PEOPLE LOST THEIR PECULIARITY =** the alliance of the two classes by which the covenant people ceased to be a distinct people, and the standing mechanism of apostasy in every age. The two lines stood defined — Cain's, the world-worshiping class with polygamy already in it (_Gen. 4:19_; _PP 81.1_), Seth's, the "children of the LORD," a "peculiar people" (_Deut. 14:1-2_) counted in the covenant pedigree (_GSAM 40.1_) — until "the sons of God saw the daughters of men that they were fair; and they took them wives of all which they chose" (_Gen. 6:2_). The sons of God are the Sethite covenant line, defined by Scripture's own genealogy — Adam "the son of God" (_Luke 3:38_) — confirmed by the testimony ("the worshipers of God called themselves the sons of God," _CTr 39.2_), and not angels, "for in the resurrection they neither marry, nor are given in marriage" (_Matt. 22:30_). The mechanism of ruin was not persecution but marriage: attraction, alliance, descent — the lawful institution of Eden (_Gen. 2:24_) taken up by sight "without consulting God" (_AH 121.3_), "little by little" after the two classes had "for some time remained separate," until "the restrictions of the seventh commandment were disregarded" and "they lost their peculiar, holy character" (_PP 81.2_; _CTr 39.2_). The result ran one direction only: "every imagination of the thoughts of his heart was only evil continually" (_Gen. 6:5_), "all flesh had corrupted his way" (_Gen. 6:12_) — the world did not rise, the church descended, for "evil communications corrupt good manners" (_1 Cor. 15:33_) — and the faithful stream shrank to one man "perfect in his generations" (_Gen. 6:8-9_) and "eight souls" (_1 Pet. 3:20_). God codified the lesson into law — "Neither shalt thou make marriages with them... for they will turn away thy son" (_Deut. 7:3-4_), "Be ye not unequally yoked... come out from among them, and be ye separate" (_2 Cor. 6:14-17_), extended to "all unions where the worldly element can have a prevailing influence" (_8MR 108.1_; _Ev 617.4_; _CCh 120.3_) — and named its church-form adultery: "the friendship of the world is enmity with God" (_Jas. 4:4_). And Christ stamped it on the end: "as the days of Noe were... marrying and giving in marriage, until the day that Noe entered into the ark" (_Matt. 24:37-39_). Between the translation of Enoch and the Flood, the peculiar people unmade themselves by alliance; the same mechanism runs to the coming of the Son of man, for "there always will be" two parties (_CTr 39.2_). When the church marries the world, the church is what is lost.

**Symbols defined here:**

- **the sons of God** = the covenant line of Seth, the professed people of God — Scripture's own idiom for Adam's faithful line ("the son of God," _Luke 3:38_) and the covenant people ("the children of the LORD your God," _Deut. 14:1_); read literally, not angels, for angels marry not (_Gen. 6:2_; _Luke 3:38_; _Deut. 14:1-2_; _Matt. 22:30_).
- **the daughters of men** = the Cainite line, the world — the daughters of the multiplying "men" of _Gen. 6:1_, the class of the man who went out from the presence of the LORD, already practicing polygamy (_Gen. 6:1-2_; _Gen. 4:16_; _Gen. 4:19_).
- **the mingling** = the alliance of the two classes by which the peculiar people lose their peculiarity — the standing mechanism of apostasy: choice by sight, union with the world, one-directional descent, ending in "all flesh had corrupted his way"; forbidden as the unequal yoke, named as the friendship of the world (_Gen. 6:1-5_; _Gen. 6:12_; _2 Cor. 6:14_; _1 Cor. 15:33_; _Jas. 4:4_).

**Symbols carried:** the two seeds and the enmity — the God-put antagonism this section shows being surrendered from the church's side (see "The Enmity — Two Seeds from One Fall"); the way of Cain and the departure from the presence of the LORD (see "Cain and Abel — the Two Worships"); the two lineages, the Sethites' mountain separation, calling on the name of the LORD, and Lamech's polygamy (see "The Two Lineages — the City and the Name"); the attack on Eden's institutions that the mingling opens is traced in full at "The Days of Noah — the Institutions of Eden Under Attack"; the last-day form of the mingling at "As in the Days of Noah — the Last Mingling"; the final visible distinction the mingling would erase at "The Seal or the Mark — the Distinction Made Visible"; the remedy at "Come Out, My People — the Pilgrim Call."

**For discussion:**

1. The sons of God "saw... that they were fair; and they took them wives of all which they chose" (_Gen. 6:2_) — and Ellen White locates the sin not in marriage but in marrying "without consulting God or seeking His guidance and counsel" (_AH 121.3_). Where in your own life is a LAWFUL thing being chosen by sight rather than by counsel — and what would consulting God first actually look like there?
2. The mingling was "little by little" (_PP 81.2_) and its law is one-directional: "evil communications corrupt good manners" (_1 Cor. 15:33_) — all flesh was corrupted, and the world never rose. We often justify close alliance with the world as missionary influence. By what Scripture test can you tell whether an association is lifting them or leveling you — and what "combination that binds as a yoke" (_8MR 108.1_; _2 Cor. 6:14_) might need to be broken?
3. When the mingling was complete, "all flesh had corrupted his way" and the faithful were "few, that is, eight souls" (_Gen. 6:12_; _1 Pet. 3:20_) — yet Noah stood "perfect in his generations" and "walked with God" (_Gen. 6:9_). Since Christ says the last generation repeats Noah's (_Matt. 24:37-39_), what does it take to be "perfect in his generations" — unmingled — in an age when the two classes look almost identical, and how is that separation an act of faith rather than of pride (_2 Cor. 6:17_)?
