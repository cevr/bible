## The Enmity — Two Seeds from One Fall

> At the gate of Eden God HIMSELF declares war — "I will put enmity" — and by one sentence divides mankind into exactly two seeds, the woman's and the serpent's. The enmity is grace, not curse: fallen man is naturally at peace with Satan, and the whole controversy from Abel's altar to the remnant is the history of that God-put antagonism — and of what happens when it is lost.

**The charter — God puts the enmity — Gen. 3:14-15:**

- _Gen. 3:14._ "And the LORD God said unto the serpent, Because thou hast done this, thou art cursed above all cattle, and above every beast of the field" — the verdict falls on the serpent first; the curse is his. What follows in v. 15 is spoken TO the serpent but given FOR the man: not more curse, but the first promise.
- _Gen. 3:15._ "And I will put enmity between thee and the woman, and between thy seed and her seed; it shall bruise thy head, and thou shalt bruise his heel" — the charter of the whole study. Mark the "I": God is the author of the enmity, not fallen man; and by one sentence He divides the race into exactly two seeds — the woman's and the serpent's — locked in a war that ends with a bruised heel and a crushed head.
  - **the enmity** = the God-PUT antagonism between the two seeds — grace, not curse. "I will put enmity" (_Gen. 3:15_): it is not native to fallen man, whose evil nature is at peace with the originator of sin; friendship with the world IS enmity with God (_Jas. 4:4_), and the world's hatred of the saints is this same enmity running outward, because Christ chose them out of the world (_John 15:19_). Defined in full at the close.
- _GC 505.2._ White: "God declares: 'I will put enmity.' This enmity is not naturally entertained. When man transgressed the divine law, his nature became evil, and he was in harmony, and not at variance, with Satan... Had not God specially interposed, Satan and man would have entered into an alliance against Heaven; and instead of cherishing enmity against Satan, the whole human family would have been united in opposition to God" — the exact grace-thesis, opening with the very words of _Gen. 3:15_: the fallen natural state is PEACE with the serpent; the enmity is God's special interposition, and without it the race is one seed only — Satan's.
- _GC 506.2._ White: "It is the grace that Christ implants in the soul which creates in man enmity against Satan. Without this converting grace and renewing power, man would continue the captive of Satan... But the new principle in the soul creates conflict where hitherto had been peace" — the enmity named outright as implanted grace: "conflict where hitherto had been peace." The antagonism is a gift, and the gift is Christ's; to feel no war with Satan is the mark of still belonging to him.

**The seed of the woman — Christ first, and His people in Him — Gal. 3:16, 29; Rom. 16:20; Matt. 13:37:**

- _Gal. 3:16._ "He saith not, And to seeds, as of many; but as of one, And to thy seed, which is Christ" — Scripture defines its own figure: the promised Seed is SINGULAR, and it is Christ. The seed of the woman is Christ first, before it is anyone else.
  - **the seed of the woman** = Christ first, and in Him His people. The singular "seed" is Christ (_Gal. 3:16_); those who are Christ's are then reckoned that same seed (_Gal. 3:29_); the Seed's head-bruising is shared with the church (_Rom. 16:20_); Christ Himself sows the class as "the good seed... the children of the kingdom" (_Matt. 13:37-38_); and the line runs to "the remnant of her seed" at the end (_Rev. 12:17_). Defined in full at the close.
- _Gal. 3:29._ "And if ye be Christ's, then are ye Abraham's seed, and heirs according to the promise" — in Him, His people become the seed. The class is defined by belonging to Christ, not by bloodline; membership is by union, not descent.
- _Rom. 16:20._ "And the God of peace shall bruise Satan under your feet shortly" — Gen. 3:15's head-bruising applied to the church: "under YOUR feet." The woman's seed includes the saints in Christ, who share the Seed's victory over the serpent.
- _Matt. 13:37._ "He that soweth the good seed is the Son of man" — the woman's seed is planted by the Son of man Himself; Christ both IS the Seed and SOWS the seed-class.

**The seed of the serpent — paternity by practice, not blood — John 8:44; Matt. 13:38-39; 1 John 3:12:**

- _John 8:44._ "Ye are of your father the devil, and the lusts of your father ye will do. He was a murderer from the beginning... When he speaketh a lie, he speaketh of his own: for he is a liar, and the father of it" — Christ defines the serpent's seed as SPIRITUAL paternity: religious men, sons of Abraham after the flesh, are "of your father the devil" because they DO his lusts. Fatherhood here is moral, not biological.
  - **the seed of the serpent** = not a bloodline but a spiritual paternity — those who belong to the devil by doing his works: "ye are of your father the devil" for "the lusts of your father ye will do" (_John 8:44_); the tares are "the children of the wicked one," sown by the devil (_Matt. 13:38-39_); Cain, a natural son of Eve, is "of that wicked one" (_1 John 3:12_); the standing test is practice (_1 John 3:8, 10_). Defined in full at the close.
- _Matt. 13:38-39._ "The field is the world; the good seed are the children of the kingdom; but the tares are the children of the wicked one; The enemy that sowed them is the devil; the harvest is the end of the world" — the two seeds are the two paternities: children of the kingdom and children of the wicked one, and no third planting. The classes grow together in one field until the harvest at the end of the world.
- _1 John 3:12._ "Not as Cain, who was of that wicked one, and slew his brother. And wherefore slew he him? Because his own works were evil, and his brother's righteous" — the proof-case: Cain, a physical son of Adam and Eve, is "of that wicked one." The first murderer belonged to the devil while sharing Abel's parents — the serpent's seed is by spirit, never by descent; and the murder itself is the enmity of the serpent's seed against the woman's.
- _COL 152.1._ White: "The Pharisee and the publican represent two great classes into which those who come to worship God are divided. Their first two representatives are found in the first two children that were born into the world" — the two classes traced to Cain and Abel at the altar: Cain "came to God with a thank offering only," but Abel "came with the blood that pointed to the Lamb of God" — the original heads of the serpent's line and the woman's.
- _PP 72.5._ White: "Cain and Abel represent two classes that will exist in the world till the close of time. One class avail themselves of the appointed sacrifice for sin; the other venture to depend upon their own merits" — exactly two classes, lasting "till the close of time," and the dividing question is the appointed sacrifice — the blood received or refused.
- _PP 74.2._ White: "Cain hated and killed his brother, not for any wrong that Abel had done, but 'because his own works were evil, and his brother's righteous.' 1 John 3:12. So in all ages the wicked have hated those who were better than themselves" — Ellen White cites John's verdict herself and generalizes it to "all ages": the serpent's seed hating the woman's is the standing pattern of history.

**The test of the two classes — doing, not descent — 1 John 3:8-10:**

- _1 John 3:8._ "He that committeth sin is of the devil; for the devil sinneth from the beginning. For this purpose the Son of God was manifested, that he might destroy the works of the devil" — the defining criterion of the serpent's seed: practice. And in the same breath the head-bruising of Gen. 3:15 restated: the Son of God manifested "that he might destroy the works of the devil" is the Seed come to crush the head.
- _1 John 3:9._ "Whosoever is born of God doth not commit sin; for his seed remaineth in him: and he cannot sin, because he is born of God" — the mirror truth for the woman's line: the divine seed "remaineth in him." The righteous class also stands by paternity — born of God, the seed abiding within — not by pedigree.
- _1 John 3:10._ "In this the children of God are manifest, and the children of the devil: whosoever doeth not righteousness is not of God, neither he that loveth not his brother" — the two classes named explicitly — "children of God" and "children of the devil" — and told apart by doing righteousness and loving the brethren. Scripture knows no third class.

**No neutral ground — to lose the enmity is to change sides — Jas. 4:4; John 15:19:**

- _Jas. 4:4._ "know ye not that the friendship of the world is enmity with God? whosoever therefore will be a friend of the world is the enemy of God" — there is no neutral ground. The enmity points one way or the other: to lose the God-put enmity toward the serpent's world is not to become neutral but to relocate the enmity — against God.
- _John 15:19._ "If ye were of the world, the world would love his own: but because ye are not of the world, but I have chosen you out of the world, therefore the world hateth you" — Christ traces the enmity forward: the world hates His people BECAUSE He has chosen them out of it. The hatred the woman's seed endures is the same God-put antagonism running outward — the hatred is the evidence of the grace.
- _SR 62.1._ White: "The descendants of Seth were called the sons of God; the descendants of Cain, the sons of men. As the sons of God mingled with the sons of men, they became corrupt and, by intermarriage with them, lost... their peculiar, holy character" — the historical anchor of the danger: MINGLING the two classes cost the woman's line its distinction. (The "sons of God" of Gen. 6:2 are here read as the covenant line of Seth, not angels: Adam was "the son of God" (_Luke 3:38_), and the covenant people are "the children of the LORD your God" (_Deut. 14:1_) — sonship by covenant standing, the reading the two-class frame requires; the mingling is traced in full in "The Mingling — How the Peculiar People Lost Their Peculiarity.")
- _PP 81.2._ White: "For some time the two classes remained separate... But in the lapse of time they ventured, little by little, to mingle with the inhabitants of the valleys. This association was productive of the worst results... they lost their peculiar, holy character" — the arc in detail: the Sethites withdrew to the mountains and were preserved; they mingled with the Cainite valleys "little by little" and were ruined. Separation preserved the enmity; incremental mingling dissolved it — the recurring ruin the enmity was put to prevent.

**The terminus — the dragon and the remnant of her seed — Rev. 12:9, 17:**

- _Rev. 12:9._ "And the great dragon was cast out, that old serpent, called the Devil, and Satan, which deceiveth the whole world" — Scripture's own identification, closing the chain: the dragon of Revelation 12 IS "that old serpent" of Eden. The war of Gen. 3:15 and the war of Rev. 12:17 are one war, with one enemy.
- _Rev. 12:17._ "And the dragon was wroth with the woman, and went to make war with the remnant of her seed, which keep the commandments of God, and have the testimony of Jesus Christ" — the end-time terminus of Gen. 3:15: the dragon (that old serpent — _Rev. 12:9_) wars on "the remnant of her seed" — the LAST of the woman's line, marked by commandment-keeping and the testimony of Jesus. The two-seed war opened at Eden runs unbroken to the close.
- _MWV2 213.1._ Miller: "it is evident by the expression 'remnant of her seed,' that it means the last of the church 'who keep the commandments of God, and have the testimony of Jesus Christ'" — the pioneer definition: the remnant of her seed = the commandment-keeping last of the church, the final generation of the woman's line under the serpent's final wrath.

**DEFINITION — THE ENMITY — TWO SEEDS FROM ONE FALL =** at the fall God Himself interposed an antagonism — "I will put enmity" (_Gen. 3:15_) — dividing mankind into exactly two seeds and only two. The seed of the woman is Christ first, the singular Seed of promise (_Gal. 3:16_), and in Him all who are His (_Gal. 3:29_), sown by the Son of man as "the children of the kingdom" (_Matt. 13:37-38_), sharing His bruising of the serpent's head — "under YOUR feet" (_Rom. 16:20_) — down to "the remnant of her seed" at the end (_Rev. 12:17_). The seed of the serpent is no bloodline but a paternity of practice: "ye are of your father the devil" because "the lusts of your father ye will do" (_John 8:44_); the tares are "the children of the wicked one," sown by the devil (_Matt. 13:38-39_); Cain, Eve's own son, was "of that wicked one" (_1 John 3:12_); "he that committeth sin is of the devil" (_1 John 3:8_), while the born of God carry the divine seed remaining in them (_1 John 3:9_) — "the children of God are manifest, and the children of the devil" (_1 John 3:10_), and Scripture admits no third class. The enmity between them is GRACE, not curse: fallen man's nature was "in harmony, and not at variance, with Satan," and without God's special interposition "the whole human family would have been united in opposition to God" (_GC 505.2_); it is "the grace that Christ implants in the soul" that "creates conflict where hitherto had been peace" (_GC 506.2_). Therefore no neutral ground exists — "the friendship of the world is enmity with God" (_Jas. 4:4_) — and the world's hatred of the saints is the same enmity running outward, for Christ has "chosen you out of the world" (_John 15:19_). History proves both edges: Cain and Abel head "two classes that will exist in the world till the close of time," divided over the appointed sacrifice (_PP 72.5_; _COL 152.1_; _PP 74.2_), and the antediluvian mingling of the Sethite and Cainite lines — "little by little" — dissolved the distinction and lost the holy character of the woman's seed (_PP 81.2_; _SR 62.1_). The standing danger this study traces is the losing of the enmity; the standing hope is that the war God declared at Eden He finishes — the dragon, "that old serpent" (_Rev. 12:9_), spends his last wrath on a remnant that still keeps the commandments of God and the testimony of Jesus (_Rev. 12:17_; _MWV2 213.1_). Two seeds, one war, no neutral ground: everything that follows in this handbook traces this single antagonism from Eden to the remnant.

**Symbols defined here:**

- **the enmity** = the God-put antagonism between the two seeds — grace, not curse: fallen man is naturally at peace with the serpent, so "I will put enmity" is a supernatural interposition of implanted grace, creating "conflict where hitherto had been peace"; to lose it by befriending the world is to become "the enemy of God," and the world's hatred of the saints is the same enmity running outward (_Gen. 3:15_; _Jas. 4:4_; _John 15:19_; _GC 505.2_; _GC 506.2_).
- **the seed of the woman** = Christ first, and in Him His people — the singular seed "which is Christ," reckoned to all who are Christ's, sown by the Son of man as the children of the kingdom, sharing the head-bruising victory, and terminating in "the remnant of her seed" who keep the commandments of God and have the testimony of Jesus (_Gen. 3:15_; _Gal. 3:16_; _Gal. 3:29_; _Matt. 13:37-38_; _Rom. 16:20_; _Rev. 12:17_).
- **the seed of the serpent** = a spiritual paternity, not a bloodline — those who belong to the devil by doing his works: "of your father the devil" by doing his lusts, "the children of the wicked one" sown as tares, Cain "of that wicked one" though Eve's own son; the test is practice — "he that committeth sin is of the devil," "whosoever doeth not righteousness is not of God" (_John 8:44_; _Matt. 13:38-39_; _1 John 3:12_; _1 John 3:8_; _1 John 3:10_).

**Symbols carried:** none — this is the opening section, establishing the charter (_Gen. 3:15_) on which the whole handbook stands: the two seeds, the paternity-by-practice that defines them, and the God-put enmity whose keeping or losing the following sections trace from Cain's altar to the dragon's war on the remnant.

**For discussion:**

1. "This enmity is not naturally entertained" (_GC 505.2_) — fallen man is at peace with Satan until grace "creates conflict where hitherto had been peace" (_GC 506.2_; _Gen. 3:15_). If felt conflict with sin is the evidence of implanted grace, what does a comfortable, conflict-free spiritual life actually indicate — and how should that change the way we pray about our own complacency?
2. Cain shared Abel's parents, yet was "of that wicked one" (_1 John 3:12_); the men of John 8 claimed Abraham, yet were "of your father the devil" because "the lusts of your father ye will do" (_John 8:44_). If seed-membership is by practice and paternity, not pedigree or profession, by what test does John say the two classes "are manifest" (_1 John 3:10_) — and where does that test land on us?
3. The Sethites kept their "peculiar, holy character" while separate, and lost it "little by little" by mingling (_PP 81.2_; _SR 62.1_) — while at the end the dragon still finds "the remnant of her seed" keeping the commandments of God (_Rev. 12:17_). What "little by little" minglings today most quietly dissolve the God-put enmity in a family or a church, and what would it cost this week to keep the line distinct?
