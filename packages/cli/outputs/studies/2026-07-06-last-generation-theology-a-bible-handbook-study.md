# Last Generation Theology — A Bible Handbook Study

**Thesis.** Man is dust made alive by the breath of God — a living soul, whole and mortal (Gen. 2:7). Sin is not a guilt inherited at birth but the consented transgression of the law (1 John 3:4; Jas. 1:14-15); yet the fallen nature we do inherit corrupts and enslaves (Rom. 7:14; Eph. 2:3). Into that very flesh the Son of God came — the second Adam, bearing the full fallen nature of man after four thousand years of sin, yet without sin (Rom. 8:3; Heb. 2:14-17; 4:15) — conceived of the Spirit and living from the manger, by faith in His Father, the surrendered life we enter by the new birth of water and the Spirit (John 3:5). Salvation is God saving His people FROM their sins, not in them (Matt. 1:21): righteousness by faith — the gospel in verity, the third angel’s message in verity — both credits Christ’s obedience to the believer and works it in him, so that complete victory over sin is provided, promised, and expected (Rom. 6:14; 1 John 5:4; Jude 24; Rev. 3:21). That message ripens a last generation who keep the commandments of God and the faith of Jesus (Rev. 14:12), stand through the time of trouble without a mediator because their sins have gone beforehand to judgment (Rev. 22:11; Dan. 12:1), and in whom the character of Christ is perfectly reproduced — the harvest that vindicates God before the universe (Mark 4:29; Rev. 14:1-5).

**Method.** After Haskell's Bible Handbook: ref → gloss, scannable; every doctrine established first from Scripture (Miller's Rule — the Bible defines its own terms), then corroborated by a central cloud of witnesses — E. J. Waggoner, A. T. Jones, S. N. Haskell, and Ellen G. White — quoted VERBATIM and verified against the corpus. This study argues the historic Adventist “last generation” understanding: full victory over sin through the indwelling Christ. On the nature of Christ it is careful and honest: He took our fallen NATURE, never our sin — nature is not guilt, and sin is in the consent — so no line may make Christ a sinner, and no line may give Him an unfallen-Adam advantage. Where a source cannot be quoted from the local corpus, the point rests on Scripture and what CAN be verified; no claim without a receipt.

# Part I — The Man

## 1. Dust and Breath — What Man Is

> Dust of the ground plus the breath of God equals a living soul — man does not HAVE a soul, he IS one: a single, undivided, mortal being; and everything this handbook says about sin and salvation happens in that one whole man.

**Dust + breath = a living soul — Gen. 2:7; 1 Cor. 15:45:**

- _Gen. 2:7._ "And the LORD God formed man of the dust of the ground, and breathed into his nostrils the breath of life; and man became a living soul." — the master text: two components, one result; man does not receive a soul, man BECOMES one — the soul is the sum, not an occupant.
  - **the living soul** = the WHOLE man himself — the sum produced when God joined dust (the body) to the breath of life; not a part inside man but the undivided person, and therefore mortal (_Gen. 2:7_; _1 Cor. 15:45_, "The first man Adam was made a living soul"; _Ezek. 18:4_, "the soul that sinneth, it shall die").
- _1 Cor. 15:45._ "And so it is written, The first man Adam was made a living soul; the last Adam was made a quickening spirit." — Paul quotes Genesis exactly: Adam WAS MADE a living soul, a created and dependent being, set over against the last Adam who gives life (see "The Second Adam — The Nature Christ Took").
- _CCh 74.4._ White: "the human form was perfect in all its arrangements, but it was without life. Then a personal, self-existing God breathed into that form the breath of life.... Man became a living soul" — the formed body was lifeless until God breathed; the soul is what the whole man became, not what was inserted.
- _MH 415.1._ White, in a parallel statement to CCh 74.4 above: "Man became a living soul. Through Christ the Word, a personal God created man and endowed him with intelligence and power." — the same paragraph read from another angle: intelligence itself is God's endowment upon the formed man, not the property of a pre-existing immortal spirit.

**The breath defined — God's life-power, not a conscious tenant — Job 33:4; 27:3; Gen. 7:22; Ps. 104:29:**

- _Job 33:4._ "The Spirit of God hath made me, and the breath of the Almighty hath given me life." — Scripture defines its own figure: the breath is the Almighty's own life-giving power, the same act as creation, not a self-conscious entity lodged in the body.
  - **the breath / spirit of life** = the impersonal, God-given life-power that animates the dust — God's loaned inspiration, sustained heartbeat by heartbeat, never a self-aware being (_Job 33:4_; _Job 27:3_, "the spirit of God is in my nostrils"; _Gen. 7:22_; _Ps. 104:29_; _Ps. 146:4_).
- _Job 27:3._ "All the while my breath is in me, and the spirit of God is in my nostrils" — Job equates "my breath" with "the spirit of God": what animates man is the breath God breathed (_Gen. 2:7_), borrowed life-power, not man's own immortal core.
- _Gen. 7:22._ "All in whose nostrils was the breath of life, of all that was in the dry land, died." — the same breath of life is in beast and man alike, and when it departs they die; it is life-power common to all creatures, not a distinct conscious human essence.
- _Ps. 104:29._ "thou takest away their breath, they die, and return to their dust" — the exact inverse of _Gen. 2:7_: take away the breath and the creature dies; life is a loan sustained moment by moment.
- _1SM 294.3._ White: "Each heartbeat, each breath, is the inspiration of Him who breathed into the nostrils of Adam the breath of life—the inspiration of the ever-present God, the great I AM." — the breath is God's continually-renewed inspiration; man lives on borrowed life, never on a self-running immortal spark.

**Death un-does Genesis 2:7 — dust to earth, breath to God — Gen. 3:19; Job 34:14-15; Eccl. 12:7; Ps. 146:4:**

- _Gen. 3:19._ "for dust thou art, and unto dust shalt thou return" — God's own verdict on man's constitution and end: what was formed of dust dissolves back to dust; the created being is dissolved, not relocated.
- _Job 34:14-15._ "If he ... gather unto himself his spirit and his breath; All flesh shall perish together, and man shall turn again unto dust." — the equation run in reverse: God gathers back His spirit and breath, and the WHOLE man perishes; withdraw the life-power and nothing conscious remains.
- _Eccl. 12:7._ "Then shall the dust return to the earth as it was: and the spirit shall return unto God who gave it." — death resolves man into his two components: dust to earth, breath to Giver. The spirit "returns" exactly as it was given (_Gen. 2:7_) — as life-power reverting to its Source, not as the man flying to heaven.
- _Ps. 146:4._ "His breath goeth forth, he returneth to his earth; in that very day his thoughts perish." — the instant the breath departs, thoughts PERISH: consciousness ends with the breath; the returning spirit carries no mind with it — decisive against a thinking soul surviving death.
- _SBBS 314.10._ Hales (Source Book): immortality is "not to be understood as being derived from any inherent virtue in his nature; as if the spirit or mind was necessarily immortal.... The divine sentence pronounced on Adam, 'Dust thou art, and unto dust shalt thou return' (Genesis 3:19), included the dissolution of the spirit also." — nothing in man's nature is inherently immortal; "dust thou art" dissolves the whole man, spirit included.

**The dead sleep — no knowledge, no praise, until the resurrection — Job 14:10-12; Eccl. 9:5; Ps. 6:5; 115:17:**

- _Job 14:10._ "But man dieth, and wasteth away: yea, man giveth up the ghost, and where is he?" — the question expects its answer: nowhere conscious; he is dissolved.
- _Job 14:12._ "So man lieth down, and riseth not: till the heavens be no more, they shall not awake, nor be raised out of their sleep." — death is a SLEEP, and its one exit is the resurrection; the sleeper waits for the waking, not for a flight.
- _Eccl. 9:5._ "the dead know not any thing, neither have they any more a reward" — no awareness, no reward yet; the undivided man is simply not conscious in death.
- _Ps. 6:5._ "For in death there is no remembrance of thee: in the grave who shall give thee thanks?" — no remembrance, no thanksgiving — no conscious activity of any kind.
- _Ps. 115:17._ "The dead praise not the LORD, neither any that go down into silence." — the dead go down into silence, not up to conscious reward; death is stillness of the whole being, not the liberation of a praising soul.
- _DD 17.2._ White: "The Bible clearly teaches that the dead do not go immediately to heaven. They are represented as sleeping until the resurrection.... man's thoughts perish." — _Eccl. 9:5_, _Ps. 146:4_, and _Job 14_ read together: no soul is awake in the intermediate state.

**The soul is mortal — God only hath immortality, and the gift comes in Christ — Ezek. 18:4; 1 Tim. 6:16; 1 Cor. 15:22, 53; 2 Tim. 1:10:**

- _Ezek. 18:4._ "Behold, all souls are mine; as the soul of the father, so also the soul of the son is mine: the soul that sinneth, it shall die." — Scripture's flat denial that the soul is deathless: since man IS a soul, the soul that sins is the whole man — and it dies. What that sin is, and how it attaches, is the next study (see "What Sin Is — Transgression, Not Inheritance").
- _1 Tim. 6:16._ "Who only hath immortality, dwelling in the light which no man can approach unto" — God ONLY hath immortality; man is mortal by nature and can receive immortality solely as a gift.
- _1 Cor. 15:22._ "For as in Adam all die, even so in Christ shall all be made alive." — all die in the mortal first man; life comes only in Christ, "made alive" at the resurrection.
- _1 Cor. 15:53._ "For this corruptible must put on incorruption, and this mortal must put on immortality." — man is "this mortal" NOW; immortality is a garment to be PUT ON at the resurrection, not a possession already carried.
- _2 Tim. 1:10._ "our Saviour Jesus Christ, who hath abolished death, and hath brought life and immortality to light through the gospel" — life and immortality come to light through Christ and the gospel: a gift brought by the Saviour, not native to fallen man.
- _GC 533.1._ White: "Immortality, promised to man on condition of obedience, had been forfeited by transgression. Adam could not transmit to his posterity that which he did not possess... And only through Christ can immortality be obtained." — forfeited in Adam, obtainable only in Christ.
- _CET 39.4._ White recalls the texts that settled her own mind: "'The soul that sinneth, it shall die.' Ezekiel 18:4. 'The living know that they shall die: but the dead know not anything.' Ecclesiastes 9:5. '...who only hath immortality.' 1 Timothy 6:15, 16." — the very spine of this section, cited together as proof the soul has no natural immortality.

**The counter-doctrine — the serpent's first sermon — Gen. 3:4:**

- _Gen. 3:4._ "And the serpent said unto the woman, Ye shall not surely die." — the first lie denies God's verdict of _Gen. 2:7_ and _3:19_: it teaches an undying something in man that death cannot touch; the immortal-soul theory is the serpent's sermon.
- _1T 342.3._ White: "The great original lie which he told to Eve in Eden, 'Ye shall not surely die,' was the first sermon ever preached on the immortality of the soul. That sermon was crowned with success, and terrible results followed." — the doctrine has a preacher, and it is the serpent.
- _DD 17.1._ White: "The theory of the immortality of the soul was one of those false doctrines that Rome, borrowing from paganism, incorporated into the religion of Christendom. Martin Luther classed it with the 'monstrous fables that form part of the Roman dunghill of decretals.'" — the lie's road into Christendom runs through paganism and Rome, not through Scripture; the Reformer threw it out.

**DEFINITION — DUST AND BREATH — WHAT MAN IS =** man is dust of the ground made alive by the breath of God, and the sum is a LIVING SOUL (_Gen. 2:7_; _1 Cor. 15:45_): he does not have a soul, he IS one — body, mind, and breath one indivisible being (_CCh 74.4_; _MH 415.1_). The breath/spirit is God's own loaned life-power (_Job 33:4_; _27:3_), the same breath that animates the beasts (_Gen. 7:22_; _Ps. 104:29_), sustained heartbeat by heartbeat (_1SM 294.3_); it is not a conscious entity, for the day it goes forth "his thoughts perish" (_Ps. 146:4_). Death therefore un-does creation: dust to earth, spirit back to the God who gave it (_Gen. 3:19_; _Eccl. 12:7_; _Job 34:14-15_), dissolving the whole man, spirit included (_SBBS 314.10_), and the whole man sleeps in unconscious silence until the resurrection (_Job 14:10-12_; _Eccl. 9:5_; _Ps. 6:5_; _115:17_; _DD 17.2_). The soul, then, is mortal — "the soul that sinneth, it shall die" (_Ezek. 18:4_) — for God "only hath immortality" (_1 Tim. 6:16_), and mortal man must PUT ON immortality in Christ at the resurrection (_1 Cor. 15:22, 53_; _2 Tim. 1:10_; _GC 533.1_; _CET 39.4_). "Ye shall not surely die" (_Gen. 3:4_) — the immortal-soul theory — is the serpent's first sermon (_1T 342.3_), imported into Christendom from paganism through Rome and rejected by Luther (_DD 17.1_). This grounds the whole handbook: sin and salvation happen in ONE undivided man — body, mind, breath — not in an immortal ghost inside him; so sin is the whole man's own act (see "What Sin Is — Transgression, Not Inheritance"), the whole man is the nature the Son of God took (see "The Second Adam — The Nature Christ Took"), and the whole man is what God saves from sin (see "What Salvation Really Is — Saved From Sin").

**Symbols defined here:**

- **the living soul** = the whole man himself — the sum produced when God joined dust (body) to the breath of life; man does not have a soul, he IS one, an indivisible and mortal unit (_Gen. 2:7_; _1 Cor. 15:45_; _Ezek. 18:4_; _Eccl. 9:5_; _Ps. 146:4_).
- **the breath / spirit of life** = the impersonal, God-given life-power that animates the dust — common to man and beast, sustained moment by moment, returning to God at death as life-power without consciousness (_Job 33:4_; _Job 27:3_; _Gen. 7:22_; _Ps. 104:29_; _Eccl. 12:7_; _Ps. 146:4_; _Job 34:14-15_).

**Symbols carried:** none — this is the opening section; it establishes the WHOLE-MAN thread (dust + breath = a living soul, mortal and undivided) carried into "What Sin Is — Transgression, Not Inheritance," "The Second Adam — The Nature Christ Took," and "What Salvation Really Is — Saved From Sin."

**For discussion:**

1. Genesis 2:7 says man "became a living soul" — he does not receive one; and "each heartbeat, each breath" is the loaned inspiration of the God who breathed into Adam's nostrils (_1SM 294.3_). If the soul is what you ARE, and life is borrowed moment by moment, what changes this week in how you treat your body and mind — and what does daily dependence on Christ look like for a person who owns no life of his own (_Gen. 2:7_; _1 Tim. 6:16_)?
2. "The dead know not any thing" (_Eccl. 9:5_), and immortality is only "put on" at the resurrection (_1 Cor. 15:53_). How does this make the resurrection — rather than death — the real center of the Christian hope, and how would you comfort a grieving friend from these texts?
3. "His breath goeth forth... in that very day his thoughts perish" (_Ps. 146:4_). How does the truth that the dead sleep until the resurrection (_Job 14:12_) guard you against every deception — spiritualism, apparitions, voices claiming to be the dead — built on the serpent's "Ye shall not surely die" (_Gen. 3:4_)?

## 2. What Sin Is — Transgression, Not Inheritance

> Sin is the transgression of the law — born only when the will consents, so guilt is by choice, never by birth; yet the fallen nature we do inherit is a real bondage, a bent that corrupts and enslaves the whole man until Christ delivers — corruption inherited: affirmed; guilt inherited: denied.

**Sin is defined by the law, not by birth — 1 John 3:4:**

- _1 John 3:4._ "Whosoever committeth sin transgresseth also the law: for sin is the transgression of the law" — the Bible's own definition: sin IS the transgression of the law, a committed act against a known standard — "whosoever COMMITTETH" — not a condition of birth.
  - **sin** = the consented transgression of God's law — a chosen act against a known standard, born when desire conceives, when the will yields (_1 John 3:4_, "sin is the transgression of the law"; _James 1:14-15_, brought forth only "when lust hath conceived"; _John 8:34_, "Whosoever committeth sin"; _Ezek. 18:20_; _Rom. 3:23_, "all have sinned"). Defined by the law, never by lineage; guilt attaches to the choice, never to the mere presence of a fallen inclination.
- _AA 555.1._ White: "'Whosoever committeth sin,' he writes, 'transgresseth also the law: for sin is the transgression of the law.'" — she lets John define sin, quoting the text verbatim and dealing "in plain terms" with sin as a DOING, an act against the law.

**Sin has a genealogy — desire solicits, the will conceives — James 1:14-15; John 8:34:**

- _James 1:14._ "But every man is tempted, when he is drawn away of his own lust, and enticed" — the drawing and the enticing are temptation, not yet sin; the inward bent solicits.
- _James 1:15._ "Then when lust hath conceived, it bringeth forth sin: and sin, when it is finished, bringeth forth death" — sin has a birthday: it is BORN only when lust CONCEIVES — when the will says yes; the genealogy runs desire → consent → sin → death, and guilt enters at the consent.
- _John 8:34._ "Whosoever committeth sin is the servant of sin" — Christ ties the bondage to the COMMITTING; the servitude of sin follows the act, not the ancestry — and the Son breaks that dominion (v. 36).
- _HP 251.3._ White: "There is no sin in having temptation, but sin comes in when temptation is yielded to." — the precise James 1 distinction: the solicitation of the fallen nature is not sin; sin is born at the yielding of the will.
- _MYP 430.1._ White: "It is Satan's act to tempt you, but your own act to yield. It is not in the power of all the host of Satan to force the tempted to transgress. There is no excuse for sin." — guilt is located at the personal act of yielding; no inherited or external force compels transgression.
- _5T 177.1._ White: "An impure thought tolerated, an unholy desire cherished, and the soul is contaminated... 'Then when lust hath conceived, it bringeth forth sin: and sin, when it is finished, bringeth forth death.'" — she traces sin's birth from a cherished, tolerated desire (the will's consent) to the act: conceived by the will, not by birth.
- _2MCP 685.2._ White: "This is the governing power in the nature of man—the power of decision, of choice. Everything depends on the right action of the will... Many will go down to ruin while hoping and desiring to overcome their evil propensities. They do not yield the will to God." — the will is the deciding power; guilt and ruin turn on its action, not on the mere existence of evil propensities.

**Guilt is personal and non-transferable — Ezek. 18:4, 20; Deut. 24:16:**

- _Ezek. 18:4._ "Behold, all souls are mine; as the soul of the father, so also the soul of the son is mine: the soul that sinneth, it shall die" — God claims every soul directly; each dies for its OWN sin, not the parent's (and the "soul" that dies is the whole man dying for his own sin, not a ghost punished for another's — see "Dust and Breath — What Man Is").
- _Ezek. 18:20._ "The soul that sinneth, it shall die. The son shall not bear the iniquity of the father, neither shall the father bear the iniquity of the son" — the flat rejection of inherited GUILT: no man is charged with another's iniquity, Adam's included.
- _Deut. 24:16._ "The fathers shall not be put to death for the children, neither shall the children be put to death for the fathers: every man shall be put to death for his own sin" — the judicial principle behind Ezekiel, written into the statute law of Israel: inherited guilt is foreign to God's government.
- _1MR 142.3._ White, preached at Minneapolis 1888 — the very session where Waggoner and Jones pressed righteousness by faith: "Well, you say, it was Adam's sin. You say, I am not guilty of that, and I am not responsible for his guilt and fall. Here all these natural tendencies are in me and I am not to blame if I act out these natural tendencies. Who is to blame? Is God?" — both sides held in one breath: not guilty of Adam's fall (inherited guilt denied), yet the natural tendencies really "in me" (corruption affirmed) — and blame attaches when we ACT them out.

**Every mouth stopped — because all have sinned — Rom. 3:19, 23; 5:12:**

- _Rom. 3:19._ "that every mouth may be stopped, and all the world may become guilty before God" — the law silences every defense; but the ground of the guilt is stated four verses on.
- _Rom. 3:23._ "For all have sinned, and come short of the glory of God" — the universal indictment rests on ACTS: "have sinned" is an active verb; every mouth is stopped because all have DONE, not because all were born.
- _Rom. 5:12._ "Wherefore, as by one man sin entered into the world, and death by sin; and so death passed upon all men, for that all have sinned" — death passed upon all, not because all inherited Adam's guilt, but "for that ALL HAVE SINNED": the universal reign of death traces to universal actual sinning, made possible by the fallen nature Adam introduced.

**Yet the inherited corruption is real — shapen in iniquity — Ps. 51:5; Eph. 2:3:**

- _Ps. 51:5._ "Behold, I was shapen in iniquity; and in sin did my mother conceive me" — David is bent from birth: a fallen NATURE inherited, not a crime charged — a condition to be delivered from, not a guilt to be sentenced for.
  - **the flesh / fallen nature** = the inherited corruption — a real, terrible bent inclining toward evil, holding dominion over the natural man and enslaving him until Christ delivers; "sin that dwelleth in me" as a resident power, but NOT itself guilt — it condemns only when obeyed (_Ps. 51:5_, "shapen in iniquity"; _Eph. 2:3_, "by nature the children of wrath"; _Rom. 7:14_, "carnal, sold under sin"; _Rom. 7:17-18_, "sin that dwelleth in me... in my flesh"; _Rom. 7:23_, "a law in my members"; _Rom. 7:24-25_, delivered "through Jesus Christ"). Corruption inherited: affirmed. Guilt inherited: denied.
- _Eph. 2:3._ "and were by nature the children of wrath, even as others" — the fallen nature is real and universal; and mark where the wrath is bound in the same verse — to "fulfilling the desires of the flesh and of the mind" — the nature condemns when OBEYED, not merely possessed.
- _CHR 26.2._ Waggoner: "David had all the passions of human nature. He says of himself, 'Behold I was shapen in iniquity; and in sin did my mother conceive me.' Psalm 51:5." — the inherited fallen nature established from Scripture: the flesh carries "all the weaknesses and sinful tendencies to which fallen human nature is subject," yet in the same argument no actual sin is conceded.
- _CHR 91.1._ Waggoner: "We likewise can all say, with David, 'Behold, I was shapen in iniquity; and in sin did my mother conceive me.' ... As a consequence, we are by nature so weak that we cannot do the things that we would." — Psalm 51:5 read as the confession of an inherited WEAKNESS that disables (echoing Rom. 7) — a bent to be delivered from by Christ's power, not a charged guilt.

**A law in the members — the bent enslaves until Christ delivers — Rom. 7:14-25:**

- _Rom. 7:14._ "For we know that the law is spiritual: but I am carnal, sold under sin" — the natural man is SOLD under sin: the fallen nature holds dominion — a slavery, not a mere guilt-tag.
- _Rom. 7:17-18._ "it is no more I that do it, but sin that dwelleth in me. For I know that in me (that is, in my flesh,) dwelleth no good thing" — Paul distinguishes the indwelling sin in the flesh from the willing self: the nature is a resident power, corrupting but not identical with the person's guilt.
- _Rom. 7:23._ "But I see another law in my members, warring against the law of my mind, and bringing me into captivity to the law of sin which is in my members" — the inherited bent operates as a LAW of the nature: it wars, it takes captive, it enslaves the natural man.
- _WOR 122.1._ Waggoner, on Rom. 7:23: "I see another law in my members, warring against the law of my mind... This sin is in our flesh... But we are slaves. Whosoever commits sin is the slave of sin. John 8:34." — the law in the members is sin dwelling in the flesh, holding the natural man in bondage until liberty comes in Christ.
- _Rom. 7:24-25._ "O wretched man that I am! who shall deliver me from the body of this death? I thank God through Jesus Christ our Lord" — the nature is not a life sentence: deliverance comes "through Jesus Christ." Corruption is curable; guilt would not be.
- _CH 440.1._ White: "Those who put their trust in Christ are not to be enslaved by any hereditary or cultivated habit or tendency... Whatever may be our inherited or cultivated tendencies to wrong, we can overcome through the power that He is ready to impart." — the hereditary tendency named as real bondage of the "lower nature," and named as OVERCOMABLE: precisely a bent, not a verdict.
- _LOF_ATJ 127.6._ Jones: "that same degenerate, sinful flesh is there with its same tendencies and desires. But the individual is no longer subject to these... a power that conquers, brings under, crucifies, and keeps under, the flesh, sinful as it is, with all its affections and lusts." — the whole thesis in one line: the inherited flesh with its tendencies REMAINS even in the converted, yet the man is "no longer subject" to it — corruption real and enslaving, yet conquerable, not guilt (see "Complete Victory — Overcoming as He Overcame").

**DEFINITION — WHAT SIN IS — TRANSGRESSION, NOT INHERITANCE =** sin is the transgression of the law (_1 John 3:4_) — a consented act, born only "when lust hath conceived," when the will yields (_James 1:14-15_; _HP 251.3_; _MYP 430.1_); temptation is not sin, and no power in earth or hell can force the yielding (_2MCP 685.2_). Guilt is therefore personal and non-transferable: "the son shall not bear the iniquity of the father" (_Ezek. 18:4, 20_; _Deut. 24:16_); every mouth is stopped and death reigns universal because "ALL HAVE SINNED" (_Rom. 3:19, 23_; _5:12_), not because all were born — universal condemnation stands on universal sinning, not on inherited guilt. Yet what we DO inherit is real and terrible: a fallen nature, "shapen in iniquity" (_Ps. 51:5_), "by nature the children of wrath" (_Eph. 2:3_), "carnal, sold under sin" with "a law in my members" warring and taking captive (_Rom. 7:14, 17-18, 23_; _WOR 122.1_) — a corruption that inclines, enslaves, and holds dominion over the natural man, but is not itself guilt; it condemns only when obeyed (_Eph. 2:3_; _1MR 142.3_; _2MCP 685.2_), and it enslaves only until Christ delivers (_Rom. 7:24-25_; _John 8:34, 36_; _LOF_ATJ 127.6_; _CH 440.1_). Distinguish plainly and hold both: original-sin GUILT (guilty at birth) — rejected by the law and the prophets; inherited CORRUPTION (a bent that enslaves until Christ delivers) — affirmed by David and Paul alike, and curable where guilt would not be: "who shall deliver me?... I thank God through Jesus Christ our Lord" (_Rom. 7:24-25_). This distinction is the hinge of the handbook: it strips the sinner of every heredity-excuse ("there is no excuse for sin"), and it frees the doctrine of the incarnation — since guilt is not a substance bred in the flesh, the need it defines is not acquittal from Adam's crime but deliverance from sin's power (see "What Salvation Really Is — Saved From Sin"); because nature is not sin, Christ could take our fallen nature and be without sin (see "The Second Adam — The Nature Christ Took"); and because sin is in the consent, the consent can — by the same Christ — be forever withheld (see "Complete Victory — Overcoming as He Overcame").

**Symbols defined here:**

- **sin** = the consented transgression of God's law — a chosen act against a known standard, born when desire conceives (the will yields); defined by the law, not by birth; guilt attaches to the choice, never to the mere presence of a fallen inclination (_1 John 3:4_; _James 1:14-15_; _John 8:34_; _Ezek. 18:20_; _Rom. 3:23_).
- **the flesh / fallen nature** = the inherited corruption — a real bent that inclines toward evil and holds the natural man in bondage until Christ delivers; "sin that dwelleth in me" as a resident power, but not itself guilt — it condemns only when obeyed (_Ps. 51:5_; _Eph. 2:3_; _Rom. 7:14, 17-18, 23-25_).

**Symbols carried:** the living soul — the one whole man, dust + breath, who sins as one and dies as one, "the soul that sinneth, it shall die" (see "Dust and Breath — What Man Is"); the fallen nature defined here is the very flesh the Son of God took, without its sin (see "The Second Adam — The Nature Christ Took"); the deliverance of _Rom. 7:25_ is entered by the new birth (see "Born of Water and of the Spirit — How He Lived, How We Begin") and carried to full dominion-breaking in the overcomer (see "Complete Victory — Overcoming as He Overcame").

In your own experience, where is the line between being tempted and consenting, and what does this distinction free a tempted believer from while leaving him responsible for?

(_1MR 142.3_) — and what inherited tendency have you been treating as a verdict instead of a bondage Christ stands ready to break (_CH 440.1_)?

# Part II — The Pattern

## 3. The Second Adam — The Nature Christ Took

> Two men stand at the head of the race: the first Adam fell in unfallen flesh; the second Adam conquered in ours — humanity as four thousand years of sin had left it, tempted in all points like as we are, yet without sin — for the difference between Christ and us is not the flesh He bore but the life He lived in it.

**Two Adams — two heads of the race — 1 Cor. 15:45-47; Rom. 5:19:**

- _1 Cor. 15:45._ "The first man Adam was made a living soul; the last Adam was made a quickening spirit" — two representative men set over against each other; where the first head of the race fell, a second stands to reverse the ruin.
  - **the second Adam** = Christ as the new head and representative of the human race, standing where the first Adam fell — but in FALLEN flesh rather than unfallen, rendering unbroken obedience where the first man disobeyed: a real re-run of Adam's probation in the actual condition of Adam's ruined descendants, so that His obedience can be reckoned to the many just as Adam's disobedience was (_1 Cor. 15:45, 47_; _Rom. 5:19_, obedience against disobedience as the only contrast between the two heads; _Heb. 2:16-17_, the seed of Abraham, "made like unto his brethren" — His stand is inside fallen humanity, not above it).
- _1 Cor. 15:46._ "Howbeit that was not first which is spiritual, but that which is natural; and afterward that which is spiritual" — the order is fixed: the natural man first, then the spiritual; the second Adam enters the natural man's arena to bring forth the spiritual.
- _1 Cor. 15:47._ "The first man is of the earth, earthy: the second man is the Lord from heaven" — the Lord from heaven enters the arena as "the second MAN": a real man of flesh, contesting the same field the first man lost.
- _Rom. 5:19._ "For as by one man's disobedience many were made sinners, so by the obedience of one shall many be made righteous" — the two heads are contrasted by obedience versus disobedience, not by two different natures; the whole weight of the figure falls on the LIFE each lived in the flesh he had.

**Made of a woman, of the seed of David — the lineage He entered — Gal. 4:4-5; Rom. 1:3; Luke 1:35; John 1:14; Phil. 2:7-8:**

- _Gal. 4:4._ "God sent forth his Son, made of a woman, made under the law" — He entered the human line through a fallen daughter of Adam, and took His stand under the law's curse where guilty man stood.
- _Gal. 4:5._ "To redeem them that were under the law, that we might receive the adoption of sons" — the reason for the placement: only One standing where we stand can redeem us out of where we stand.
- _Rom. 1:3._ "made of the seed of David according to the flesh" — not a body new-made as Adam's was in Eden, but David's actual lineage — David, who wrote "in sin did my mother conceive me" (Ps. 51:5); the flesh He took was that hereditary stream, four thousand years down from Eden.
- _CHR 26.2._ Waggoner: "the flesh which He assumed had all the weaknesses and sinful tendencies to which fallen human nature is subject, is shown by the statement that He 'was made of the seed of David according to the flesh.' David had all the passions of human nature." — the pioneer reads Rom. 1:3 exactly so: David's seed, not a sinless being's, and Ps. 51:5 supplied as the receipt.
- _Luke 1:35._ "that holy thing which shall be born of thee shall be called the Son of God" — descended from a fallen woman, the hereditary line truly Mary's, yet the child is HOLY: the nature ours by descent, the character sinless from conception, both truths spoken by the angel in one breath (how that life was lived is walked in "Born of Water and of the Spirit — How He Lived, How We Begin").
- _John 1:14._ "And the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us" — MADE flesh, not clothed in the appearance of it; real, present, dwelling flesh, and in it the Father's glory beheld.
- _Phil. 2:7._ "made himself of no reputation, and took upon him the form of a servant, and was made in the likeness of men" — He emptied Himself all the way down into real, humbled humanity.
- _Phil. 2:8._ "being found in fashion as a man, he humbled himself, and became obedient unto death, even the death of the cross" — and in that humanity the triumph is one word: OBEDIENT — obedience unto death.

**In the likeness of sinful flesh — real flesh, and sin condemned in it — Rom. 8:3-4:**

- _Rom. 8:3._ "God sending his own Son in the likeness of sinful flesh, and for sin, condemned sin in the flesh" — the law was "weak through the flesh," so God met sin on its own ground: the Son came in the likeness of SINFUL flesh — not the likeness of sinless flesh — and there, IN the flesh, condemned sin by never once yielding to it.
  - **sinful flesh** = the fallen, hereditary, degenerate human nature — weakened by four thousand years of sin, subject to infirmity and tempted from within and without — which Christ actually took ("likeness of sinful flesh" is real fallen flesh, not a mere appearance of it), and in which He never once consented to sin; it names the nature He bore, never any sin He committed, for nature is not sin and His consent never came (_Rom. 8:3_, sin condemned IN the flesh, not committed by Him; _Rom. 1:3_, the seed of David; _Heb. 2:14_, "took part of the same"; _Heb. 4:15_, "in all points tempted like as we are, yet without sin"; _2 Cor. 5:21_, "made him to be sin for us, who knew no sin").
- _WOR 126.6._ Waggoner: "There is a common idea that this means that Christ simulated sinful flesh; that he did not take upon himself actual sinful flesh, but only what appeared to be such. But the Scriptures do not teach such a thing... He took the same flesh that all have who are born of woman." — the phantom-flesh escape hatch closed; "likeness" is not pretence.
- _Rom. 8:4._ "That the righteousness of the law might be fulfilled in us, who walk not after the flesh, but after the Spirit" — the purpose of v. 3: because sin was met and mastered in the very flesh we wear, the law's righteousness can now be fulfilled IN US; His victory in fallen flesh is the pledge of ours.
- _WOR 127.2._ Waggoner: "Even so Christ came in the same sinful flesh, yet he was without sin, because he was always led by the Spirit." — both truths in one sentence, and the reason named: the LIFE ("always led by the Spirit"), not a different nature.

**The same flesh and blood — in all things like unto His brethren — Heb. 2:14-18:**

- _Heb. 2:14._ "Forasmuch then as the children are partakers of flesh and blood, he also himself likewise took part of the same" — THE SAME: the same flesh and blood the children have, taken "that through death he might destroy him that had the power of death, that is, the devil."
- _Heb. 2:15._ "And deliver them who through fear of death were all their lifetime subject to bondage" — He entered the bondage to break it from inside.
- _Heb. 2:16._ "For verily he took not on him the nature of angels; but he took on him the seed of Abraham" — not an unfallen angel-nature; a son of Abraham's fallen line.
- _Heb. 2:17._ "Wherefore in all things it behoved him to be made like unto his brethren, that he might be a merciful and faithful high priest" — IN ALL THINGS: "the same" and "in all things" bar every escape hatch that would hand Him a different, unfallen humanity.
- _Heb. 2:18._ "For in that he himself hath suffered being tempted, he is able to succour them that are tempted" — the temptation was real SUFFERING, not a staged demonstration; only one tempted in our flesh can succour the tempted.
- _CWCP 27.1-2._ Jones: "He was made in the likeness of men, as men are, just where they are. 'The Word was made flesh.' He 'took part of the same' flesh and blood as that of which the children of men are partakers, as they are since man has fallen into sin." — Phil. 2, John 1:14, Heb. 2:14, and Gal. 4:4 walked as one thread: the flesh as it is SINCE the fall.
- _3SM 197.3._ White: "But many say that Jesus was not like us... But Paul writes, 'Verily he took not on him the nature of angels; but he took on him the seed of Abraham. Wherefore in all things it behooved him to be made like unto his brethren...'" — she answers the very objection this section confronts by driving straight to Heb. 2:16-17.

**Humanity at its weakest — four thousand years down from Eden — Isa. 53:2-3:**

- _Isa. 53:2._ "he shall grow up before him as a tender plant, and as a root out of a dry ground: he hath no form nor comeliness" — prophecy fixed the condition beforehand: a root out of DRY ground, humanity with no advantage of soil and no reserves.
- _Isa. 53:3._ "a man of sorrows, and acquainted with grief... he was despised, and we esteemed him not" — He wore humanity at its weakest and most unlovely; degenerate flesh, not glorified Adam-flesh.
- _DA 48.6._ White: "It would have been an almost infinite humiliation for the Son of God to take man's nature, even when Adam stood in his innocence in Eden. But Jesus accepted humanity when the race had been weakened by four thousand years of sin. Like every child of Adam He accepted the results of the working of the great law of heredity." — the pre-fall theory settled: not Eden's flesh, but the line as heredity had left it.
- _DA 117.1-2._ White: "Christ took upon Him the infirmities of degenerate humanity... He took the nature of man, with the possibility of yielding to temptation." — degenerate humanity, all its liabilities, a real possibility of failure — or He could not have been placed in Adam's position at all.

**Tempted in all points — yet without sin — the double guard — Heb. 4:15; 2 Cor. 5:21; 1 Pet. 2:22:**

- _Heb. 4:15._ "we have not an high priest which cannot be touched with the feeling of our infirmities; but was in all points tempted like as we are, yet without sin" — both guards in one verse: IN ALL POINTS TEMPTED LIKE AS WE ARE (full identity of nature and temptation), YET WITHOUT SIN (absolute sinlessness). Hold both with equal force; drop either and the High Priest is lost.
- _2 Cor. 5:21._ "he hath made him to be sin for us, who knew no sin" — the strongest pairing in Scripture: MADE to be sin (the full weight of fallen flesh and the sinner's condemnation laid on Him) — who KNEW NO SIN (consent never came). Nature and character held apart in six words.
- _1 Pet. 2:22._ "Who did no sin, neither was guile found in his mouth" — in the fallen flesh He bore, the record is spotless: not once.
- _1SM 253.1._ White: "Christ, who knew not the least taint of sin or defilement, took our nature in its deteriorated condition." — both truths in one line: the deteriorated nature ours, the defilement never His.
- _1SM 256.1._ White: "In taking upon Himself man's nature in its fallen condition, Christ did not in the least participate in its sin... He was touched with the feeling of our infirmities, and was in all points tempted like as we are. And yet He knew no sin." — from the chapter titled "Sinlessness of Christ's Human Nature": fallen nature, zero participation in its sin.
- _FLB 49.4._ White: "He could have sinned; He could have fallen, but not for one moment was there in Him an evil propensity." — quoted honestly: the risk was real ("He could have fallen") and the sinlessness total; the guard is about consent and character — no evil cherished — not a retraction of the fallen nature He took, for nature is not sin (see "What Sin Is — Transgression, Not Inheritance").

**The difference is the life, not the flesh — Heb. 5:8-9:**

- _Heb. 5:8._ "Though he were a Son, yet learned he obedience by the things which he suffered" — a real moral warfare, really fought: obedience LEARNED in suffering, not exempted from it.
- _Heb. 5:9._ "And being made perfect, he became the author of eternal salvation unto all them that obey him" — perfected through that warfare, He is author of salvation to all who by the same surrender obey.
- _CWCP 84.1._ Jones: "Christ attained it in human flesh in this world and thus made and consecrated a way by which, in Him, every believer can attain it." — perfection of character attained IN human flesh opens the same road to the believer (walked out in "Complete Victory — Overcoming as He Overcame").
- _DA 664.4._ White: "Jesus revealed no qualities, and exercised no powers, that men may not have through faith in Him. His perfect humanity is that which all His followers may possess, if they will be in subjection to God as He was." — the closing conviction: the difference is the life lived, and that life is offered to us.
- _LOF_ATJ 60.2._ Jones: "There is a mystery in how God can be manifest in such sinful flesh as yours." — the doctrine pressed home personally: the flesh He took is YOUR flesh, and that is the ground of your hope.

**DEFINITION — THE SECOND ADAM — THE NATURE CHRIST TOOK =** Christ is the last Adam, the second man (_1 Cor. 15:45-47_), the new head of the race whose obedience is set against the first man's disobedience (_Rom. 5:19_) — and He re-ran Adam's probation NOT in Adam's unfallen Eden flesh but in the actual condition of Adam's ruined descendants. He was made of a woman, made under the law (_Gal. 4:4-5_), of the seed of David according to the flesh (_Rom. 1:3_; cf. Ps. 51:5), made flesh indeed (_John 1:14_; _Phil. 2:7-8_), in the likeness of sinful flesh — real fallen flesh, not a simulation (_Rom. 8:3_; _WOR 126.6_) — the same flesh and blood as the children, the seed of Abraham, made like unto His brethren in all things (_Heb. 2:14-17_): humanity as four thousand years of heredity had left it, degenerate, with all its liabilities (_Isa. 53:2-3_; _DA 48.6_; _DA 117.1-2_). In that flesh He was in all points tempted like as we are, YET WITHOUT SIN (_Heb. 4:15_); made to be sin, yet He knew no sin (_2 Cor. 5:21_); He did no sin (_1 Pet. 2:22_); that which was born is "that holy thing" (_Luke 1:35_) — for "sinful flesh" names the nature He bore, never any sin He committed: nature is not sin, and consent never came (_1SM 253.1_; _1SM 256.1_; _FLB 49.4_). The difference between Christ and us is therefore not the flesh He wore but the life He lived in it — obedience learned through suffering, "always led by the Spirit" (_Heb. 5:8-9_; _WOR 127.2_) — and the purpose of the whole arrangement is Rom. 8:4: sin condemned in the flesh "that the righteousness of the law might be fulfilled in us." This is the ground Waggoner (_CHR_, 1890) and Jones (_CWCP_, 1905) developed from the 1888 Minneapolis message on righteousness by faith, and that Ellen White guarded through the same 1890s controversy (_1SM 253-256_; _FLB 49.4_; _3SM 197.3_): a Saviour near enough to be tempted as we are, sinless enough to save, whose victory in our flesh is the pledge and pattern of ours.

**Symbols defined here:**

- **the second Adam** = Christ as the new head and representative of the human race, standing where the first Adam fell — in fallen flesh, rendering unbroken obedience where the first man disobeyed, a real re-run of Adam's probation in the condition of Adam's ruined descendants, His obedience reckoned to the many as Adam's disobedience was (_1 Cor. 15:45, 47_; _Rom. 5:19_; _Heb. 2:16-17_).
- **sinful flesh** = the fallen, hereditary, degenerate human nature — weakened by four thousand years of sin, tempted from within and without — which Christ actually took and in which He never once consented to sin; the nature He bore, never any sin He committed (_Rom. 8:3_; _Rom. 1:3_; _Heb. 2:14_; _Heb. 4:15_; _2 Cor. 5:21_).

**Symbols carried:** the living soul — the first Adam "was made a living soul" (_1 Cor. 15:45_), the whole undivided man the second Adam took (defined in "Dust and Breath — What Man Is"); sin as consented transgression, and the fallen nature that is not itself guilt — the distinction that lets Christ take our flesh without our sin (defined in "What Sin Is — Transgression, Not Inheritance"). Carried forward: the Spirit-led life by which He overcame in this flesh is walked in "Born of Water and of the Spirit — How He Lived, How We Begin", and His victory in fallen flesh as the pledge of ours is taken up in "Complete Victory — Overcoming as He Overcame".

**For discussion:**

1. Heb. 2:17 says "in all things it behoved him to be made like unto his brethren." If Christ had taken Adam's unfallen nature instead of ours, what becomes of Heb. 2:18 — could He still "succour them that are tempted," and could you still bring Him your actual temptations?
2. Rom. 8:3 ends in Rom. 8:4: sin was condemned in the flesh "that the righteousness of the law might be fulfilled in us." What does Christ's obedience in your flesh — not in a better flesh — mean for the sin you have privately called unconquerable?
3. He "himself hath suffered being tempted" and is therefore "able to succour them that are tempted" (_Heb. 2:18_). When temptation comes this week, what changes in your praying if the High Priest you approach has felt the full pull of your flesh — and never once consented?

## 4. Born of Water and of the Spirit — How He Lived, How We Begin

> Christ began human life conceived of the Holy Ghost and lived it in unbroken dependence on the indwelling Father — and what He was from His first birth, we become by the second: born of water and of the Spirit, cleansing joined to power, a new creature partaking of the divine nature.

**Conceived of the Holy Ghost — the life begins in the Spirit — Matt. 1:20; Luke 1:35:**

- _Matt. 1:20._ "fear not to take unto thee Mary thy wife: for that which is conceived in her is of the Holy Ghost" — His human life begins IN the Spirit; before a word is spoken or a work done, the Son enters our flesh already inside the Spirit-filled, God-surrendered existence — He never knew any other.
- _Luke 1:35._ "The Holy Ghost shall come upon thee, and the power of the Highest shall overshadow thee: therefore also that holy thing which shall be born of thee shall be called the Son of God" — mark the two agents of His birth: the Holy Ghost, and the power of the Highest; this exact vocabulary returns upon the church (_Acts 1:8_).
- _1SM 226.2._ White: "In His humanity He was a partaker of the divine nature. In His incarnation He gained in a new sense the title of the Son of God" — in OUR nature (see "The Second Adam — The Nature Christ Took") He was Himself "a partaker of the divine nature" — the very phrase afterward written over the believer (_2 Pet. 1:4_).
- _MWM 302.1._ Millerite conference report (Memoirs of William Miller): the first advent "took place in the days of Herod, when He was conceived of the Holy Ghost, born of the Virgin Mary" — a settled first-advent fact: the Spirit-filled life this section traces starts at His conception.

**From childhood the Father's will was His life — Luke 2:49; Ps. 40:8:**

- _Luke 2:49._ "How is it that ye sought me? wist ye not that I must be about my Father's business?" — twelve years old, and the Father's will is already the "must" of His existence; the surrendered life did not begin at Jordan — it was His from the first.
- _Ps. 40:8._ "I delight to do thy will, O my God: yea, thy law is within my heart" — the Messianic key: the law WITHIN the heart, obedience by delight and not constraint — the very thing the new birth writes in us (_Eze. 36:26-27_).

**How He lived — the Son can do nothing of Himself — John 5:19, 30; 14:10; Heb. 5:8-9; John 6:57; Gal. 2:20:**

- _John 5:19._ "The Son can do nothing of himself, but what he seeth the Father do" — the astonishing disclaimer: in the flesh He took, Christ renounces independent power; He lived as man must live — by dependence.
- _John 5:30._ "I can of mine own self do nothing... I seek not mine own will, but the will of the Father which hath sent me" — the confession restated: not His own will but the Father's; the surrendered will is the mechanism of the sinless life.
- _John 14:10._ "the words that I speak unto you I speak not of myself: but the Father that dwelleth in me, he doeth the works" — the works were the Father's, wrought through an indwelt, surrendered man — the very arrangement offered to us.
- _DA 208.2._ White: "So utterly was Christ emptied of self that He made no plans for Himself. He accepted God's plans for Him... So should we depend upon God, that our lives may be the simple outworking of His will" — His "can do nothing of Himself" means total surrender and total dependence — and the sentence ends on us: "so should we."
- _SC 75.2._ White: "The Saviour has said of Himself, 'The Son can do nothing of Himself;' 'the Father that dwelleth in Me, He doeth the works.'... We shall work as He worked; we shall manifest the same spirit" — _John 5:19_ and _14:10_ read as one truth and handed straight to the believer.
- _DA 363.1._ White: "As a man He supplicated the throne of God till His humanity was charged with a heavenly current that should connect humanity with divinity... His experience is to be ours" — the channel named: AS A MAN He drew from God; power came by dependence, prayed for and received — and the paragraph closes by handing that experience over.
- _Heb. 5:8-9._ "Though he were a Son, yet learned he obedience by the things which he suffered; and being made perfect, he became the author of eternal salvation unto all them that obey him" — obedience LEARNED, in suffering flesh: the sinless life was a lived acquisition, not an exercised advantage — and its fruit is salvation "unto all them that obey him."
- _CHR 27.3._ Waggoner: "not only counted as a sinner but actually taking upon Himself sinful nature... 'in all points tempted like as we are, yet without sin'" — the nature was ours (see "The Second Adam — The Nature Christ Took"); the sinlessness was in never consenting — for sin is in the consent, not the flesh (see "What Sin Is — Transgression, Not Inheritance").
- _DA 117.2._ White: "our Saviour took humanity, with all its liabilities. He took the nature of man, with the possibility of yielding to temptation. We have nothing to bear which He has not endured" — therefore His victory was won by no advantage withheld from us; what He used, we may use.
- _John 6:57._ "As the living Father hath sent me, and I live by the Father: so he that eateth me, even he shall live by me" — Christ names His own life-principle and hands it over in the same breath: the dependence He lived by becomes the dependence we live by.
- _Gal. 2:20._ "the life which I now live in the flesh I live by the faith of the Son of God" — the mechanism made explicit: the indwelt, surrendered life is lived BY FAITH — and not a faith we manufacture, but "the faith of the Son of God," His own faith imparted.

**What He was by birth, we become by the new birth — John 3:3-7:**

- _John 3:3._ "Except a man be born again, he cannot see the kingdom of God" — not reform, not resolve: a BIRTH; nothing less can even see the kingdom.
- _John 3:4._ "How can a man be born when he is old? can he enter the second time into his mother's womb, and be born?" — Nicodemus's bewilderment measures the demand: the change required is as total as beginning life over.
- _John 3:5._ "Except a man be born of water and of the Spirit, he cannot enter into the kingdom of God" — the charter of the second birth: water AND Spirit, together, or no entrance.
  - **the new birth (born again / born of water and of the Spirit)** = the Spirit re-creating the whole man — "an entire change of the entire man" (_TDG 48.4_), a new heart and new spirit given (_Eze. 36:26-27_), making a "new creature" (_2 Cor. 5:17_) able to see and enter the kingdom — the second birth that puts a man where Christ stood from His first: inside the Spirit-filled, God-surrendered life (_John 3:5-7_; _Titus 3:5_; _2 Pet. 1:4_).
- _John 3:6._ "That which is born of the flesh is flesh; and that which is born of the Spirit is spirit" — the flesh reproduces only itself (see "What Sin Is — Transgression, Not Inheritance"); only a second parentage produces a second kind of man.
- _John 3:7._ "Marvel not that I said unto thee, Ye must be born again" — His "must" was the Father's business (_Luke 2:49_); ours is the birth that brings us into it.
- _DA 171.5._ White: "Nicodemus knew that Christ here referred to water baptism and the renewing of the heart by the Spirit of God" — water = baptism; Spirit = the renewed heart; the two are one requirement, not two.
- _TDG 48.4._ White: "Transformation of heart means an entire change of the entire man... it is an inward work, and yet, it is seen, because it works outward from within" — the new birth is total, and it shows.

**The washing — the laver of regeneration — cleansing joined to power — Titus 3:5; Eph. 5:26; Eze. 36:25-27:**

- _Titus 3:5._ "Not by works of righteousness which we have done, but according to his mercy he saved us, by the washing of regeneration, and renewing of the Holy Ghost" — the two clauses are one act: washing (cleansing) joined to renewing (power), and neither is by works.
  - **the washing / the laver of regeneration (regeneration by water, word, and Spirit)** = the cleansing of regeneration in which washing and power are inseparable — the clean water (_Eze. 36:25_), the word (_Eph. 5:26_), and the renewing Spirit (_Titus 3:5_) are one act, which both removes the filthiness AND puts the Spirit within to cause the cleansed one to walk in God's statutes (_Eze. 36:27_; _Zech. 4:6_).
- _Eph. 5:26._ "That he might sanctify and cleanse it with the washing of water by the word" — the washing water is the word: the cleansing is wrought by water and word and Spirit together (_John 3:5_; _Titus 3:5_).
- _Eze. 36:25._ "Then will I sprinkle clean water upon you, and ye shall be clean: from all your filthiness, and from all your idols, will I cleanse you" — the OT washing-promise: God does the sprinkling and God does the cleansing.
- _Eze. 36:26._ "A new heart also will I give you, and a new spirit will I put within you: and I will take away the stony heart out of your flesh, and I will give you an heart of flesh" — the washing is joined to a transplant: stony heart out, heart of flesh in — the new birth in prophecy.
- _Eze. 36:27._ "And I will put my spirit within you, and cause you to walk in my statutes, and ye shall keep my judgments, and do them" — cleansing (v. 25) is joined to power (v. 27): the indwelling Spirit CAUSES the walk — the delight-obedience Christ had from birth (_Ps. 40:8_) now promised to us.
- _SD 105.3._ White: "All true reformation begins with soul-cleansing. It is by the washing of regeneration and the renewing of the mind through the power of the Holy Spirit, that a change is wrought in the life" — washing and power never separated; a cleansing without the Spirit's renewing is no regeneration.

**Not by might — the power named and offered — Zech. 4:6; Acts 1:8:**

- _Zech. 4:6._ "Not by might, nor by power, but by my spirit, saith the LORD of hosts" — the power-source of the whole re-creation named once for all: human might is expressly ruled out — the Spirit-not-flesh principle by which Christ lived and by which we are re-made.
- _COL 66.1._ White: "As the plant receives the sunshine, the dew, and the rain, we are to open our hearts to the Holy Spirit. The work is to be done 'not by might, nor by power, but by My Spirit, saith the Lord of hosts'" — after the birth, growth runs by the same law: receiving, not achieving.
- _Acts 1:8._ "ye shall receive power, after that the Holy Ghost is come upon you: and ye shall be witnesses unto me" — hear _Luke 1:35_ again: the Holy Ghost "come upon," the power of the Highest — the overshadowing of His birth becomes the endowment of ours.

**The result — a new creature, partaker of the divine nature — 2 Cor. 5:17; 2 Pet. 1:4:**

- _2 Cor. 5:17._ "Therefore if any man be in Christ, he is a new creature: old things are passed away; behold, all things are become new" — the finished result: not the old man improved but a new creation, living from the same source Christ lived from.
- _2 Pet. 1:4._ "that by these ye might be partakers of the divine nature, having escaped the corruption that is in the world through lust" — the very phrase _1SM 226.2_ used of Christ's humanity is now written over the believer: what He was, we are made.
- _CWCP 43.4._ Jones: "God sending His own Son in the likeness of sinful flesh... condemned sin in the flesh: that the righteousness of the law might be fulfilled in us, who walk not after the flesh, but after the Spirit... for to make in Himself of twain [God and man] one new man" — the purpose of the incarnation lands here: righteousness fulfilled IN US who walk after the Spirit; the "one new man" is the new creature of _2 Cor. 5:17_ — the ground of "Complete Victory — Overcoming as He Overcame."

**DEFINITION — BORN OF WATER AND OF THE SPIRIT — HOW HE LIVED, HOW WE BEGIN =** Christ's human life began in the Spirit — conceived of the Holy Ghost (_Matt. 1:20_; _MWM 302.1_), "that holy thing" made by the Holy Ghost and the power of the Highest (_Luke 1:35_), Himself in His humanity "a partaker of the divine nature" (_1SM 226.2_) — and it was lived the whole way in surrendered dependence: the Father's business from childhood (_Luke 2:49_), the law within the heart (_Ps. 40:8_), "the Son can do nothing of himself" (_John 5:19, 30_), "the Father that dwelleth in me, he doeth the works" (_John 14:10_; _DA 208.2_; _SC 75.2_; _DA 363.1_), obedience learned in suffering flesh (_Heb. 5:8-9_). He overcame by no power unavailable to us (_DA 117.2_; _CHR 27.3_), and He hands the arrangement over intact: "I live by the Father: so he that eateth me, even he shall live by me" (_John 6:57_) — a life lived thereafter "by the faith of the Son of God" (_Gal. 2:20_). What He was from His first birth we become by the second: born of water and of the Spirit (_John 3:3-7_) — the washing of regeneration and renewing of the Holy Ghost (_Titus 3:5_), the washing of water by the word (_Eph. 5:26_), the sprinkled clean water, the given heart, and the indwelling Spirit that causes the walk (_Eze. 36:25-27_) — an entire change of the entire man (_TDG 48.4_), cleansing joined to power, not by might nor by power but by His Spirit (_Zech. 4:6_), the Holy Ghost coming upon us as upon Mary (_Luke 1:35_ → _Acts 1:8_). The result is a new creature (_2 Cor. 5:17_), a partaker of the divine nature (_2 Pet. 1:4_; _CWCP 43.4_) — a man begun where Christ began, living from the source Christ lived from, so that overcoming as He overcame (see "Complete Victory — Overcoming as He Overcame") is not imitation from without but the outworking of the same indwelt life.

**Symbols defined here:**

- **the new birth (born again / born of water and of the Spirit)** = the Spirit re-creating the whole man — an entire change of the entire man, a new heart and new spirit given, so that the man becomes a new creature able to see and enter the kingdom; the second birth that puts a man where Christ stood from His first birth, inside the Spirit-filled, God-surrendered life (_John 3:3-7_; _2 Cor. 5:17_; _Eze. 36:26-27_; _Titus 3:5_; _2 Pet. 1:4_; _TDG 48.4_).
- **the washing / the laver of regeneration (regeneration by water, word, and Spirit)** = the cleansing of regeneration in which washing and power are joined and inseparable — water sprinkled clean, the word that sanctifies, and the renewing of the Holy Ghost are one act, removing filthiness AND supplying the indwelling Spirit that causes the cleansed one to walk in God's statutes; not by might or works, but by the Spirit (_Titus 3:5_; _Eph. 5:26_; _Eze. 36:25-27_; _Zech. 4:6_; _SD 105.3_).

**Symbols carried:** the second Adam and sinful flesh — the nature He lived this life in (see "The Second Adam — The Nature Christ Took"); sin as consented transgression and the flesh / fallen nature that reproduces only itself (see "What Sin Is — Transgression, Not Inheritance"); the living soul — the new birth re-creates the WHOLE man because man is one undivided whole (see "Dust and Breath — What Man Is").

1. Christ said, "The Son can do nothing of himself" (_John 5:19_) and "I live by the Father" (_John 6:57_). Where in this week's decisions are you still living "of your own self" — and what would it look like, concretely, to live those hours by dependence as He did?
2. Ezekiel 36:25-27 joins the cleansing to the Spirit who "causes" obedience, and Titus 3:5 joins the washing to the renewing. Which half have you been attempting alone — cleansing without power, or power without cleansing — and what does receiving both as ONE act change about how you pray?
3. Jesus was born into the Spirit-filled life; we must be born into it (_John 3:7_). If Acts 1:8 is the believer's birthright and not merely the apostles' history, what would you begin asking God for differently tomorrow morning?

# Part III — The Remedy

## 5. What Salvation Really Is — Saved From Sin

> The angel named the Saviour by His work — "he shall save his people FROM their sins" — from sin's guilt, from sin's dominion, and at last from sin's very presence, by the indwelling Christ restoring the image of God in the soul; a gospel that leaves men under sin's rule until death is not the everlasting gospel.

**The Saviour is named by His work — Matt. 1:21, 23; Ps. 130:8:**

- _Matt. 1:21._ "thou shalt call his name JESUS: for he shall save his people from their sins" — the name is the work: He saves His people FROM their sins — not from the penalty only, and never leaving them IN them; Jesus (Heb. _Yeshua_) = "Jehovah saves," and what Jehovah saves from is the sin itself.
  - **salvation** = God saving man FROM sin — from its guilt (justification, the pardon of _Matt. 1:21_), from its power and dominion (sanctification, _Rom. 6:14, 18, 22_; _John 8:36_), and at last from its very presence (glorification, _1 John 3:2_); the restoration of the image of God in the soul (_Col. 3:10_; _2 Cor. 3:18_), effected by the indwelling Christ (_Col. 1:27_; _Gal. 2:20_), reaching "to the uttermost" (_Heb. 7:25_) — not a legal status only but a Person possessed and a nature renewed.
- _Matt. 1:23._ "they shall call his name Emmanuel, which being interpreted is, God with us" — the paired name gives the method: He saves from sin by being God WITH us — and, the mystery carried to its end, God IN us (_Col. 1:27_); the Person present is the power that saves.
- _Ps. 130:8._ "And he shall redeem Israel from all his iniquities" — the Old Testament promise Matthew 1:21 fulfils: redemption FROM all iniquities, not toleration of them; "all" leaves no reserved sin under which the redeemed remain.
- _EVCO 18.1._ Waggoner: "It is a message of salvation from sin" — the everlasting gospel named in one sentence, grounded on the angel's word to Joseph in Matthew 1:21.

**What sin is, He treats as bondage — and breaks it — John 8:34-36:**

- _John 8:34._ "Whosoever committeth sin is the servant of sin" — Christ's diagnosis: sin is not a debt only but a slavery; the consented transgression (see "What Sin Is — Transgression, Not Inheritance") makes the sinner a slave.
- _John 8:35._ "the servant abideth not in the house for ever: but the Son abideth ever" — the slave has no standing in the house; only the Son can confer a sonship that abides.
- _John 8:36._ "If the Son therefore shall make you free, ye shall be free indeed" — the freedom He gives is not nominal; "indeed" — it actually severs the slavery.
- _DA 466.3._ White: "Christ came to break the shackles of sin-slavery from the soul" — quoting John 8:36 itself; the soul that refuses to give himself to God "may talk of freedom, but he is in the most abject slavery" — the pardon that leaves the chains on is not the freedom Christ gives.

**Made free from sin — guilt and dominion dealt with together — Rom. 6:6, 14, 18, 22:**

- _Rom. 6:6._ our old man is crucified with Him, "that the body of sin might be destroyed, that henceforth we should not serve sin" — the cross's stated purpose in the believer: not forgiven continuance in sin, but the end of sin's service.
- _Rom. 6:14._ "For sin shall not have dominion over you: for ye are not under the law, but under grace" — this is grace's own definition: deliverance from sin's DOMINION; a gospel that leaves men under sin's rule contradicts the very clause that defines being "under grace" (walked in full in "Complete Victory — Overcoming as He Overcame").
- _Rom. 6:18._ "Being then made free from sin, ye became the servants of righteousness" — the section's term verbatim, in the present tense: freedom from sin is a stated fact for the believer, issuing at once in a new servitude — to righteousness.
- _Rom. 6:22._ "But now being made free from sin, and become servants to God, ye have your fruit unto holiness, and the end everlasting life" — the whole arc in one verse: freedom now, holiness as fruit, everlasting life at the end.
- _WOR 71.1._ Waggoner: the blessedness of the man to whom God imputes righteousness is "the blessedness of sins forgiven, and of freedom from the power of sin" — pardon and power are one blessedness, never separated in the gospel.
- _LOF_ATJ 147.4._ Jones, on Rom. 6:18: "Being then made free from sin, YE BECAME THE SERVANTS OF RIGHTEOUSNESS.... He says you are, and when He says you are, it is so.... He has made you so, for He says so" — the deliverance is received on God's word, as fact.
- _LOF_ATJ 85.2._ Jones: the power of God is given for "deliverance from sin and from the power of it, the reign of righteousness in the life" — and he rebukes the truncated gospel: "Many believe and receive the grace of God for the salvation from sins that are past but are content with that and do not give it the same place in the soul to reign against the power of sin."
- _GTI 190.2._ Waggoner: coming to Jesus, the Mediator of the new covenant, "we become free from sin,—from transgression of the law" — with "grace to help us in the hour of temptation" and "cleansing from all sin"; the freedom reaches the very hour of the trial.

**The righteousness of the law fulfilled IN us — God works the obedience — Rom. 8:3-4; Eze. 36:25-27:**

- _Rom. 8:3._ "God sending his own Son in the likeness of sinful flesh, and for sin, condemned sin in the flesh" — in the very flesh He took (see "The Second Adam — The Nature Christ Took"), sin was condemned — dethroned in its own stronghold.
- _Rom. 8:4._ "That the righteousness of the law might be fulfilled in us, who walk not after the flesh, but after the Spirit" — the purpose clause of the incarnation: not imputed righteousness only, but the law's righteousness wrought IN the believer by the Spirit.
- _GTI 199.1._ Waggoner: "And why does He make us free from sin?—In order that we may live free from sin" — Christ, who has power over all flesh, strengthens us by His Spirit "that the righteousness of the law may be fulfilled in us."
- _Eze. 36:25._ "Then will I sprinkle clean water upon you, and ye shall be clean: from all your filthiness, and from all your idols, will I cleanse you" — the covenant promise beneath the whole New Testament: cleansing from ALL filthiness, defilement removed, not managed.
- _Eze. 36:26-27._ "A new heart also will I give you, and a new spirit will I put within you... I will put my spirit within you, and cause you to walk in my statutes" — God Himself works the walking; salvation from sin's power is a divine operation, not a human resolve — the new-birth promise in covenant form (see "Born of Water and of the Spirit — How He Lived, How We Begin").

**The power of it is not a status but a Person — Col. 1:27; Gal. 2:20:**

- _Col. 1:27._ "which is Christ in you, the hope of glory" — the mystery of the gospel is not a ledger entry only but a Person possessed; the indwelling Saviour is Himself the pledge that the work will be finished.
- _Gal. 2:20._ "I am crucified with Christ: nevertheless I live; yet not I, but Christ liveth in me: and the life which I now live in the flesh I live by the faith of the Son of God" — the mechanism of salvation from sin's power: the old self crucified, and an indwelling Life living the new life "in the flesh" by faith — not the man's effort, but Christ's own living.
- _CT 194.1._ White: "When Christ is formed within, the hope of glory, then the truth of God will so act upon the natural temperament that its transforming power will be seen in changed characters" — the authority of God over the man "not in name only, but in reality and truth."
- _EVCO 23.2._ Waggoner: "the power by which Christ saves men from sin is the power by which He created the worlds" — the everlasting gospel preaches Christ "the creative power of God"; salvation from sin is a Creator's act, a re-creation, not a reform.

**What is being restored: the image of God — Col. 3:9-10; 2 Cor. 3:18:**

- _Col. 3:9._ "seeing that ye have put off the old man with his deeds" — the old man is put OFF, deeds and all; salvation does not whitewash him, it removes him.
- _Col. 3:10._ "put on the new man, which is renewed in knowledge after the image of him that created him" — the new man is renewed after the Creator's own image: salvation reverses the fall by re-creating in the soul the likeness that sin marred.
- _2 Cor. 3:18._ "beholding as in a glass the glory of the Lord, are changed into the same image from glory to glory" — the process named: progressive transformation into Christ's image by beholding Him, wrought "as by the Spirit of the Lord" — Colossians 3:10 in motion.
- _DA 37.3._ White: "Jesus came to restore in man the image of his Maker. None but Christ can fashion anew the character that has been ruined by sin" — He came "to reshape the marred character after the pattern of His divine character."
- _Ed 125.2._ White: the central theme of the whole Bible is "the redemption plan, the restoration in the human soul of the image of God" — salvation from sin IS image-restoration; the two are one definition.

**To the uttermost — sins taken away, the devil's works destroyed — Heb. 7:25; 1 John 3:5, 8; Titus 2:14:**

- _Heb. 7:25._ "he is able also to save them to the uttermost that come unto God by him, seeing he ever liveth to make intercession for them" — the section's term verbatim: to the UTTERMOST — completely, to the last extremity of sin's hold; His unbroken priestly life guarantees a salvation that reaches all the way, not a partial rescue.
- _1 John 3:5._ "he was manifested to take away our sins; and in him is no sin" — removal, not mere covering; the sinless One was manifested for the express purpose of taking sin AWAY.
- _1 John 3:8._ "For this purpose the Son of God was manifested, that he might destroy the works of the devil" — sin is the devil's work; a salvation that leaves that work intact in the saved fails the very purpose for which the Son was manifested.
- _Titus 2:14._ "that he might redeem us from all iniquity, and purify unto himself a peculiar people, zealous of good works" — Christ's self-gift has twin objects: redemption from all iniquity and purification of a people; guilt and defilement dealt with together.
- _PCP 48.2._ White: "He takes away our sins, and makes us partakers of His holiness.... He is able to save unto the uttermost all who come unto God by Him" — removal of sin joined to impartation of holiness; the two halves of the uttermost salvation.

**And at last, from sin's presence — a people without spot — 1 John 3:2-3; Eph. 5:25-27:**

- _1 John 3:2._ "now are we the sons of God... when he shall appear, we shall be like him; for we shall see him as he is" — glorification: the last stage of salvation, deliverance from sin's very PRESENCE at His appearing.
- _1 John 3:3._ "And every man that hath this hope in him purifieth himself, even as he is pure" — the future likeness works backward: the hope of being like Him purifies NOW, "even as he is pure."
- _Eph. 5:25-26._ "Christ also loved the church, and gave himself for it; That he might sanctify and cleanse it with the washing of water by the word" — the present tense of His love: sanctifying and cleansing now.
- _Eph. 5:27._ "That he might present it to himself a glorious church, not having spot, or wrinkle, or any such thing; but that it should be holy and without blemish" — the end of the everlasting gospel: a people actually cleansed from sin, not merely accounted so — the church He presents at last (ripened in "The Harvest — A People in Whom Christ Is Vindicated").

**DEFINITION — WHAT SALVATION REALLY IS — SAVED FROM SIN =** salvation is God saving His people FROM their sins (_Matt. 1:21_; _Ps. 130:8_) — from sin's guilt in justification, the pardon of _Matt. 1:21_ ("the blessedness of sins forgiven," _WOR 71.1_), from sin's power and dominion in sanctification ("being then made free from sin," _Rom. 6:14, 18, 22_; "free indeed," _John 8:36_), and at last from sin's very presence in glorification ("we shall be like him," _1 John 3:2_; "not having spot, or wrinkle," _Eph. 5:27_). It is the restoration of the image of God in the soul (_Col. 3:9-10_; _2 Cor. 3:18_; _DA 37.3_; _Ed 125.2_), and its power is not a legal status only but a Person possessed — "Christ in you, the hope of glory" (_Col. 1:27_), the crucified-with-Christ life in which "Christ liveth in me" (_Gal. 2:20_) — the same creative power that made the worlds (_EVCO 23.2_) now working in the believer what God promised in the covenant: "I will... cause you to walk in my statutes" (_Eze. 36:25-27_; _Rom. 8:3-4_). Christ was manifested "to take away our sins" and "destroy the works of the devil" (_1 John 3:5, 8_), and He "is able also to save them to the uttermost" (_Heb. 7:25_) — therefore, as the message that came to the fore at Minneapolis in 1888 and was preached in the years following pressed home (_LOF_ATJ 85.2_; _GTI 199.1_), a gospel that receives grace only for "sins that are past" while leaving sin's power enthroned, a gospel that leaves men under sin's dominion until death, is not the everlasting gospel; it is salvation IN sin, and the angel's word forbids it: He saves His people FROM their sins.

**Symbols defined here:**

- **salvation** = God saving man FROM sin — from its guilt (justification), from its power and dominion (sanctification), and at last from its very presence (glorification); the restoration of the image of God in the soul by the indwelling Christ — not a legal status only but a Person possessed and a nature renewed — reaching to the uttermost (_Matt. 1:21_; _Ps. 130:8_; _Rom. 6:18, 22_; _John 8:36_; _Col. 3:10_; _2 Cor. 3:18_; _Col. 1:27_; _Gal. 2:20_; _Heb. 7:25_; _1 John 3:2-3, 5, 8_; _Titus 2:14_).

**Symbols carried:** sin = the consented transgression of the law, the slavery here broken (see "What Sin Is — Transgression, Not Inheritance"); the likeness of sinful flesh in which sin was condemned = the nature Christ took (see "The Second Adam — The Nature Christ Took"); the new heart and the washing = the new birth by water and the Spirit (see "Born of Water and of the Spirit — How He Lived, How We Begin"); the faith by which Christ lives in the believer is walked in full in "Righteousness by Faith — The Third Angel’s Message in Verity," and the dominion broken here is proved complete in "Complete Victory — Overcoming as He Overcame."

1. _Matt. 1:21_ says He saves His people "from their sins," and Jones warns of those who "receive the grace of God for the salvation from sins that are past but are content with that." Where in your own life have you quietly settled for pardon without deliverance — and what would it mean this week to claim _Rom. 6:14_ as a promise rather than an ideal?
2. _Rom. 6:14_ makes freedom from sin's dominion the very definition of being "under grace." Is there a "reserved sin" you have quietly accepted as permanent — and what does "I will... cause you to walk in my statutes" (_Eze. 36:27_) promise about that very thing?
3. _Col. 1:27_ and _Gal. 2:20_ make salvation a Person possessed, not a status only. In the next real temptation, what is the practical difference between trusting a changed record in heaven and trusting Christ living in you at that moment?

## 6. Righteousness by Faith — The Third Angel’s Message in Verity

> The remedy for sin is a righteousness not our own — its very name is THE LORD OUR RIGHTEOUSNESS: Christ's obedience COUNTED to the believer for his title, and Christ Himself LIVING in the believer for his fitness — one gift, received and lived by faith alone; and this, said the messenger of the Lord, is the third angel's message in verity.

**The righteousness of God, without the law, upon all them that believe — Rom. 3:21-28; 5:1:**

- _Rom. 3:21._ "But now the righteousness of God without the law is manifested, being witnessed by the law and the prophets" — the remedy is not a better law-keeping manufactured by man but a righteousness GOD supplies; the law and the prophets witnessed to it all along.
  - **righteousness by faith** = Christ's own righteousness given to the believer in two inseparable modes: IMPUTED — His obedience counted to the one who believes, the TITLE (_Rom. 3:24-28_; _Rom. 4:3-5_); and IMPARTED — the same Christ living in the believer by faith, the FITNESS (_Gal. 2:20_; _Rom. 8:3-4_; _1 Cor. 1:30_). Defined in full at the close.
- _Rom. 3:22._ "Even the righteousness of God which is by faith of Jesus Christ unto all and upon all them that believe: for there is no difference" — "upon all": a robe placed on the believer from outside himself, without distinction of persons.
- _Rom. 3:24._ "Being justified freely by his grace through the redemption that is in Christ Jesus" — FREELY: grace is the spring, the redemption in Christ the channel; nothing of the sinner's enters the price.
- _Rom. 3:25._ "Whom God hath set forth to be a propitiation through faith in his blood, to declare his righteousness for the remission of sins that are past" — the blood, laid hold of by faith, remits sins; and the remitting DECLARES God's righteousness rather than compromising it.
- _Rom. 3:26._ "that he might be just, and the justifier of him which believeth in Jesus" — the wonder of the gospel in one clause: God remains JUST — the law unbent — in the very act of justifying the believing sinner; mercy does not bend the law whose transgression is sin (see "What Sin Is — Transgression, Not Inheritance").
- _Rom. 3:27._ "Where is boasting then? It is excluded. By what law? of works? Nay: but by the law of faith" — no flesh glories in His presence; the ground of acceptance is faith, not performance.
- _Rom. 3:28._ "Therefore we conclude that a man is justified by faith without the deeds of the law" — the apostolic conclusion stated as doctrine: justification is by faith, wholly apart from the deeds of the law.
- _Rom. 5:1._ "Therefore being justified by faith, we have peace with God through our Lord Jesus Christ" — the immediate fruit: a settled standing, peace that rests on Another's righteousness and therefore cannot be shaken by our own record.
- _FLB 107.2._ White: "The grace of Christ is freely to justify the sinner without merit or claim on his part. Justification is a full, complete pardon of sin. The moment a sinner accepts Christ by faith, that moment he is pardoned. The righteousness of Christ is imputed to him, and he is no more to doubt God's forgiving grace." — justification defined: full pardon, imputed righteousness, received the moment faith accepts, "without merit or claim" on the sinner's part.
- _FW 103.2._ White: "Pardon and justification are one and the same thing. Through faith, the believer passes from the position of a rebel, a child of sin and Satan, to the position of a loyal subject of Christ Jesus, not because of an inherent goodness, but because Christ receives him as His child by adoption." — the rebel changes standing on Christ's account, not his own; no inherent goodness enters the transaction.

**Counted for righteousness — the imputed title — Gen. 15:6; Rom. 4:3-5, 22-25:**

- _Gen. 15:6._ "And he believed in the LORD; and he counted it to him for righteousness." — the gospel's original arithmetic, laid down in Moses: faith COUNTED for righteousness; justification by faith is no New Testament innovation but the covenant from the beginning.
  - **imputed for righteousness / counted / reckoned** = to credit to the believer's account a righteousness he did not earn — the legal ground of the believer's title to heaven (_Gen. 15:6_; _Rom. 4:3-5_; _Rom. 4:22-25_; _2 Cor. 5:21_; _Phil. 3:9_).
- _Rom. 4:3._ "For what saith the scripture? Abraham believed God, and it was counted unto him for righteousness." — Paul rests his whole case on "what saith the scripture"; the doctrine is as old as Abraham.
- _Rom. 4:4._ "Now to him that worketh is the reward not reckoned of grace, but of debt." — earnings are wages, and wages are debt; God is made debtor to no man — so a righteousness earned would be no gift at all.
- _Rom. 4:5._ "But to him that worketh not, but believeth on him that justifieth the ungodly, his faith is counted for righteousness." — the scandal and the glory of the gospel: God justifies the UNGODLY who believe; the working stops, and the counting begins.
- _Rom. 4:22-23._ "And therefore it was imputed to him for righteousness. Now it was not written for his sake alone, that it was imputed to him" — Abraham's ledger is a specimen page written for the world, not a private privilege.
- _Rom. 4:24._ "But for us also, to whom it shall be imputed, if we believe on him that raised up Jesus our Lord from the dead" — the same crediting, on the same terms, to every believer now.
- _Rom. 4:25._ "Who was delivered for our offences, and was raised again for our justification." — the ground of the imputed title is not a fiction but the delivered-up and risen Christ Himself.
- _FW 18.1._ White: "The point that has been urged upon my mind for years is the imputed righteousness of Christ... I have made it the subject of nearly every discourse and talk that I have given to the people." — imputed righteousness is the Heaven-urged burden of the message, not a peripheral point.
- _CIHS 48.2._ White: "The incense, ascending with the prayers of Israel, represents the merits and intercession of Christ, His perfect righteousness, which through faith is imputed to His people, and which can alone make the worship of sinful beings acceptable to God." — the sanctuary preaches the same doctrine: imputed righteousness ALONE makes a sinner's worship acceptable.

**The righteousness is a Person — Jer. 23:6; 1 Cor. 1:30; Phil. 3:9; 2 Cor. 5:21:**

- _Jer. 23:6._ "In his days Judah shall be saved... and this is his name whereby he shall be called, THE LORD OUR RIGHTEOUSNESS." — the Messiah's own name settles the question forever: our righteousness is not a quality we manufacture but a Person we receive.
- _1 Cor. 1:30._ "But of him are ye in Christ Jesus, who of God is made unto us wisdom, and righteousness, and sanctification, and redemption" — the same Christ is MADE UNTO US both righteousness (the imputed title) and sanctification (the imparted fitness): one Person supplies both, and there is no righteousness of any kind apart from being "in Christ Jesus."
- _Phil. 3:9._ "And be found in him, not having mine own righteousness, which is of the law, but that which is through the faith of Christ, the righteousness which is of God by faith" — Paul the Pharisee throws his own law-righteousness away to be "found in him," wearing a righteousness whose whole origin is God.
- _2 Cor. 5:21._ "For he hath made him to be sin for us, who knew no sin; that we might be made the righteousness of God in him." — the great exchange, imputation in its sharpest form: our sin laid on the Sinless One, that His righteousness be MADE ours "in him."
- _DA 300.1._ White: "The proud heart strives to earn salvation; but both our title to heaven and our fitness for it are found in the righteousness of Christ." — the exact frame of this section: TITLE (imputed) and FITNESS (imparted), both found in His righteousness, neither self-earned.
- _CHR 62.1._ Waggoner: "At last the sinner, weary of the vain struggle to get righteousness from the law, listens to the voice of Christ and flees to His outstretched arms. Hiding in Christ, he is covered with His righteousness... With the righteousness which is 'through the faith of Christ, the righteousness which is of God by faith' (Philippians 3:9), Paul was sure that he would stand secure in the day of Christ." — the 1888 messenger on _Phil. 3:9_: the vain striving ends where the covering begins, and the covered man possesses the very righteousness the law requires.

**The just shall live by faith — Hab. 2:4; Rom. 1:16-17:**

- _Hab. 2:4._ "Behold, his soul which is lifted up is not upright in him: but the just shall live by his faith." — the two ways set side by side: the self-lifted soul is not upright in him; the just man LIVES — from justification onward — by his faith.
  - **the just shall live by faith** = the believer enters righteousness by faith and continues the entire Christian life by that same faith — total dependence on the word of God; the just man lives by precisely the principle by which he became just (_Hab. 2:4_; _Rom. 1:17_; _Gen. 15:6_; _Rom. 4:3-5_).
- _Rom. 1:16._ "For I am not ashamed of the gospel of Christ: for it is the power of God unto salvation to every one that believeth" — the gospel is not advice but POWER, God's own power, unto salvation — the deliverance from sin walked in "What Salvation Really Is — Saved From Sin."
- _Rom. 1:17._ "For therein is the righteousness of God revealed from faith to faith: as it is written, The just shall live by faith." — the righteousness revealed is GOD'S, not man's, and it is received "from faith to faith" — faith at the gate and faith the whole way; the banner text of the Reformation and of 1888.
- _LOF_ATJ 38.8._ Jones: "Men must not only become just by faith—by dependence upon the word of God—but being just, we must live by faith. The just man lives in precisely the same way and by precisely the same thing that he becomes just." — righteousness by faith is continuous, not a one-time transaction followed by law-works.
- _LOF_ATJ 38.9._ Jones: "We become just by faith; faith is entire dependence on the word of God. We, being just, must live by precisely the same thing by which we become just; that is, by entire dependence upon the word of God." — the faith that justifies and the faith that sanctifies are one faith, and Jones defines it: entire dependence on the word of God.

**Imparted — Christ living in the believer — Gal. 2:20; Rom. 8:3-4; Gal. 5:6:**

- _Gal. 2:20._ "I am crucified with Christ: nevertheless I live; yet not I, but Christ liveth in me: and the life which I now live in the flesh I live by the faith of the Son of God, who loved me, and gave himself for me." — the formula of imparted righteousness: the old "I" crucified, and the life now lived is Christ's own life within — the surrendered life entered at the new birth (see "Born of Water and of the Spirit — How He Lived, How We Begin").
- _Rom. 8:3._ "For what the law could not do, in that it was weak through the flesh, God sending his own Son in the likeness of sinful flesh, and for sin, condemned sin in the flesh" — what the law could not do in flesh it was weak through, God did IN that very flesh: the Son came into the nature where sin had reigned and condemned sin there (see "The Second Adam — The Nature Christ Took").
- _Rom. 8:4._ "That the righteousness of the law might be fulfilled in us, who walk not after the flesh, but after the Spirit." — the stated purpose of the whole remedy: the law's righteousness fulfilled IN us — imputed righteousness never stops short of imparted obedience; real obedience is the design, never an option.
- _Gal. 5:6._ "For in Jesus Christ neither circumcision availeth any thing, nor uncircumcision; but faith which worketh by love." — the faith that justifies is never a dead assent: it WORKS, and works by love — the imparted life is the living activity of the very faith that received the title.

**Works — excluded as the root, required as the fruit — Eph. 2:8-10; James 2:17-18:**

- _Eph. 2:8._ "For by grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God" — salvation whole is the gift of God: grace the source, faith the receiving hand, and even that not of ourselves.
- _Eph. 2:9._ "Not of works, lest any man should boast." — works shut out at the root, absolutely; boasting is left no ground to stand on.
- _Eph. 2:10._ "For we are his workmanship, created in Christ Jesus unto good works, which God hath before ordained that we should walk in them." — the same breath that shuts works out as the root ordains them as the fruit: the saved man is God's WORKMANSHIP, created unto good works — this is the balance the whole section owns.
- _James 2:17._ "Even so faith, if it hath not works, is dead, being alone." — the guard against the counterfeit: a workless "faith" is a corpse, and a corpse justifies no one.
- _James 2:18._ "shew me thy faith without thy works, and I will shew thee my faith by my works" — works are faith's only visible evidence — the proof of the living faith, never its purchase price.
- _AA 563.1._ White: "John did not teach that salvation was to be earned by obedience; but that obedience was the fruit of faith and love... The sanctified heart is in harmony with the precepts of God's law." — the balance in one line: obedience the fruit, never the root — and the sanctified heart really does harmonize with the law.
- _COL 312.2._ White: "Righteousness is right doing, and it is by their deeds that all will be judged. Our characters are revealed by what we do. The works show whether the faith is genuine." — the judgment reads the works because the works read the faith.

**The message identified — Minneapolis, 1888, and the verity — 1SM 372:**

- _1SM 372.2._ White: "Several have written to me, inquiring if the message of justification by faith is the third angel's message, and I have answered, 'It is the third angel's message, in verity.'" — the definitive identification (The Review and Herald, April 1, 1890): the message of justification by faith proclaimed by E. J. Waggoner and A. T. Jones at the 1888 Minneapolis General Conference is not a theme beside the third angel's message but that message itself, in verity — the gospel content of the third angel, whose fruit is walked in "The Commandments of God and the Faith of Jesus."
- _1SM 372.1._ White: "there is no danger in presenting this doctrine as it is set forth in the Scriptures. If there had not been a remissness in the past to properly instruct the people of God, there would not now be a necessity of calling a special attention to it." — righteousness by faith is to be preached fully and fearlessly, "as it is set forth in the Scriptures"; the "special attention" was owed to past neglect, not to novelty.

**DEFINITION — RIGHTEOUSNESS BY FAITH — THE THIRD ANGEL’S MESSAGE IN VERITY =** Christ's own righteousness given to the believer in two inseparable modes. IMPUTED: his faith is "counted... for righteousness" (_Gen. 15:6_; _Rom. 4:3-5_), written "for us also, to whom it shall be imputed" (_Rom. 4:22-25_) — the believer is "justified freely by his grace" through faith in Christ's blood, "without the deeds of the law," so that God is at once "just, and the justifier" (_Rom. 3:24-28_); the sinless One was "made... sin for us... that we might be made the righteousness of God in him" (_2 Cor. 5:21_); this is the believer's TITLE, and its fruit is peace with God (_Rom. 5:1_; _FLB 107.2_; _FW 103.2_). IMPARTED: the same Christ made unto us "righteousness, and sanctification" (_1 Cor. 1:30_) lives His own life in the believer — "not I, but Christ liveth in me" (_Gal. 2:20_) — fulfilling the righteousness of the law IN us in the very flesh where He condemned sin (_Rom. 8:3-4_; see "The Second Adam — The Nature Christ Took"), through "faith which worketh by love" (_Gal. 5:6_); this is the believer's FITNESS, and "both our title to heaven and our fitness for it are found in the righteousness of Christ" (_DA 300.1_). It is "the righteousness of God," not man's (_Rom. 1:17_; _3:21-22_; _Phil. 3:9_); its very Name is THE LORD OUR RIGHTEOUSNESS (_Jer. 23:6_); it is entered by faith and lived by that same faith — entire dependence on the word of God (_Hab. 2:4_; _LOF_ATJ 38.8-9_) — and its works are excluded as the root of salvation and required as its fruit (_Eph. 2:8-10_; _James 2:17-18_; _AA 563.1_; _COL 312.2_). This message, carried by Waggoner and Jones from Minneapolis in 1888, is "the third angel's message, in verity" (_1SM 372.2_): the remedy that saves FROM sin (see "What Salvation Really Is — Saved From Sin"), provides the complete victory walked in "Complete Victory — Overcoming as He Overcame," and ripens the commandment-keeping people of "The Commandments of God and the Faith of Jesus."

**Symbols defined here:**

- **righteousness by faith** = Christ's own righteousness given to the believer in two inseparable modes: IMPUTED — His obedience counted to the believer, the title (_Gen. 15:6_; _Rom. 3:24-28_; _Rom. 4:3-5, 22-25_; _2 Cor. 5:21_; _Phil. 3:9_); and IMPARTED — Christ living in the believer by faith, fulfilling the law's righteousness in him, the fitness (_Rom. 8:3-4_; _Gal. 2:20_; _Gal. 5:6_; _1 Cor. 1:30_; _DA 300.1_). Its Name is THE LORD OUR RIGHTEOUSNESS (_Jer. 23:6_); obedience is its fruit, never its root, and never optional (_Eph. 2:8-10_; _James 2:17-18_).
- **imputed for righteousness / counted / reckoned** = to credit to the believer's account a righteousness he did not earn — faith "counted... for righteousness" to him "that worketh not, but believeth on him that justifieth the ungodly"; the legal ground of the believer's title (_Gen. 15:6_; _Rom. 4:3-5_; _Rom. 4:22-25_; _2 Cor. 5:21_; _FW 18.1_).
- **the just shall live by faith** = the believer becomes just by faith and continues the whole Christian life by that same faith — entire dependence on the word of God; self-lifted works excluded from first to last (_Hab. 2:4_; _Rom. 1:16-17_; _LOF_ATJ 38.8-9_).

**Symbols carried:** sin = consented transgression of the law — the thing pardoned in justification and dethroned by the indwelling Christ (see "What Sin Is — Transgression, Not Inheritance"); sinful flesh and the second Adam — the very nature in which the Son came and "condemned sin in the flesh" (_Rom. 8:3_; see "The Second Adam — The Nature Christ Took"); the new birth — the entrance into the "not I, but Christ" life (_Gal. 2:20_; see "Born of Water and of the Spirit — How He Lived, How We Begin"); salvation = God saving His people FROM their sins, the deliverance this righteousness works (see "What Salvation Really Is — Saved From Sin"). The victory this faith provides is walked in "Complete Victory — Overcoming as He Overcame," and the people it ripens in "The Commandments of God and the Faith of Jesus."

## 7. Complete Victory — Overcoming as He Overcame

> Must the believer always fall short, sinning and repenting till death? Scripture answers with a charter, not a wish: sin SHALL NOT have dominion; whatsoever is born of God overcometh; God is able to keep you from falling — the same victory Christ won, by the same faith, in the same flesh, "even as I also overcame."

**The charter promise — sin dethroned — Rom. 6:6, 14, 22:**

- _Rom. 6:14._ "For sin shall not have dominion over you: for ye are not under the law, but under grace." — the charter of this whole section: sin SHALL NOT rule the believer, stated as a future certainty, not a wish; grace, not law-under-condemnation, is the ground of the deliverance.
- _Rom. 6:6._ "our old man is crucified with him, that the body of sin might be destroyed, that henceforth we should not serve sin" — the mechanism of the promise: union with the crucified Christ, not native effort; the body of sin destroyed so that we "should not serve sin" — "not I, but Christ liveth in me" (_Gal. 2:20_; see "Righteousness by Faith — The Third Angel’s Message in Verity").
- _Rom. 6:22._ "But now being made free from sin, and become servants to God, ye have your fruit unto holiness, and the end everlasting life" — the freedom is present and actual, bearing fruit unto holiness; the gospel result is genuine liberty from sin's service, not managed failure (see "What Salvation Really Is — Saved From Sin").
- _WOR 110.3._ Waggoner, reading the whole of the fourteenth verse: "Sin has no dominion over those who yield themselves servants to righteousness... transgression of the law has no place in them who are not under the law" — sin's dominion is actually broken, not re-labeled.
- _WOR 198.5._ Waggoner meets the objection of this section head-on: "A questioner may say, 'You make it out that we ought never to sin any more, - you leave no room for sin.' But is not that what the Bible says?... There is such a thing as a complete surrender to Christ, - when we give up everything, and then trust to his power to keep us in that state. And I thank God that he is able to do it." — the exact question of this section, answered from Rom. 6:14 itself: no, we ought never to sin any more, and Christ is able to keep the fully surrendered.

**Born of God — the life that does not practise sin — 1 John 3:8-9; 5:4, 18:**

- _1 John 3:8._ "For this purpose the Son of God was manifested, that he might destroy the works of the devil." — the incarnation's stated purpose is the undoing of sin in the believer, not its accommodation; a gospel that leaves sin reigning defeats the very errand of Bethlehem.
- _1 John 3:9._ "Whosoever is born of God doth not commit sin; for his seed remaineth in him: and he cannot sin, because he is born of God." — while the divine seed remains, the born-of-God life does not practise sin — not a sinlessness native to the flesh apart from Christ, but the new-birth life that cannot sin so long as it abides in Him (see "Born of Water and of the Spirit — How He Lived, How We Begin").
- _1 John 5:4._ "For whatsoever is born of God overcometh the world: and this is the victory that overcometh the world, even our faith." — the overcomer defined, and the means named in the same breath: faith — the identical faith by which Christ Himself overcame.
  - **the overcomer** = one in whom the born-of-God life triumphs over sin and the world — not by native strength but by Christ living out in fallen flesh the very victory He Himself won (_1 John 5:4_, "even our faith"; _1 John 5:18_, "keepeth himself, and that wicked one toucheth him not"; _Rev. 3:21_, "even as I also overcame"; _Gal. 2:20_, "not I, but Christ liveth in me"; _Rom. 6:14, 22_) — the normal Christian of Revelation's promises, "made free from sin," over whom sin "shall not have dominion."
- _1 John 5:18._ "whosoever is born of God sinneth not; but he that is begotten of God keepeth himself, and that wicked one toucheth him not" — kept, guarded, untouched; the overcomer's security is real, not aspirational.
- _CWCP 50.1._ Jones: "That faith which He exercised and by which He obtained the victory over the world, the flesh, and the devil—that faith is His free gift to every lost man in the world. And thus 'this is the victory that overcometh the world, even our faith.'" — the faith Christ exercised, by which He "trusted God for salvation from sin and power to keep from sinning," is His free gift to every lost man: the overcomer overcomes by Christ's own victory-faith, not by manufacture but by donation.

**Provision complete — no temptation compels sin — Jude 24; 1 Cor. 10:13; 2 Cor. 2:14:**

- _Jude 24._ "Now unto him that is able to keep you from falling, and to present you faultless before the presence of his glory with exceeding joy" — the keeping power and the faultless standing are both His work; falling is never made necessary.
- _1 Cor. 10:13._ "God is faithful, who will not suffer you to be tempted above that ye are able; but will with the temptation also make a way to escape" — with EVERY temptation a guaranteed exit: no temptation ever compels sin; sin is therefore always avoidable, and every fall is a way of escape not taken.
- _2 Cor. 2:14._ "Now thanks be unto God, which always causeth us to triumph in Christ" — ALWAYS, with no exception clause for necessary defeat; triumph, not managed failure, is the standing gospel norm.
- _UL 255.4._ White: "a provision has been made whereby we may overcome.... He who truly believes in Christ is made partaker of the divine nature, and has power that he can appropriate under every temptation. He will not fall under temptation or be left to defeat." — the provision covers EVERY temptation; "he will not fall."
- _RH January 14, 1909, par. 6._ White, a second witness: "In time of trial he will claim the promises, and by these escape the corruptions that are in the world through lust." — the way of escape of _1 Cor. 10:13_ is taken by claiming the promises in the moment of trial.

**Grace sufficient — strength perfected in the very weakness — 2 Cor. 12:9; Phil. 4:13:**

- _2 Cor. 12:9._ "My grace is sufficient for thee: for my strength is made perfect in weakness." — provision matched precisely to weakness; His strength is perfected exactly where we are weakest, so no infirmity is an excuse for sin.
- _Phil. 4:13._ "I can do all things through Christ which strengtheneth me." — "all things": nothing God requires lies outside the enabling of the indwelling Christ.
- _MH 482.3._ White: "Said the tried apostle Paul: 'He said unto me, My grace is sufficient for thee: for My strength is made perfect in weakness.... for when I am weak, then am I strong.'" — the very infirmities that seem to excuse defeat are where the power of Christ rests.
- _SC 47.1._ White: "Everything depends on the right action of the will.... You can give Him your will; He will then work in you to will and to do according to His good pleasure. Thus your whole nature will be brought under the control of the Spirit of Christ." — where the provision is appropriated: the believer yields the will, God works the doing; the choice to serve God is always available, so no known sin need be retained.

**Even as I also overcame — the same victory, in the same flesh — Heb. 4:15; Rev. 3:21; 2:7; 3:12:**

- _Heb. 4:15._ "was in all points tempted like as we are, yet without sin" — Christ met temptation on our ground, in our infirmities, and did not fall: proof that being tempted never makes sinning inevitable (the flesh He met it in: see "The Second Adam — The Nature Christ Took").
- _Rev. 3:21._ "To him that overcometh will I grant to sit with me in my throne, even as I also overcame" — the capstone: the SAME victory, held to the same standard, by the same faith, in the same flesh; Christ's overcoming is the pattern, not an exception.
- _Rev. 2:7._ "To him that overcometh will I give to eat of the tree of life" — every church-letter reward is conditioned "to him that overcometh"; the overcomer is the assumed normal Christian, and Eden restored is his inheritance.
- _Rev. 3:12._ "Him that overcometh will I make a pillar in the temple of my God, and he shall go no more out" — the overcomer's final settled security; the reiterated promise fixes overcoming as the condition Christ everywhere expects and enables.
- _3SM 139.3._ White guards the "even as" from every escape-hatch: "If Christ had a special power which it is not the privilege of man to have, Satan would have made capital of this matter.... a man, tempted as a man, rendering the obedience of a man." — no advantage unavailable to us; the victory is genuinely reproducible.
- _ST January 8, 1902, par. 11._ White: "He asked and received power to enable Him to overcome in the conflict. And those who walk in God's way may have the same power.... As He overcame, so we may overcome." — same power, same victory; and He commands "Be ye therefore perfect" because "He knows that it is possible" — the mark is not lowered.
- _3SM 195.3._ White answers the section's question in so many words: "Shall we keep on sinning as though it were an impossibility for us to overcome? How are we to overcome? As Christ overcame, and that is the only way.... I will cooperate with divine power and through grace be conqueror." — no, we need not keep on sinning; the will, refusing surrender to Satan, co-operates with divine power.
- _CH 574.1._ White marks the cost: "It is only through obedience and continual effort that we shall overcome as Christ overcame." — the victory is real and reproducible, but not automatic; many desire the overcomer's reward who are not willing to endure the overcomer's toil, self-denial, and watching.

**The mark never lowered — the Advocate for a fall that is possible, never necessary — 1 John 2:1; Matt. 1:21; Titus 2:14:**

- _1 John 2:1._ "these things write I unto you, that ye sin not. And if any man sin, we have an advocate with the Father" — the aim stated plainly: "that ye sin not"; the Advocate is the provision for the one who DOES fall, but the "if" guards the mark — falling is possible, never necessary, and the standard is never lowered to managed failure.
- _Matt. 1:21._ "thou shalt call his name JESUS: for he shall save his people from their sins" — His very name is the errand: salvation FROM sins, not accommodation in them; a gospel of managed failure contradicts the name Jesus (see "What Salvation Really Is — Saved From Sin").
- _Titus 2:14._ "that he might redeem us from all iniquity, and purify unto himself a peculiar people, zealous of good works" — ALL iniquity: the redemption's design is complete cleansing, not partial; no known sin need be retained.
- _COL 69.1._ White: "Christ is waiting with longing desire for the manifestation of Himself in His church. When the character of Christ shall be perfectly reproduced in His people, then He will come to claim them as His own." — complete victory — Christ's own character in His people — is not an unreachable ideal but the very thing heaven waits for (ripened in "The Harvest — A People in Whom Christ Is Vindicated").

**DEFINITION — COMPLETE VICTORY — OVERCOMING AS HE OVERCAME =** the answer of Scripture to "must we always fall short?" is a charter of promises with no exception clause: sin "shall not have dominion" (_Rom. 6:14_) because the old man is crucified with Christ and the body of sin destroyed (_Rom. 6:6_), leaving the believer "made free from sin" (_Rom. 6:22_) — and Waggoner presses the plain sense: we ought never to sin any more, for Christ is able to keep the fully surrendered (_WOR 198.5_; _WOR 110.3_). The Son of God was manifested to DESTROY the works of the devil (_1 John 3:8_), and the born-of-God life does not practise sin while the seed remains (_1 John 3:9_; _5:18_); the overcomer overcomes by "even our faith" (_1 John 5:4_) — the identical victory-faith by which Christ Himself overcame, given free to every man (_CWCP 50.1_). The provision is complete on every side: able to keep from falling (_Jude 24_), a way of escape with every temptation (_1 Cor. 10:13_), always caused to triumph (_2 Cor. 2:14_), grace sufficient and strength perfected in the very weakness we would plead as excuse (_2 Cor. 12:9_; _Phil. 4:13_; _MH 482.3_) — so no temptation compels sin, no infirmity excuses it, no known sin need be retained (_Titus 2:14_), and the believer "will not fall under temptation or be left to defeat" (_UL 255.4_; _RH January 14, 1909_). The means is faith, appropriated at the one point man controls — the will (_SC 47.1_; _3SM 195.3_). The standard is Christ's own overcoming, "even as I also overcame" (_Rev. 3:21_), tempted in all points yet without sin, in the same flesh (_Heb. 4:15_; see "The Second Adam — The Nature Christ Took"), by no special power unavailable to us (_3SM 139.3_; _ST January 8, 1902_), through obedience and continual effort (_CH 574.1_) — and it is the assumed condition of every church-letter promise (_Rev. 2:7_; _3:12_). The mark is never lowered: "that ye sin not," with an Advocate if any man sin (_1 John 2:1_) — the fall possible, never necessary — for His name pledges salvation from sins (_Matt. 1:21_), His self-gift redeems from ALL iniquity (_Titus 2:14_), and He waits for His character to be perfectly reproduced in His people (_COL 69.1_).

**Symbols defined here:**

- **the overcomer** = one in whom the born-of-God life triumphs over sin and the world — not by native strength but by Christ living out in fallen flesh the very victory He Himself won; the same faith (_1 John 5:4_), the same keeping (_1 John 5:18_), the same victory (_Rev. 3:21_, "even as I also overcame"; _Gal. 2:20_) — the normal Christian assumed by every church-letter promise (_Rev. 2:7_; _3:12_), "made free from sin" (_Rom. 6:22_) under the charter "sin shall not have dominion" (_Rom. 6:14_).

**Symbols carried:** sin = the consented transgression of the law — the dominion here dethroned (see "What Sin Is — Transgression, Not Inheritance"); the flesh / fallen nature — the weakness in which grace is made perfect, enslaving only until Christ delivers (see "What Sin Is — Transgression, Not Inheritance"); the second Adam and sinful flesh — the nature in which the pattern-victory was won and in which we overcome (see "The Second Adam — The Nature Christ Took"); the new birth — the "born of God" life of 1 John, by which the overcoming life is entered (see "Born of Water and of the Spirit — How He Lived, How We Begin"); salvation = saved FROM sin (see "What Salvation Really Is — Saved From Sin"); righteousness by faith — the faith that justifies is the same faith that overcomes, and this victory is its full fruit (see "Righteousness by Faith — The Third Angel’s Message in Verity"); the faith of Jesus, defined in full at "The Commandments of God and the Faith of Jesus."

**For discussion:**

1. Romans 6:14 is worded as a promise — "sin SHALL NOT have dominion over you" — not a command to try harder. Do we secretly read it as "sin should not"? What changes in the fight with a besetting sin when deliverance is trusted as God's pledged work through union with the crucified Christ (Rom. 6:6; 1 John 5:4) rather than attempted by our unaided resolve?
2. 1 Corinthians 10:13 guarantees "with the temptation also a way to escape." Recall the last temptation you fell under: was there truly no escape — or an escape unused? In light of SC 47.1 — "you can give Him your will; He will then work in you" — what would "claiming the promises" (RH January 14, 1909) and giving God the will have looked like at that exact moment?
3. Revelation 3:21 promises the throne "even as I also overcame," and 3SM 139.3 insists Christ had no special power unavailable to us. What excuse for a cherished sin does that leave — and what comfort does 1 John 2:1 add for the one who has fallen, without either truth lowering the mark "that ye sin not"?

# Part IV — The Last Generation

## 8. The Commandments of God and the Faith of Jesus

> The three angels' messages are the everlasting gospel in judgment-hour form, and they end by describing the people they produce — a patient company keeping BOTH the commandments of God AND the faith of Jesus: law and gospel held together, ripened to stand.

**The everlasting gospel preached in the hour of judgment — Rev. 14:6-7; Rom. 1:16-17:**

- _Rev. 14:6._ "And I saw another angel fly in the midst of heaven, having the everlasting gospel to preach unto them that dwell on the earth, and to every nation, and kindred, and tongue, and people" — not a new gospel but the one eternal good news, borne worldwide in the last hour; whatever the three messages are, they are gospel first.
- _Rev. 14:7._ "Fear God, and give glory to him; for the hour of his judgment is come: and worship him that made heaven, and earth, and the sea, and the fountains of waters" — the gospel preached in a judgment-hour setting: fear God, give Him glory, worship the Creator — present truth because the hour of His judgment IS come.
- _17LtMs, Lt 183, 1902, par. 4._ White: "The subject of the first angel's message is the everlasting gospel" — the gospel is the substance the judgment-hour messages carry; take the gospel out and no message remains.
- _Rom. 1:16._ "For I am not ashamed of the gospel of Christ: for it is the power of God unto salvation to every one that believeth" — the everlasting gospel of _Rev. 14:6_ is POWER, not mere information; it actually saves the believer it finds.
- _Rom. 1:17._ "For therein is the righteousness of God revealed from faith to faith: as it is written, The just shall live by faith." — the gospel reveals a righteousness of God received by faith; the message of Revelation 14 carries righteousness by faith (see "Righteousness by Faith — The Third Angel's Message in Verity").

**The judgment-hour call is the whole duty of man — Eccl. 12:13-14; Dan. 7:10:**

- _Eccl. 12:13._ "Let us hear the conclusion of the whole matter: Fear God, and keep his commandments: for this is the whole duty of man" — the identical twofold call the first and third angels sound (_Rev. 14:7, 12_); the message is not a novelty but the conclusion of the whole matter.
- _Eccl. 12:14._ "For God shall bring every work into judgment, with every secret thing, whether it be good, or whether it be evil" — Solomon's reason is the angel's reason: the duty is pressed because the judgment comes (_Rev. 14:7_).
- _Dan. 7:10._ "thousand thousands ministered unto him, and ten thousand times ten thousand stood before him: the judgment was set, and the books were opened" — the heavenly courtroom whose hour has come (_Rev. 14:7_); the open books make the message urgent, for a people must be ripened who can stand there (see "The Time of Trouble — Standing Without a Mediator").

**The third angel — two worships sorted, and a people described — Rev. 14:9, 12:**

- _Rev. 14:9._ "If any man worship the beast and his image, and receive his mark in his forehead, or in his hand" — the third angel's warning against false worship stands over against the true worship of v. 7; two loyalties are being sorted out in the judgment hour.
  - **the third angel's message** = the everlasting gospel (_Rev. 14:6_) in its judgment-hour form: righteousness by faith producing a commandment-keeping people — not law apart from gospel, for the message ends by describing the people it makes, who keep BOTH "the commandments of God, and the faith of Jesus" (_Rev. 14:9-12_; _Rom. 1:16-17_; _Eccl. 12:13-14_; _Dan. 7:10_).
- _1SM 372.2._ White: "Several have written to me, inquiring if the message of justification by faith is the third angel's message, and I have answered, 'It is the third angel's message, in verity.'" — the keystone, her 1890 answer: Rev. 14:9-12 is gospel, the very righteousness by faith carried from "Righteousness by Faith — The Third Angel's Message in Verity."
- _Rev. 14:12._ "Here is the patience of the saints: here are they that keep the commandments of God, and the faith of Jesus." — the three messages END in a people: patient, commandment-keeping, holding the faith of Jesus — law and gospel together, neither alone.
  - **the faith of Jesus** = not merely faith IN Jesus (though it includes trust in His ability to save fully) but the faith OF Jesus — the very faith by which Christ Himself overcame, now living in the saints (_Rev. 3:21_, "even as I also overcame"; _Heb. 12:2_, "the author and finisher of our faith"; _Gal. 2:20_, "the faith of the Son of God"; _1 John 5:4_) — the gospel half of the message that makes the law half possible.
- _CIHS 167.1._ White: "The result of an acceptance of these messages is given in the word: 'Here are they that keep the commandments of God, and the faith of Jesus.' In order to be prepared for the judgment, it is necessary that men should keep the law of God. That law will be the standard of character in the judgment" — the messages are measured by their result: a people prepared to stand.
- _TMR 127.1._ Smith: the message "is given in the period of the 'patience of the saints'... And this period of the saints' patience is marked by a most important fact, viz., the keeping of the commandments of God and the faith of Jesus" — the pioneer reading: the third message is identified by the people it produces.
- _AJB 301.1._ Bates: "the third giving a most solemn warning against false worship, and presenting the commandments of God and the faith of Jesus" — the historical anchor: from the late 1840s the earliest Sabbatarian Adventists read the third message so; this is the doctrinal foundation of the movement.

**The faith OF Jesus — the faith by which He overcame — Rev. 3:21; Heb. 12:2; Gal. 2:20:**

- _Rev. 3:21._ "To him that overcometh will I grant to sit with me in my throne, even as I also overcame, and am set down with my Father in his throne" — "even as I ALSO overcame": Christ overcame by faith in the flesh He took (see "The Second Adam — The Nature Christ Took"), and grants the overcomer the same faith and the same seat (see "Complete Victory — Overcoming as He Overcame").
- _Heb. 12:2._ "Looking unto Jesus the author and finisher of our faith; who for the joy that was set before him endured the cross" — Jesus is not merely the object of faith but its author and finisher; He exercised faith Himself, enduring the cross, and that same faith He originates and perfects in the saints.
- _Gal. 2:20._ "the life which I now live in the flesh I live by the faith of the Son of God, who loved me, and gave himself for me" — the faith OF the Son of God living in the believer: Christ's own faith reproduced in the saint, the very mechanism by which the crucified-with-Christ life is lived out in the flesh.
- _CWCP 50.1._ Jones: "That faith which He exercised and by which He obtained the victory over the world, the flesh, and the devil—that faith is His free gift to every lost man in the world" — the pioneer witness to the subjective-genitive sense: the faith by which Christ Himself overcame is the very faith He gives, which is the faith of Jesus the third angel's message names.
- _3SM 172.3._ White: "What constitutes the faith of Jesus, that belongs to the third angel's message? Jesus becoming our sin-bearer that He might become our sin-pardoning Saviour... He came to our world and took our sins that we might take His righteousness. And faith in the ability of Christ to save us amply and fully and entirely is the faith of Jesus." — EGW defines the symbol this section owns: trust in Christ's FULL ability to save, grounded in the great exchange.
- _TMR 134.2._ Smith: "This term is used in distinction from the commandments of God... it is spoken of as being kept in the same manner that the commandments of God are kept" — Smith's own point reaches only this far: the faith of Jesus is the gospel half, distinct from yet kept alongside the law half. The overcoming-faith sense led with above is not Smith's reading but Jones's, on the Bible spine (_Rev. 3:21_; _Heb. 12:2_; _Gal. 2:20_; _CWCP 50.1_) — a Scripture-grounded sense wide enough to hold all three witnesses: the faith Jones names, which Christ exercised and gives freely; the faith EGW names, which trusts Christ's ability to save fully; and the gospel Smith names, kept as the commandments are kept.

**That faith works by love and produces the keeping — 1 John 5:3-4; John 14:15:**

- _1 John 5:3._ "For this is the love of God, that we keep his commandments: and his commandments are not grievous" — commandment-keeping is the fruit of love, not a burden set against the gospel; this is HOW they of _Rev. 14:12_ keep.
- _1 John 5:4._ "whatsoever is born of God overcometh the world: and this is the victory that overcometh the world, even our faith" — the victory IS faith: the overcoming faith of _Rev. 3:21_ is the faith of _Rev. 14:12_, producing obedience in the born-again (see "Born of Water and of the Spirit — How He Lived, How We Begin").
- _John 14:15._ "If ye love me, keep my commandments." — Christ's own order: love first, obedience following; the faith of Jesus working by love yields the commandment-keeping of _Rev. 14:12_.

**The same twofold mark — the remnant the dragon wars against — Rev. 12:17; 19:10:**

- _Rev. 12:17._ "And the dragon was wroth with the woman, and went to make war with the remnant of her seed, which keep the commandments of God, and have the testimony of Jesus Christ." — the same twofold mark identifies the remnant; the people of _Rev. 14:12_ are the object of the dragon's final war.
- _Rev. 19:10._ "the testimony of Jesus is the spirit of prophecy" — Scripture defines its own term: the second mark of the remnant is self-interpreted within the book.
- _TMR 114.1._ Smith: "The preaching of the commandments of God and the faith of Jesus. This is the cause of the conflict between the dragon and the last fragment of the church... It is because the saints are keeping all the commandments of God that the dragon makes war upon them" — _Rev. 14:12_ and _Rev. 12:17_ name one people; the keeping provokes the war.

**The book closes as chapter 14 described — Rev. 22:14:**

- _Rev. 22:14._ "Blessed are they that do his commandments, that they may have right to the tree of life, and may enter in through the gates into the city." — Revelation's last benediction falls on the very people the three messages produce; the commandment-keeping, faith-of-Jesus company inherits the city (see "The Harvest — A People in Whom Christ Is Vindicated").

**DEFINITION — THE COMMANDMENTS OF GOD AND THE FAITH OF JESUS =** the third angel's message is the everlasting gospel (_Rev. 14:6_; _17LtMs, Lt 183, 1902_) preached in the hour of God's judgment (_Rev. 14:7_; _Dan. 7:10_; _Eccl. 12:13-14_) — the power of God unto salvation, revealing the righteousness of God by faith (_Rom. 1:16-17_) — righteousness by faith, "the third angel's message, in verity" (_1SM 372.2_; see "Righteousness by Faith — The Third Angel's Message in Verity"). It is not law apart from gospel, for the messages END by describing the people they produce: "here are they that keep the commandments of God, and the faith of Jesus" (_Rev. 14:12_; _CIHS 167.1_; _TMR 127.1_) — the same twofold-marked remnant the dragon wars against (_Rev. 12:17_; _TMR 114.1_), whose second mark Scripture self-interprets as the spirit of prophecy (_Rev. 19:10_). The faith of Jesus is the faith BY which He Himself overcame (_Rev. 3:21_; _CWCP 50.1_), which He authors and finishes (_Heb. 12:2_), which lives in the believer as His own life of faith reproduced (_Gal. 2:20_), and which trusts His ability "to save us amply and fully and entirely" (_3SM 172.3_) — the gospel half kept alongside the law half (_TMR 134.2_). That faith is itself "the victory that overcometh the world" (_1 John 5:4_), working by love to keep commandments that are not grievous (_1 John 5:3_; _John 14:15_). Law and gospel are therefore not rivals in the message but its two marks on one people: the commandments show what the faith of Jesus produces, and the faith of Jesus supplies what the commandments demand. The judgment-hour setting makes this present truth — the message must ripen a people who can stand when the books are opened (_CIHS 167.1_; see "The Time of Trouble — Standing Without a Mediator"), and the book closes as chapter 14 described: "they that do his commandments" enter through the gates into the city (_Rev. 22:14_; see "The Harvest — A People in Whom Christ Is Vindicated").

**Symbols defined here:**

- **the third angel's message** = the everlasting gospel in its judgment-hour form: righteousness by faith producing a commandment-keeping people — never law apart from gospel (_Rev. 14:6-7_; _Rev. 14:9-12_; _Rom. 1:16-17_; _1SM 372.2_; _AJB 301.1_).
- **the faith of Jesus** = the very faith by which Christ overcame, authored and finished by Him, now living in the saints and producing commandment-keeping; trust in His ability to save amply, fully, and entirely (_Rev. 14:12_; _Rev. 3:21_; _Heb. 12:2_; _Gal. 2:20_; _1 John 5:3-4_; _CWCP 50.1_; _3SM 172.3_; _TMR 134.2_).

**Symbols carried:** righteousness by faith — imputed and imparted, here named as the message itself (see "Righteousness by Faith — The Third Angel's Message in Verity"); the overcomer — the same victory, by the same faith, "even as I also overcame" (see "Complete Victory — Overcoming as He Overcame"); the second Adam / sinful flesh — the flesh in which that overcoming faith was first lived (see "The Second Adam — The Nature Christ Took"); sin as the consented transgression of the law the saints here keep (see "What Sin Is — Transgression, Not Inheritance").

## 9. The Time of Trouble — Standing Without a Mediator

> Before Christ comes, His intercession ceases and every character is fixed forever: the saints then live in the sight of a holy God without a mediator — plunged into the time of Jacob's trouble, yet saved out of it, because their sins went beforehand to judgment while the Advocate still pleaded.

**Every case decided first — the cleansing of the sanctuary — Dan. 8:14; Rev. 3:5; Dan. 12:10:**

- _Dan. 8:14._ "Unto two thousand and three hundred days; then shall the sanctuary be cleansed" — the cleansing of the sanctuary is the final phase of Christ's priestly work: the investigative judgment that decides every case before He ceases to intercede.
- _Rev. 3:5._ "He that overcometh... I will not blot out his name out of the book of life, but I will confess his name before my Father, and before his angels" — Christ's confession of the overcomer's name before the Father is His intercessory act, finished for each case in the judgment; the overcomer of "Complete Victory — Overcoming as He Overcame" is the one whose name stands unblotted when the pleading ends.
- _Dan. 12:10._ "Many shall be purified, and made white, and tried; but the wicked shall do wickedly... but the wise shall understand" — the purifying is finished BEFORE Michael stands up; the victory work belongs to the hour when the Advocate still pleads.
- _GC 490.2._ White: "When the work of the investigative judgment closes, the destiny of all will have been decided for life or death. Probation is ended a short time before the appearing of the Lord in the clouds of heaven" — the sanctuary-cleansing of _Dan. 8:14_ runs straight into the Rev. 22:11-12 decree.

**The decree that closes probation — Rev. 22:11-12:**

- _Rev. 22:11._ "He that is unjust, let him be unjust still: and he which is filthy, let him be filthy still: and he that is righteous, let him be righteous still: and he that is holy, let him be holy still" — the word that fixes every character forever; no one changes sides after it is spoken.
  - **the close of probation** = the moment Christ's priestly mediation ends and every case is decided — each character sealed in what it has become, the decree spoken, the temple shut (_Rev. 22:11-12_; _Rev. 15:8_, "no man was able to enter into the temple"; _Lev. 16:17_; _Dan. 8:14_; _Dan. 12:1_). Defined in full at the close.
- _Rev. 22:12._ "And, behold, I come quickly; and my reward is with me, to give every man according as his work shall be" — the fixing of character and the rewarding advent are one connected event; the door of mercy shuts because He comes.
- _GC 613.2._ White: "Then Jesus ceases His intercession in the sanctuary above. He lifts His hands and with a loud voice says, 'It is done;'... 'He that is unjust, let him be unjust still... and he that is holy, let him be holy still.' Revelation 22:11. Every case has been decided for life or death. Christ has made the atonement for His people and blotted out their sins" — the Rev. 22:11 decree IS the ceasing of intercession.
- _EW 71.2._ White: "But there will be no time then to do it and no Mediator to plead their cause before the Father. Before this time the awfully solemn declaration has gone forth, 'He that is unjust, let him be unjust still... and he that is holy, let him be holy still'" — no Mediator after the declaration; whatever overcoming remains must be done before it.

**The temple shut — no ministration while the plagues fall — Lev. 16:17; Rev. 15:8; 16:1:**

- _Lev. 16:17._ "And there shall be no man in the tabernacle of the congregation when he goeth in to make an atonement in the holy place, until he come out" — the type: while the high priest finishes the atonement, no man may be in the sanctuary; when he comes out, the atonement is done.
- _Rev. 15:8._ "And the temple was filled with smoke from the glory of God, and from his power; and no man was able to enter into the temple, till the seven plagues of the seven angels were fulfilled" — the antitype: the heavenly sanctuary closed by God's own glory; no priest, no ministration, no intercession while the plagues fall.
- _Rev. 16:1._ "Go your ways, and pour out the vials of the wrath of God upon the earth" — the plagues begin the instant the temple closes (15:8 → 16:1); the wrath is unmingled with mercy precisely because the Mediator no longer stands between God and man.
- _DAR 643.1._ Smith: "there will therefore be no ministration in the sanctuary during this time... for Christ is then no longer a mediator; mercy, which has long stayed the hand of vengeance, pleads no more; the servants of God are all sealed" — the pioneer reading of Rev. 15:8: closed temple = ended mediation = a sealed people.
- _GC 614.1._ White: "When He leaves the sanctuary, darkness covers the inhabitants of the earth. In that fearful time the righteous must live in the sight of a holy God without an intercessor" — the section's key phrase, matching the shut temple of _Rev. 15:8_.

**Michael stands up — the trouble such as never was — Dan. 12:1:**

- _Dan. 12:1._ "And at that time shall Michael stand up, the great prince which standeth for the children of thy people: and there shall be a time of trouble, such as never was since there was a nation... and at that time thy people shall be delivered, every one that shall be found written in the book" — Christ rises from His mediatorial seat, His priestly work finished; the instant He ceases to intercede the unequalled trouble breaks — yet every name written in the book is delivered out of it.
- _MWV2 108.2._ Miller: "Michael, in this passage, must mean Christ; he is the great Prince, and Prince of princes" — the earliest pioneer voice: Michael who "stands up" is Christ, the priestly Prince whose standing marks the end of His pleading.
- _GC 425.1._ White: "Those who are living upon the earth when the intercession of Christ shall cease in the sanctuary above are to stand in the sight of a holy God without a mediator. Their robes must be spotless, their characters must be purified from sin by the blood of sprinkling" — the thesis in her own words: the purifying must be finished beforehand, because no mediator remains after.

**The time of Jacob's trouble — the name and the type — Jer. 30:6-7; Gen. 32:24-28; Hos. 12:4:**

- _Jer. 30:6._ "wherefore do I see every man with his hands on his loins, as a woman in travail, and all faces are turned into paleness?" — the anguish after mercy's door has shut: travail-pangs and pale faces, with no intercessor to plead the case.
- _Jer. 30:7._ "Alas! for that day is great, so that none is like it: it is even the time of Jacob's trouble; but he shall be saved out of it" — the name text, matching Daniel's "such as never was"; the saints are not saved FROM the trouble but delivered THROUGH it.
  - **the time of Jacob's trouble** = the unequalled final anguish of the sealed saints after probation closes — the saints marked so the destroyer cannot touch them, wrestling without an intercessor as Jacob wrestled alone in the night, yet "saved out of it" (_Jer. 30:6-7_; _Dan. 12:1_; _Gen. 32:24, 26, 28_; _Hos. 12:4_; _Rev. 7:3_; _Eze. 9:6_). Defined in full at the close.
- _Gen. 32:24._ "And Jacob was left alone; and there wrestled a man with him until the breaking of the day" — the historical type: alone in the night, expecting Esau's sword, wrestling the Angel — the sealed saint pleading alone in the final crisis.
- _Gen. 32:26._ "I will not let thee go, except thou bless me" — the language of the saints in the time of trouble: clinging in agony to a God whose face seems hidden, prevailing not by merit but by holding fast His covenant.
- _Gen. 32:28._ "Thy name shall be called no more Jacob, but Israel: for as a prince hast thou power with God and with men, and hast prevailed" — the supplanter becomes the prince who prevails; the victory granted only because his sin had been confessed and put away before that night (_GC 620.1_).
- _Hos. 12:4._ "Yea, he had power over the angel, and prevailed: he wept, and made supplication unto him" — the inspired commentary on the wrestling: he prevailed by tears and clinging prayer, not force; so the saints overcome by the wrestling of a heart that will not let God go.
- _GC 619.3._ White: "They lay hold of the strength of God as Jacob laid hold of the Angel; and the language of their souls is: 'I will not let Thee go, except Thou bless me'" — Gen. 32:26 drawn straight onto the saints in the trouble.
- _GC 620.1._ White: "if the people of God had unconfessed sins to appear before them while tortured with fear and anguish, they would be overwhelmed... But while they have a deep sense of their unworthiness, they have no concealed wrongs to reveal. Their sins have gone beforehand to judgment and have been blotted out" — why they can endure without a mediator: nothing remains on the books to condemn them.

**Only the sealed stand — Rev. 7:3; Eze. 9:6; Mal. 3:2:**

- _Rev. 7:3._ "Hurt not the earth, neither the sea, nor the trees, till we have sealed the servants of our God in their foreheads" — before probation closes, the servants of God are sealed — settled into the truth so they cannot be moved; the sealing must be finished before the temple shuts (_Rev. 15:8_), and the sealed alone stand without a mediator.
- _Eze. 9:6._ "but come not near any man upon whom is the mark: and begin at my sanctuary" — the type of the sealing before the slaughter: the destroyer may not touch any who bear the mark; and judgment begins at the house of God (cf. _1 Pet. 4:17_).
- _Mal. 3:2._ "But who may abide the day of his coming? and who shall stand when he appeareth? for he is like a refiner's fire, and like fullers' soap" — the question the whole crisis answers: only those refined BEFORE the day, cleansed by the blood while the Advocate still pleaded, can stand in the sight of a holy God without an intercessor.
- _COL 69.1._ White: "When the character of Christ shall be perfectly reproduced in His people, then He will come to claim them as His own" — the character-perfecting must be finished while He pleads; the ripening of that people is the burden of "The Harvest — A People in Whom Christ Is Vindicated."

**DEFINITION — THE TIME OF TROUBLE — STANDING WITHOUT A MEDIATOR =** before the second coming Christ's priestly intercession ends: the cleansing of the sanctuary (_Dan. 8:14_) — the investigative judgment — decides every case while Christ still confesses names before the Father (_Rev. 3:5_), and the decree "he that is unjust, let him be unjust still... he that is holy, let him be holy still" fixes every character forever, "a short time before the appearing of the Lord" (_Rev. 22:11-12_; _GC 490.2_; _GC 613.2_; _EW 71.2_). The heavenly temple, like the tabernacle on the Day of Atonement (_Lev. 16:17_), is then shut — "filled with smoke... and no man was able to enter" (_Rev. 15:8_) — and the plagues fall unmingled with mercy (_Rev. 16:1_; _DAR 643.1_) while Michael, Christ the great Prince (_MWV2 108.2_), stands up from His mediatorial seat (_Dan. 12:1_). The saints then "live in the sight of a holy God without an intercessor" (_GC 614.1_; _GC 425.1_) — not without Christ's presence or the Spirit, but with every case decided — through "a time of trouble, such as never was," "even the time of Jacob's trouble" (_Dan. 12:1_; _Jer. 30:7_): like Jacob alone in the night they wrestle with travail-pangs and pale faces (_Jer. 30:6_; _Gen. 32:24_), clinging with "I will not let thee go, except thou bless me" (_Gen. 32:26_; _GC 619.3_), prevailing as he did by weeping and supplication (_Hos. 12:4_; _Gen. 32:28_). They endure only because the preparation was finished FIRST: purified, made white, and tried before Michael stood up (_Dan. 12:10_); sealed in their foreheads before the winds hurt (_Rev. 7:3_; _Eze. 9:6_); refined before the day that asks "who shall stand?" (_Mal. 3:2_); their "sins have gone beforehand to judgment and have been blotted out" (_GC 620.1_). This is why the victory message precedes the crisis — a people must cease from sin while the Advocate still pleads, that the character of Christ be perfectly reproduced before He comes to claim them (_COL 69.1_) — and it ends in deliverance: "he shall be saved out of it" (_Jer. 30:7_), "delivered, every one that shall be found written in the book" (_Dan. 12:1_).

**Symbols defined here:**

- **the close of probation** = the moment Christ's priestly mediation ends and every character is fixed forever — every case decided in the cleansing of the sanctuary, the decree of Rev. 22:11 spoken because "I come quickly," the temple shut by the glory of God with no ministration while the plagues fall, Michael standing up from His mediatorial seat (_Rev. 22:11-12_; _Rev. 15:8_; _Lev. 16:17_; _Dan. 8:14_; _Dan. 12:1_).
- **the time of Jacob's trouble** = the unequalled final anguish of the righteous after probation closes — the sealed saints, marked so the destroyer cannot touch them, wrestling without an intercessor as Jacob wrestled alone, prevailing by weeping and supplication because their sins have gone beforehand to judgment, and "saved out of it," every name written in the book (_Jer. 30:6-7_; _Dan. 12:1_; _Gen. 32:24, 26, 28_; _Hos. 12:4_; _Rev. 7:3_; _Eze. 9:6_).

**Symbols carried:** the overcomer — the one whose name Christ confesses and never blots out (see "Complete Victory — Overcoming as He Overcame"); sin as consented transgression of the law — the saints stand here because they ceased from it while the Advocate still pleaded (see "What Sin Is — Transgression, Not Inheritance"); sinful flesh — the saints stand in the same fallen nature in which Christ overcame, He never having consented to sin (see "The Second Adam — The Nature Christ Took"); the faith of Jesus and the third angel's message — the judgment-hour message that ripens the people who can stand (see "The Commandments of God and the Faith of Jesus"; see "Righteousness by Faith — The Third Angel’s Message in Verity"); the character of Christ perfectly reproduced — ripened in full in "The Harvest — A People in Whom Christ Is Vindicated."

## 10. The Harvest — A People in Whom Christ Is Vindicated

> The whole chain ends where the parable said it would: when the character of Christ is perfectly reproduced in His people the grain is ripe, the sickle falls, and in a faultless remnant the accuser is answered and God is vindicated before the watching universe.

**The harvest law — the ripeness of the grain sets the hour of the sickle — Mark 4:26-29; Rev. 14:15; James 5:7-8:**

- _Mark 4:26-27._ "So is the kingdom of God, as if a man should cast seed into the ground; And should sleep, and rise night and day, and the seed should spring and grow up, he knoweth not how" — the kingdom works as a crop works: sown, growing unseen, a fixed law moving toward a harvest.
- _Mark 4:28._ "For the earth bringeth forth fruit of herself; first the blade, then the ear, after that the full corn in the ear" — growth is ordered and gradual, and its goal is not blade or ear but FULL corn: maturity.
- _Mark 4:29._ "But when the fruit is brought forth, immediately he putteth in the sickle, because the harvest is come" — the ripeness of the fruit, not a date on a calendar, brings the sickle; the moment the crop is ready, the reaping is IMMEDIATE.
  - **the harvest / the ripe grain** = the ripened likeness of Christ reproduced in His people — character matured from blade to full corn — which is itself the signal for the sickle; when the fruit is fully brought forth, Christ immediately reaps, so the ripeness of the crop sets the hour of His coming (_Mark 4:28-29_; _Rev. 14:15_, "the harvest of the earth is ripe"; _James 5:7-8_, the husbandman waiting for "the precious fruit of the earth"; _Rev. 19:7-8_, the wife made ready).
- _Rev. 14:15._ "Thrust in thy sickle, and reap: for the time is come for thee to reap; for the harvest of the earth is ripe" — heaven's own reading of _Mark 4:29_ enacted at the end of the world: the time to reap IS the ripeness of the harvest; Christ comes when the crop is ready.
- _James 5:7._ "...Behold, the husbandman waiteth for the precious fruit of the earth, and hath long patience for it, until he receive the early and latter rain" — the Husbandman Himself waits with long patience — not for time to pass but for grain to ripen under the two rains.
- _James 5:8._ "Be ye also patient; stablish your hearts: for the coming of the Lord draweth nigh" — the coming draws nigh exactly as the fruit ripens; the waiting saints and the waiting Lord wait for the same thing.
- _COL 69.1._ White: "Christ is waiting with longing desire for the manifestation of Himself in His church. When the character of Christ shall be perfectly reproduced in His people, then He will come to claim them as His own." — the keystone, written directly on _Mark 4:29_: the ripe fruit IS the reproduced character of Christ; He tarries for the harvest of His own likeness, then comes.
- _DA 671.2._ White: "Christ has given His Spirit as a divine power to overcome all hereditary and cultivated tendencies to evil, and to impress His own character upon His church." — how the grain ripens: the Spirit overcomes every hereditary and cultivated tendency to evil and stamps Christ's character on the church — the birth of "Born of Water and of the Spirit — How He Lived, How We Begin" grown to full corn.

**The sealed firstfruits — the 144,000 stand with the Lamb — Rev. 7:2-4; 14:1-5; Zeph. 3:13:**

- _Rev. 7:2-3._ "Hurt not the earth, neither the sea, nor the trees, till we have sealed the servants of our God in their foreheads" — the winds of strife are held back until the servants of God are sealed; the sealing must finish before the end can break.
- _Rev. 7:4._ "And I heard the number of them which were sealed: and there were sealed an hundred and forty and four thousand of all the tribes of the children of Israel" — the sealed company numbered and named.
  - **the 144,000 / the remnant** = the last generation — the sealed servants of God, redeemed from among men as firstfruits, who follow the Lamb whithersoever He goeth and in whose mouth is found no guile: without fault before the throne, the remnant of Israel that shall not do iniquity nor speak lies; in them the gospel's full power is demonstrated (_Rev. 7:2-4_; _Rev. 14:1-5_; _Zeph. 3:13_; _Eph. 5:27_; _Jude 1:24_).
- _LDE 219.4._ White: "Just as soon as the people of God are sealed in their foreheads—it is not any seal or mark that can be seen, but a settling into the truth, both intellectually and spiritually, so they cannot be moved—just as soon as God's people are sealed and prepared for the shaking, it will come." — the seal defined: a settling into truth so fixed the sealed cannot be moved — the inward reality behind the faultless standing.
- _Rev. 14:1._ "a Lamb stood on the mount Sion, and with him an hundred forty and four thousand, having his Father's name written in their foreheads" — the sealed of _Rev. 7_ seen standing with the Lamb; the seal is the FATHER'S NAME — His character — written in the mind.
- _Rev. 14:3._ "they sung as it were a new song before the throne... and no man could learn that song but the hundred and forty and four thousand, which were redeemed from the earth" — a song of experience no other company can learn; their history is unrepeatable.
- _Rev. 14:4._ "...These are they which follow the Lamb whithersoever he goeth. These were redeemed from among men, being the firstfruits unto God and to the Lamb" — FIRSTFRUITS: harvest language again — the first ripe sheaf of the field, proof that the harvest law has run its full course in human flesh.
- _Rev. 14:5._ "And in their mouth was found no guile: for they are without fault before the throne of God" — the crowning trait of the last generation: guileless mouths, faultless standing — and not before men, but before the THRONE.
- _Zeph. 3:13._ "The remnant of Israel shall not do iniquity, nor speak lies; neither shall a deceitful tongue be found in their mouth: for they shall feed and lie down, and none shall make them afraid" — the Old Testament twin of _Rev. 14:5_: the remnant shall NOT DO iniquity, and no deceit is in their mouth — salvation from sin demonstrated in a people (see "What Salvation Really Is — Saved From Sin").
- _SSP 250.1._ Haskell: "In their mouth was found no guile; for the soul temple had been so thoroughly cleansed before leaving earth that the human mouth became a channel for the words of God.... This will be the mind of those who are sealed. They are without fault; because Christ's righteousness covers them as a garment.... They overcame by the blood of the Lamb." — three of the section's own terms in one witness — no guile, without fault, overcame by the blood — with _Zeph. 3:13_ in its printed margin; the faultlessness is Christ's righteousness as a garment, not human merit.

**Faultless — kept and presented by His power, not their merit — Jude 1:24; Eph. 5:27; Rev. 19:7-8:**

- _Jude 1:24._ "Now unto him that is able to keep you from falling, and to present you faultless before the presence of his glory with exceeding joy" — the keeping and the presenting are both HIS act: "faultless" (_Rev. 14:5_) is a doxology to His ability, not a boast of theirs.
- _Eph. 5:27._ "That he might present it to himself a glorious church, not having spot, or wrinkle, or any such thing; but that it should be holy and without blemish" — HE presents it to HIMSELF: the spotless church is Christ's own workmanship, the end for which He gave Himself.
- _Rev. 19:7._ "the marriage of the Lamb is come, and his wife hath made herself ready" — the readiness is real: the ripeness is actual, not a legal fiction; the harvest and the wedding are one event under two figures.
- _Rev. 19:8._ "And to her was granted that she should be arrayed in fine linen, clean and white: for the fine linen is the righteousness of saints" — "made herself ready," yet the arraying "was GRANTED": the righteousness of saints is a granted robe — righteousness by faith to the very end (see "Righteousness by Faith — The Third Angel’s Message in Verity").
- _1SM 366.1._ White: "God requires the entire surrender of the heart, before justification can take place; and in order for man to retain justification, there must be continual obedience, through active, living faith that works by love and purifies the soul." — the granted robe and the actual obedience are not rivals: the harvest of a guileless people is justification by faith retained and ripened, not a work replacing it.

**The vindication — the accuser answered before the watching universe — Eph. 3:10; Rev. 12:10-11:**

- _Eph. 3:10._ "To the intent that now unto the principalities and powers in heavenly places might be known by the church the manifold wisdom of God" — the demonstration has an audience: the unfallen universe learns the wisdom of God BY THE CHURCH.
- _16MR 115.1._ White: "In heaven Satan had declared that the sin of Adam revealed that human beings could not keep the law of God.... Christ came to unmask the deceiver.... The Majesty of heaven would undertake the cause of man, and with the same facilities that man may obtain, stand the test and proving of God as man must stand it." — the charge on trial: that the law cannot be kept in human flesh; Christ answered it "with the same facilities that man may obtain" (see "The Second Adam — The Nature Christ Took").
- _Rev. 12:10._ "Now is come salvation... for the accuser of our brethren is cast down, which accused them before our God day and night" — courtroom language: Satan is the ACCUSER, and the day-and-night accusation is silenced not by argument but by evidence.
- _Rev. 12:11._ "And they overcame him by the blood of the Lamb, and by the word of their testimony; and they loved not their lives unto the death" — the evidence named: THEY overcame HIM, by the blood and by testimony; the vindication of God is worked out in overcomers.
- _DA 671.3._ White: "The very image of God is to be reproduced in humanity. The honor of God, the honor of Christ, is involved in the perfection of the character of His people." — the stake named plainly: God's own honor rides on the perfected character of His people — the manifold wisdom of _Eph. 3:10_ shown in the last generation.

**"Even as I also overcame" — the whole chain ripens here — Rev. 3:21:**

- _Rev. 3:21._ "To him that overcometh will I grant to sit with me in my throne, even as I also overcame, and am set down with my Father in his throne" — the last promise to the last church: the overcomer's victory is of one kind with Christ's own — same nature, same power, same throne.
- _2SP 39.3._ White: "It was in the order of God that Christ should take upon himself the form and nature of fallen man, that he might be made perfect through suffering, and himself endure the strength of Satan's fierce temptations..." — the ground of "even as I": He overcame in the nature of fallen man, the very flesh in which the accuser said the law could not be kept (see "The Second Adam — The Nature Christ Took").
- _CWCP 84.1._ Jones: "Perfection, perfection of character, is the Christian goal—perfection attained in human flesh in this world. Christ attained it in human flesh in this world and thus made and consecrated a way by which, in Him, every believer can attain it." — the pioneer spine: the harvest is attainable because the Forerunner already ripened in this soil (see "Complete Victory — Overcoming as He Overcame").
- _GC 425.1._ White: "Those who are living upon the earth when the intercession of Christ shall cease in the sanctuary above are to stand in the sight of a holy God without a mediator. Their robes must be spotless, their characters must be purified from sin by the blood of sprinkling.... This work is more clearly presented in the messages of Revelation 14." — the ripe grain and the close of intercession joined — and joined by the witness herself to Revelation 14 (see "The Time of Trouble — Standing Without a Mediator").
- _GC 614.1._ White: "When He leaves the sanctuary, darkness covers the inhabitants of the earth. In that fearful time the righteous must live in the sight of a holy God without an intercessor." — the setting of the final demonstration: the sealed stand faultless while no mediator pleads — the harvest standing ripe in the field after the rain has ceased.
- _COL 415.5._ White: "The last rays of merciful light, the last message of mercy to be given to the world, is a revelation of His character of love. The children of God are to manifest His glory. In their own life and character they are to reveal what the grace of God has done for them." — the message and the demonstration are one thing: the third angel's message ends as a people in whom the character it preached is visible (see "The Commandments of God and the Faith of Jesus").

**DEFINITION — THE HARVEST — A PEOPLE IN WHOM CHRIST IS VINDICATED =** the end of the whole chain is a crop. The kingdom grows as grain grows — first the blade, then the ear, after that the full corn — and "when the fruit is brought forth, immediately he putteth in the sickle, because the harvest is come" (_Mark 4:26-29_): the ripeness of the grain, not an arbitrary date, sets the hour of the sickle (_Rev. 14:15_), and the Husbandman waits with long patience through early and latter rain for "the precious fruit of the earth" (_James 5:7-8_). The ripe fruit is the character of Christ perfectly reproduced in His people (_COL 69.1_), wrought by the Spirit overcoming every hereditary and cultivated tendency to evil (_DA 671.2_). The reaped sheaf is the 144,000 — sealed in their foreheads before the winds break (_Rev. 7:2-4_), settled into the truth so they cannot be moved (_LDE 219.4_), bearing the Father's name, following the Lamb whithersoever He goeth, firstfruits unto God, "and in their mouth was found no guile: for they are without fault before the throne of God" (_Rev. 14:1-5_) — the remnant that "shall not do iniquity" (_Zeph. 3:13_). Their faultlessness is His act, not their merit: He is "able to keep you from falling, and to present you faultless" (_Jude 1:24_); He presents to Himself a church without spot or wrinkle (_Eph. 5:27_); the bride's readiness is a GRANTED arraying in the righteousness of saints (_Rev. 19:7-8_), Christ's righteousness covering them as a garment (_SSP 250.1_), justification by faith retained through living obedience (_1SM 366.1_). And the demonstration has an audience: by the church the manifold wisdom of God is made known to principalities and powers (_Eph. 3:10_); Satan's ancient charge — that the law cannot be kept in human flesh (_16MR 115.1_) — is answered when a people overcome the accuser "by the blood of the Lamb, and by the word of their testimony" (_Rev. 12:10-11_), overcoming "even as I also overcame" (_Rev. 3:21_): in the same fallen nature Christ took (_2SP 39.3_), by the way He consecrated in human flesh (_CWCP 84.1_), standing at last without a mediator in spotless robes (_GC 425.1_; _GC 614.1_). Here every thread of the handbook ripens — the living soul in whom the ripening happens ("Dust and Breath — What Man Is"), sin as transgression ("What Sin Is — Transgression, Not Inheritance"), the nature Christ took ("The Second Adam — The Nature Christ Took"), the new birth ("Born of Water and of the Spirit — How He Lived, How We Begin"), salvation from sin ("What Salvation Really Is — Saved From Sin"), the granted robe ("Righteousness by Faith — The Third Angel’s Message in Verity"), the overcomer ("Complete Victory — Overcoming as He Overcame"), the judgment-hour message ("The Commandments of God and the Faith of Jesus"), the standing without an intercessor ("The Time of Trouble — Standing Without a Mediator") — for the honor of God is involved in the perfection of the character of His people (_DA 671.3_), and the last message of mercy is His character of love made visible in them (_COL 415.5_). When that fruit is brought forth, immediately He puts in the sickle.

**Symbols defined here:**

- **the harvest / the ripe grain** = the ripened likeness of Christ reproduced in His people — character matured from blade to full corn — which is itself the signal for the sickle; the ripeness of the crop, not an arbitrary date, sets the hour of His coming (_Mark 4:26-29_; _Rev. 14:15_; _James 5:7-8_; _Rev. 19:7-8_; _COL 69.1_).
- **the 144,000 / the remnant** = the last generation — the sealed servants of God, redeemed from among men as firstfruits, following the Lamb whithersoever He goeth, without guile and without fault before the throne, the remnant of Israel that shall not do iniquity nor speak lies; in them the gospel's power is demonstrated and God is vindicated (_Rev. 14:1-5_; _Rev. 7:2-4_; _Zeph. 3:13_; _Eph. 5:27_; _Jude 1:24_; _LDE 219.4_).

**Symbols carried:** the living soul — the whole man in whom this ripening happens (see "Dust and Breath — What Man Is"); sin as transgression, the thing this people has ceased to do (see "What Sin Is — Transgression, Not Inheritance"); the fallen nature Christ took, the flesh in which the vindication is won (see "The Second Adam — The Nature Christ Took"); the new birth by the Spirit, the planting of the grain that ripens here (see "Born of Water and of the Spirit — How He Lived, How We Begin"); salvation as salvation FROM sin, here demonstrated in a people (see "What Salvation Really Is — Saved From Sin"); the granted robe of righteousness by faith (see "Righteousness by Faith — The Third Angel’s Message in Verity"); overcoming "even as I also overcame" (see "Complete Victory — Overcoming as He Overcame"); the commandment-keeping, faith-of-Jesus people the message produces (see "The Commandments of God and the Faith of Jesus"); the standing without a mediator, the setting of the final demonstration (see "The Time of Trouble — Standing Without a Mediator").

**For discussion:**

1. _Mark 4:29_ and _Rev. 14:15_ both hang the sickle on one condition — "the harvest of the earth is ripe" — and _COL 69.1_ names the ripeness: the character of Christ "perfectly reproduced in His people." If the crop's maturity, not the calendar, sets the hour, what does the Husbandman's "long patience" (_James 5:7-8_) say about why He tarries — and about what our surrender today has to do with His coming?
2. The 144,000 are "without fault before the throne of God" (_Rev. 14:5_), yet Jude says it is HE who "is able to keep you from falling, and to present you faultless" (_Jude 1:24_), and the bride's fine linen is "granted" her (_Rev. 19:8_). How does holding both — real sinlessness, and all of it His gift worn "as a garment" (_SSP 250.1_) — keep the call to perfection from collapsing into either presumption or despair?
3. _Eph. 3:10_ says the watching "principalities and powers" learn the manifold wisdom of God "by the church," and _Rev. 12:10-11_ says the accuser is cast down by a people who "overcame him by the blood of the Lamb." When you resist temptation this week, whose reputation is actually on trial (_DA 671.3_) — and how does fighting for God's vindication rather than your own record change the way you fight?

## Appendix — Symbol Dictionary

Every symbol defined in this handbook, alphabetically (leading articles ignored), with its receipts and owning section.

- **the 144,000 / the remnant** = the last generation — the sealed servants of God, redeemed from among men as firstfruits, following the Lamb whithersoever He goeth, without guile and without fault before the throne, the remnant of Israel that shall not do iniquity nor speak lies; in them the gospel's power is demonstrated and God is vindicated (_Rev. 14:1-5_; _Rev. 7:2-4_; _Zeph. 3:13_; _Eph. 5:27_; _Jude 1:24_; _LDE 219.4_). — defined in "The Harvest — A People in Whom Christ Is Vindicated".
- **the breath / spirit of life** = the impersonal, God-given life-power that animates the dust — common to man and beast, sustained moment by moment, returning to God at death as life-power without consciousness (_Job 33:4_; _Job 27:3_; _Gen. 7:22_; _Ps. 104:29_; _Eccl. 12:7_; _Ps. 146:4_; _Job 34:14-15_). — defined in "Dust and Breath — What Man Is".
- **the close of probation** = the moment Christ's priestly mediation ends and every character is fixed forever — every case decided in the cleansing of the sanctuary, the decree of Rev. 22:11 spoken because "I come quickly," the temple shut by the glory of God with no ministration while the plagues fall, Michael standing up from His mediatorial seat (_Rev. 22:11-12_; _Rev. 15:8_; _Lev. 16:17_; _Dan. 8:14_; _Dan. 12:1_). — defined in "The Time of Trouble — Standing Without a Mediator".
- **the faith of Jesus** = the very faith by which Christ overcame, authored and finished by Him, now living in the saints and producing commandment-keeping; trust in His ability to save amply, fully, and entirely (_Rev. 14:12_; _Rev. 3:21_; _Heb. 12:2_; _Gal. 2:20_; _1 John 5:3-4_; _CWCP 50.1_; _3SM 172.3_; _TMR 134.2_). — defined in "The Commandments of God and the Faith of Jesus".
- **the flesh / fallen nature** = the inherited corruption — a real bent that inclines toward evil and holds the natural man in bondage until Christ delivers; "sin that dwelleth in me" as a resident power, but not itself guilt — it condemns only when obeyed (_Ps. 51:5_; _Eph. 2:3_; _Rom. 7:14, 17-18, 23-25_). — defined in "What Sin Is — Transgression, Not Inheritance".
- **the harvest / the ripe grain** = the ripened likeness of Christ reproduced in His people — character matured from blade to full corn — which is itself the signal for the sickle; the ripeness of the crop, not an arbitrary date, sets the hour of His coming (_Mark 4:26-29_; _Rev. 14:15_; _James 5:7-8_; _Rev. 19:7-8_; _COL 69.1_). — defined in "The Harvest — A People in Whom Christ Is Vindicated".
- **imputed for righteousness / counted / reckoned** = to credit to the believer's account a righteousness he did not earn — faith "counted... for righteousness" to him "that worketh not, but believeth on him that justifieth the ungodly"; the legal ground of the believer's title (_Gen. 15:6_; _Rom. 4:3-5_; _Rom. 4:22-25_; _2 Cor. 5:21_; _FW 18.1_). — defined in "Righteousness by Faith — The Third Angel’s Message in Verity".
- **the just shall live by faith** = the believer becomes just by faith and continues the whole Christian life by that same faith — entire dependence on the word of God; self-lifted works excluded from first to last (_Hab. 2:4_; _Rom. 1:16-17_; _LOF_ATJ 38.8-9_). — defined in "Righteousness by Faith — The Third Angel’s Message in Verity".
- **the living soul** = the whole man himself — the sum produced when God joined dust (body) to the breath of life; man does not have a soul, he IS one, an indivisible and mortal unit (_Gen. 2:7_; _1 Cor. 15:45_; _Ezek. 18:4_; _Eccl. 9:5_; _Ps. 146:4_). — defined in "Dust and Breath — What Man Is".
- **the new birth (born again / born of water and of the Spirit)** = the Spirit re-creating the whole man — an entire change of the entire man, a new heart and new spirit given, so that the man becomes a new creature able to see and enter the kingdom; the second birth that puts a man where Christ stood from His first birth, inside the Spirit-filled, God-surrendered life (_John 3:3-7_; _2 Cor. 5:17_; _Eze. 36:26-27_; _Titus 3:5_; _2 Pet. 1:4_; _TDG 48.4_). — defined in "Born of Water and of the Spirit — How He Lived, How We Begin".
- **the overcomer** = one in whom the born-of-God life triumphs over sin and the world — not by native strength but by Christ living out in fallen flesh the very victory He Himself won; the same faith (_1 John 5:4_), the same keeping (_1 John 5:18_), the same victory (_Rev. 3:21_, "even as I also overcame"; _Gal. 2:20_) — the normal Christian assumed by every church-letter promise (_Rev. 2:7_; _3:12_), "made free from sin" (_Rom. 6:22_) under the charter "sin shall not have dominion" (_Rom. 6:14_). — defined in "Complete Victory — Overcoming as He Overcame".
- **righteousness by faith** = Christ's own righteousness given to the believer in two inseparable modes: IMPUTED — His obedience counted to the believer, the title (_Gen. 15:6_; _Rom. 3:24-28_; _Rom. 4:3-5, 22-25_; _2 Cor. 5:21_; _Phil. 3:9_); and IMPARTED — Christ living in the believer by faith, fulfilling the law's righteousness in him, the fitness (_Rom. 8:3-4_; _Gal. 2:20_; _Gal. 5:6_; _1 Cor. 1:30_; _DA 300.1_). Its Name is THE LORD OUR RIGHTEOUSNESS (_Jer. 23:6_); obedience is its fruit, never its root, and never optional (_Eph. 2:8-10_; _James 2:17-18_). — defined in "Righteousness by Faith — The Third Angel’s Message in Verity".
- **salvation** = God saving man FROM sin — from its guilt (justification), from its power and dominion (sanctification), and at last from its very presence (glorification); the restoration of the image of God in the soul by the indwelling Christ — not a legal status only but a Person possessed and a nature renewed — reaching to the uttermost (_Matt. 1:21_; _Ps. 130:8_; _Rom. 6:18, 22_; _John 8:36_; _Col. 3:10_; _2 Cor. 3:18_; _Col. 1:27_; _Gal. 2:20_; _Heb. 7:25_; _1 John 3:2-3, 5, 8_; _Titus 2:14_). — defined in "What Salvation Really Is — Saved From Sin".
- **the second Adam** = Christ as the new head and representative of the human race, standing where the first Adam fell — in fallen flesh, rendering unbroken obedience where the first man disobeyed, a real re-run of Adam's probation in the condition of Adam's ruined descendants, His obedience reckoned to the many as Adam's disobedience was (_1 Cor. 15:45, 47_; _Rom. 5:19_; _Heb. 2:16-17_). — defined in "The Second Adam — The Nature Christ Took".
- **sin** = the consented transgression of God's law — a chosen act against a known standard, born when desire conceives (the will yields); defined by the law, not by birth; guilt attaches to the choice, never to the mere presence of a fallen inclination (_1 John 3:4_; _James 1:14-15_; _John 8:34_; _Ezek. 18:20_; _Rom. 3:23_). — defined in "What Sin Is — Transgression, Not Inheritance".
- **sinful flesh** = the fallen, hereditary, degenerate human nature — weakened by four thousand years of sin, tempted from within and without — which Christ actually took and in which He never once consented to sin; the nature He bore, never any sin He committed (_Rom. 8:3_; _Rom. 1:3_; _Heb. 2:14_; _Heb. 4:15_; _2 Cor. 5:21_). — defined in "The Second Adam — The Nature Christ Took".
- **the third angel's message** = the everlasting gospel in its judgment-hour form: righteousness by faith producing a commandment-keeping people — never law apart from gospel (_Rev. 14:6-7_; _Rev. 14:9-12_; _Rom. 1:16-17_; _1SM 372.2_; _AJB 301.1_). — defined in "The Commandments of God and the Faith of Jesus".
- **the time of Jacob's trouble** = the unequalled final anguish of the righteous after probation closes — the sealed saints, marked so the destroyer cannot touch them, wrestling without an intercessor as Jacob wrestled alone, prevailing by weeping and supplication because their sins have gone beforehand to judgment, and "saved out of it," every name written in the book (_Jer. 30:6-7_; _Dan. 12:1_; _Gen. 32:24, 26, 28_; _Hos. 12:4_; _Rev. 7:3_; _Eze. 9:6_). — defined in "The Time of Trouble — Standing Without a Mediator".
- **the washing / the laver of regeneration (regeneration by water, word, and Spirit)** = the cleansing of regeneration in which washing and power are joined and inseparable — water sprinkled clean, the word that sanctifies, and the renewing of the Holy Ghost are one act, removing filthiness AND supplying the indwelling Spirit that causes the cleansed one to walk in God's statutes; not by might or works, but by the Spirit (_Titus 3:5_; _Eph. 5:26_; _Eze. 36:25-27_; _Zech. 4:6_; _SD 105.3_). — defined in "Born of Water and of the Spirit — How He Lived, How We Begin".
