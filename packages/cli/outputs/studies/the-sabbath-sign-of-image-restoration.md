# The Sabbath — The Sign That the Image Is Being Restored

**Salvation is the restoration of God's image in man. The Sabbath is the sign that the restoration is actually happening — in your heart, by His power, not yours. So the real question was never "why should we keep it?" The question is: why do we resist it?**

## Prep Reading

- Genesis 1:26-27; 2:1-3
- Exodus 31:13-17; 20:8-11
- Ezekiel 20:12; 36:26-27
- Jeremiah 31:33
- 2 Peter 1:4
- Galatians 4:22-28
- Hebrews 3:18–4:11

> _This study is a sequel to "What Salvation Really Is — The Image Restored." That study answered_ what _salvation is. This one answers a different question:_ how do you know it's happening to you? _Heaven gave one weekly sign._

---

## The Foundation — Salvation IS the Image Restored

Before the Sabbath can be a _sign_ of anything, we have to know what it is a sign _of_. So build the foundation first: what salvation actually is.

- **Made in the image**
  - "Let us make man in our image, after our likeness... So God created man in his own image" (Gen 1:26-27)
  - Humanity was designed to be a reflection of God. That is the starting point of everything.

- **The image marred**
  - "And Adam... begat a son in his own likeness, after his image; and called his name Seth" (Gen 5:3)
  - Gen 1:27 — created in _God's_ image. Gen 5:3 — children bearing _Adam's_ image. Sin defaced the portrait.
  - "as we have borne the image of the earthy, we shall also bear the image of the heavenly" (1 Cor 15:49)
  - [→] The whole plan of salvation exists to reverse what happened between Gen 1:27 and Gen 5:3. But _what is_ this image? Define it.

- **The image = God's glory = His name = His character**
  - Moses asks to see it: "I beseech thee, shew me thy glory" (Ex 33:18)
  - God answers not with sight but with His _name_: "I will... proclaim the name of the LORD before thee" (Ex 33:19)
  - And the name is a list of character traits: "The LORD, The LORD God, merciful and gracious, longsuffering, and abundant in goodness and truth" (Ex 34:6)
  - [→] Glory = name = character. God's glory is not a light-show; it is His goodness. _That_ is the image stamped on man at creation — and defaced by sin.

- **Christ = the image made visible**
  - "he that hath seen me hath seen the Father" (John 14:9)
  - "the express image of his person" (Heb 1:3); "the light of the knowledge of the glory of God in the face of Jesus Christ" (2 Cor 4:6)
  - [→] Same request as Moses (Ex 33), better answer: the character of God walking among us.

- **The character = love = the law**
  - "God is love" (1 John 4:8)
  - "Thou shalt love the Lord thy God... and thy neighbour as thyself... this do, and thou shalt live" (Luke 10:27-28)
  - The law is summed up in love; God _is_ love. So the law is the **transcript of God's character** — Law = Love = Character = Image. Every link is a plain verse, not an overlay.

- **So salvation = re-creating that image in you**
  - "And have put on the new man, which is renewed in knowledge after the image of him that created him" (Col 3:10)
  - "whom he did foreknow, he also did predestinate to be conformed to the image of his Son" (Rom 8:29)
  - Not merely pardon (clearing the record) — _restoration_ (remaking the person). "He shall save his people FROM their sins" (Matt 1:21) — not in them, from them.
  - [→] So to restore the image is to restore the law of love _in the heart_. Keep that thought — it is exactly where the Sabbath enters.

[ILL] A child finds a faded, cracked portrait in the attic — barely recognizable. That is Gen 1:27 after the fall. Then the father himself walks in the door. That is the incarnation. The portrait told you what the family was meant to look like; the Person shows you in the flesh. Salvation is the family resemblance coming back.

[→] If salvation = the image restored, one question becomes unavoidable: **is it actually being restored in me? Did God give any sign that the work is underway?** He did. One. Weekly.

## The Standard Cannot Move — Because God Cannot

- **God does not change**
  - "For I am the LORD, I change not" (Mal 3:6)
  - "Jesus Christ the same yesterday, and to day, and for ever" (Heb 13:8)

- **Therefore His character cannot change**
  - The law is the transcript of that character. A changeless God = a changeless law.
  - You cannot edit one commandment without claiming God's character shifted.
  - [→] This matters for the Sabbath: if the standard of the restored image is fixed, then the fourth commandment is fixed _with it_. The image being rebuilt in you is the _same_ image — Sabbath included.

[Q] **"But isn't the Sabbath the one part of the law that got fulfilled / set aside?"**
→ A shadow is set aside when its substance comes (the ceremonial feast-sabbaths, Col 2:16-17 — those _were_ nailed to the cross). But the weekly Sabbath is not a shadow pointing forward; it is a memorial pointing back to a finished creation (Ex 20:11) — and forward to a finished re-creation. A memorial of God's completed work cannot expire while He is still the One who completes the work.

## God Names the Sign Himself — and It Is the Sabbath

Here the thesis stops being our inference and becomes God's own statement.

- **"That ye may know that I sanctify you"**
  - "Verily my sabbaths ye shall keep: for it is a sign between me and you throughout your generations; **that ye may know that I am the LORD that doth sanctify you**" (Ex 31:13)
  - "Moreover also I gave them my sabbaths, to be a sign between me and them, **that they might know that I am the LORD that sanctify them**" (Eze 20:12)
  - "hallow my sabbaths; and they shall be a sign between me and you, that ye may know that I am the LORD your God" (Eze 20:20)

- **Read the grammar — it carries the whole doctrine**
  - The Sabbath is a **sign**. A sign points to something real.
  - It points to this: **that you may KNOW** — not guess, not hope — that **I, the LORD, am the one DOING the sanctifying.**
  - Subject of the verb _sanctify_ = God. Not you. The Sabbath is not the sign that _you_ sanctify yourself by resting. It is the sign that _He_ is sanctifying you.
  - [→] Sanctification = the progressive restoration of the image (Col 3:10; 2 Cor 3:18). So the Sabbath is the sign that **the image is being restored — and that He is the one restoring it.**

[Q] **"Hold on — Exodus 31:13 says 'my sabbath*s*,' plural. Those are the ceremonial feast-sabbaths that were nailed to the cross (Col 2:16-17), not the weekly Sabbath. So this 'sign of sanctification' is about the abolished feast-days, isn't it?"**
→ This is the one objection that, if true, would sink the whole study. So let the passage answer for itself — read three more verses in the same breath (Miller's Rule 5: Scripture is its own expositor):

- **The text defines its own plural — and it is the weekly seventh day.** The same paragraph that says "my sabbath*s*" immediately drops to the singular and names which one: "Ye shall keep **the sabbath**... in the **seventh** is the sabbath of rest, holy to the LORD" (Ex 31:14-15), "a **perpetual covenant**... a sign between me and the children of Israel **for ever: for in six days the LORD made heaven and earth, and on the seventh day he rested**" (Ex 31:16-17). That last line is verbatim the **fourth commandment** (Ex 20:11) — creation, not the sanctuary calendar. The "sabbaths" of v.13 _are_ the weekly Sabbath; the chapter says so itself.
- **The plural never meant "ceremonial" — Scripture proves the reverse.** When God lists the ceremonial feast-sabbaths in Leviticus 23, He sets them apart _from_ the weekly Sabbath using the plural for the **weekly** one: "These are the feasts of the LORD... **Beside the sabbaths of the LORD**" (Lev 23:37-38). "The sabbath*s* of the LORD" (plural) = the recurring **weekly** Sabbath, expressly distinguished from the feasts. So a plural proves nothing for the skeptic; it is simply how Hebrew names the weekly institution recurring "throughout your generations."
- **The context is moral law, not feast law.** In Ezekiel the sabbath sits among "my **statutes**... my **judgments**, which if a man do, he shall even live in them" (Eze 20:11-13, 19-20) — the language of the abiding moral law a man _lives_ by, not the temporary ritual a man _outgrows_.
- [→] The objection actually backfires: the very passage it quotes ties the sign to **creation week and a perpetual covenant** — which is precisely the weekly Sabbath and nothing else. The skeptic's proof-text is our proof-text.

[DYK🔎] The same God who said "Let us make man in our image" (Gen 1:26) said "I am the LORD that doth sanctify you" (Ex 31:13). The Maker of the image is the Re-maker of the image. The Sabbath is His signature on both works.

- **Pioneer / Spirit-of-Prophecy confirmation** (a witness in harmony with the text, not an independent authority):

  > "The Sabbath given to the world as the sign of God as the Creator is also the sign of Him as the Sanctifier. **The power that created all things is the power that re-creates the soul in His own likeness.** To those who keep holy the Sabbath day it is the sign of sanctification.... oneness with Him in character." — _Testimonies, vol. 6_, p. 350

  > "Then the Sabbath is a sign of Christ's power to make us holy. And it is given to all whom Christ makes holy." — _Desire of Ages_, p. 288
  - [→] Note the exact link they draw: **create → re-create in His own likeness → the image → the Sabbath as its sign.** This is the bridge from "what salvation is" to "how you know it's happening."

## Whose Work Is It? — The Covenant of Promise

The Sabbath is a sign that **God** does the work. Scripture has one unbroken thread proving God does for us what we cannot do for ourselves: the covenant of _promise_.

- **The first promise — Eden**
  - "I will put enmity... it shall bruise thy head" (Gen 3:15)
  - The very first gospel word is a divine _I will_. Man does nothing but believe it. God promises to crush the serpent.

- **The new-covenant promise — the law written within**
  - "I will put my law in their inward parts, and write it in their hearts" (Jer 31:33)
  - Every verb is God's. The law moves from stone to flesh — _He_ does the writing.

- **The promise restated — new heart, indwelling Spirit**
  - "A new heart also will I give you... I will take away the stony heart... I will put my spirit within you, and cause you to walk in my statutes" (Eze 36:26-27)
  - Walking in His statutes is not the achievement that earns the new heart — it is the _evidence_ the Spirit entered. He causes the walking.

- **The dead don't raise themselves**
  - "Behold, I will cause breath to enter into you, and ye shall live" (Eze 37:5 — the dry bones)
  - You cannot resurrect yourself. The whole covenant is God animating what was dead.

- **The promise produces His nature in you — the keystone**
  - "Whereby are given unto us exceeding great and precious **promises: that by these ye might be partakers of the divine nature**" (2 Pet 1:4)
  - This is Genesis 1:26 reopened. To be a partaker of the divine nature **is** to bear the image again.
  - The mechanism named outright: **by the promises** → you partake of the divine nature → the image is restored. Not by striving. By the promise believed.

- **So believers are children of PROMISE — Isaac, not Ishmael**
  - "Abraham had two sons, the one by a bondmaid, the other by a freewoman. But he who was of the bondwoman was born after the flesh; but he of the freewoman was by promise" (Gal 4:22-23)
  - "Now we, brethren, **as Isaac was, are the children of promise**" (Gal 4:28)
  - Ishmael = the flesh trying to produce God's promise by its own effort. Isaac = God doing the impossible because He said He would.
  - [→] The true believer is an Isaac: the product of God's promise, not man's striving. The divine nature reproduced in him is God's doing (2 Pet 1:4), not his own achievement.

[ILL] Sarah was ninety. There was no way to _work_ Isaac into existence — Ishmael was that attempt, and it only made things worse. Isaac came one way only: God promised, and God did it. That is sanctification. You cannot work the image of God into your own character. He promised; He does it. Your part is to believe the promise — and to stop trying to be your own Sarah.

## So What Is Resting? — Confessing He Finishes the Work

Now the Sabbath's _meaning_ snaps into place.

- **Creation rest = work already finished**
  - "And on the seventh day God ended his work... And God blessed the seventh day, and sanctified it: because that in it he had rested" (Gen 2:1-3)
  - God did not rest because He was tired. He rested because the work was _complete_. The Sabbath is the monument to a _finished_ work.

- **To keep it is to enter HIS finished work, ceasing your own**
  - "There remaineth therefore a rest to the people of God. For he that is entered into his rest, he also **hath ceased from his own works, as God did from his**" (Heb 4:9-10)
  - "we which have believed do enter into rest... although the works were finished from the foundation of the world" (Heb 4:3)
  - [→] Sabbath rest = the weekly confession: _I am not the one finishing this. He is._ You lay down your tools because you have laid down the delusion that you sanctify yourself. It is Isaac's day, not Ishmael's.

- **The Lord of the Sabbath is the Creator who re-creates**
  - "The sabbath was made for man... Therefore the Son of man is Lord also of the sabbath" (Mark 2:27-28)
  - "All things were made by him; and without him was not any thing made that was made" (John 1:3)
  - The One who made you is the One who remakes you — and He set this day as the sign of both.

## Why THIS Commandment? — The One You Cannot Reason Your Way To

If the Sabbath is the chosen sign of pure trust, it makes sense that it is the one command unlike all the others.

- **Nine commandments are written on the conscience**
  - "the Gentiles, which have not the law, do by nature the things contained in the law... the work of the law written in their hearts" (Rom 2:14-15)
  - Every culture, with no Bible, independently arrives at: don't murder, don't steal, don't lie, honour your parents. Reason and conscience reconstruct them.

- **The Sabbath is the one you cannot deduce — only receive**
  - Nothing in nature tells you _the seventh day specifically_ is holy. No logic gets you from "rest is healthy" to "Saturday, not Sunday, is the LORD's." It must be **revealed** — "Remember" (Ex 20:8); He had to _tell_ us.
  - [→] That is precisely what qualifies it as the **sign**. A sign of loyalty must rest on His word alone, not on what you'd have figured out anyway. You keep it _because He said it_ — which is the whole nature of faith.

[Q] **"But Exodus 20:11 gives a reason — creation. So it's not arbitrary, right?"**
→ Correct — and that strengthens the point, it doesn't weaken it. The Sabbath actually has the _most_ stated reason of any commandment: "For in six days the LORD made heaven and earth... wherefore the LORD blessed the sabbath day" (Ex 20:11). But notice _what kind_ of reason it is: not a self-evident ethical truth your conscience supplies, but a **revealed historical fact about God** you could never have deduced. You don't reason your way to which day; you take Him at His word about what He did and when. So it is not arbitrary — it is the purest possible test of whether you will trust _His_ account of reality over your own. That is exactly the kind of command God would choose as the sign.

[DYK🔎] This is why the Sabbath, not any of the other nine, became the world's battleground. You can counterfeit a day of rest. You cannot counterfeit _which_ day without openly substituting human authority for God's word — which is the whole controversy in miniature: His word, or man's reasoning, as the ground of worship.

## The Flip — The Real Question

We have been asking the wrong question all along.

- **"Why should we keep it?"** frames the Sabbath as a burden needing justification — as if rest, the gift of Eden, has to defend itself.
- The honest question, once you see what the Sabbath signifies, is: **"Why do we resist it?"**

- **What resistance actually is**
  - To resist the sign of His sanctifying work is to resist _being sanctified by Him_ — to insist on doing God's work yourself.
  - It is to choose Ishmael over Isaac: the flesh laboring to produce what only the promise can give.
  - Scripture names the one and only thing that keeps a person out of God's rest — and it is not lack of effort:
    > "they could not enter in **because of unbelief**" (Heb 3:19)
    > "Let us labour therefore to enter into that rest, lest any man fall after the same example of **unbelief**" (Heb 4:11)
  - [→] The barrier to the rest is never works. It is unbelief — refusing to be a child of promise. Resistance to the Sabbath is, at bottom, resistance to letting God do for you what you cannot do for yourself.

[ILL] A drowning man fights the lifeguard. Not because he wants to drown — because surrendering feels like dying. To stop thrashing and go limp in another's arms is the hardest thing in the water. The Sabbath asks you to go limp: to stop working your own salvation and rest in the One who finished it. The resistance you feel toward it is the exact measure of how much you still trust your own thrashing.

## The Chain — Laid Out

1. **Salvation = the image restored** (Col 3:10; Rom 8:29) — Study 3's conclusion.
2. **God cannot change → the law/image cannot change** (Mal 3:6; Heb 13:8) — the standard is fixed, Sabbath included.
3. **God names the Sabbath His sign of sanctifying** (Ex 31:13; Eze 20:12) — "that ye may know that _I_ sanctify you."
4. **Sanctification = the image being re-created** — "the power that created... re-creates the soul in His own likeness" (6T 350).
5. **The work is by promise, not flesh** — Gen 3:15 → Jer 31:33 → Eze 36:26-27.
6. **The promise reproduces His nature in you** — "by these [promises]... partakers of the divine nature" (2 Pet 1:4) = the image again.
7. **So believers are children of promise** — Isaac, not Ishmael (Gal 4:22-28).
8. **Resting = confessing He finished it** — "ceased from his own works, as God did" (Heb 4:9-10).
9. **The Sabbath is the one command you cannot reason to** — must be revealed → the pure test of trust.
10. **Therefore the question flips** — not "why keep it?" but "why resist it?" — and resistance is unbelief (Heb 3:19; 4:11).

Every link is a plain verse you can read with your own open Bible. The Sabbath is not a freestanding duty bolted onto the gospel. It is the gospel's weekly sign: _God is restoring His image in me, and I have stopped trying to do it myself._

---

## Appeal

The Sabbath was never a test of whether you can rest one day in seven. It is the test of whether you will let God finish what only God can finish — the rebuilding of His own image in you.

To keep it from the heart is to say: _I am Isaac. I am here because He promised, and He keeps His promises. I lay down my work because His is finished. The image I could never repair, He is restoring — and this day is His sign that He is doing it._

So stop asking the Sabbath to justify itself. Ask your own heart the better question. When you feel the pull to resist the day — to fill it, to work through it, to reason it away — that resistance is not really about a day. It is the old Ishmael in you, still trying to do God's work with God's promise standing right there.

Will you cease from your own works, as He did from His? Will you receive the sign — and with it, the thing the sign points to: His own image, re-created in you, by His own hand?

> _"To all who receive the Sabbath as a sign of Christ's creative and redeeming power, it will be a delight. Seeing Christ in it, they delight themselves in Him.... While it calls to mind the lost peace of Eden, it tells of peace restored through the Saviour."_ — Desire of Ages, p. 289
