## End-Time Events Timeline: The Beginning of the End — The Final Countdown, Study 1

### The Reward of the Wicked

> "Only with thine eyes shalt thou behold and see the reward of the wicked." This series traces every event from now until Christ's coming and beyond.

- **Ps. 91:8** — Only with our eyes will we behold the reward of the wicked; the faithful do not receive it, but Scripture warns that such a reward is real.
- **Dan. 12:1** — A time of trouble such as never was is coming, yet God's people will be delivered.
- **Rev. 14:9, 10** — Those who worship the beast and his image and receive his mark in hand or forehead shall drink the wine of the wrath of God.
- **Rev. 16:1, 2** — The vials of God's wrath are poured out on those who have the mark of the beast and worship his image.

### Two Books, One Prophecy

> "The books of Daniel and the Revelation are one. One is a prophecy, the other a revelation; one a book sealed, the other a book opened." — CTr 344.4

- **Dan. 12:4** — Daniel is told to shut up the words and seal the book to the time of the end, when many shall run to and fro and knowledge be increased; the time of the end is 1798.
- **Rev. 10:2** — In the angel's hand is a little book open, his right foot on the sea and his left on the earth — the companion open book to Daniel's sealed one.

### The Prophetic Scope of Daniel and Revelation

> An overview of where each chapter lands in history, so symbols in one book can be compared with the other.

- **Dan. 2** — The dream image of gold, silver, brass, and iron traces world kingdoms from Babylon to the end of time.
- **Dan. 7** — The lion, bear, leopard, and dreadful beast with ten horns and a little horn give closer focus on these kingdoms, especially until 1798.
- **Dan. 8** — The ram and he-goat carry the history from Medo-Persia through Greece and beyond.
- **Dan. 9** — The seventy weeks prophecy concerning Jesus and the sanctuary.
- **Dan. 11** — Beginning again at Medo-Persia ("there are three kings yet in Persia"), tracing kings through history.
- **Dan. 12** — Michael stands up, probation closes, and the time of trouble follows, with the time prophecies recapped.
- **Rev. 2-3** — The seven churches portray church history all the way to Christ's coming.
- **Rev. 5-8** — The seven seals, including the four horsemen of the apocalypse.
- **Rev. 8-9** — The seven trumpets.
- **Rev. 10-11** — History from around 1798 to 1844.
- **Rev. 12** — The history of pagan Rome.
- **Rev. 13** — Papal Rome and America enter the prophetic stage.
- **Rev. 14** — The 144,000 and the three final (three angels') messages; the 144,000 also appear in Rev. 7.
- **Rev. 15-18** — The judgment of the beast — a whole block of chapters on this last-day subject.
- **Rev. 20** — The thousand years, or millennium.
- **Rev. 21-22** — The new heaven and the new earth.

### Why and How We Study Prophecy

- **John 16:13** — The Spirit of truth guides into all truth and shows things to come; prophecy is for everyone willing to be led.
- **Dan. 12:10** — Many shall be purified and made white; the wicked shall not understand, but the wise shall understand.
- **James 1:5** — If any lack wisdom, let him ask of God, who gives liberally, and it shall be given him.
- **Rev. 1:1-3** — The Revelation of Jesus Christ shows His servants things which must shortly come to pass; blessed are they that hear and _keep_ — prophecy demands action. 8T 301
- **2 Tim. 3:16** — All Scripture is given by inspiration and is profitable for doctrine; all of it helps us understand prophecy. (Review and Herald, Nov 25, 1884, endorses Father Miller's Bible rules.)
- **Isa. 28:10** — Precept upon precept, line upon line, here a little and there a little — let the Bible explain the Bible.

### Bible Words and Their Meaning

> A foundational exercise: let Scripture define its own symbols. By Miller's Rule 11, a word is figurative when a literal reading would do violence to the laws of nature.

- **Dan. 7:17, 23** — The four great beasts are four kings, and the fourth beast is the fourth kingdom; a beast represents a kingdom.
- **Dan. 7:24** — The ten horns out of this kingdom are ten kings; horns are kings or divisions of a kingdom.
- **Rev. 17:15** — The waters where the harlot sits are peoples, multitudes, nations, and tongues; sea and waters mean peoples — so earth (dry ground) means an unpopulated area.
- **Jer. 6:2** — The daughter of Zion is likened to a comely and delicate woman; a woman represents God's church — pure or impure.
- **Ezek. 4:6** — God appoints each day for a year; a prophetic day equals a year, so 1260 days equals 1260 years.
- **Num. 14:34** — "Each day for a year" — the same day-for-a-year principle confirmed.
- **Isa. 28:7** — The priest and prophet err through wine and stumble in judgment; the wine of Babylon means false doctrine that corrupts one's view of prophecy. Drink instead the pure, unfermented wine of God's truth.

### A Preview of the Final Events

> Each event below is anchored to one key text; later studies will define the beast and prove each point from the Bible.

- **Rev. 13:15** — The image of the beast is given life, speaks, and causes that as many as would not worship it should be killed — the image of the beast and the death decree.
- **Rev. 13:16** — He causes all, small and great, rich and poor, free and bond, to receive a mark in their right hand or in their foreheads — the mark of the beast.
- **Rev. 13:17** — No man may buy or sell except he that has the mark, the name of the beast, or the number of his name — the no-buy-no-sell crisis.
- **Dan. 12:1** — A time of trouble such as never was, after which God's people are delivered — the time of trouble.
- **Jer. 30:7** — That day is great, none is like it; it is the time of Jacob's trouble, but he shall be saved out of it.
- **Rev. 7:1-4** — Four angels hold the four winds until the servants of God are sealed in their foreheads — 144,000 sealed; prophetic events are held back for the sealing.
- **Dan. 12:1; Matt. 25:10; Gen. 7:16** — When Michael (Jesus) stands up, probation closes; like the shut door at the wedding and the Lord shutting Noah in the ark, no one can then enter — the close of probation.
- **Rev. 15:1** — Seven angels have the seven last plagues, in which is filled up the wrath of God — the seven last plagues.
- **Rev. 17:12** — The ten horns are ten kings who receive no kingdom as yet but receive power as kings one hour with the beast — the coming new world order.

### A Diligent Study to Avoid the Mark

> "When God sends to men warnings... He requires every person endowed with reasoning powers to heed the message." — DD 37

- **Rev. 14:9-11** — The fearful judgments against worshiping the beast and his image should lead all to a diligent study of the prophecies, to learn what the mark of the beast is and how to avoid receiving it. DD 37
- **2 Tim. 4:3** — The time has fully come when men will not endure sound doctrine; the multitudes reject Bible truth because it interferes with worldly desires. DD 37

### Questions Answered: The Special Resurrection and the Seven Thunders

- **Dan. 12:1, 2** — Many (not all) who sleep in the dust shall awake, some to everlasting life and some to shame — a special resurrection around the time of trouble, raising both the righteous who died under the three angels' messages and those who pierced Christ.
- **Rev. 10:3, 4** — Seven thunders utter their voices, but John is told to seal them and write them not — the one sealing passage in Revelation, a window of events to be understood only after they occur.
- **Rev. 10:5-7** — The angel swears that there should be time no longer; when the seventh angel sounds, the mystery of God is finished — prophetic time ends about 1798 with the opening of Daniel's book. (CTr 344.4: the seven thunders were "a delineation of events that would transpire under the first and second angels' messages" — between 1837 and 1843.)

### Questions Answered: Beasts, Kingdoms, and the Stone

- **Dan. 8:1-7** — Daniel's vision of a ram and a flying he-goat breaks the laws of nature, signaling figurative, prophetic language.
- **Dan. 8:20, 21** — The ram is the kings of Media and Persia; the rough goat is the king of Grecia, and the great horn is its first king (Alexander) — proving a beast is a kingdom, interpreted in the same chapter.
- **Rev. 17:12** — The ten kings who reign one hour with the beast are the powers of the end-time; literal kingdom names like Medo-Persia, Grecia, and Rome are not figurative because they do no violence to nature.
- **Dan. 2** — The kingdoms run from Babylon through Medo-Persia, Greece, pagan and papal Rome, America, and the final ten kings (the same ten of Rev. 17); the stone that smites the image is God's kingdom, not yet fulfilled — we are still "in the feet."
