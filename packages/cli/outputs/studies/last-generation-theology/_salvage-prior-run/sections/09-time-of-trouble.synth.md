## The Time of Trouble — Standing Without a Mediator

> Before Christ comes, His intercession ceases and every character is fixed forever: the saints then live in the sight of a holy God without a mediator — plunged into the time of Jacob's trouble, yet saved out of it, because their sins went beforehand to judgment while the Advocate still pleaded.

**Every case decided first — the cleansing of the sanctuary — Dan. 8:14; Rev. 3:5; Dan. 12:10:**

- _Dan. 8:14._ "Unto two thousand and three hundred days; then shall the sanctuary be cleansed" — the cleansing of the sanctuary is the final phase of Christ's priestly work: the investigative judgment that decides every case before He ceases to intercede.
- _Rev. 3:5._ "He that overcometh... I will not blot out his name out of the book of life, but I will confess his name before my Father, and before his angels" — Christ's confession of the overcomer's name before the Father is His intercessory act, finished for each case in the judgment; the overcomer of "Complete Victory — Overcoming as He Overcame" is the one whose name stands unblotted when the pleading ends.
- _Dan. 12:10._ "Many shall be purified, and made white, and tried; but the wicked shall do wickedly... but the wise shall understand" — the purifying is finished BEFORE Michael stands up; the victory work belongs to the hour when the Advocate still pleads.
- _GC 490.2._ White: "When the work of the investigative judgment closes, the destiny of all will have been decided for life or death. Probation is ended a short time before the appearing of the Lord in the clouds of heaven" — the sanctuary-cleansing of _Dan. 8:14_ runs straight into the Rev. 22:11-12 decree.

**The decree that closes probation — Rev. 22:11-12:**

- _Rev. 22:11._ "He that is unjust, let him be unjust still: and he which is filthy, let him be filthy still: and he that is righteous, let him be righteous still: and he that is holy, let him be holy still" — the word that fixes every character forever; no one changes sides after it is spoken.
  - **the close of probation** = the moment Christ's priestly mediation ends and every case is decided — each character sealed in what it has become, the decree spoken, the temple shut (_Rev. 22:11-12_; _Rev. 15:8_, "no man was able to enter into the temple"; _Lev. 16:17_; _Dan. 8:14_; _Dan. 12:1_). Defined in full at the close.
- _Rev. 22:12._ "And, behold, I come quickly; and my reward is with me, to give every man according as his work shall be" — the fixing of character and the rewarding advent are one connected event; the door of mercy shuts because He comes.
- _GC 613.2._ White: "Then Jesus ceases His intercession in the sanctuary above. He lifts His hands and with a loud voice says, 'It is done;'... 'He that is unjust, let him be unjust still... and he that is holy, let him be holy still.' Revelation 22:11. Every case has been decided for life or death. Christ has made the atonement for His people and blotted out their sins" — the Rev. 22:11 decree IS the ceasing of intercession.
- _EW 71.2._ White: "But there will be no time then to do it and no Mediator to plead their cause before the Father. Before this time the awfully solemn declaration has gone forth, 'He that is unjust, let him be unjust still... and he that is holy, let him be holy still'" — no Mediator after the declaration; whatever overcoming remains must be done before it.

**The temple shut — no ministration while the plagues fall — Lev. 16:17; Rev. 15:8; 16:1:**

- _Lev. 16:17._ "And there shall be no man in the tabernacle of the congregation when he goeth in to make an atonement in the holy place, until he come out" — the type: while the high priest finishes the atonement, no man may be in the sanctuary; when he comes out, the atonement is done.
- _Rev. 15:8._ "And the temple was filled with smoke from the glory of God, and from his power; and no man was able to enter into the temple, till the seven plagues of the seven angels were fulfilled" — the antitype: the heavenly sanctuary closed by God's own glory; no priest, no ministration, no intercession while the plagues fall.
- _Rev. 16:1._ "Go your ways, and pour out the vials of the wrath of God upon the earth" — the plagues begin the instant the temple closes (15:8 → 16:1); the wrath is unmingled with mercy precisely because the Mediator no longer stands between God and man.
- _DAR 643.1._ Smith: "there will therefore be no ministration in the sanctuary during this time... for Christ is then no longer a mediator; mercy, which has long stayed the hand of vengeance, pleads no more; the servants of God are all sealed" — the pioneer reading of Rev. 15:8: closed temple = ended mediation = a sealed people.
- _GC 614.1._ White: "When He leaves the sanctuary, darkness covers the inhabitants of the earth. In that fearful time the righteous must live in the sight of a holy God without an intercessor" — the section's key phrase, matching the shut temple of _Rev. 15:8_.

**Michael stands up — the trouble such as never was — Dan. 12:1:**

- _Dan. 12:1._ "And at that time shall Michael stand up, the great prince which standeth for the children of thy people: and there shall be a time of trouble, such as never was since there was a nation... and at that time thy people shall be delivered, every one that shall be found written in the book" — Christ rises from His mediatorial seat, His priestly work finished; the instant He ceases to intercede the unequalled trouble breaks — yet every name written in the book is delivered out of it.
- _MWV2 108.2._ Miller: "Michael, in this passage, must mean Christ; he is the great Prince, and Prince of princes" — the earliest pioneer voice: Michael who "stands up" is Christ, the priestly Prince whose standing marks the end of His pleading.
- _GC 425.1._ White: "Those who are living upon the earth when the intercession of Christ shall cease in the sanctuary above are to stand in the sight of a holy God without a mediator. Their robes must be spotless, their characters must be purified from sin by the blood of sprinkling" — the thesis in her own words: the purifying must be finished beforehand, because no mediator remains after.

**The time of Jacob's trouble — the name and the type — Jer. 30:6-7; Gen. 32:24-28; Hos. 12:4:**

- _Jer. 30:6._ "wherefore do I see every man with his hands on his loins, as a woman in travail, and all faces are turned into paleness?" — the anguish after mercy's door has shut: travail-pangs and pale faces, with no intercessor to plead the case.
- _Jer. 30:7._ "Alas! for that day is great, so that none is like it: it is even the time of Jacob's trouble; but he shall be saved out of it" — the name text, matching Daniel's "such as never was"; the saints are not saved FROM the trouble but delivered THROUGH it.
  - **the time of Jacob's trouble** = the unequalled final anguish of the sealed saints after probation closes — wrestling without an intercessor as Jacob wrestled alone in the night, yet "saved out of it" (_Jer. 30:6-7_; _Dan. 12:1_; _Gen. 32:24, 26, 28_; _Hos. 12:4_). Defined in full at the close.
- _Gen. 32:24._ "And Jacob was left alone; and there wrestled a man with him until the breaking of the day" — the historical type: alone in the night, expecting Esau's sword, wrestling the Angel — the sealed saint pleading alone in the final crisis.
- _Gen. 32:26._ "I will not let thee go, except thou bless me" — the language of the saints in the time of trouble: clinging in agony to a God whose face seems hidden, prevailing not by merit but by holding fast His covenant.
- _Gen. 32:28._ "Thy name shall be called no more Jacob, but Israel: for as a prince hast thou power with God and with men, and hast prevailed" — the supplanter becomes the prince who prevails; the victory granted only because his sin had been confessed and put away before that night (_GC 620.1_).
- _Hos. 12:4._ "Yea, he had power over the angel, and prevailed: he wept, and made supplication unto him" — the inspired commentary on the wrestling: he prevailed by tears and clinging prayer, not force; so the saints overcome by the wrestling of a heart that will not let God go.
- _GC 619.3._ White: "They lay hold of the strength of God as Jacob laid hold of the Angel; and the language of their souls is: 'I will not let Thee go, except Thou bless me'" — Gen. 32:26 drawn straight onto the saints in the trouble.
- _GC 620.1._ White: "if the people of God had unconfessed sins to appear before them while tortured with fear and anguish, they would be overwhelmed... But while they have a deep sense of their unworthiness, they have no concealed wrongs to reveal. Their sins have gone beforehand to judgment and have been blotted out" — why they can endure without a mediator: nothing remains on the books to condemn them.

**Only the sealed stand — Rev. 7:3; Eze. 9:6; Mal. 3:2:**

- _Rev. 7:3._ "Hurt not the earth, neither the sea, nor the trees, till we have sealed the servants of our God in their foreheads" — before probation closes, the servants of God are sealed — settled into the truth so they cannot be moved; the sealing must be finished before the temple shuts (_Rev. 15:8_), and the sealed alone stand without a mediator.
- _Eze. 9:6._ "but come not near any man upon whom is the mark: and begin at my sanctuary" — the type of the sealing before the slaughter: the destroyer may not touch any who bear the mark; and judgment begins at the house of God (cf. _1 Pet. 4:17_).
- _Mal. 3:2._ "But who may abide the day of his coming? and who shall stand when he appeareth? for he is like a refiner's fire, and like fullers' soap" — the question the whole crisis answers: only those refined BEFORE the day, cleansed by the blood while the Advocate still pleaded, can stand in the sight of a holy God without an intercessor.
- _COL 69.1._ White: "When the character of Christ shall be perfectly reproduced in His people, then He will come to claim them as His own" — the character-perfecting must be finished while He pleads; the ripening of that people is the burden of "The Harvest — A People in Whom Christ Is Vindicated."

**DEFINITION — THE TIME OF TROUBLE — STANDING WITHOUT A MEDIATOR =** before the second coming Christ's priestly intercession ends: the cleansing of the sanctuary (_Dan. 8:14_) — the investigative judgment — decides every case while Christ still confesses names before the Father (_Rev. 3:5_), and the decree "he that is unjust, let him be unjust still... he that is holy, let him be holy still" fixes every character forever, "a short time before the appearing of the Lord" (_Rev. 22:11-12_; _GC 490.2_; _GC 613.2_; _EW 71.2_). The heavenly temple, like the tabernacle on the Day of Atonement (_Lev. 16:17_), is then shut — "filled with smoke... and no man was able to enter" (_Rev. 15:8_) — and the plagues fall unmingled with mercy (_Rev. 16:1_; _DAR 643.1_) while Michael, Christ the great Prince (_MWV2 108.2_), stands up from His mediatorial seat (_Dan. 12:1_). The saints then "live in the sight of a holy God without an intercessor" (_GC 614.1_; _GC 425.1_) — not without Christ's presence or the Spirit, but with every case decided — through "a time of trouble, such as never was," "even the time of Jacob's trouble" (_Dan. 12:1_; _Jer. 30:7_): like Jacob alone in the night they wrestle with travail-pangs and pale faces (_Jer. 30:6_; _Gen. 32:24_), clinging with "I will not let thee go, except thou bless me" (_Gen. 32:26_; _GC 619.3_), prevailing as he did by weeping and supplication (_Hos. 12:4_; _Gen. 32:28_). They endure only because the preparation was finished FIRST: purified, made white, and tried before Michael stood up (_Dan. 12:10_); sealed in their foreheads before the winds hurt (_Rev. 7:3_; _Eze. 9:6_); refined before the day that asks "who shall stand?" (_Mal. 3:2_); their "sins have gone beforehand to judgment and have been blotted out" (_GC 620.1_). This is why the victory message precedes the crisis — a people must cease from sin while the Advocate still pleads, that the character of Christ be perfectly reproduced before He comes to claim them (_COL 69.1_) — and it ends in deliverance: "he shall be saved out of it" (_Jer. 30:7_), "delivered, every one that shall be found written in the book" (_Dan. 12:1_).

**Symbols defined here:**

- **the close of probation** = the moment Christ's priestly mediation ends and every character is fixed forever — every case decided in the cleansing of the sanctuary, the decree of Rev. 22:11 spoken because "I come quickly," the temple shut by the glory of God with no ministration while the plagues fall, Michael standing up from His mediatorial seat (_Rev. 22:11-12_; _Rev. 15:8_; _Lev. 16:17_; _Dan. 8:14_; _Dan. 12:1_; _Mal. 3:2_).
- **the time of Jacob's trouble** = the unequalled final anguish of the righteous after probation closes — the sealed saints, marked so the destroyer cannot touch them, wrestling without an intercessor as Jacob wrestled alone, prevailing by weeping and supplication because their sins have gone beforehand to judgment, and "saved out of it," every name written in the book (_Jer. 30:6-7_; _Dan. 12:1_; _Gen. 32:24, 26, 28_; _Hos. 12:4_; _Rev. 7:3_; _Eze. 9:6_; _Rev. 3:5_).

**Symbols carried:** the overcomer — the one whose name Christ confesses and never blots out (see "Complete Victory — Overcoming as He Overcame"); sin as consented transgression of the law — the saints stand here because they ceased from it while the Advocate still pleaded (see "What Sin Is — Transgression, Not Inheritance"); sinful flesh — the saints stand in the same fallen nature in which Christ overcame, He never having consented to sin (see "The Second Adam — The Nature Christ Took"); the faith of Jesus and the third angel's message — the judgment-hour message that ripens the people who can stand (see "The Commandments of God and the Faith of Jesus"; see "Righteousness by Faith — The Third Angel’s Message in Verity"); the character of Christ perfectly reproduced — ripened in full in "The Harvest — A People in Whom Christ Is Vindicated."

**For discussion:**

1. The decree of _Rev. 22:11_ does not change anyone; it fixes each person in what he has already become. Jacob prevailed in his night of wrestling only because his sin had been confessed and put away before it came (_Gen. 32:26-28_; _GC 620.1_). What would the time of trouble find "unconfessed" in you if the decree went forth tonight — and what does it mean that the blotting out of sins is offered only NOW, while the Advocate still pleads (_Rev. 3:5_; _Dan. 8:14_)?
2. Jacob prevailed not by force but by weeping and supplication (_Hos. 12:4_), and only because his sin was already confessed before that night (_Gen. 32:28_; _GC 620.1_, "their sins have gone beforehand to judgment"). How does daily confession now determine whether the hidden face of God then produces despair or prevailing prayer?
3. _Dan. 12:10_ puts the purifying BEFORE the standing up of Michael: "many shall be purified, and made white, and tried." If the sealing settles the servants of God so they cannot be moved (_Rev. 7:3_; _Eze. 9:6_), which daily choices are settling you — into the truth, or into the "still" of _Rev. 22:11_ on the wrong side of it?
