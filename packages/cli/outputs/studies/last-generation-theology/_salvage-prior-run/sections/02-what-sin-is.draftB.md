## What Sin Is — Transgression, Not Inheritance

> Sin is the transgression of the law — born only when the will consents, so guilt is by choice, never by birth; yet the fallen nature we do inherit is a real bondage, a bent that corrupts and enslaves the whole man until Christ delivers — corruption inherited: affirmed; guilt inherited: denied.

**Sin is defined by the law, not by the birth — 1 John 3:4:**

- _1 John 3:4._ "Whosoever committeth sin transgresseth also the law: for sin is the transgression of the law" — the Bible's own definition: sin IS the transgression of the law, a committed act against a known standard — "whosoever COMMITTETH" — not a condition of birth.
  - **sin** = the consented transgression of God's law — a chosen act against a known standard, born when desire conceives, when the will yields (_1 John 3:4_, "sin is the transgression of the law"; _James 1:14-15_, brought forth only "when lust hath conceived"; _John 8:34_, "Whosoever committeth sin"; _Ezek. 18:20_; _Rom. 3:23_, "all have sinned"). Defined by the law, never by lineage; guilt attaches to the choice, not to the presence of a fallen inclination.
- _AA 555.1._ White: "'Whosoever committeth sin,' he writes, 'transgresseth also the law: for sin is the transgression of the law.'" — she lets John define sin, quoting the text verbatim: sin is dealt with as a DOING, an act against the law.

**Sin has a genealogy — desire solicits, the will conceives — James 1:14-15:**

- _James 1:14._ "But every man is tempted, when he is drawn away of his own lust, and enticed" — the drawing and the enticing are temptation, not yet sin; the bent solicits.
- _James 1:15._ "Then when lust hath conceived, it bringeth forth sin: and sin, when it is finished, bringeth forth death" — sin has a birthday: it is BORN only when lust CONCEIVES — when the will consents; the genealogy runs desire → consent → sin → death, and guilt enters at the consent.
- _HP 251.3._ White: "There is no sin in having temptation, but sin comes in when temptation is yielded to." — the precise James 1 distinction: the solicitation of the fallen nature is not sin; sin is born at the yielding.
- _MYP 430.1._ White: "It is Satan's act to tempt you, but your own act to yield. It is not in the power of all the host of Satan to force the tempted to transgress. There is no excuse for sin." — guilt is located at the personal act of yielding; no inherited or external force compels transgression.
- _5T 177.1._ White: "An impure thought tolerated, an unholy desire cherished, and the soul is contaminated... 'Then when lust hath conceived, it bringeth forth sin: and sin, when it is finished, bringeth forth death.'" — she traces sin's birth from a CHERISHED desire to the act: conceived by the will, not by birth.
- _2MCP 685.2._ White: "This is the governing power in the nature of man—the power of decision, of choice. Everything depends on the right action of the will... Many will go down to ruin while hoping and desiring to overcome their evil propensities. They do not yield the will to God." — the will is the deciding power; guilt and ruin turn on its action, not on the mere existence of evil propensities.

**Guilt is personal and non-transferable — Ezek. 18:4, 20; Deut. 24:16:**

- _Ezek. 18:4._ "Behold, all souls are mine; as the soul of the father, so also the soul of the son is mine: the soul that sinneth, it shall die" — God claims every soul directly; each dies for its own sin, not the parent's (and the "soul" that dies is the whole man dying for his own sin, not a ghost punished for another's — see "Dust and Breath — What Man Is").
- _Ezek. 18:20._ "The soul that sinneth, it shall die. The son shall not bear the iniquity of the father, neither shall the father bear the iniquity of the son" — the flat rejection of inherited GUILT: no man is charged with another's iniquity, Adam's included.
- _Deut. 24:16._ "The fathers shall not be put to death for the children, neither shall the children be put to death for the fathers: every man shall be put to death for his own sin" — the judicial principle behind Ezekiel, written into the statute law of Israel: inherited guilt is foreign to God's law.
- _1MR 142.3._ White, preached at Minneapolis 1888 — the very session where Waggoner and Jones pressed righteousness by faith: "Well, you say, it was Adam's sin. You say, I am not guilty of that, and I am not responsible for his guilt and fall. Here all these natural tendencies are in me and I am not to blame if I act out these natural tendencies. Who is to blame? Is God?" — both sides held in one breath: not guilty of Adam's fall (inherited guilt denied), yet the natural tendencies really "in me" (corruption affirmed) — and blame attaches when we ACT them out.

**Every mouth stopped — because all have sinned — Rom. 3:19, 23; 5:12:**

- _Rom. 3:19._ "that every mouth may be stopped, and all the world may become guilty before God" — the law silences every defense; but the ground of the guilt is stated four verses on.
- _Rom. 3:23._ "For all have sinned, and come short of the glory of God" — the universal indictment rests on ACTS: "have sinned" is an active verb; every mouth is stopped because all have DONE, not because all were born.
- _Rom. 5:12._ "Wherefore, as by one man sin entered into the world, and death by sin; and so death passed upon all men, for that all have sinned" — death passed upon all, not because all inherited Adam's guilt, but "for that ALL HAVE SINNED": the universal reign of death traces to universal actual sinning, made possible by the fallen nature Adam introduced.

**Yet the inherited corruption is real — shapen in iniquity — Ps. 51:5; Eph. 2:3:**

- _Ps. 51:5._ "Behold, I was shapen in iniquity; and in sin did my mother conceive me" — the inherited corruption is real: David is bent from birth. This is a fallen NATURE, not a charged crime — a condition to be delivered from, not a guilt to be sentenced for.
  - **the flesh / fallen nature** = the inherited corruption — a real, terrible bent inclining toward evil, holding dominion over the natural man and enslaving him until Christ delivers; "sin that dwelleth in me" as a resident power, but NOT itself guilt — it condemns only when obeyed (_Ps. 51:5_, "shapen in iniquity"; _Eph. 2:3_, "by nature the children of wrath"; _Rom. 7:14_, "sold under sin"; _Rom. 7:17-18_, "sin that dwelleth in me... in my flesh"; _Rom. 7:23_, "a law in my members"; _Rom. 7:24-25_, delivered "through Jesus Christ"). Corruption inherited: affirmed. Guilt inherited: denied.
- _Eph. 2:3._ "and were by nature the children of wrath, even as others" — the fallen nature is real and universal; and mark where the wrath is bound: to "fulfilling the desires of the flesh and of the mind" — the nature condemns when OBEYED, not merely possessed.
- _CHR 26.2._ Waggoner: "David had all the passions of human nature. He says of himself, 'Behold I was shapen in iniquity; and in sin did my mother conceive me.' Psalm 51:5." — the inherited fallen nature established from Scripture: the flesh carries "weaknesses and sinful tendencies," yet in the same argument no actual sin is conceded.
- _CHR 91.1._ Waggoner: "We likewise can all say, with David, 'Behold, I was shapen in iniquity; and in sin did my mother conceive me.' ... As a consequence, we are by nature so weak that we cannot do the things that we would." — Psalm 51:5 read as the confession of an inherited WEAKNESS that disables (echoing Rom. 7) — a bent to be delivered from, not a guilt to be sentenced for.

**A law in the members — the bent enslaves until Christ delivers — Rom. 7:14-25; John 8:34:**

- _Rom. 7:14._ "For we know that the law is spiritual: but I am carnal, sold under sin" — the natural man is SOLD under sin: the fallen nature holds dominion — a slavery, not a mere guilt-tag.
- _Rom. 7:17-18._ "it is no more I that do it, but sin that dwelleth in me. For I know that in me (that is, in my flesh,) dwelleth no good thing" — Paul distinguishes the indwelling sin in the flesh from the willing self: the nature is a resident power, corrupting, but not identical with the person's guilt.
- _Rom. 7:23._ "But I see another law in my members, warring against the law of my mind, and bringing me into captivity to the law of sin which is in my members" — the inherited bent operates as a LAW of the nature: it wars, it takes captive, it enslaves the natural man.
- _John 8:34._ "Whosoever committeth sin is the servant of sin" — Christ ties the bondage to the COMMITTING: the servitude follows the act; and the Son breaks the dominion (v. 36).
- _Rom. 7:24-25._ "O wretched man that I am! who shall deliver me from the body of this death? I thank God through Jesus Christ our Lord" — the nature is not a life sentence: deliverance comes "through Jesus Christ." The bent enslaves UNTIL Christ delivers — corruption is curable; guilt would not be.
- _WOR 122.1._ Waggoner, on Rom. 7:23: "'I see another law in my members, warring against the law of my mind...' ... This sin is in our flesh... But we are slaves. Whosoever commits sin is the slave of sin. John 8:34." — the law in the members is sin dwelling in the flesh, holding the natural man in bondage until liberty comes in Christ.
- _LOF_ATJ 127.6._ Jones: "that same degenerate, sinful flesh is there with its same tendencies and desires. But the individual is no longer subject to these... a power that conquers, brings under, crucifies, and keeps under, the flesh, sinful as it is, with all its affections and lusts." — the whole thesis in one line: the inherited flesh with its tendencies REMAINS even in the converted, yet the man is "no longer subject" to it — corruption real and enslaving, yet conquerable, not guilt.
- _CH 440.1._ White: "Those who put their trust in Christ are not to be enslaved by any hereditary or cultivated habit or tendency... Whatever may be our inherited or cultivated tendencies to wrong, we can overcome through the power that He is ready to impart." — the hereditary tendency named as real bondage of the lower nature, and named as OVERCOMABLE: precisely a bent, not a verdict.

**DEFINITION — WHAT SIN IS — TRANSGRESSION, NOT INHERITANCE =** sin is the transgression of the law (_1 John 3:4_) — a consented act, born only "when lust hath conceived," when the will yields (_James 1:14-15_; _HP 251.3_; _MYP 430.1_) — and guilt is therefore personal and non-transferable: "the son shall not bear the iniquity of the father" (_Ezek. 18:4, 20_; _Deut. 24:16_); every mouth is stopped and death reigns universal because "ALL HAVE SINNED" (_Rom. 3:19, 23_; _Rom. 5:12_), not because all were born. Yet what we DO inherit is real and terrible: a fallen nature, "shapen in iniquity" (_Ps. 51:5_), "by nature the children of wrath" (_Eph. 2:3_), "sold under sin" with "a law in my members" warring and taking captive (_Rom. 7:14, 17-18, 23_; _WOR 122.1_) — a bent that corrupts and ENSLAVES the natural man, but is not itself guilt; it condemns only when obeyed (_Eph. 2:3_; _1MR 142.3_; _2MCP 685.2_), and it enslaves only until Christ delivers (_Rom. 7:24-25_; _John 8:34, 36_; _LOF_ATJ 127.6_; _CH 440.1_). Distinguish plainly and hold both: original-sin GUILT (guilty at birth) — rejected; inherited CORRUPTION (a bondage to be broken) — affirmed. This distinction is the hinge of the handbook: it strips the sinner of every heredity-excuse ("there is no excuse for sin"), and it frees the doctrine of the incarnation — since guilt is not a substance bred in the flesh, the need it defines is not acquittal from Adam's crime but deliverance from sin's power (see "What Salvation Really Is — Saved From Sin"); because nature is not sin, Christ could take our fallen nature and be without sin (see "The Second Adam — The Nature Christ Took"); and because sin is in the consent, the consent can — by the same Christ — be forever withheld (see "Complete Victory — Overcoming as He Overcame").

**Symbols defined here:**

- **sin** = the consented transgression of God's law — a chosen act against a known standard, born when desire conceives, when the will yields; guilt attaches to the choice, never to the mere presence of a fallen inclination (_1 John 3:4_; _James 1:14-15_; _John 8:34_; _Ezek. 18:20_; _Rom. 3:23_).
- **the flesh / fallen nature** = the inherited corruption — a real bent that inclines toward evil and holds the natural man in bondage until Christ delivers; "sin that dwelleth in me" as a resident power, but not itself guilt — it condemns only when obeyed (_Ps. 51:5_; _Eph. 2:3_; _Rom. 7:14, 17-18, 23-25_).

**Symbols carried:** the living soul — the one whole man, dust + breath, who sins as one and dies as one, "the soul that sinneth, it shall die" (see "Dust and Breath — What Man Is"); the fallen nature defined here is the very flesh the Son of God took, without its sin (see "The Second Adam — The Nature Christ Took"); the deliverance of _Rom. 7:25_ is entered by the new birth (see "Born of Water and of the Spirit — How He Lived, How We Begin") and carried to full dominion-breaking in the overcomer (see "Complete Victory — Overcoming as He Overcame").

**For discussion:**

1. James 1:14-15 puts the birth of sin at the moment lust "conceives" — and White adds, "There is no sin in having temptation, but sin comes in when temptation is yielded to" (_HP 251.3_). What does this distinction free a tempted believer from, and what does it leave him responsible for?
2. "The son shall not bear the iniquity of the father" (_Ezek. 18:20_). What excuse drawn from heredity, upbringing, or "that's just my nature" does this verse take away — and what hope does _Rom. 7:24-25_ put in its place?
3. Paul is "sold under sin," captive to "a law in my members" (_Rom. 7:14, 23_). For the bent you inherited, are you relying on resolve — "hoping and desiring to overcome" (_2MCP 685.2_) — or on the delivering power of Christ (_CH 440.1_)? How would the difference show in practice this week?
