## Righteousness by Faith — The Third Angel’s Message in Verity

> The remedy for sin is a righteousness not our own — its very name is THE LORD OUR RIGHTEOUSNESS: Christ's obedience COUNTED to the believer for his title, and Christ Himself LIVING in the believer for his fitness — one gift, received and lived by faith alone; and this, said the messenger of the Lord, is the third angel's message in verity.

**The righteousness of God, without the law, upon all them that believe — Rom. 3:21-28; 5:1:**

- _Rom. 3:21._ "But now the righteousness of God without the law is manifested, being witnessed by the law and the prophets" — the remedy is not a better law-keeping manufactured by man but a righteousness GOD supplies; the law and the prophets witnessed to it all along.
  - **righteousness by faith** = Christ's own righteousness given to the believer in two inseparable modes: IMPUTED — His obedience counted to the one who believes, the TITLE (_Rom. 3:24-28_; _Rom. 4:3-5_); and IMPARTED — the same Christ living in the believer by faith, the FITNESS (_Gal. 2:20_; _Rom. 8:3-4_; _1 Cor. 1:30_). Defined in full at the close.
- _Rom. 3:22._ "Even the righteousness of God which is by faith of Jesus Christ unto all and upon all them that believe: for there is no difference" — "upon all": a robe placed on the believer from outside himself, without distinction of persons.
- _Rom. 3:24._ "Being justified freely by his grace through the redemption that is in Christ Jesus" — FREELY: grace is the spring, the redemption in Christ the channel; nothing of the sinner's enters the price.
- _Rom. 3:25._ "Whom God hath set forth to be a propitiation through faith in his blood, to declare his righteousness for the remission of sins that are past" — the blood, laid hold of by faith, remits sins; and the remitting DECLARES God's righteousness rather than compromising it.
- _Rom. 3:26._ "that he might be just, and the justifier of him which believeth in Jesus" — the wonder of the gospel in one clause: God remains JUST — the law unbent — in the very act of justifying the believing sinner; mercy does not bend the law whose transgression is sin (see "What Sin Is — Transgression, Not Inheritance").
- _Rom. 3:27._ "Where is boasting then? It is excluded. By what law? of works? Nay: but by the law of faith" — no flesh glories in His presence; the ground of acceptance is faith, not performance.
- _Rom. 3:28._ "Therefore we conclude that a man is justified by faith without the deeds of the law" — the apostolic conclusion stated as doctrine: justification is by faith, wholly apart from the deeds of the law.
- _Rom. 5:1._ "Therefore being justified by faith, we have peace with God through our Lord Jesus Christ" — the immediate fruit: a settled standing, peace that rests on Another's righteousness and therefore cannot be shaken by our own record.
- _FLB 107.2._ White: "The grace of Christ is freely to justify the sinner without merit or claim on his part. Justification is a full, complete pardon of sin. The moment a sinner accepts Christ by faith, that moment he is pardoned. The righteousness of Christ is imputed to him, and he is no more to doubt God's forgiving grace." — justification defined: full pardon, imputed righteousness, received the moment faith accepts, "without merit or claim" on the sinner's part.
- _FW 103.2._ White: "Pardon and justification are one and the same thing. Through faith, the believer passes from the position of a rebel, a child of sin and Satan, to the position of a loyal subject of Christ Jesus, not because of an inherent goodness, but because Christ receives him as His child by adoption." — the rebel changes standing on Christ's account, not his own; no inherent goodness enters the transaction.

**Counted for righteousness — the imputed title — Gen. 15:6; Rom. 4:3-5, 22-25:**

- _Gen. 15:6._ "And he believed in the LORD; and he counted it to him for righteousness." — the gospel's original arithmetic, laid down in Moses: faith COUNTED for righteousness; justification by faith is no New Testament innovation but the covenant from the beginning.
  - **imputed for righteousness / counted / reckoned** = to credit to the believer's account a righteousness he did not earn — the legal ground of the believer's title to heaven (_Gen. 15:6_; _Rom. 4:3-5_; _Rom. 4:22-25_; _2 Cor. 5:21_; _Phil. 3:9_).
- _Rom. 4:3._ "For what saith the scripture? Abraham believed God, and it was counted unto him for righteousness." — Paul rests his whole case on "what saith the scripture"; the doctrine is as old as Abraham.
- _Rom. 4:4._ "Now to him that worketh is the reward not reckoned of grace, but of debt." — earnings are wages, and wages are debt; God is made debtor to no man — so a righteousness earned would be no gift at all.
- _Rom. 4:5._ "But to him that worketh not, but believeth on him that justifieth the ungodly, his faith is counted for righteousness." — the scandal and the glory of the gospel: God justifies the UNGODLY who believe; the working stops, and the counting begins.
- _Rom. 4:22-23._ "And therefore it was imputed to him for righteousness. Now it was not written for his sake alone, that it was imputed to him" — Abraham's ledger is a specimen page written for the world, not a private privilege.
- _Rom. 4:24._ "But for us also, to whom it shall be imputed, if we believe on him that raised up Jesus our Lord from the dead" — the same crediting, on the same terms, to every believer now.
- _Rom. 4:25._ "Who was delivered for our offences, and was raised again for our justification." — the ground of the imputed title is not a fiction but the delivered-up and risen Christ Himself.
- _FW 18.1._ White: "The point that has been urged upon my mind for years is the imputed righteousness of Christ... I have made it the subject of nearly every discourse and talk that I have given to the people." — imputed righteousness is the Heaven-urged burden of the message, not a peripheral point.
- _CIHS 48.2._ White: "The incense, ascending with the prayers of Israel, represents the merits and intercession of Christ, His perfect righteousness, which through faith is imputed to His people, and which can alone make the worship of sinful beings acceptable to God." — the sanctuary preaches the same doctrine: imputed righteousness ALONE makes a sinner's worship acceptable.

**The righteousness is a Person — Jer. 23:6; 1 Cor. 1:30; Phil. 3:9; 2 Cor. 5:21:**

- _Jer. 23:6._ "In his days Judah shall be saved... and this is his name whereby he shall be called, THE LORD OUR RIGHTEOUSNESS." — the Messiah's own name settles the question forever: our righteousness is not a quality we manufacture but a Person we receive.
- _1 Cor. 1:30._ "But of him are ye in Christ Jesus, who of God is made unto us wisdom, and righteousness, and sanctification, and redemption" — the same Christ is MADE UNTO US both righteousness (the imputed title) and sanctification (the imparted fitness): one Person supplies both, and there is no righteousness of any kind apart from being "in Christ Jesus."
- _Phil. 3:9._ "And be found in him, not having mine own righteousness, which is of the law, but that which is through the faith of Christ, the righteousness which is of God by faith" — Paul the Pharisee throws his own law-righteousness away to be "found in him," wearing a righteousness whose whole origin is God.
- _2 Cor. 5:21._ "For he hath made him to be sin for us, who knew no sin; that we might be made the righteousness of God in him." — the great exchange, imputation in its sharpest form: our sin laid on the Sinless One, that His righteousness be MADE ours "in him."
- _DA 300.1._ White: "The proud heart strives to earn salvation; but both our title to heaven and our fitness for it are found in the righteousness of Christ." — the exact frame of this section: TITLE (imputed) and FITNESS (imparted), both found in His righteousness, neither self-earned.
- _CHR 62.1._ Waggoner: "At last the sinner, weary of the vain struggle to get righteousness from the law, listens to the voice of Christ and flees to His outstretched arms. Hiding in Christ, he is covered with His righteousness... With the righteousness which is 'through the faith of Christ, the righteousness which is of God by faith' (Philippians 3:9), Paul was sure that he would stand secure in the day of Christ." — the 1888 messenger on _Phil. 3:9_: the vain striving ends where the covering begins, and the covered man possesses the very righteousness the law requires.

**The just shall live by faith — Hab. 2:4; Rom. 1:16-17:**

- _Hab. 2:4._ "Behold, his soul which is lifted up is not upright in him: but the just shall live by his faith." — the two ways set side by side: the self-lifted soul is not upright in him; the just man LIVES — from justification onward — by his faith.
  - **the just shall live by faith** = the believer enters righteousness by faith and continues the entire Christian life by that same faith — total dependence on the word of God; the just man lives by precisely the principle by which he became just (_Hab. 2:4_; _Rom. 1:17_; _Gen. 15:6_; _Rom. 4:3-5_).
- _Rom. 1:16._ "For I am not ashamed of the gospel of Christ: for it is the power of God unto salvation to every one that believeth" — the gospel is not advice but POWER, God's own power, unto salvation — the deliverance from sin walked in "What Salvation Really Is — Saved From Sin."
- _Rom. 1:17._ "For therein is the righteousness of God revealed from faith to faith: as it is written, The just shall live by faith." — the righteousness revealed is GOD'S, not man's, and it is received "from faith to faith" — faith at the gate and faith the whole way; the banner text of the Reformation and of 1888.
- _LOF_ATJ 38.8._ Jones: "Men must not only become just by faith—by dependence upon the word of God—but being just, we must live by faith. The just man lives in precisely the same way and by precisely the same thing that he becomes just." — righteousness by faith is continuous, not a one-time transaction followed by law-works.
- _LOF_ATJ 38.9._ Jones: "We become just by faith; faith is entire dependence on the word of God. We, being just, must live by precisely the same thing by which we become just; that is, by entire dependence upon the word of God." — the faith that justifies and the faith that sanctifies are one faith, and Jones defines it: entire dependence on the word of God.

**Imparted — Christ living in the believer — Gal. 2:20; Rom. 8:3-4; Gal. 5:6:**

- _Gal. 2:20._ "I am crucified with Christ: nevertheless I live; yet not I, but Christ liveth in me: and the life which I now live in the flesh I live by the faith of the Son of God, who loved me, and gave himself for me." — the formula of imparted righteousness: the old "I" crucified, and the life now lived is Christ's own life within — the surrendered life entered at the new birth (see "Born of Water and of the Spirit — How He Lived, How We Begin").
- _Rom. 8:3._ "For what the law could not do, in that it was weak through the flesh, God sending his own Son in the likeness of sinful flesh, and for sin, condemned sin in the flesh" — what the law could not do in flesh it was weak through, God did IN that very flesh: the Son came into the nature where sin had reigned and condemned sin there (see "The Second Adam — The Nature Christ Took").
- _Rom. 8:4._ "That the righteousness of the law might be fulfilled in us, who walk not after the flesh, but after the Spirit." — the stated purpose of the whole remedy: the law's righteousness fulfilled IN us — imputed righteousness never stops short of imparted obedience; real obedience is the design, never an option.
- _Gal. 5:6._ "For in Jesus Christ neither circumcision availeth any thing, nor uncircumcision; but faith which worketh by love." — the faith that justifies is never a dead assent: it WORKS, and works by love — the imparted life is the living activity of the very faith that received the title.

**Works — excluded as the root, required as the fruit — Eph. 2:8-10; James 2:17-18:**

- _Eph. 2:8._ "For by grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God" — salvation whole is the gift of God: grace the source, faith the receiving hand, and even that not of ourselves.
- _Eph. 2:9._ "Not of works, lest any man should boast." — works shut out at the root, absolutely; boasting is left no ground to stand on.
- _Eph. 2:10._ "For we are his workmanship, created in Christ Jesus unto good works, which God hath before ordained that we should walk in them." — the same breath that shuts works out as the root ordains them as the fruit: the saved man is God's WORKMANSHIP, created unto good works — this is the balance the whole section owns.
- _James 2:17._ "Even so faith, if it hath not works, is dead, being alone." — the guard against the counterfeit: a workless "faith" is a corpse, and a corpse justifies no one.
- _James 2:18._ "shew me thy faith without thy works, and I will shew thee my faith by my works" — works are faith's only visible evidence — the proof of the living faith, never its purchase price.
- _AA 563.1._ White: "John did not teach that salvation was to be earned by obedience; but that obedience was the fruit of faith and love... The sanctified heart is in harmony with the precepts of God's law." — the balance in one line: obedience the fruit, never the root — and the sanctified heart really does harmonize with the law.
- _COL 312.2._ White: "Righteousness is right doing, and it is by their deeds that all will be judged. Our characters are revealed by what we do. The works show whether the faith is genuine." — the judgment reads the works because the works read the faith.

**The message identified — Minneapolis, 1888, and the verity — 1SM 372:**

- _1SM 372.2._ White: "Several have written to me, inquiring if the message of justification by faith is the third angel's message, and I have answered, 'It is the third angel's message, in verity.'" — the definitive identification (The Review and Herald, April 1, 1890): the message of justification by faith proclaimed by E. J. Waggoner and A. T. Jones at the 1888 Minneapolis General Conference is not a theme beside the third angel's message but that message itself, in verity — the gospel content of the third angel, whose fruit is walked in "The Commandments of God and the Faith of Jesus."
- _1SM 372.1._ White: "there is no danger in presenting this doctrine as it is set forth in the Scriptures. If there had not been a remissness in the past to properly instruct the people of God, there would not now be a necessity of calling a special attention to it." — righteousness by faith is to be preached fully and fearlessly, "as it is set forth in the Scriptures"; the "special attention" was owed to past neglect, not to novelty.

**DEFINITION — RIGHTEOUSNESS BY FAITH — THE THIRD ANGEL’S MESSAGE IN VERITY =** Christ's own righteousness given to the believer in two inseparable modes. IMPUTED: his faith is "counted... for righteousness" (_Gen. 15:6_; _Rom. 4:3-5_), written "for us also, to whom it shall be imputed" (_Rom. 4:22-25_) — the believer is "justified freely by his grace" through faith in Christ's blood, "without the deeds of the law," so that God is at once "just, and the justifier" (_Rom. 3:24-28_); the sinless One was "made... sin for us... that we might be made the righteousness of God in him" (_2 Cor. 5:21_); this is the believer's TITLE, and its fruit is peace with God (_Rom. 5:1_; _FLB 107.2_; _FW 103.2_). IMPARTED: the same Christ made unto us "righteousness, and sanctification" (_1 Cor. 1:30_) lives His own life in the believer — "not I, but Christ liveth in me" (_Gal. 2:20_) — fulfilling the righteousness of the law IN us in the very flesh where He condemned sin (_Rom. 8:3-4_; see "The Second Adam — The Nature Christ Took"), through "faith which worketh by love" (_Gal. 5:6_); this is the believer's FITNESS, and "both our title to heaven and our fitness for it are found in the righteousness of Christ" (_DA 300.1_). It is "the righteousness of God," not man's (_Rom. 1:17_; _3:21-22_; _Phil. 3:9_); its very Name is THE LORD OUR RIGHTEOUSNESS (_Jer. 23:6_); it is entered by faith and lived by that same faith — entire dependence on the word of God (_Hab. 2:4_; _LOF_ATJ 38.8-9_) — and its works are excluded as the root of salvation and required as its fruit (_Eph. 2:8-10_; _James 2:17-18_; _AA 563.1_; _COL 312.2_). This message, carried by Waggoner and Jones from Minneapolis in 1888, is "the third angel's message, in verity" (_1SM 372.2_): the remedy that saves FROM sin (see "What Salvation Really Is — Saved From Sin"), provides the complete victory walked in "Complete Victory — Overcoming as He Overcame," and ripens the commandment-keeping people of "The Commandments of God and the Faith of Jesus."

**Symbols defined here:**

- **righteousness by faith** = Christ's own righteousness given to the believer in two inseparable modes: IMPUTED — His obedience counted to the believer, the title (_Gen. 15:6_; _Rom. 3:24-28_; _Rom. 4:3-5, 22-25_; _2 Cor. 5:21_; _Phil. 3:9_); and IMPARTED — Christ living in the believer by faith, fulfilling the law's righteousness in him, the fitness (_Rom. 8:3-4_; _Gal. 2:20_; _Gal. 5:6_; _1 Cor. 1:30_; _DA 300.1_). Its Name is THE LORD OUR RIGHTEOUSNESS (_Jer. 23:6_); obedience is its fruit, never its root, and never optional (_Eph. 2:8-10_; _James 2:17-18_).
- **imputed for righteousness / counted / reckoned** = to credit to the believer's account a righteousness he did not earn — faith "counted... for righteousness" to him "that worketh not, but believeth on him that justifieth the ungodly"; the legal ground of the believer's title (_Gen. 15:6_; _Rom. 4:3-5_; _Rom. 4:22-25_; _2 Cor. 5:21_; _FW 18.1_).
- **the just shall live by faith** = the believer becomes just by faith and continues the whole Christian life by that same faith — entire dependence on the word of God; self-lifted works excluded from first to last (_Hab. 2:4_; _Rom. 1:16-17_; _LOF_ATJ 38.8-9_).

**Symbols carried:** sin = consented transgression of the law — the thing pardoned in justification and dethroned by the indwelling Christ (see "What Sin Is — Transgression, Not Inheritance"); sinful flesh and the second Adam — the very nature in which the Son came and "condemned sin in the flesh" (_Rom. 8:3_; see "The Second Adam — The Nature Christ Took"); the new birth — the entrance into the "not I, but Christ" life (_Gal. 2:20_; see "Born of Water and of the Spirit — How He Lived, How We Begin"); salvation = God saving His people FROM their sins, the deliverance this righteousness works (see "What Salvation Really Is — Saved From Sin"). The victory this faith provides is walked in "Complete Victory — Overcoming as He Overcame," and the people it ripens in "The Commandments of God and the Faith of Jesus."

**For discussion:**

1. "Now to him that worketh is the reward not reckoned of grace, but of debt. But to him that worketh not, but believeth on him that justifieth the ungodly, his faith is counted for righteousness" (_Rom. 4:4-5_). Where in your religious life are you quietly working for wages — treating obedience, devotions, or reform as a claim on God — and what would change today if both your title and your fitness were sought only in the righteousness of Christ (_DA 300.1_)?
2. Jones says the just man "lives in precisely the same way and by precisely the same thing that he becomes just" (_LOF_ATJ 38.8_; _Hab. 2:4_; _Rom. 1:17_, "from faith to faith"). Many begin by faith and then continue by effort. How do you tell the difference between obedience that is the fruit of living faith (_Gal. 5:6_; _AA 563.1_) and obedience that has quietly become the root — earning all over again?
3. Ellen White answered that justification by faith "is the third angel's message, in verity" (_1SM 372.2_) and that "there is no danger in presenting this doctrine as it is set forth in the Scriptures" (_1SM 372.1_). Does the message you actually carry — in your home, your class, your witness — center where Heaven centered it: God "just, and the justifier of him which believeth in Jesus" (_Rom. 3:26_)? If not, what neglected "special attention" does it need from you?
