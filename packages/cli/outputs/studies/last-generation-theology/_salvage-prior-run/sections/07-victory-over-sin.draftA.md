## Complete Victory — Overcoming as He Overcame

> Must the believer always fall short? Scripture answers with a charter, not a wish: sin SHALL NOT have dominion; whatsoever is born of God overcometh; God is able to keep you from falling — the same victory Christ won, by the same faith, in the same flesh, "even as I also overcame."

**The charter promise — sin dethroned — Rom. 6:6, 14, 22:**

- _Rom. 6:14._ "For sin shall not have dominion over you: for ye are not under the law, but under grace." — the charter of the section: a future certainty, not an aspiration; grace, not law-under-condemnation, is the ground of the deliverance.
- _Rom. 6:6._ "our old man is crucified with him, that the body of sin might be destroyed, that henceforth we should not serve sin" — the mechanism of the promise: union with the crucified Christ, not native effort; the body of sin destroyed so that we "should not serve sin."
- _Rom. 6:22._ "But now being made free from sin, and become servants to God, ye have your fruit unto holiness" — the freedom is present and actual, bearing fruit unto holiness; the gospel result is genuine liberty from sin's service, not managed failure (see "What Salvation Really Is — Saved From Sin").
- _WOR 110.3._ Waggoner, reading the whole verse: "transgression of the law has no place in them who are not under the law" — sin's dominion actually broken, not re-labeled.
- _WOR 198.5._ Waggoner meets the objection of this section head-on: "A questioner may say, 'You make it out that we ought never to sin any more, - you leave no room for sin.' But is not that what the Bible says?... There is such a thing as a complete surrender to Christ, - when we give up everything, and then trust to his power to keep us in that state. And I thank God that he is able to do it." — the pioneer answer to "must we always fall short?" is no; and Christ is able to keep.

**Born of God — the life that does not practise sin — 1 John 3:8-9; 5:4, 18:**

- _1 John 3:8._ "For this purpose the Son of God was manifested, that he might destroy the works of the devil." — the incarnation's stated purpose is the undoing of sin in the believer, not its accommodation.
- _1 John 3:9._ "Whosoever is born of God doth not commit sin; for his seed remaineth in him: and he cannot sin, because he is born of God." — while the divine seed remains, the born-of-God life does not practise sin — not a native sinlessness of the flesh apart from Christ, but a nature that cannot sin while it abides in Him (the new-birth life of "Born of Water and of the Spirit — How He Lived, How We Begin").
- _1 John 5:4._ "For whatsoever is born of God overcometh the world: and this is the victory that overcometh the world, even our faith." — the overcomer defined, and the means named in the same breath: faith.
  - **the overcomer** = one in whom the born-of-God life triumphs over sin and the world — not by native strength but by Christ living out in fallen flesh the very victory He Himself won (_1 John 5:4_, "even our faith"; _1 John 5:18_, "keepeth himself, and that wicked one toucheth him not"; _Rev. 3:21_, "even as I also overcame"; _Gal. 2:20_, "not I, but Christ liveth in me"; _Rom. 6:14, 22_).
- _1 John 5:18._ "he that is begotten of God keepeth himself, and that wicked one toucheth him not" — kept, guarded, untouched; the overcomer's security is real, not aspirational.
- _CWCP 50.1._ Jones: "this victory of His it is that has brought to every man in the world divine faith by which every man can... find the power of God to deliver him from sin and to keep him from sinning... And thus 'this is the victory that overcometh the world, even our faith.'" — the faith Christ exercised, by which He obtained the victory over the world, the flesh, and the devil, is His free gift to every lost man: the overcomer overcomes by Christ's own victory-faith.

**Provision complete — no temptation compels sin — Jude 24; 1 Cor. 10:13; 2 Cor. 2:14:**

- _Jude 24._ "Now unto him that is able to keep you from falling, and to present you faultless before the presence of his glory with exceeding joy" — the keeping power and the faultless standing are both His work; falling is never made necessary.
- _1 Cor. 10:13._ "God is faithful, who will not suffer you to be tempted above that ye are able; but will with the temptation also make a way to escape" — with EVERY temptation an escape is guaranteed by God's own faithfulness; no temptation ever compels sin.
- _2 Cor. 2:14._ "Now thanks be unto God, which always causeth us to triumph in Christ" — ALWAYS, with no exception clause for necessary defeat; triumph, not managed failure, is the standing gospel norm.
- _UL 255.4._ White: "a provision has been made whereby we may overcome.... He will not fall under temptation or be left to defeat." — provision under every temptation, appropriated by the partaker of the divine nature.
- _RH January 14, 1909, par. 6._ White, a second witness: "In time of trial he will claim the promises, and by these escape the corruptions that are in the world through lust." — the way of escape of _1 Cor. 10:13_ is taken by claiming the promises in the moment of trial.

**Grace sufficient — strength perfected in the very weakness — 2 Cor. 12:9; Phil. 4:13:**

- _2 Cor. 12:9._ "My grace is sufficient for thee: for my strength is made perfect in weakness." — provision matched precisely to weakness; His strength is perfected exactly where we are weakest, so no infirmity is an excuse for sin.
- _Phil. 4:13._ "I can do all things through Christ which strengtheneth me." — "all things": nothing God requires lies outside the enabling of the indwelling Christ.
- _MH 482.3._ White: "Said the tried apostle Paul: 'He said unto me, My grace is sufficient for thee: for My strength is made perfect in weakness.'" — the very infirmities that seem to excuse defeat are where the power of Christ rests.
- _SC 47.1._ White: "Everything depends on the right action of the will.... You can give Him your will; He will then work in you to will and to do according to His good pleasure." — where the provision is appropriated: the believer yields the will, God works the doing; the choice to serve God is always available, so no known sin need be retained.

**Even as I also overcame — the same victory, in the same flesh — Heb. 4:15; Rev. 3:21; 2:7; 3:12:**

- _Heb. 4:15._ "was in all points tempted like as we are, yet without sin" — temptation, even in our infirmities, never made sinning inevitable; He met it on our ground and did not fall (the flesh He met it in: see "The Second Adam — The Nature Christ Took").
- _Rev. 3:21._ "To him that overcometh will I grant to sit with me in my throne, even as I also overcame" — the capstone: the SAME victory, held to the same standard, by the same faith, in the same flesh; Christ's overcoming is the pattern, not an exception.
- _Rev. 2:7._ "To him that overcometh will I give to eat of the tree of life" — every church-letter reward is conditioned "to him that overcometh"; the overcomer is the assumed normal Christian, and Eden restored is his inheritance.
- _Rev. 3:12._ "Him that overcometh will I make a pillar in the temple of my God, and he shall go no more out" — the overcomer's final settled security; the reiterated promise fixes overcoming as the condition Christ everywhere expects and enables.
- _3SM 139.3._ White guards the "even as": "If Christ had a special power which it is not the privilege of man to have, Satan would have made capital of this matter.... a man, tempted as a man, rendering the obedience of a man." — no advantage unavailable to us; the victory is genuinely reproducible.
- _ST January 8, 1902, par. 11._ White: "He asked and received power to enable Him to overcome in the conflict. And those who walk in God's way may have the same power.... As He overcame, so we may overcome." — same power, same victory; and He commands "Be ye therefore perfect" because "He knows that it is possible."
- _3SM 195.3._ White answers the section's question in so many words: "Shall we keep on sinning as though it were an impossibility for us to overcome? How are we to overcome? As Christ overcame, and that is the only way.... I will cooperate with divine power and through grace be conqueror." — the will, refusing surrender to Satan, co-operating with divine power.
- _CH 574.1._ White: "It is only through obedience and continual effort that we shall overcome as Christ overcame." — the victory is real and reproducible, but costly: many desire the overcomer's reward who are not willing to endure the overcomer's toil, self-denial, and watching.

**The mark never lowered — the Advocate for a fall that is possible, never necessary — 1 John 2:1; Matt. 1:21; Titus 2:14:**

- _1 John 2:1._ "these things write I unto you, that ye sin not. And if any man sin, we have an advocate with the Father" — the aim stated plainly: "that ye sin not"; the Advocate is the provision for the one who DOES fall, but the "if" guards the mark — falling is possible, never necessary, and the standard is never lowered to it.
- _Matt. 1:21._ "thou shalt call his name JESUS: for he shall save his people from their sins" — His very name is the errand: salvation FROM sins, not accommodation in them (see "What Salvation Really Is — Saved From Sin"); a gospel of managed failure contradicts the name Jesus.
- _Titus 2:14._ "that he might redeem us from all iniquity, and purify unto himself a peculiar people, zealous of good works" — ALL iniquity: the redemption's design is complete cleansing, not partial.
- _COL 69.1._ White: "When the character of Christ shall be perfectly reproduced in His people, then He will come to claim them as His own." — the goal of the whole provision: complete victory — Christ's own character in His people — is exactly what heaven is waiting to accomplish (ripened in "The Harvest — A People in Whom Christ Is Vindicated").

**DEFINITION — COMPLETE VICTORY — OVERCOMING AS HE OVERCAME =** the believer need not keep sinning: sin "shall not have dominion" (_Rom. 6:14_) because the old man is crucified with Christ and the body of sin destroyed (_Rom. 6:6_), leaving him "made free from sin" (_Rom. 6:22_) — and Waggoner presses the plain sense: we ought never to sin any more, for Christ is able to keep the fully surrendered (_WOR 198.5_; _WOR 110.3_). The Son of God was manifested to destroy the devil's works (_1 John 3:8_), and the born-of-God life does not practise sin while the seed remains (_1 John 3:9_; _5:18_); the overcomer overcomes by "even our faith" (_1 John 5:4_) — the identical victory-faith by which Christ Himself overcame, given free to every man (_CWCP 50.1_). The provision is complete on every side: able to keep from falling (_Jude 24_), a way of escape with every temptation (_1 Cor. 10:13_), always caused to triumph (_2 Cor. 2:14_), grace sufficient and strength perfected in the very weakness (_2 Cor. 12:9_; _Phil. 4:13_; _MH 482.3_) — so no temptation compels sin, no infirmity excuses it, and the believer "will not fall under temptation or be left to defeat" (_UL 255.4_; _RH January 14, 1909_). The standard is Christ's own overcoming, "even as I also overcame" (_Rev. 3:21_), in the same flesh (_Heb. 4:15_; see "The Second Adam — The Nature Christ Took"), by no special power (_3SM 139.3_), through the yielded will co-operating with divine power (_SC 47.1_; _3SM 195.3_), through obedience and continual effort (_CH 574.1_) — and it is the assumed condition of every church-letter promise (_Rev. 2:7_; _3:12_). The mark is never lowered: "that ye sin not," with an Advocate if any man sin (_1 John 2:1_) — the fall possible, never necessary — for His name pledges salvation from sins (_Matt. 1:21_), His self-gift redeems from ALL iniquity (_Titus 2:14_), and He waits for His character to be perfectly reproduced in His people (_COL 69.1_).

**Symbols defined here:**

- **the overcomer** = one in whom the born-of-God life triumphs over sin and the world — not by native strength but by Christ living out in fallen flesh the very victory He Himself won; the normal Christian of Revelation's promises, "made free from sin," over whom sin "shall not have dominion" (_1 John 5:4_; _1 John 5:18_; _Rev. 3:21_; _Rev. 2:7_; _3:12_; _Gal. 2:20_; _Rom. 6:14, 22_).

**Symbols carried:** sin = the consented transgression of the law — the dominion here dethroned (see "What Sin Is — Transgression, Not Inheritance"); the flesh / fallen nature — the weakness in which grace is made perfect, enslaving only until Christ delivers (see "What Sin Is — Transgression, Not Inheritance"); the second Adam and sinful flesh — the ground on which He overcame and on which we overcome (see "The Second Adam — The Nature Christ Took"); the new birth — the "born of God" life of 1 John (see "Born of Water and of the Spirit — How He Lived, How We Begin"); salvation = saved FROM sin (see "What Salvation Really Is — Saved From Sin"); righteousness by faith — the faith that justifies is the same faith that overcomes (see "Righteousness by Faith — The Third Angel’s Message in Verity").

**For discussion:**

1. Romans 6:14 is a promise, not a command — "sin SHALL NOT have dominion over you." Do we secretly read it as "sin should not"? What changes in the fight with a besetting sin when deliverance is trusted as God's pledged work through union with the crucified Christ (Rom. 6:6) rather than our unaided resolve?
2. 1 Corinthians 10:13 guarantees "with the temptation also a way to escape." Recall the last temptation you fell under: was there truly no escape — or an escape unused? What would it look like, practically, to "claim the promises" in the moment of trial (RH January 14, 1909) instead of after the fall?
3. 1 John 2:1 holds two lines at once: "that ye sin not" AND "if any man sin, we have an advocate." How do we keep both — refusing to lower the mark to managed failure, and refusing despair after a fall — without letting either truth cancel the other?
