## The Three Persons and Our Salvation — Why It Matters

> Salvation is not the act of one Person but the shared work of three: the Father plans and gives the Son; the Son redeems by taking our nature and overcoming in it; the Spirit applies the redemption and dwells in the believer — and unless all three are real and divine, the victory is neither truly won nor truly ours.

**The Father plans and gives the Son — John 3:16; 1 John 4:14; Titus 3:4:**

- _John 3:16._ "For God so loved the world, that he gave his only begotten Son, that whosoever believeth in him should not perish, but have everlasting life." — the plan opens at the Father's heart: love that gives the Son so the believer has everlasting life.
  - **the plan of salvation** = the joint, co-operating work of the three Persons of the Godhead — the Father plans and gives the Son; the Son redeems by taking our nature and overcoming in it; the Spirit applies the redemption and dwells in the believer; no one Person works alone (_John 3:16_; _1 John 4:14_; _Titus 3:4-6_; _John 14:16-18_; _Heb. 2:14-18_; _Ev 615.1_).
- _1 John 4:14._ "And we have seen and do testify that the Father sent the Son to be the Saviour of the world." — the Father sends, the Son saves; the joint work confessed by eyewitnesses.
- _Titus 3:4._ "But after that the kindness and love of God our Saviour toward man appeared," — the Father's kindness and love appearing is the source of the whole saving work (see "The Father — the Source and the Sender").
- _SC 13.1._ White: "God permitted His beloved Son... to come from a world of indescribable glory, to a world marred and blighted with sin... He permitted Him to leave the bosom of His love, the adoration of the angels, to suffer shame, insult, humiliation, hatred, and death." — the Father's part: giving and permitting His beloved Son, at the head of the plan.
- _1888 75.1._ White: "God gave His only-begotten Son to die for us, to suffer the penalty of the [broken] law of God... how Jesus Christ, when He came to our world, laid aside His royal robes and His kingly crown and clothed His divinity with humanity... The Father sent His Son here." — the Father gave and sent the Son, who clothed divinity with humanity — the incarnation at the heart of the plan.

**The Son redeems by taking our very flesh — Heb. 2:14, 17-18; Rom. 8:3-4:**

- _Heb. 2:14._ "Forasmuch then as the children are partakers of flesh and blood, he also himself likewise took part of the same; that through death he might destroy him that had the power of death, that is, the devil;" — the Son takes our very flesh and blood so that by death He could destroy the devil — redemption by taking our nature (see "The Nature He Took — Full Fallen Nature, Yet Without Sin").
- _Heb. 2:17._ "Wherefore in all things it behoved him to be made like unto his brethren, that he might be a merciful and faithful high priest in things pertaining to God, to make reconciliation for the sins of the people." — made like His brethren in all things to be the merciful, faithful High Priest who reconciles.
- _Heb. 2:18._ "For in that he himself hath suffered being tempted, he is able to succour them that are tempted." — because He was tempted in our nature and overcame, He is able to succour the tempted.
- _Rom. 8:3._ "For what the law could not do, in that it was weak through the flesh, God sending his own Son in the likeness of sinful flesh, and for sin, condemned sin in the flesh:" — God sends His own Son in the likeness of sinful flesh and condemns sin in the very flesh where it reigned.
  - **the victory** = Christ's overcoming of sin, the world, and the devil in the very flesh He took from us, made the believer's own by faith and through the indwelling Spirit — won not apart from us in another nature, but in our nature, so the law's righteousness is fulfilled in us (_1 John 5:4-5_; _Rom. 8:3-4_; _Heb. 2:18_; _PCP 24.5_; _CH 592.2_).
- _Rom. 8:4._ "That the righteousness of the law might be fulfilled in us, who walk not after the flesh, but after the Spirit." — Christ's victory in our nature becomes ours: the law's righteousness fulfilled in us who walk after the Spirit.
- _1SM 256.1._ White: "In taking upon Himself man's nature in its fallen condition, Christ did not in the least participate in its sin. He was subject to the infirmities and weaknesses by which man is encompassed... and was in all points tempted like as we are. And yet He knew no sin." — the Son took our fallen nature yet without sin — the basis of a victory truly won in our flesh.
- _PCP 24.5._ White: "Christ left His high command, took upon Himself the nature of man... Bearing our nature, He was true to God's standard of righteousness, gaining the victory over Satan. He was tempted in all points like as we are, yet He was without sin." — Christ overcame Satan in our nature, proving the law can be kept.
- _MB 13.1._ White: "In all the affliction of humanity He is afflicted; and 'in that He Himself hath suffered being tempted, He is able to succor them that are tempted.' Isaiah 63:9; Hebrews 2:18." — because the Son suffered temptation in our humanity, He is able to succour the tempted.

**The Spirit applies the redemption and dwells within — Rom. 5:5; Titus 3:5-6; Rom. 8:9, 11:**

- _Rom. 5:5._ "And hope maketh not ashamed; because the love of God is shed abroad in our hearts by the Holy Ghost which is given unto us." — the Spirit applies the Father's love inwardly: the love of God shed abroad in our hearts.
- _Titus 3:5._ "Not by works of righteousness which we have done, but according to his mercy he saved us, by the washing of regeneration, and renewing of the Holy Ghost;" — He saves by mercy through the washing of regeneration and renewing of the Holy Ghost; the Spirit applies the salvation (see "The Holy Spirit — Who He Is, a Divine Person").
- _Titus 3:6._ "Which he shed on us abundantly through Jesus Christ our Saviour;" — the Spirit is shed on us abundantly through Christ — Father, Son, and Spirit named together in one saving act.
- _Rom. 8:9._ "But ye are not in the flesh, but in the Spirit, if so be that the Spirit of God dwell in you. Now if any man have not the Spirit of Christ, he is none of his." — to belong to Christ is to have His Spirit dwelling within; the indwelling is the mark of salvation applied.
- _Rom. 8:11._ "But if the Spirit of him that raised up Jesus from the dead dwell in you, he that raised up Christ from the dead shall also quicken your mortal bodies by his Spirit that dwelleth in you." — the Spirit of Him that raised up Jesus indwells and will quicken our mortal bodies — resurrection power applied now.
- _DA 320.3._ White: "And 'if the Spirit of Him that raised up Jesus from the dead dwell in you, He that raised up Christ from the dead shall also quicken your mortal bodies by His Spirit that dwelleth in you.' Romans 8:11... This is the word of comfort wherewith He bids us comfort one another." — the Spirit's indwelling and resurrection power applied to the believer.

**The three Persons in concert — John 14:16-18:**

- _John 14:16._ "And I will pray the Father, and he shall give you another Comforter, that he may abide with you for ever;" — the Son prays, the Father gives the Spirit — the three Persons in concert; the Comforter abides for ever.
- _John 14:17._ "Even the Spirit of truth; whom the world cannot receive, because it seeth him not, neither knoweth him: but ye know him; for he dwelleth with you, and shall be in you." — the Spirit of truth dwells with and shall be in the believer — the indwelling that applies salvation.
- _John 14:18._ "I will not leave you comfortless: I will come to you." — by the Spirit, Christ Himself comes to His own; the indwelling is His real presence.
- _Ev 615.1._ White: "The Comforter that Christ promised to send... is the Spirit in all the fullness of the Godhead... There are three living persons of the heavenly trio; in the name of these three great powers — the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit — those who receive Christ by living faith are baptized." — the three Persons together in the plan, co-operating with those who receive Christ.

**The Saviour must be truly God for the victory to be real — 1 John 5:4-5:**

- _1 John 5:4._ "For whatsoever is born of God overcometh the world: and this is the victory that overcometh the world, even our faith." — Christ's overcoming made ours by faith — the term and the symbol of this section.
- _1 John 5:5._ "Who is he that overcometh the world, but he that believeth that Jesus is the Son of God?" — the overcomer is the one who believes Jesus is the Son of God: faith fastened on a real, divine Saviour (see "The Son — Self-Existent, Without Beginning").
- _DA 530.3._ White: "In Christ is life, original, unborrowed, underived. 'He that hath the Son hath life.' 1 John 5:12. The divinity of Christ is the believer's assurance of eternal life." — the self-existent Saviour whose underived life undergirds the assurance this section closes on; the victory is real only because the One who won it is fully God.
- _CH 592.2._ White: "'This is the victory that overcometh the world, even our faith.' 1 John 5:4. The world has become a lazar house of sin... We are not to practice its ways or follow its customs." — the victory term applied to daily overcoming by faith.
- _PREX1 15.2._ Litch (1842): "As the Son of God, the only begotten of the Father, he is also possessed of a self-existent living principle, distinct from the blood of Adam... In this mysterious being 'dwelleth all the fulness of the Godhead bodily.'... 'The children being partakers of flesh and blood, himself also took part of the same.'" — an early Millerite voice (still framing that living principle as "derived from the Father," in the developing christology of his day) reaching toward the dual reality this section rests on: a Son possessed of fulness of Godhead who took our flesh and blood.
- _PREX1 17.3._ Litch (1842): "The spirit or nature of Jesus Christ, by which he was raised from the dead, is imparted to every true believer in the Son of God; and 'if the spirit of Him that raised up Jesus from the dead dwell in you, he that raised up Christ from the dead shall quicken your mortal bodies by his spirit which dwelleth in you.' Romans 8:11." — the same early voice applying Rom. 8:11: the Spirit that raised Jesus imparted to the believer.

**The assurance that flows from it — Heb. 4:16:**

- _Heb. 4:16._ "Let us therefore come boldly unto the throne of grace, that we may obtain mercy, and find grace to help in time of need." — the assurance that flows from the incarnation: a tempted High Priest invites us to come boldly for grace to help.

**DEFINITION — THE THREE PERSONS AND OUR SALVATION — WHY IT MATTERS =** the doctrine of the Godhead is not a cold metaphysic but the architecture of redemption itself, for salvation is the shared act of three Persons in perfect concert. The **plan of salvation** runs: the Father loved and gave and sent His only-begotten Son (_John 3:16_; _1 John 4:14_; _Titus 3:4_; _SC 13.1_; _1888 75.1_); the Son, possessed of self-existent, underived life (_DA 530.3_), took our very flesh and blood in its fallen condition yet without sin (_Heb. 2:14, 17_; _Rom. 8:3_; _1SM 256.1_), and there, in our nature, condemned sin and gained the **victory** over Satan (_Rom. 8:3-4_; _PCP 24.5_); the Spirit, in all the fullness of the Godhead (_Ev 615.1_), then sheds that love abroad and dwells within, applying the finished work, marking out those who are Christ's, and quickening their mortal bodies with resurrection power (_Rom. 5:5_; _John 14:16-18_; _Titus 3:5-6_; _Rom. 8:9, 11_; _DA 320.3_). The pioneer corroboration is a developing one — from Josiah Litch's 1842 reach toward a Son of fulness of Godhead who took our flesh (_PREX1 15.2_; _PREX1 17.3_), on to Ellen White's mature "three living persons of the heavenly trio" (_Ev 615.1_). Why it matters: because the victory was won in our flesh and not apart from us, it is made the believer's own possession by faith — "this is the victory that overcometh the world, even our faith" (_1 John 5:4-5_; _CH 592.2_) — so that the law's righteousness is fulfilled in us who walk after the Spirit (_Rom. 8:4_). The tempted, sympathizing High Priest now bids us come boldly to the throne for grace to help (_Heb. 2:18_; _Heb. 4:16_; _MB 13.1_). What the three Persons are determines what salvation can be: a real Father who gives, a real divine Son who overcomes in our nature, a real Spirit who applies it within. Deny any one Person — the Father who gives, the Son who is fully God yet took our flesh, the Spirit who applies — and the plan collapses, and the victory is neither truly won nor truly ours.

**Symbols defined here:**

- **the plan of salvation** = the joint, co-operating work of the three Persons of the Godhead — the Father plans and gives the Son, the Son redeems by taking our nature and overcoming in it, the Spirit applies the redemption and indwells; no one Person works alone (_John 3:16_; _1 John 4:14_; _Titus 3:4-6_; _John 14:16-18_; _Heb. 2:14-18_; _Ev 615.1_).
- **the victory** = Christ's overcoming of sin, the world, and the devil in the very flesh He took from us, made the believer's own by faith and through the indwelling Spirit — won in our nature, not apart from it, so the law's righteousness is fulfilled in us who walk after the Spirit (_1 John 5:4-5_; _Rom. 8:3-4_; _Heb. 2:18_; _PCP 24.5_; _CH 592.2_).

**Symbols carried:** the Father as Source and Sender (defined in "The Father — the Source and the Sender"); the Son's self-existent, underived life (defined in "The Son — Self-Existent, Without Beginning"); the Holy Spirit as a divine Person, the Comforter who indwells (defined in "The Holy Spirit — Who He Is, a Divine Person"); the fallen nature Christ took yet without sin (defined in "The Nature He Took — Full Fallen Nature, Yet Without Sin"); the incarnation, divinity clothed with humanity (defined in "The Word Made Flesh — Truly God and Truly Man").

**For discussion:**

1. The text shows each Person at work in one saving act — the Father giving (_John 3:16_), the Son overcoming in our flesh (_Rom. 8:3_), the Spirit indwelling (_Rom. 8:9-11_). Trace one act of salvation through all three Persons: what does each do, and why could the work not be finished if any were left out?
2. _1 John 5:4-5_ ties "the victory that overcometh the world" to believing "that Jesus is the Son of God." Why does the reality of the victory depend on the Son being both truly God (_DA 530.3_) and truly partaker of our flesh (_Heb. 2:14_)?
3. _Heb. 2:18_ and _4:16_ ground bold approach to God in the fact that our High Priest was "tempted" in our nature. What temptation are you facing this week, and how does a Saviour who "is able to succour them that are tempted" change how you bring it to the throne of grace?
