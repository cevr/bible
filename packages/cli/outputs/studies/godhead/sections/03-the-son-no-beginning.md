## The Son — Self-Existent, Without Beginning

> The One born at Bethlehem had "goings forth from of old, from everlasting" — He was the Word who already WAS at the beginning, who has life in Himself: eternal, self-existent, with no origin in time.

**The Word who already WAS — John 1:1, 14, 18; Micah 5:2:**

- _John 1:1._ "In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God" — at the absolute beginning the Word already _was_ (not "came to be"); He was with God yet was God, distinct in person, identical in essence. The cornerstone text.
  - **the Son / the Word** = the eternal, self-existent second Person of the Godhead — He who "was" at the beginning, who "was God," by whom all things were made, whose "goings forth" are "from everlasting," in whom is life "in himself"; therefore without origin in time, defined by glory and essence and not by a starting point (_John 1:1_; _Micah 5:2_; _John 8:58_; _John 5:26_; _Col. 1:16-17_; _Heb. 1:8_; _Rev. 1:8_). Defined in full at the close.
- _Micah 5:2._ "out of thee shall he come forth... whose goings forth [have been] from of old, from everlasting" — the ruler born at Bethlehem has "goings forth" reaching back into eternity; the One born was already the Eternal, so His birth in time did not begin His being.
- _John 1:14._ "the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us... the glory as of the only begotten of the Father" — the Word who already was eternal was _made_ flesh; "only begotten" is defined here by _glory_, not by a moment of origin.
  - **only begotten** (Gk. _monogenes_) = unique, of the same essence and divine glory as the Father — NOT origin in time. Scripture defines it by "the glory as of the only begotten" and by the abiding relation "in the bosom of the Father"; where "begotten" is dated at all, it is dated to the resurrection (_Acts 13:33_ quoting _Ps. 2:7_), proving it does not mark the genesis of the Son's being (_John 1:14_; _John 1:18_; _Acts 13:33_; _Ps. 2:7_). Defined in full at the close.
- _John 1:18._ "the only begotten Son, which is in the bosom of the Father, he hath declared [him]" — the Son is eternally "in the bosom of the Father," an abiding relation of intimacy, not a clue to a starting point; He declares (exegetes) the unseen God because He shares His nature.
- _PP 34.1._ White: "Christ, the Word, the only begotten of God, was one with the eternal Father—one in nature, in character, in purpose.... His 'goings forth have been from of old, from everlasting.' Micah 5:2" — EGW strings the section's exact spine together and reads "only begotten" as "one with the eternal Father."
- _GC 493.1._ White: "Christ the Word, the Only Begotten of God, was one with the eternal Father,—one in nature, in character, and in purpose" — decisive: the "Only Begotten" is one with the _eternal_ Father in nature, so "only begotten" denotes shared essence, not origin in time.
- _DA 19.1._ White: "From the days of eternity the Lord Jesus Christ was one with the Father; He was 'the image of God,' the image of His greatness and majesty, 'the outshining of His glory'" — "from the days of eternity" anchors His oneness with the Father before all time.

**I AM — the timeless self-existence of the Name — John 8:58; Ex. 3:14:**

- _John 8:58._ "Before Abraham was, I am" — not "I was" but "I AM"; Christ claims the timeless self-existence of the divine Name (_Ex. 3:14_, "I AM THAT I AM"). He had no prior interval before which He merely existed; He simply IS.

**Alpha and Omega — eternal in both directions — Rev. 1:8, 17-18; 22:13:**

- _Rev. 1:8._ "I am Alpha and Omega, the beginning and the ending, saith the Lord, which is, and which was, and which is to come, the Almighty" — Christ is first and last letter; He bounds all things but is bounded by nothing. "Which was" as surely as "which is to come": eternal in both directions, the Almighty.
- _Rev. 22:13._ "I am Alpha and Omega, the beginning and the end, the first and the last" — the risen Christ (cf. _Rev. 22:16_) takes the eternity-title to Himself: the First, none before Him; the Last, none after. Self-existence stated as identity.
- _Rev. 1:17-18._ "I am the first and the last: [I am] he that liveth, and was dead; and, behold, I am alive for evermore... and have the keys of hell and of death" — the same "first and last" that Jehovah claims in Isaiah belongs to Christ; the One who died lives "for evermore," His life inextinguishable.

**Life in Himself — original, unborrowed, underived — John 5:26; Heb. 13:8:**

- _John 5:26._ "as the Father hath life in himself; so hath he given to the Son to have life in himself" — the Son has "life in himself," the very self-existence that defines the Father; original, underived life resides in the Son as in the Father.
- _Heb. 13:8._ "Jesus Christ the same yesterday, and to day, and for ever" — immutable across all time; what has no change and no end equally has no beginning. His being is constant through all ages.
- _DA 530.3._ White: "In Christ is life, original, unborrowed, underived. 'He that hath the Son hath life.' 1 John 5:12. The divinity of Christ is the believer's assurance of eternal life" — the keystone phrase for self-existence: Christ's life is not received from another, the very definition of self-existent Deity.
- _1SM 296.2._ White: "The Word, who was with God, and who was God, had this life.... But the life of Christ was unborrowed. No one can take this life from Him.... In Him was life, original, unborrowed, underived" — expounding _John 1:1-4_ and _John 10:18_: the Word's life is unborrowed and cannot be taken, the basis of His power over His own death.

**Creator of all — therefore not among the created — Col. 1:15-18; Heb. 1:6, 8:**

- _Col. 1:15._ "Who is the image of the invisible God, the firstborn of every creature" — "firstborn" (Gk. _prototokos_) is the Heir over all creation, not the first item in it; as "image of the invisible God" He represents the unseen Father.
  - **firstborn** (Gk. _prototokos_) = preeminence and heirship, a conferred title of rank — NOT first-created, not birth-order. God "makes" the youngest son His firstborn (_Ps. 89:27_); the firstborn is the Creator of all things, hence not among them (_Col. 1:16_); the parallel "firstborn from the dead" is explained as "that in all things he might have the preeminence" (_Col. 1:18_); and the firstborn receives the worship of angels (_Heb. 1:6_). Defined in full at the close.
- _Col. 1:16-17._ "by him were all things created... all things were created by him, and for him: And he is before all things, and by him all things consist" — He created _all_ things, therefore cannot be among created things; He is "before all things" and actively upholds the universe ("consist" = holds together), the mark of self-existent Deity.
- _Col. 1:18._ "who is the beginning, the firstborn from the dead; that in all [things] he might have the preeminence" — the same word "firstborn" is paired with "from the dead" and explained by its purpose: PREEMINENCE. Scripture's own definition of firstborn = supremacy.
- _Ps. 89:27._ "Also I will make him [my] firstborn, higher than the kings of the earth" — God "makes" David firstborn though David was the youngest son (_1 Sam. 16_), proving "firstborn" is a conferred title of rank ("higher than the kings"), not a statement of birth-order.
- _Heb. 1:6._ "when he bringeth in the firstbegotten into the world, he saith, And let all the angels of God worship him" — the firstbegotten receives the worship of all angels; worship belongs to God alone (_Ex. 20_). A created being could not be worshipped; the firstborn is the worshipped Son.
- _Heb. 1:8._ "unto the Son [he saith], Thy throne, O God, [is] for ever and ever" — the Father directly addresses the Son as "O God" whose throne is "for ever and ever"; an everlasting throne and the title God exclude any beginning to the Son's existence.
- _1SM 247.3._ White: "If Christ made all things, He existed before all things.... Christ was God essentially, and in the highest sense. He was with God from all eternity, God over all, blessed forevermore" — confirms the _Col. 1:16-17_ reasoning: the Creator of all cannot be among created things; "God from all eternity" excludes a beginning.
- _CHR 21.1._ Waggoner: "Neither should we imagine that Christ is a creature, because Paul calls Him (Colossians 1:15) 'The First-born of every creature' for the very next verses show Him to be Creator and not a creature.... He is above all creation and not a part of it" — pioneer corroboration on "firstborn": the Firstborn is Creator, not creature, exactly the reading of firstborn as preeminence.

**"Begotten" dated to the resurrection — Acts 13:33; Ps. 2:7; Heb. 7:3:**

- _Acts 13:33._ "he hath raised up Jesus again; as it is also written in the second psalm... Thou art my Son, this day have I begotten thee" — Paul applies "this day have I begotten thee" to the RESURRECTION, not to a pre-creation origin; "begotten" is the language of installation and declaration, decisively unlinking it from a beginning in time.
- _Ps. 2:7._ "the LORD hath said unto me, Thou [art] my Son; this day have I begotten thee" — the "this day" of begetting is a coronation decree, cited by _Acts 13:33_ of the resurrection; even the OT "begotten" text describes enthronement, not the genesis of the Son.
- _Heb. 7:3._ Melchizedek, "Without father, without mother, without descent, having neither beginning of days, nor end of life; but made like unto the Son of God" — Melchizedek is a type "made like unto the Son of God" precisely in "having neither beginning of days, nor end of life"; the antitype, the Son, truly has no beginning of days.

**The child born is the everlasting God — Isa. 9:6; Prov. 8:22-23:**

- _Isa. 9:6._ "unto us a child is born, unto us a son is given... his name shall be called Wonderful, Counsellor, The mighty God, The everlasting Father, The Prince of Peace" — the child born is named "The mighty God, The everlasting Father"; the One who entered time as a son is Himself eternal Deity, and the begetting in Bethlehem did not begin His being.
- _Prov. 8:22-23._ "The LORD possessed me in the beginning of his way, before his works of old. I was set up from everlasting, from the beginning, or ever the earth was" — Wisdom personified was "set up from everlasting," before any created work; EGW cites this exact text as proof of Christ's eternal pre-existence.
- _1SM 247.4._ White: "The Lord Jesus Christ, the divine Son of God, existed from eternity, a distinct person, yet one with the Father.... 'I was set up from everlasting, from the beginning, or ever the earth was'" — the conclusion stated plainly and grounded in _Prov. 8_, the same text the spine uses.
- _Ev 615.2._ White: "Christ is the pre-existent, self-existent Son of God.... In speaking of his pre-existence, Christ carries the mind back through dateless ages. He assures us that there never was a time when He was not in close fellowship with the eternal God" — the mature, settled statement: "self-existent" and "never... a time when He was not." Directly answers "did Christ have a beginning?" — No.

**The pioneer development — honest history — CHR 22.1; 21.2:**

- _CHR 22.1._ Waggoner: "the point is that Christ is a begotten Son and not a created subject.... since He is the only-begotten son of God, He is of the very substance and nature of God and possesses by birth all the attributes of God.... So He has 'life in Himself'" — Waggoner already pressing toward the mature view: "of the very substance and nature of God" and "life in Himself" (_John 5:26_), the same essence-language Scripture uses, even while his "begotten" wording lagged.
- _CHR 21.2._ Waggoner: "There was a time when Christ proceeded forth and came from God, from the bosom of the Father (John 8:42; 1:18), but that time was so far back in the days of eternity that to finite comprehension it is practically without beginning" — an honest witness to the era's transitional theology: Waggoner (1890), defending Christ's full divinity, still read "begotten" as a quasi-beginning ("there was a time") — a semi-Arian residue that the texts and EGW's later "self-existent" / "never a time" statements (_Ev 615.2_; _DA 530.3_) corrected.

**DEFINITION — THE SON, SELF-EXISTENT, WITHOUT BEGINNING =** the second Person of the Godhead is the Word who already "was" at the absolute beginning, who "was God," in whom is life "in himself" (_John 1:1_; _John 5:26_) — therefore eternal and self-existent, with no origin in time. His "goings forth" are "from of old, from everlasting" (_Micah 5:2_); He claims the timeless Name, "Before Abraham was, I am" (_John 8:58_; _Ex. 3:14_); He is "Alpha and Omega... which is, and which was, and which is to come, the Almighty," eternal in both directions (_Rev. 1:8_; _22:13_; _1:17-18_); His life is "original, unborrowed, underived" (_DA 530.3_; _1SM 296.2_), and the Father addresses Him as "O God" on an everlasting throne (_Heb. 1:8_). The two titles that seem to teach a beginning do not: **firstborn** (_prototokos_) is a conferred rank of preeminence and heirship — God "makes" the youngest His firstborn (_Ps. 89:27_), the Firstborn is the Creator of all and so not among the created (_Col. 1:16-18_; _1SM 247.3_), and He receives the worship due God alone (_Heb. 1:6_); and **only begotten** (_monogenes_) is uniqueness of glory and shared essence — "one with the eternal Father" (_GC 493.1_; _PP 34.1_) — while the only dated "begetting" Scripture gives is the resurrection (_Acts 13:33_ quoting _Ps. 2:7_). The child born at Bethlehem is named "The mighty God, The everlasting Father" (_Isa. 9:6_); Wisdom was "set up from everlasting" (_Prov. 8:22-23_); the antitype of Melchizedek has "neither beginning of days, nor end of life" (_Heb. 7:3_). The pioneer development is honest history: Waggoner (1890) exalted Christ's full divinity and "very substance... of God" yet still read "begotten" as a near-beginning (_CHR 22.1_; _21.2_) — a transitional reading that Ellen White's later word settled: "Christ is the pre-existent, self-existent Son of God... there never was a time when He was not" (_Ev 615.2_).

**Symbols defined here:**

- **the Son / the Word** = the eternal, self-existent second Person of the Godhead — He who "was" at the beginning, "was God," by whom all things were made, whose "goings forth" are "from everlasting," in whom is life "in himself"; without origin in time, defined by glory and essence (_John 1:1_; _Micah 5:2_; _John 8:58_; _John 5:26_; _Col. 1:16-17_; _Heb. 1:8_; _Rev. 1:8_; _Ev 615.2_; _DA 530.3_).
- **only begotten** (_monogenes_) = uniqueness and shared divine essence/glory, NOT origin in time — defined by "glory as of the only begotten" and the abiding relation "in the bosom of the Father," with the only dated "begetting" placed at the resurrection (_John 1:14_; _John 1:18_; _Acts 13:33_; _Ps. 2:7_; _GC 493.1_).
- **firstborn** (_prototokos_) = preeminence and heirship, a conferred title of rank — NOT first-created; the Firstborn is Creator not creature (_Col. 1:16_), worshipped by angels (_Heb. 1:6_), and "firstborn from the dead... that in all things he might have the preeminence" (_Col. 1:15_; _Col. 1:18_; _Ps. 89:27_; _CHR 21.1_).

**Symbols carried:** none — this opening doctrinal section originates the SON as eternal and self-existent, the foundation on which "The Father — the Source and the Sender" and "The Holy Spirit" stand as the three Persons of the one Godhead.
