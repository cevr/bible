# Research: The Locusts Are the Children of the East

**Thesis under test:** The locusts of Rev 9:3 are Bible-defined not only as God-commissioned armies (Joel 1–2) but specifically as the peoples of the Arabian east, because Judges calls Midian + Amalek + "the children of the east" _'arbeh_ — the same Hebrew word the KJV elsewhere renders "locust."

**Verdict: the Hebrew-word claim HOLDS.** Judges 6:5 and 7:12 "grasshoppers" = **H697 'arbeh**, the identical word rendered "locust" in Exodus 10 (the plague), Joel 1:4; 2:25 (the LORD's army), Psalm 105:34, Nahum 3:15-17, etc. Judges does NOT use the other word H2284 (chagab). Details under Strand 2.

---

## Strand 1 — Judges: the 'arbeh hordes named and located

**Judges 6:1-6 (KJV)**

> 1 And the children of Israel did evil in the sight of the LORD: and the LORD delivered them into the hand of Midian seven years.
> 2 And the hand of Midian prevailed against Israel: and because of the Midianites the children of Israel made them the dens which are in the mountains, and caves, and strong holds.
> 3 And so it was, when Israel had sown, that the Midianites came up, and the Amalekites, and **the children of the east**, even they came up against them;
> 4 And they encamped against them, and destroyed the increase of the earth, till thou come unto Gaza, and left no sustenance for Israel, neither sheep, nor ox, nor ass.
> 5 For they came up with their cattle and their tents, and they came **as grasshoppers [H697 'arbeh] for multitude**; for both they and their **camels were without number**: and they entered into the land to destroy it.
> 6 And Israel was greatly impoverished because of the Midianites; and the children of Israel cried unto the LORD.

**Judges 6:33**

> Then all the Midianites and the Amalekites and the children of the east were gathered together, and went over, and pitched in the valley of Jezreel.

**Judges 7:12**

> And the Midianites and the Amalekites and all the children of the east lay along in the valley **like grasshoppers [H697 'arbeh] for multitude**; and their camels were without number, as the sand by the sea side for multitude.

**Judges 8:10**

> Now Zebah and Zalmunna were in Karkor, and their hosts with them, about fifteen thousand men, all that were left of all the hosts of **the children of the east**: for there fell an hundred and twenty thousand men that drew sword.

Note the fixed triad in all three musters: Midianites + Amalekites + children of the east — and the signature details that recur in the Arabian-east texts of Strand 3: **tents, cattle, camels without number** (cf. Jer 49:29, 32; Job 1:3).

## Strand 2 — Strong's: Judges' "grasshopper" IS the locust word

**H697 'arbeh** — "a locust (from its rapid increase) — grasshopper, locust."

KJV renderings of H697 (full verse list from the concordance):

| Rendered "locust(s)"                                            | Rendered "grasshopper(s)"                              |
| --------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| Exodus 10:4, 12, 13, 14, 19 (×2) — the Egyptian plague          | **Judges 6:5** — Midian/Amalek/children of the east    |
| Leviticus 11:22                                                 | **Judges 7:12** — same triad                           |
| Deuteronomy 28:38                                               | Job 39:20                                              |
| 1 Kings 8:37; 2 Chronicles 6:28                                 | Jeremiah 46:23 ("they are more than the grasshoppers") |
| Psalm 78:46; 105:34; 109:23                                     |                                                        |
| Proverbs 30:27 ("the locusts have no king")                     |                                                        |
| **Joel 1:4; 2:25** — "the locust... my great army which I sent" |                                                        |
| Nahum 3:15, 17                                                  |                                                        |

So the very word God uses for His "great army" in Joel (1:4; 2:25, H697) is the word the Spirit chose for the camel-riding desert confederacy in Judges. The KJV translators' "grasshoppers" obscures in English what is one word in Hebrew.

**H2284 chagab** ("of uncertain derivation; a locust") — the _other_ word, checked honestly: it occurs in Leviticus 11:22, Numbers 13:33 ("we were in our own sight as grasshoppers"), 2 Chronicles 7:13, Ecclesiastes 12:5, Isaiah 40:22. **Judges is not in the H2284 list.** Judges uses H697 only. The claim does not rest on a homonym or a weaker synonym.

Bonus locust-army texts (KJV):

> **Joel 1:4** — That which the palmerworm hath left hath the locust [H697] eaten...
> **Joel 1:6** — For a nation is come up upon my land, strong, and without number, whose **teeth are the teeth of a lion**... (cf. Rev 9:8)
> **Joel 2:11** — And the LORD shall utter his voice before **his army**: for his camp is very great...
> **Joel 2:25** — ...the locust [H697]... **my great army which I sent among you**.

Joel supplies the _commission_ (locust = army God sends); Judges supplies the _address_ (the locust-multitude = the children of the east); Proverbs 30:27 even supplies the negative mark — natural locusts "have no king," but Rev 9:11's locusts **have** a king, so they are men, not insects.

## Strand 3 — "The east" is the Arabian world by Bible definition

**Genesis 25:6** — Abraham's other sons sent into Arabia:

> But unto the sons of the concubines, which Abraham had, Abraham gave gifts, and sent them away from Isaac his son, while he yet lived, **eastward, unto the east country**.

**Genesis 25:12-18** — Ishmael's twelve princes (Kedar among them), settled from Havilah to Shur — the Arabian peninsula:

> 12 Now these are the generations of Ishmael, Abraham's son, whom Hagar the Egyptian, Sarah's handmaid, bare unto Abraham:
> 13 And these are the names of the sons of Ishmael... the firstborn of Ishmael, Nebajoth; and **Kedar**, and Adbeel, and Mibsam,
> 14 And Mishma, and Dumah, and Massa,
> 15 Hadar, and Tema, Jetur, Naphish, and Kedemah:
> 16 These are the sons of Ishmael... **twelve princes according to their nations**.
> 17 And these are the years of the life of Ishmael, an hundred and thirty and seven years...
> 18 And **they dwelt from Havilah unto Shur, that is before Egypt, as thou goest toward Assyria**: and he died in the presence of all his brethren.

**Job 1:3** — the east = tent-and-camel country:

> His substance also was seven thousand sheep, and **three thousand camels**... so that this man was **the greatest of all the men of the east**.

**1 Kings 4:30**

> And Solomon's wisdom excelled the wisdom of all **the children of the east country**, and all the wisdom of Egypt.

**Isaiah 11:14** — "them of the east" listed with Edom, Moab, Ammon:

> But they shall fly upon the shoulders of the Philistines toward the west; they shall spoil **them of the east** together: they shall lay their hand upon **Edom and Moab; and the children of Ammon** shall obey them.

**Jeremiah 49:28-33** — Kedar (Ishmael's son, Gen 25:13) explicitly = "the men of the east," with the same Judges signature (tents, flocks, camels):

> 28 Concerning **Kedar**... thus saith the LORD; Arise ye, go up to Kedar, and spoil **the men of the east**.
> 29 **Their tents and their flocks** shall they take away: they shall take to themselves their curtains, and all their vessels, and **their camels**...
> 30 Flee, get you far off, dwell deep, O ye inhabitants of Hazor...
> 31 Arise, get you up unto the wealthy nation, that dwelleth without care... which have neither gates nor bars, which dwell alone.
> 32 And **their camels shall be a booty, and the multitude of their cattle a spoil**...
> 33 And Hazor shall be a dwelling for dragons, and a desolation for ever...

**Ezekiel 25:4** (against Ammon) and **25:10** (Ammon with Moab):

> 25:4 — Behold, therefore I will deliver thee to **the men of the east** for a possession, and they shall set their palaces in thee... they shall eat thy fruit, and they shall drink thy milk.
> 25:10 — Unto **the men of the east** with the Ammonites, and will give them in possession, that the Ammonites may not be remembered among the nations.

Chain: Ishmael → east country (Gen 25:6, 18); Kedar = men of the east (Jer 49:28); the lands of Ammon, Moab, Edom handed to the men of the east (Ezek 25:4, 10; Isa 11:14). These are the deserts of Arabia, Jordan, and the wider Arab world — today's Muslim east. The Bible's "children of the east" is a stable geographic-ethnic term for the Arabian desert peoples.

## Strand 4 — Revelation 9:1-11 (KJV)

> 1 And the fifth angel sounded, and I saw a star fall from heaven unto the earth: and to him was given the key of the bottomless pit.
> 2 And he opened the bottomless pit; and there arose a smoke out of the pit, as the smoke of a great furnace; and the sun and the air were darkened by reason of the smoke of the pit.
> 3 And there came out of the smoke **locusts** upon the earth: and unto them was given power, as the scorpions of the earth have power.
> 4 And it was commanded them that they should not hurt the grass of the earth, neither any green thing, neither any tree; but only those men which have not the seal of God in their foreheads.
> 5 And to them it was given that they should not kill them, but that they should be tormented five months: and their torment was as the torment of a scorpion, when he striketh a man.
> 6 And in those days shall men seek death, and shall not find it; and shall desire to die, and death shall flee from them.
> 7 And the shapes of the locusts were like unto **horses prepared unto battle**; and on their heads were as it were crowns like gold, and their faces were as the faces of men.
> 8 And they had hair as the hair of women, and their **teeth were as the teeth of lions**.
> 9 And they had breastplates, as it were breastplates of iron; and the sound of their wings was as the sound of chariots of many horses running to battle.
> 10 And they had tails like unto scorpions, and there were stings in their tails: and their power was to hurt men five months.
> 11 And **they had a king over them**, which is the angel of the bottomless pit, whose name in the Hebrew tongue is Abaddon, but in the Greek tongue hath his name Apollyon.

Internal markers tying back to the OT lexicon: lions' teeth (Joel 1:6), battle-horses (Joel 2:4), a king (contra Prov 30:27 — these locusts are men under a head), and a commission with limits (Joel 2:11; Judges 6:1 "the LORD delivered them into the hand of Midian").

## Strand 5 — Pioneer confirmation (full paragraphs, exact refcodes)

### Uriah Smith, _Daniel and the Revelation_ (1897)

**DAR 471.3** (introducing the fifth trumpet, quoting Keith):

> "The Roman emperor was not strengthened by the conquests which he achieved; and a way was prepared at the same time, and by the same means, for the multitude of **Saracens from Arabia, like locusts from the same region**, who, propagating in their course the dark and delusive Mohammedan creed, speedily overspread both the Persian and the Roman empire."

**DAR 472.1** (the fallen star and the key):

> "'The spirit of fraud and enthusiasm, whose abode is not in the heavens,' was let loose on earth. The bottomless pit needed but a key to open it, and that key was the fall of Chosroes. He had contemptuously torn the letter of an obscure citizen of Mecca. But when from his 'blaze of glory' he sunk into the 'tower of darkness,' which no eye could penetrate, the name of Chosroes was suddenly to pass into oblivion before that of Mohammed; and the crescent seemed but to wait its rising till the falling of the star. Chosroes, after his entire discomfiture and loss of empire, was murdered in the year 628; and the year 629 is marked by 'the conquest of Arabia,' and 'the first war of the Mohammedans against the Roman empire.' ... 'The robbers were the apostles of Mohammed, and their fanatic valor emerged from the desert.'"

**DAR 472.2** (Smith's own comment — the pit = the Arabian desert):

> "The Bottomless Pit. — The meaning of this term may be learned from the Greek ἄβυσσος, which is defined 'deep, bottomless, profound,' and may refer to any waste, desolate, and uncultivated place. It is applied to the earth in its original state of chaos. Genesis 1:2. In this instance it may appropriately refer to **the unknown wastes of the Arabian desert, from the borders of which issued the hordes of Saracens, like swarms of locusts**. And the fall of Chosroes, the Persian king, may well be represented as the opening of the bottomless pit, inasmuch as it prepared the way for the followers of Mohammed to issue from their obscure country, and propagate their delusive doctrines with fire and sword, till they had spread their darkness over all the Eastern empire."

**DAR 473.2** (on verse 2, the smoke):

> "Like the noxious and even deadly vapor which the winds, particularly from the southwest, diffuse **in Arabia**, Mohammedanism spread from thence its pestilential influence, — arose as suddenly and spread as widely as smoke arising out of the pit, the smoke of a great furnace. Such is a suitable symbol of the religion of Mohammed, of itself, or as compared with the pure light of the gospel of Jesus. It was not, like the latter, a light from heaven, but a smoke out of the bottomless pit."

**DAR 473.4** (on verse 3 — THE key paragraph):

> "A false religion was set up, which, although the scourge of transgressions and idolatry, filled the world with darkness and delusion; and swarms of Saracens, like locusts, overspread the earth, and speedily extended their ravages over the Roman empire, from east to west. The hail descended from the frozen shores of the Baltic; the burning mountain fell upon the sea from Africa; and **the locusts (the fit symbol of the Arabs) issued from Arabia, their native region**. They came as destroyers, propagating a new doctrine, and stirred up to rapine and violence by motives of interest and religion."

**DAR 473.5** (the scorpion power = Arab character):

> "A still more specific illustration may be given of the power, like unto that of scorpions, which was given them. Not only was their attack speedy and vigorous, but 'the nice sensibility of honor, which weighs the insult rather than the injury, sheds its deadly venom on **the quarrels of the Arabs**; an indecent action, a contemptuous word, can be expiated only by the blood of the offender; and such is their patient inveteracy, that they expect whole months and years the opportunity of revenge.'"

### The Judges/Gideon link in the pioneers — NOT FOUND

Searched honestly: "grasshopper(s)", "Gideon", and "children of the east" across DAR, SSTR, PREX1, PREX2, TMR, MWV1, MWV2. **No pioneer connects Judges 6-7 to the fifth-trumpet locusts.** ("Children of the east" hits in PREX1 76.1, PREX2 11.1, MWV1 228.1, DAR 647.2 are all unrelated contexts — Luke 13, Seleucid history, Isa 11:14 in another setting, Rev 16 kings of the east.) The pioneers reached Arabia through natural history (locust habitat) and through Gibbon's history of the Saracens; the Judges lexical argument is an _additional, independent_ line of Bible-defines-Bible evidence that lands on the same answer they reached.

## Synthesis

1. Joel defines the locust (H697) as an army God commissions and sends ("my great army," Joel 2:25); Proverbs 30:27 adds that real locusts have no king — so Rev 9's king-led locusts (9:11) must be symbolic men.
2. Judges supplies the missing **geographic** key: the only people-group the Bible ever calls 'arbeh is the camel-riding desert confederacy of Midian + Amalek + "the children of the east" (Judges 6:3-5; 7:12) — and the KJV's "grasshoppers" there is the very same Hebrew word (H697) rendered "locust" in Exodus 10, Joel, Psalms, and Nahum. Confirmed against H2284: Judges does not use the other word.
3. "The children/men of the east" is Bible-fixed Arabian geography: Abraham sent Ishmael's line and the concubines' sons "eastward, unto the east country" (Gen 25:6, 18); Kedar, Ishmael's son, IS "the men of the east" (Jer 49:28); the lands of Ammon, Moab, and Edom are given over to "the men of the east" (Ezek 25:4, 10; Isa 11:14) — the deserts of the Arab/Muslim world today.
4. Therefore when locusts pour out of the smoke in Rev 9:3, the Bible's own lexicon has already pointed the symbol at the Islamic east: locust = God-sent scourge-army (Joel) of the Arabian desert peoples (Judges + Gen 25 + Jer 49 + Ezek 25).
5. Smith, White, and Litch fixed the fifth trumpet on the Saracens "issued from Arabia, their native region" (DAR 473.4; SSTR 38.2; PREX2 169.3) on natural-history and historical grounds; the Judges argument they never used arrives at the identical address — making the Saracen identification a matter of Scripture's own definition, with the pioneer exposition as confirmation, not invention.
