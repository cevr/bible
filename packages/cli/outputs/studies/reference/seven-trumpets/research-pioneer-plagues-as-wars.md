# Did the Pioneers Read the Seven Last Plagues as Wars?

Research brief for the Seven Trumpets study.

## Short answer

The answer needs one clear distinction.

**William Miller came close to this view.**

Miller treated the trumpets and vials as related historical lines of judgment.
He said the trumpets judged the Roman kingdom.
He said the vials judged Papal Rome.
He interpreted several vials through massacres, wars, revolutions, and changes of government.

However, Miller did not make all seven vials into wars.
He read the first vial as the exposure of Roman corruption.
He read the fourth vial as deist opposition to the gospel.
He also expected literal hail in the last vial.

**Josiah Litch and the early Seventh-day Adventist writers did not teach that all seven plagues were wars.**

They placed the seven plagues under the seventh trumpet.
They treated them as the third woe after the first six trumpets.
They also called them real and literal judgments.
However, they kept the listed effects.
These effects include sores, poisoned water, heat, darkness, famine, earthquake, hail, and war.
They placed the military gathering under the sixth plague.
They placed the battle under the seventh plague.

The expanded corpus gives stronger evidence from Joseph Bates, J. H. Waggoner,
and A. T. Jones.
They placed the plagues in the seventh-trumpet period.
Jones joined this period to universal war and Armageddon.
However, Jones also separated the war from the plagues.
He said that five plagues pass before the gathering to battle under plague six.

Therefore, the evidence supports this statement:

> The pioneers linked the seven last plagues to the trumpet sequence.
> Some pioneers included literal war in the last plagues.
> William Miller interpreted several vials as historical wars.
> I found no pioneer who made all seven last plagues a second series of seven literal wars.

## The three claims

### 1. The plagues are literal judgments

Several pioneers taught this claim.

Josiah Litch wrote:

> "The vials, I must regard, as producing: the same literal effects ascribed to them in the 16th chapter of Revelation."
>
> — Josiah Litch, *Prophetic Expositions*, vol. 1, **PREX1 177.1**

Litch compared the plagues with the plagues of Egypt.
He expected a real sore, real blood in the waters, real heat, real darkness, and real destruction.

James White wrote:

> "These plagues are evidently shadowed forth by the plagues of Egypt, and will be as real and literal as those were."
>
> — James White, *A Brief Exposition of the Angels of Revelation XIV*, **BEARF 21.3**

J. N. Andrews asked:

> "Why will not the antitype be as real and literal?"
>
> — J. N. Andrews, *The Three Messages of Revelation 14:6-12*, **TMR 117.3**

Uriah Smith wrote:

> "There is no apparent reason why this should not be regarded as strictly literal."
>
> — Uriah Smith, *Daniel and the Revelation*, **DAR 644.1**

These writers did not use *literal* to mean *military*.
They used *literal* to mean that the judgments produce real physical effects.

### 2. One or more plagues include war

Several pioneers taught this claim.

Litch said the sixth plague prepares the nations for war.
He said:

> "The sixth vial is to prepare the way for the battle of that great day of God Almighty."
>
> — Josiah Litch, *Prophetic Expositions*, vol. 1, **PREX1 180.1**

He then gave the order of the last two plagues:

> "Being gathered by the sixth vial, under the seventh vial the battle will be fought."
>
> — Josiah Litch, *Prophetic Expositions*, vol. 1, **PREX1 183.2**

Andrews included war among the calamities of the plague period:

> "These dreadful calamities ... may be mentioned as ... pestilence, famine, and earthquake, and in the battle of the great day of God Almighty."
>
> — J. N. Andrews, *The Judgment. Its Events and Their Order*, **JEO 93.3**

Smith gave the same order:

> "The gathering of the nations having taken place under the sixth vial, the battle remains to be fought under the seventh."
>
> — Uriah Smith, *Daniel and the Revelation*, **DAR 653.2**

These passages support war inside the plague period.
They do not support seven separate wars.

### 3. All seven plagues form a war series that parallels or continues the trumpets

I found no text that makes this full claim.

Two related ideas do appear.

First, Miller treated the trumpets and vials as parallel historical lines.
Second, later pioneers placed the plagues under the seventh trumpet.

Neither idea makes all seven plagues into wars.

A. T. Jones later made the relation very clear.
He said that every trumpet announces war.
He also said that the plagues form part of the seventh trumpet.
However, he did not identify the seven plagues with seven wars.

## William Miller held the closest view

Miller described four repeated lines in Revelation.
He wrote:

> "The seven trumpets are a history of seven peculiar and heavy judgments sent upon the earth, or Roman kingdom. And the seven vials are the seven last plagues sent upon Papal Rome."
>
> — William Miller, *Miller's Works*, vol. 2, **MWV2 177.1**

Miller called these lines "four different streams of history."
This makes the vials parallel to the trumpets.
It does not make the vials a simple continuation of trumpet six.

Miller also joined both groups as judgments on the Roman world.
He wrote:

> "The three woes, and seven last plagues, were all to be sent upon the earth and sea, which denote wars, revolutions, and changing of governments."
>
> — William Miller, *Miller's Works*, vol. 2, **MWV2 211.1**

He then said that war would close the history of the fourth kingdom.
This is the strongest general statement for the proposed view.

Miller's exposition gives exact historical examples.

- He read the second vial as the 1572 massacre of the Huguenots.
  See **MWV2 221.1**.
- He read the third vial as "destructive war."
  He applied it to wars in Spain, Portugal, France, England, and Germany.
  See **MWV2 222.1**.
- He read the fifth vial through the French Revolution and Napoleon's wars.
  See **MWV2 222.1**.
- He read the sixth vial through the decline of the Ottoman Empire.
  He linked it to the fifth and sixth trumpets.
  See **MWV2 222.1**.
- He expected the seventh vial to bring "anger, war, and bloodshed" across the nations.
  See **MWV2 225.1**.

Miller still did not call every vial a war.

- He read the first vial as the Protestant exposure of Roman corruption.
  See **MWV2 220.3**.
- He read the fourth vial as angry deist opposition to Scripture.
  See **MWV2 222.1**.
- He thought the final hail was probably literal hail.
  See **MWV2 225.1** and **MWV2 229.1**.

Miller summarized his position with careful language:

> "Six of these plagues have accomplished in as literal a manner as we could expect, after a fair and scriptural explanation of the figures and metaphors used."
>
> — William Miller, *Miller's Works*, vol. 2, **MWV2 229.2**

Thus, Miller used literal history after he explained the symbols.
He did not read every plague image as a direct physical description.

## Litch changed from the historical-vial view

Litch records a clear change in his own view.
He wrote:

> "The time has been when I was disposed to fall in with the prevailing opinion; but more recently have been constrained to dissent from that view."
>
> — Josiah Litch, *Prophetic Expositions*, vol. 1, **PREX1 175.1**

The "prevailing opinion" placed at least six vials in past history.
Litch instead placed all seven plagues in the future.
He placed them after the Second Advent in this 1842 work.

Litch still connected the plagues with the trumpets.
He wrote:

> "Having passed through the six trumpets ... we stand ... on the verge of the great crisis when the seventh trump must sound."
>
> — Josiah Litch, *Prophetic Expositions*, vol. 2, **PREX2 224.1**

He then stated:

> "Under the seventh trumpet the seven vials are to be poured out."
>
> — Josiah Litch, *Prophetic Expositions*, vol. 2, **PREX2 224.1**

This is a continuation in prophetic order.
It is not a second series of military conquests.

## Early Seventh-day Adventist teaching

James White followed the same broad order.

He said the first six trumpets destroyed the divisions of the Roman Empire.
He said:

> "Under the first four trumpets the two western divisions fell, and under the fifth and sixth the eastern empire was crushed."
>
> — James White, *The Sounding of the Seven Trumpets of Revelation 8 and 9*, **SSTR 2.1**

He then placed the final fall under trumpet seven:

> "Under the seventh trumpet great Babylon entire will sink to rise no more at all."
>
> — James White, *The Sounding of the Seven Trumpets of Revelation 8 and 9*, **SSTR 2.1**

Later in the same work, he said the anger of the nations would be followed by the seven last plagues.
See **SSTR 68.2**.

Andrews made the distinction exact:

> "They are by this term distinguished from those plagues inflicted under the six trumpets."
>
> — J. N. Andrews, *The Judgment. Its Events and Their Order*, **JEO 90.1**

He then placed the last plagues under trumpet seven:

> "The seven last plagues come under the seventh trumpet."
>
> — J. N. Andrews, *The Judgment. Its Events and Their Order*, **JEO 93.2**

He called them the third woe.
He also listed pestilence, famine, earthquake, and battle.
See **JEO 93.2-3**.

Andrews gave the same position in an earlier work:

> "The seven plagues are poured out under the sounding of that angel; hence the plagues are future, and constitute the third woe."
>
> — J. N. Andrews, *The Three Messages of Revelation 14:6-12*, **TMR 116.2**

This position makes the last plagues the final woe of the trumpet sequence.
It also keeps them distinct from the judgments under trumpets one through six.

Uriah Smith kept the same distinction.
He argued for real physical effects under the first five plagues.
He placed the gathering of military powers under plague six.
He placed the battle under plague seven.
See **DAR 641.1-653.5**.

## Evidence from the expanded corpus

### Joseph Bates

Bates placed the last plagues under the seventh trumpet.
He wrote:

> "The Bridegroom came to the marriage under the sounding of the seventh trumpet, and after the third angel's message, and before six of the seven plagues are poured out."
>
> — Joseph Bates, *Second Advent Way Marks and High Heaps*, **BP2 103.1**

Bates also called current famine and national distress judgments under the third woe.
He called these judgments "emphatically the last plagues on Babylon."
See **BP2 94.1**.

This is an early form of the seventh-trumpet relation.
It does not make all seven plagues military wars.

### J. H. Waggoner

J. H. Waggoner made the trumpet order direct.
He wrote:

> "It is easily proved that the seven last plagues, in which 'is filled up the wrath of God,' Revelation 15:1, are poured out under this trumpet."
>
> — Joseph Harvey Waggoner, *Refutation of the Doctrine Called The Age to Come*, **RDAC 19.1**

He identified this trumpet as the seventh trumpet.
He called it a woe.
He then placed a pending "great battle, or universal war" under the sixth plague.
See **RDAC 30.1**.
He said that the great battle is fought under the plagues.
See **RDAC 30.2**.

This view joins war to the plague period.
It does not make each plague a war.

### A. T. Jones

Jones gave the strongest new evidence for the proposed relation.
He first defined all seven trumpets as war announcements:

> "The trumpet itself is the symbol of war. And in this alone is a suggestion that the seven trumpets would announce wars."
>
> — Alonzo T. Jones, *The Bible Echo*, **BEST May 25, 1896, page 155.3**

Jones said that trumpets one through six cover the fall of Western and Eastern Rome.
He said that trumpet seven gives the fall of all nations and the world.
See **BEST May 25, 1896, page 155.3**.

Jones then distinguished universal war from the seven plagues:

> "But it is not only that the nations are angry and that war must come, dreadful and universal; this is not all."
>
> — Alonzo T. Jones, *The Bible Echo*, **BEST June 1, 1896, page 164.3**

The next paragraph identifies God's wrath as the seven last plagues.
It says that they end with the end of the seventh trumpet.
See **BEST June 1, 1896, page 164.4**.

Jones repeated this distinction:

> "This is not simply a great time of trouble by war amongst the nations; it is even more than this."
>
> — Alonzo T. Jones, *The Bible Echo*, **BEST June 15, 1896, page 179.5**

He said that the trouble includes war and the judgments of God.
He named the seven plagues as the greatest element of this trouble.
See **BEST June 15, 1896, page 179.5**.

Jones also placed Revelation 16 inside the seventh trumpet:

> "The events of Revelation 14:6-20 and 16:1-21 are but an explanation in full of those mentioned in Revelation 11:18, 19, as the events of the sounding of the Seventh Trumpet unto its ending."
>
> — Alonzo T. Jones, *The Great Nations of To-day*, **GNT 116.1**

He then said that the last plague brings the earthquake and hail under trumpet seven.
See **GNT 116.2**.

Jones joined the plague period to Armageddon in strong words:

> "The seven last plagues are inseparably connected with that great day of battle and war."
>
> — Alonzo T. Jones, *The World's Greatest Issues*, **WGI 97.1**

However, he also gave a strict order.
He said that the plagues start before the nations gather to battle.
He said that five plagues pass before the gathering under plague six.
See **WGI 97.1** and **WGI 98.2-4**.

This evidence is decisive for the distinction.
Jones connected all seven plagues with the final war period.
He did not interpret all seven plagues as military wars.

## Conclusion

The proposed view combines two real pioneer ideas.

1. The first six trumpets show historical judgments through war.
2. The seven last plagues occur under the seventh trumpet and complete the third woe.

The pioneers did teach both ideas.

However, they did not turn every plague into a war.
Miller's older model came closest.
He made several vials into wars and revolutions in a parallel historical line.
Litch left that model.
The early Seventh-day Adventist model treated the plagues as future physical judgments.
It included the final military gathering and battle under plagues six and seven.

The expanded evidence makes the result firmer.
J. H. Waggoner placed the plagues under trumpet seven.
A. T. Jones said that the seven trumpets announce wars.
Jones also placed the seven plagues inside trumpet seven.
He joined the plague period to universal war and Armageddon.
Yet he said that five plagues pass before the military gathering.

The safest historical statement is:

> Miller interpreted several vials as war judgments that ran beside the trumpet judgments.
> Later pioneers placed the seven literal plagues under the seventh trumpet.
> They saw the plagues as the third woe after the six trumpets.
> Jones joined that final plague period to universal war and Armageddon.
> They did not teach that all seven plagues were seven literal wars.
