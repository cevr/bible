## 1 — The Scoreboard (5 min)

- Night 1: relative truth collapsed; we wrote a spec — five criteria for an
  absolute moral truth — and took a wager: _if a source met all five, would you
  follow it?_
- Night 2: criterion 2 went on trial. Daniel's manuscripts physically predate
  the fulfillments; the 70 weeks landed on AD 27; a published 1838 calculation
  named August 1840 — and friend and foe alike conceded the event.
- Four criteria evidenced. One remains: **benevolent — does it actually FIX our
  problem?**
- Jer 29:11 — "thoughts of peace, and not of evil" — that is the claim on trial
  tonight.
- [→] Tonight is not more proof THAT the Book is divine. Tonight is what the
  divine Book SAYS — about the thing every one of us has against God.

## 2 — The Question Every Worldview Must Answer (10 min)

- The riddle (traditionally ascribed to Epicurus, via Lactantius, _De Ira Dei_
  13 — authorship debated; the challenge stands regardless): willing but not
  able? not all-powerful. Able but not willing? not all-good. Both? then where
  does suffering come from?
- [→] Rule of the night, stated up front: a complete answer must do two things —
  **explain where suffering came from, and offer a real END to it.** Every view,
  including the Bible's, gets the same two questions.
- Materialism — honest, but no "why" and no end: "no design, no purpose… nothing
  but blind pitiless indifference" (Dawkins, _River Out of Eden_, ch. 4).
  Diagnosis without cure. (Respect: materialists fight suffering hard; it is
  their metaphysics that offers no remedy, not their character.)
- Buddhism — takes both questions seriously: suffering from craving; end by
  extinguishing craving (Four Noble Truths). The cure releases the sufferer by
  releasing the attachments — the very stuff love is made of.
- Hinduism — karma explains everything precisely (nothing is random), and moksha
  ends the cycle; the tension: present suffering reads as earned.
- Islam — suffering as test (Qur'an 67:2) under God's decree, justice in the
  hereafter: explains AND promises an end — deferred wholly to the next life.
- New Age — "suffering is illusion / low vibration": instant-feeling fix that
  breaks on real, external suffering (the documented victim-blaming critique —
  state it as critics state it, no mockery).
- The Bible itself asks the question FIRST: Job 30:26; Eccl 4:1 ("tears of the
  oppressed… no comforter"); Hab 1:2-3 ("Why dost thou shew me iniquity?").
- [→] And its answer has a different shape: suffering is an **intruder** — and
  it will be **abolished**. Person kept. World restored. Guaranteed from outside
  the system. A claim to be weighed — so let's weigh it.

## 3 — Love Requires Freedom (7 min)

- 1 John 4:8 — "God is love." Not "God loves" — love is His essence. Every move
  in tonight's story must be consistent with this, or the story fails.
- A forced "I love you" is not love. Love requires freedom; freedom creates risk.
- Gen 1:26-27 — image of God = moral agency, not automation.
- Deut 30:19 — "I have set before you life and death… therefore choose life."
  He stacks the appeal — and still leaves the choice.
- Josh 24:15 — "choose you this day." Isa 1:18 — "Come now, and let us reason
  together" — appeal to minds, never fear.
- [→] If creatures can truly choose love, they can truly refuse it. The question
  was never whether freedom would be tested — but what God would DO when it was.

## 4 — The Rebellion and the Accusation (10 min)

- Rev 12:7-9 — "war in heaven… that old serpent, called the Devil, and Satan" —
  the explicit anchor text; no interpretation needed.
- Eze 28:12-17 — "perfect in thy ways from the day that thou wast created, till
  iniquity was found in thee" — spoken of the king of Tyre, long read as the
  power behind him: evil began in the HIGHEST created being, not a design flaw.
  (Say the dual reading out loud — honesty is the brand.)
- Isa 14:12-14 — the five "I wills" (a taunt against Babylon's king — the same
  self-exaltation pattern). "I will be like the most High."
- John 8:44 — "a liar, and the father of it" — the weapon was never force; it
  was deception. This was a war of WORDS.
- Rev 12:10 — "the ACCUSER of our brethren… accused them before our God day and
  night." Pivot: this is a courtroom, not a battlefield.
- The four charges (name them — vague "problem of evil" is unanswerable; the
  actual accusation is specific):
  1. God is selfish and tyrannical (cf. Gen 3:5 — "God doth know…").
  2. His law is arbitrary and impossible.
  3. He buys loyalty — worship is bribery (Job 1:9-11 — "Doth Job fear God for
     nought?").
  4. He is unjust and vengeful.
- The cast: universe = watching jury (1 Cor 4:9 — "a spectacle… to angels");
  earth = the exhibit; Satan = prosecutor; God = the accused.
- Job 2:7 — watch who does the striking: "So went SATAN forth… and smote Job."
  God permits; the accuser afflicts. (Job 1:21 — Job, in his grief, credits God;
  the narration tells us who actually struck. Always pair them.)

## 5 — Why Not Just Destroy Him? (6 min)

- Because you cannot execute an accusation. Kill the accuser and the accusation
  survives — whispered forever.
- A defendant who destroys his accuser looks guilty. A defendant who answers
  with evidence wins in the open. **Force ≠ vindication.**
- If God had vaporized Lucifer on the spot, the watching universe would have
  served Him from fear — and fear is exactly what the accusation said His
  government ran on. The charge would be PROVEN by the rebuttal.
- Rom 3:4 — "that thou mightest be justified in thy sayings, and mightest
  OVERCOME WHEN THOU ART JUDGED." God consents to be judged. (Central verse —
  land it hard.)
- 1 Cor 4:9 / Eph 3:10 — the audience is real; the trial is public.
- [→] So the claims must be tested. Evil must be allowed to show what it IS — so
  the verdict, when it comes, stands forever and beyond all question.

## 6 — The Test Comes to Earth (7 min)

- Gen 2:16-17 — total freedom ("of EVERY tree thou mayest freely eat"), one
  test, stakes stated plainly.
- Gen 3:1-5 — the same lie, retold for humans: "Ye shall not surely die… ye
  shall be as gods" — God is holding out on you. The accusation, repackaged.
- Gen 3:6 — humanity switched sides. We believed the accuser — and the dominion
  of this world changed hands.
- Luke 4:5-6 — "All this power… for that is DELIVERED unto me" — and Jesus does
  not dispute the claim. John 12:31 — "the prince of this world."
- Rom 5:12 / Rom 6:23 — death entered; Gen 3:17-19 — thorns, toil, decay.
- [→] The broken world you see is NOT the world God made. It is the rebellion's
  exhibit, running its course in front of the universe. (2 Cor 4:4 — and its
  ruler works to keep the lights off.)

## 7 — Suffering Explained (8 min)

- Gal 6:7 — "whatsoever a man soweth, that shall he also reap"; Hos 8:7 — sow
  wind, reap whirlwind. Much suffering is HARVEST, not punishment — consequence
  built into a moral universe.
- Jas 1:13-15 — "Let no man say when he is tempted, I am tempted of God" — the
  chain runs desire → sin → death. Not authored by God.
- Matt 13:24-28 — the tares: "**An enemy hath done this.**" Suffering has a
  hostile author.
- Matt 13:29 — then why not rip it out NOW? "Lest while ye gather up the tares,
  ye root up also the wheat." A surgical strike on evil harvests the innocent
  with it — every life is entangled with every other. The only clean end is at
  the harvest.
- Lam 3:33 — "He doth not afflict WILLINGLY." Isa 63:9 — "In all their
  affliction HE was afflicted."
- John 11:35 — "Jesus wept." The shortest verse in the Book: God incarnate,
  weeping at a graveside He was about to empty.
- Heb 4:15 — touched with the feeling of our infirmities. Whatever else you say
  about the Bible's God — He did not stay out of the pain.

## 8 — The Cross: the Accusation Answered (10 min)

- The prosecutor's sharpest charge: if the law is just, the lawbreaker cannot be
  pardoned — justice and mercy cannot coexist. Pick one, God.
- Rom 5:8 — "while we were yet SINNERS, Christ died for us." John 3:16-17 —
  "gave his only begotten Son… NOT to condemn the world."
- God did not waive the law — He absorbed it. He did not change the rules to
  excuse us; He paid them. Himself. (2 Cor 5:19 — "God was in Christ,
  reconciling the world unto himself.")
- Isa 53:4-6 — "wounded for our transgressions… with his stripes we are healed"
  — written ~700 years before the event, and physically preserved in the Great
  Isaiah Scroll (~125 BC) — a copy that predates the crucifixion. (THIS is where
  the scroll argument is valid — use it here, not for Cyrus.)
- Col 2:14-15 — the cross "made a shew of them OPENLY" — at Calvary the watching
  universe saw the accuser torture and kill the only innocent man. Whatever
  sympathy his case still had, died there.
- John 12:31-32 — "NOW is the judgment of this world; now shall the prince of
  this world be cast out." 1 John 3:8 / Heb 2:14 — the works of the devil
  destroyed through death itself.
- John 15:13 — "Greater love hath no man than this." He would rather die than
  lose you. **That is criterion 5 — answered in blood.**

## 9 — Why Is It Still Running? (4 min)

- 2 Pet 3:9 — "not slack… but LONGSUFFERING… not willing that any should
  perish." The delay is a rescue window, not absence.
- Matt 24:14 — the message reaches all nations, "and THEN shall the end come."
  Mission-driven timeline with a defined endpoint.
- Rev 6:10-11 — even the martyrs ask "How long?" — the Book owns the impatience
  and answers it. Hab 2:3 — "the vision is yet for an APPOINTED time… it will
  surely come" (closing the loop Habakkuk opened in §2).

## 10 — How It Ends (8 min)

- John 14:1-3 — "I will come again, and receive you unto myself." Acts 1:11 —
  "this same Jesus… shall SO come in like manner" — literal, visible,
  eyewitness-anchored.
- 1 Thess 4:16-17 — the dead in Christ rise; reunion, not loss.
- Rev 20:11-12 — "the books were opened… judged out of those things which were
  written" — the judgment is evidence-based and PUBLIC. God ends the case the
  way He ran it: in the open.
- Nah 1:9 — "**affliction shall not rise up the second time.**" Won't it just
  happen again? No — because the question was answered in public, once, before
  the whole universe. Destruction at the START would have planted fear;
  extermination at the END vindicates love — the same act means opposite things
  before and after the evidence is in.
- Rev 21:1-4 — "God shall wipe away all tears… no more death, neither sorrow."
  The tears of Eccl 4:1 — wiped personally.
- Rev 22:1-5 — no more curse; the tree of life; "they shall see his face." Eden,
  bookended.
- Isa 65:17, 21-25 — houses, vineyards, labor that isn't futile, wolf and lamb —
  not clouds and harps: a restored EARTH. 1 Cor 2:9 — and that picture is the
  floor, not the ceiling.
- [→] The two questions, answered: WHERE FROM? An intruder — real rebellion,
  real fall. DOES IT END? Abolished — person kept, world restored, guaranteed
  from outside the system.
