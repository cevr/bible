### Introduction (5 min)

- **The question that fractured the world.** Before you can know what the Bible
  _means_, you must settle _how_ it is to be read. Every great division between
  truth and error did not begin with a different Bible — it began with a
  different way of reading the same Bible.
- **Go back to the very first words the enemy ever spoke.** Not a denial. A
  question — about interpretation. Genesis 3:1: "Now the serpent was more subtil
  than any beast of the field which the LORD God had made. And he said unto the
  woman, **Yea, hath God said,** Ye shall not eat of every tree of the garden?"
  `[Write on board: "Yea, hath God said?" (Gen 3:1)]`
- **Watch the play, step by step.** He does not start by contradicting God; he
  unsettles the plain word — _did God really say that? did He mean it like that?_
  - **Loosen it (v.2-3).** Eve answers, and already the word drifts: "neither
    shall ye touch it" — words God never said. Once you handle the word loosely,
    you have already lost it.
  - **Reverse it (v.4).** Only now the open contradiction: "Ye shall not surely
    die."
  - **Offer a "higher" hidden meaning (v.5).** "Your eyes shall be opened, and ye
    shall be as gods, knowing good and evil." The lie always offers a deeper,
    secret meaning behind the plain command. `[Write on board: Question -> Loosen
-> Reverse -> "Higher" hidden meaning]`
- **The fall was a hermeneutical event.** Every false system since has run this
  same play. So the question for tonight is the oldest question in the world,
  pressed on you and me: _will I take God at His plain word, or supply my own
  meaning?_
- **Roadmap:** (1) the Bible's own rule for reading itself; (2) the counterfeit
  that despises the plain word; (3) the return to the sure word.

### Section 1: The Bible's Own Rule — The Author Holds the Meaning (10 min)

- **The Bible is not silent about how to read the Bible.** It tells us plainly.
  And notice — these are not one man's clever rules. This is what Scripture
  teaches about itself.
- **Rule 1 — The Author holds the meaning, not the reader. (2 Peter 1:20-21)**
  - "Knowing this first, that no prophecy of the scripture is of any **private
    interpretation.** For the prophecy came not in old time by the will of man:
    but **holy men of God spake as they were moved by the Holy Ghost.**"
  - The same Spirit who _gave_ the word must _interpret_ it — not the private,
    ingenious mind of the reader. `[Write on board: No PRIVATE interpretation
(2 Pet 1:20)]`
  - (RQ) When I read, am I asking "what did the Author mean?" — or "what can I
    make this mean?" **(RQ)**
- **Rule 2 — The word is light, not a riddle. (2 Peter 1:19)**
  - "a **more sure word** of prophecy... a **light** that shineth in a dark
    place." Sure and light-giving — not a code needing a mystic key.
- **Rule 3 — Gather everything before you conclude. (Isaiah 28:9-10)**
  - "precept upon precept, precept upon precept; line upon line, line upon line;
    here a little, and there a little." Doctrine is built by gathering _all_ that
    Scripture says — comparing, not importing.
- **Rule 4 — Scripture is its own dictionary. (1 Corinthians 2:13)**
  - "**comparing spiritual things with spiritual.**" The Bible defines its own
    symbols. [EGW] "The Bible is its own expositor. One passage will prove to be
    a key that will unlock other passages, and in this way light will be shed
    upon the hidden meaning of the word. By comparing different texts treating on
    the same subject... the true meaning of the Scriptures will be made evident."
    (CE 85.1) `[Write on board: Bible = its own expositor (CE 85.1)]`
- **Rule 5 — Never break Scripture against itself. (John 10:35)**
  - Out of the lips of Christ Himself: "**the scripture cannot be broken.**" Its
    unity is the test. No interpretation may pit one text against another.
- **Rule 6 — It is sufficient and clear. (2 Timothy 3:15-17)**
  - The holy scriptures "are able to make thee wise unto salvation," and "All
    scripture is given by inspiration of God, and is profitable for doctrine."
    Enough. Clear. Inspired.
- **Rule 7 — Add nothing; take nothing away. (Proverbs 30:5-6; Revelation 22:18-19)**
  - "Every word of God is pure... **Add thou not unto his words,** lest he reprove
    thee, and thou be found a liar." And the closing warning of the whole Book:
    "If any man shall add unto these things, God shall add unto him the
    plagues..." To supply a hidden meaning the words do not carry is to **add** to
    the word. `[Write on board: Add nothing. Take nothing away. (Prov 30:6)]`
- **This is the Reformers' ground.** [EGW] Of Zwingli: "He saw that it must be
  **its own interpreter.** He dared not attempt to explain Scripture to sustain a
  preconceived theory or doctrine, but held it his duty to learn what is its
  direct and obvious teaching." (GC 173.2) The pioneers did not invent this
  method — they _recovered_ the Bible's own.

### Section 2: The Counterfeit — Origen and the "Hidden Sense" (10 min) [*]

- **There is another way to read the Bible. It has a name, a city, and a date.**
  And it is the open door to every error.
- **The poisoned root: Alexandria. (GEP 386)** [EGW] The pagan philosopher
  Ammonius Saccas set out "to bring all sects and religions into harmony,"
  doing "much violence to the sentiments and opinions of all parties... and
  **particularly by allegorical interpretations to remove all impediments out of
  his way.**" (GEP 386.1) He clothed an "AEgyptiaco-Platonic philosophy" —
  pantheism, the eternity of the world — in the language of Scripture.
  `[Write on board: Alexandria: allegory used to "remove impediments" (GEP 386)]`
- **The method passes into the church.** [EGW] His followers "believed the
  language of Scripture to contain two meanings; the one obvious... the other
  recondite, and concealed under the words, like a nut by the shell. **The former
  they neglected, as of little value,** their study chiefly being to extract the
  latter." (GEP 386.4)
- **Origen perfects it.** [EGW] "Origen imbibed all the allegorical and
  mystifying processes of both Ammonius and Clement, and **multiplied upon them
  from his own wild imagination.** He was not content with finding two meanings in
  the Scriptures... but took the secondary sense, the hidden meaning, and added
  to it four additional meanings of his own." (GEP 387.2; same record in TTR
  218.4)
- **Here is the heart of the lie — they _despised the plain word._** [EGW]
  Origen taught the Scriptures have a threefold sense, and "as the body is the
  baser part of man, so the literal is the baser sense of Scripture; and as the
  body often betrays good men into sin, so **the literal sense of Scripture often
  leads into error.**" (GEP 388.1) The plain meaning "was to be despised and
  separated as far as possible from the hidden sense, and counted of the least
  possible worth." (GEP 388.2) `[Write on board: "the literal is the BASER sense"
— Origen (GEP 388)]`
- **Hear Origen in his own words** — and judge for yourself which voice this is:
  - [EGW] "**The source of many evils lies in adhering to the carnal or external
    part of Scripture. Those who do so will not attain to the kingdom of God. Let
    us therefore seek after the spirit and substantial fruit of the word, which
    are hidden and mysterious.**" (GEP 388.3, quoting Origen)
  - And the logical end of it: "**The Scriptures are of little use to those who
    understand them as they are written.**" (GEP 388.5, quoting Origen)
  - (RQ) "Hath God said?" — is that the serpent's voice, or Origen's? It is the
    **same voice.** _Don't take it as it is written; seek the hidden, mysterious
    meaning._ **(RQ)**
- **Where this method leads.** [EGW] It was "in that dismal mixture of downright
  heathenism, and the profession and forms of Christianity in the philosophical
  schools of Ammonius Saccas, Clement, and Origen, in Alexandria," that the
  mainstay of the papacy was born. (ECE 95.2) When the plain word is despised,
  the people are soon forbidden to read it at all.
- **Now you have your answer.** _Why so many competing interpretations? Why so
  much contention over "what the Bible means"?_ Because **two methods are in the
  field.** Once the plain sense is despised and a "hidden meaning" is admitted,
  there is no longer any control on meaning — every interpreter supplies his own
  secret key, and the Bible becomes a wax nose, twisted to any shape. The
  division is not in the Book. It is in the method men bring to the Book.
  `[Write on board: Division is in the METHOD, not the Book]`
- **Guard the line: illustration vs. interpretation.** A figure the text itself
  marks is honest; a hidden meaning the interpreter imports is not. [EGW] John
  Bunyan, in Bedford jail, "wrote his wonderful **allegory** of the pilgrim's
  journey from the land of destruction to the celestial city... [it] has guided
  many feet into the path of life." (GC 252.2) Bunyan's allegory _teaches_ the
  plain truth as an illustration; Origen's allegory _replaces_ it as an
  interpretation. One serves the word. The other supplants it.

### Section 3: The Return — The Sure Word (6 min)

- **The remedy is not new. It is the old paths.**
- **Be a Berean. (Acts 17:11)** "These were more noble... in that they received
  the word with all readiness of mind, and **searched the scriptures daily,
  whether those things were so.**" They tested every claim by the plain word —
  not by an authority's hidden key. `[Write on board: "searched the scriptures
daily" (Acts 17:11)]`
- **Hold the more sure word. (2 Peter 1:19)** The Author's light, shining in a
  dark place — sufficient to guide us home.
- **Apply the one test. (Isaiah 8:20)** "**To the law and to the testimony: if
  they speak not according to this word, it is because there is no light in
  them.**" One test, and it is the plain word.
- **This is exactly what the pioneers recovered.** [EGW] Of William Miller — and
  note, this is a hostile editor reporting: "Mr. Miller is a **great stickler for
  literal interpretations; never admitting the figurative, unless absolutely
  required** to make correct sense or meet the event which is intended to be
  pointed out." (MWV1 16.2) And on that single method — Scripture its own
  interpreter — the great prophecies opened: [EGW] "**Following his rule of making
  Scripture its own interpreter,** Miller learned that a day in symbolic prophecy
  represents a year." (GC 324.3)
- **Miller's own confession of the rule.** Where a figure is plainly marked, the
  Bible itself explains the figure: "**For the word of God is its own expositor,
  or it can be of no manner of use to us;** for if we have to apply to any other
  rule, to explain the Bible, then the other rule would be tantamount... and the
  Bible must fall of course. But it is not so." (MWV2 233.3) `[Write on board:
Take God at His plain word — let the Spirit interpret it by itself]`
- **This is the foundation under all Bible study.** Get this wrong, and every
  doctrine after it is at the mercy of the interpreter's imagination. Get this
  right, and the Bible stands up as one harmonious whole.

### Conclusion and Appeal (4 min)

- **Recap — two voices, two methods.**
  - The serpent's voice: _"Yea, hath God said?"_ → loosen → reverse → seek the
    hidden meaning. (Gen 3) The plain word despised. (GEP 388)
  - The Saviour's voice: "**It is written... by every word that proceedeth out of
    the mouth of God.**" (Matthew 4:4) The plain word honored, "the scripture
    cannot be broken." (John 10:35) `[Write on board: "Yea, hath God said?" vs.
"It is written"]`
- **And remember what — Who — the Word is.** "And the Word was made flesh, and
  dwelt among us... full of grace and truth." (John 1:14) To despise the plain
  word is to despise the One who is the Word. To take Him at His word is to take
  hold of Christ Himself.
- **The covenant decision — this is yours to make tonight.** Two methods stand in
  the field. You will read your Bible by one of them. There is no third way.
  - **Will I take God at His plain word** — letting the Spirit who wrote it
    interpret it by itself, gathering every text, breaking nothing, **adding
    nothing and taking nothing away** —
  - **or will I supply my own meaning,** reaching past the plain word for a
    "higher," hidden sense of my own making?
- **Tonight, before God, settle it:**
  - "I take my stand on the **sure word.** When God has spoken plainly, I will not
    ask 'hath God said?' — I will say, **'God hath said,'** and obey." **(RQ)**
  - "I lay down my private key. I let the Bible be its own expositor."
  - "I will be a Berean — I will search the Scriptures daily, and test every voice
    by the law and the testimony." (Acts 17:11; Isaiah 8:20)
- **The foundation is firm.** "How firm a foundation, ye saints of the Lord, is
  laid for your faith **in His excellent word!**" Build your house on the Rock of
  the plain word of God — and when the floods come, it will stand.
