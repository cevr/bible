Dear Brethren at Heritage and Hope Ministries,

Grace and peace be unto you through our Lord Jesus Christ. I write as one who has been blessed by your ministry and who values your labor in defence of the faith once delivered to the saints. I have listened with profit to many of your presentations, and I thank God for the courage you have shown in the present conflict over Adventist history and doctrine. It is because I esteem this work that I venture to lay before you a question which has rested upon my mind. I trust that you will receive these thoughts in the same brotherly spirit in which they are written, and that together we may bring every position to the law and to the testimony.

In your presentation on the seven trumpets, you state that Heritage and Hope follows the historicist method. You defend the historic Adventist understanding of Revelation 9 and its connection with 1844. At about [10:08 in the trumpet presentation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jiJQi8KHjEI&t=608s), your guest makes the important statement that the trumpets are "not ...philosophies or theologies, but ...armies." This is sound ground. Prophecy presents real powers acting in real history, and the record of their work confirms the word which God has spoken.

It was therefore with concern that I heard a different rule used in the presentation on Daniel 11. At about [20:20 in the Daniel 11 presentation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vcEs2hnH9CY&t=1220s), your guest says that everything changes from literal to spiritual after the cross. He says that there is no more literal Israel and no more literal sanctuary. On this basis, the king of the south is changed from Egypt into atheism, and the king of the north is changed into the papacy. Brethren, let us consider whether we can use one rule for the trumpets and another for Daniel 11. If the trumpets remain armies and not philosophies, should not the kings of Daniel 11 remain real powers unless the Scripture itself directs us to another meaning?

We all acknowledge that the New Testament identifies the covenant people of God in Christ. Romans 2:28-29 speaks of the circumcision of the heart, and Galatians 3:29 declares that those who are Christ's are Abraham's seed. Yet this precious truth does not establish a rule by which every king, nation, army, ship, place, and direction after the cross becomes a spiritual idea. Christ Himself foretold literal events after Calvary. Luke 21:20-24 speaks of Jerusalem compassed with armies, of Judea, flight, sword, and captivity. Acts 1:11 promises the real return of Christ in the same manner as His ascension, and 1 Corinthians 15:20-23 promises a real resurrection. The cross has not changed these truths into philosophies.

The statement concerning the sanctuary deserves special thought. Hebrews 8:1-2 calls the heavenly sanctuary "the true tabernacle, which the Lord pitched, and not man," while Revelation 11:19 shows the temple of God in heaven and the ark within that temple. The heavenly sanctuary is not earthly, but neither is it unreal. If the words "no more a literal sanctuary" were intended only to distinguish the heavenly sanctuary from the earthly type, I would earnestly ask that this be made plain. Your presentation on *Questions on Doctrine* defends the sanctuary and the continuing ministry of Christ. It would be a grief if language used in the interpretation of Daniel 11 should weaken the very doctrine which you are seeking to uphold.

Let us now look at the chapter itself. The angel begins, "And now will I shew thee the truth" (Daniel 11:2). He names Persia and Greece, places the early kings in the north and south, and identifies the southern territory through Egypt in verse 8. Your presentation places the cross in verses 20-22, yet the prophecy continues through verse 35 with leagues, provinces, armies, land, ships, sword, flame, captivity, and spoil. These are still real acts in history after the cross. Surely one rule should govern the whole chapter.

The old historicist exposition did not leave this historical path at verse 35. Uriah Smith understood the persecution in that verse to reach to the close of the 1260 years in 1798, when the time of the end began (DAR 264.2; 489.1). He then identified the wilful and atheistic power of verses 36-39 with Revolutionary France (DAR 265.1), while S. N. Haskell followed the same line and pointed to France as the nation which publicly denied the existence of God (SDP 241.1). Thus atheism was a character of the French power. It did not become a new definition of the king of the south.

Verse 40 then brought three distinct powers upon the field. Smith retained Egypt as the king of the south and identified the power holding the old northern territory as Ottoman Turkey (DAR 273.3). France entered Egypt in 1798. The decisive Battle of the Pyramids was fought on July 23, and the Egyptian Mamelukes made only a feeble resistance (DAR 275.1). Then, after the French fleet was destroyed at Aboukir, the Ottoman sultan declared war against France on September 2. Smith therefore read the verse in its plain relations: Egypt, the king of the south, pushed at France; Turkey, the king of the north, came against France; and France was the power meant by "him" (DAR 275.2). Brethren, let us consider the force of this interpretation. It preserves the time, the geography, the grammar, and the three historical actors without turning any king into a philosophy.

The closing verses make the matter still plainer. They speak of chariots, horsemen, ships, countries, Egypt, Libya, Ethiopia, Edom, Moab, Ammon, the east, the north, seas, a mountain, and a palace. The chapter gives no notice that these words have received new meanings. It nowhere defines the king of the south as atheism or the king of the north as the papacy. When Revelation intends Egypt to carry a spiritual meaning, it plainly says that the great city "spiritually is called Sodom and Egypt" (Revelation 11:8). Daniel 11 contains no such declaration. Shall we not allow the plain words of the prophecy to have their proper bearing?

The connection between Daniel 11 and Daniel 12 brings another solemn question before us. Daniel 11:44 says that the final king goes forth "with great fury to destroy, and utterly to make away many." Verse 45 then declares, "he shall come to his end, and none shall help him." The next words are, "And at that time shall Michael stand up" (Daniel 12:1). Ellen White quotes this verse and applies it to the close of Christ's intercession in the heavenly sanctuary (GC 613.1-2). She also says that when our High Priest finishes His work, "He will stand up," and the seven last plagues will then be poured out (EW 36.1).

The order is therefore clear. The king comes to his end; Michael stands; probation closes; and the plagues fall. Yet in the time of trouble which follows, Romanism and apostate Protestantism unite in the decree against those who keep the Sabbath (GC 615.2). The fifth plague then falls upon the seat of the beast and fills his kingdom with darkness (Revelation 16:10-11). Uriah Smith understood this plague as a direct judgment upon the papacy, with Rome as its seat and the Catholic world as its kingdom (TBI 230.1; STTHD 303.1). If the papacy is the king who has already come to his end before Michael stands, how can it afterward lead in the death decree and remain to receive the fifth plague? Would not the prophetic order be more harmonious if the power that comes to its end in Daniel 11:45 is distinct from the beast power that continues through the plagues?

William Miller gave us a sound rule when he wrote, "Scripture must be its own expositor." He also taught that words should be understood literally when they make good sense and do no violence to the simple laws of nature. Ellen White placed these rules before those who were preaching the third angel's message and said that they had studied on the same plan as Father Miller (Review and Herald, November 25, 1884, paragraphs 23-24). She also wrote:

> "The language of the Bible should be explained according to its obvious meaning, unless a symbol or figure is employed." - *The Great Controversy*, p. 598.3.

Daniel 11:40-45 makes good sense as real conflict, and the chapter does not mark its kings as new symbols. The pioneer record adds light here. James White argued from the parallel lines of Daniel 2, 7, 8, and 11 that Rome remained the final power. His position was literal historicism, not a philosophy or a mindset. In his 1877 editorial, he considered whether the seat of the Roman beast would move to the United States instead of the Turkish government moving to Palestine. He expected a concrete Roman power to occupy real geography, even while he urged the brethren not to press unfulfilled details beyond the evidence.

Uriah Smith later identified the king of the north as Turkey, and this view became broad in published Adventist teaching. E. J. Waggoner identified Turkey as the king of the north in 1897; A. T. Jones did the same in 1900; and S. N. Haskell followed the same line in *The Story of Daniel the Prophet*. James White and these later writers did not agree on the identity of the power, but they stood upon common historicist ground. Each expected a real power to act in real history. None changed the king into a philosophy.

The seventh trumpet bears the same witness. James White taught that its sounding occupies a prophetic period (SSTR 67.5-6), Uriah Smith placed its beginning in 1844 (DAR 493.2-3), and J. N. Andrews taught that the seven last plagues fall under its sounding and constitute the third woe (TMR 116.2). The sixth plague gathers the kings of the earth to "the battle of that great day of God Almighty" (Revelation 16:12-16). S. N. Haskell called Armageddon the final battle which closes all earthly scenes and placed it under the sixth plague (BHB 128.2, 128.6). Uriah Smith placed the gathering under the sixth vial and the battle itself under the seventh (DAR 653.2). Their common testimony is unmistakable: Armageddon is a real final conflict, not a philosophy or state of mind. Under the seventh trumpet, probation closes, the plagues fall, the nations gather, and the judgments of God bring earthly kingdoms to their end.

Ellen White did not name the final king of the north, but she kept Daniel 11 in the field of history and real events. In 1904, while the world was stirred with the spirit of war, she wrote that the prophecy of Daniel 11 had nearly reached its complete fulfillment (9T 14.2). She also declared that much of the history described in this prophecy would be repeated (13MR 394.1). These statements harmonize with the plain historicist reading of a chapter which begins with Persia and Greece and continues through the conflict of earthly powers to the standing up of Michael.

Louis F. Were stated the later method in unmistakable words. In *The King of the North at Jerusalem* (1949), he wrote that entry into the Christian era causes "an automatic transition from literal to spiritual Babylon." He extended this rule to Jerusalem, Israel, Rome, the nations, the lands, and the compass directions, and he made the papacy the spiritual king of the north. The statement in your Daniel 11 presentation closely follows this method. Whether it came directly through Were is not the main question. The question is whether the rule comes from Scripture or has been placed over Scripture.

Your presentation on *Questions on Doctrine* gives this matter added weight. You trace a movement from *Questions on Doctrine*, through LeRoy Froom, to the later crisis surrounding Desmond Ford. You show how a change in method can affect the sanctuary, the atonement, 1844, the year-day principle, and the trumpets. I believe we should consider the same danger in Daniel 11. The doctrines and the men are not identical, yet a common method can work through different teachings. When a rule not found in the text is placed over the text, the plain meaning of Scripture is made to serve a system.

For this reason, the warning concerning the alpha and the omega comes solemnly to mind. Ellen White said that *Living Temple* contained "the alpha of deadly heresies" (1SM 200.1), and that Scripture was wrongly used in its teaching (1SM 203.2). She warned also of false theories which "spiritualize the doctrines of present truth" until the distinction between the substance and the shadow is lost (MM 87.4). I do not say that these men taught the same doctrine, nor do I place upon any man a name which Inspiration has not given. I ask only that we recognize the danger of a method which gives plain prophetic terms new meanings without authority from the Word itself.

Brethren, shall we not bring these lines together and allow each to have its proper bearing? Where does Daniel 11 tell us that its kings have changed into philosophies? Where does Scripture define the king of the south as atheism, or the king of the north as the papacy in verses 40-45? How does that reading account for the three distinct actors in verse 40, the Battle of the Pyramids, and Turkey's declaration of war against France in the very year when the time of the end began? If the papal king ends before Michael stands, how does Romanism continue into the death decree and the fifth plague? How does this interpretation agree with your strong statement that the trumpet powers are armies and not philosophies? How does the phrase "no more a literal sanctuary" agree with the true tabernacle of Hebrews 8:1-2 and the heavenly temple of Revelation 11:19? And how does this view answer the literal historicism found in both the Rome position of James White and the Turkey position of Smith, Waggoner, Jones, and Haskell?

I offer these questions as a brother and a fellow student of prophecy. Your ministry has benefited me, and it is my earnest desire that your defence of historic Adventism remain strong and consistent. It would bring me joy to hear you examine this matter in a future program and apply Miller's rules to Daniel 11:40 through Daniel 12:1. Let us come to the Word with teachable hearts and allow Scripture to explain Scripture. May the Lord preserve us from every human rule which could hide its plain testimony, and may He guide us together into a deeper knowledge and love of His truth.

With Christian respect and gratitude,

Cristian
