# _Righteousness by Faith_ against the pioneer frame

Reviewed 2026-09-25 against the frame in `.claude/skills/film/frame/` (the 1889 principles and the topic files). It reviews the first cut, now `src/films/righteousness-by-faith-v1`; the paths below are as they were then. The rebuild in `src/films/righteousness-by-faith` answers each finding. The quotations below are verified records in `righteousness-by-faith-frame-review.jsonl` beside this file and in the frame's `.jsonl` files. Nothing here has been applied to the film yet.

Checked:

- the script: `apps/animations/src/films/righteousness-by-faith/script.ts`;
- the drawings that stage the flagged beats: `scenes/exchange.ts`, `scenes/robe.ts`, `scenes/eighteen88.ts`;
- the sources file: `apps/animations/script/sources.md`.

Every quotation below was checked against the corpus. Film lines are quoted from `script.ts` with the `{cue}` marks removed.

**What already fits the frame.** Several choices need no change:

- The film keeps Rom 8:3 in Scripture's own words.
- Faith is "the hand", not the Saviour (DA 175).
- The robe "takes the sin away" rather than cloaking it (CHR 65).
- The film moves from imputed to imparted righteousness.

The findings below are about what the 1889 statement and the 1888 sources put next to these points and the film leaves out. They run from most to least important. Each gives the beat, what the film says or shows, the pioneer source, and a suggested fix.

## 1. `exchange`: the cross shadow closes the atonement at the cross

- **Film:** "He took our place, and gave us his. Ellen White said it in one line: “Christ was treated as we deserve, that we might be treated as He deserves.”" The picture: "A cross shadow falls across the page on 'treated as we deserve'." The next beat, `robe`, cuts to Zechariah. No later beat returns to what Christ does after the cross.
- **Pioneer source:** the 1889 statement's own note to Principle II names this view in order to reject it.

  > We object to the view that the atonement was made upon the cross, because it is utterly contrary to the type, which placed the atonement at the end of the yearly sanctuary service, not at the beginning

  — **FP1889 147.3** · 1889 _Fundamental Principles_, note to Principle II

  > what Christ did upon the cross was to provide a divine sacrifice for the world, sufficient to save all, and offered it to every one who will accept of it; that he then, through the merits of his offering, acts as mediator with the Father till time shall end, securing the forgiveness of sins for all who seek him for it; and that, as the last service of his priesthood, he will blot out the sins of all who have repented and been converted (Acts 3:19), the atonement not being completed till this work of blotting out sin is done.

  — **FP1889 147.3** · 1889 _Fundamental Principles_, note to Principle II

  > The intercession of Christ in man’s behalf in the sanctuary above is as essential to the plan of salvation as was His death upon the cross. By His death He began that work which after His resurrection He ascended to complete in heaven.

  — **GC 489.1** · Ellen G. White, _The Great Controversy_ (1911)

- **Fix:** Keep the cross shadow on "treated", then let it hand off to the ascension. Christ is the sacrifice who becomes the mediator. A spoken bridge could use Rom 4:25, "raised again for our justification", or Heb 7:25, "he ever liveth to make intercession". Do not call the cross "the atonement" anywhere in narration or captions.

## 2. Whole film: Christ's present work as High Priest never appears

- **Film:** No scene draws a sanctuary, altar, mercy seat, throne or intercession. `within` cites Heb 8:10 but not the chapter's opening on the heavenly High Priest (Heb 8:1-2). `serpent` quotes a letter of 1891 and John 3:14.
- **Pioneer source:** the letter behind the film's own "most precious message" line goes straight on to the heavenly courts. It then sets the brazen serpent inside a sanctuary picture.

  > Christ is pleading for the church in the heavenly courts above, pleading for those for whom He paid the redemption price of His own life blood.

  — **10LtMs, Lt 57, 1895, par. 44** · Ellen G. White, Letter 57, 1895, the paragraph after the "most precious message" paragraph. It is printed as TM 92.1, where "life blood" reads "lifeblood".

  > As the high priest sprinkled the warm blood upon the mercy seat while the fragrant cloud of incense ascended before God, so while we confess our sins, and plead the efficacy of Christ’s atoning blood, our prayers are to ascend to heaven fragrant with the merits of Christ’s character. … As the serpent was lifted up in the wilderness by Moses, and all that had been bitten by the fiery serpents were bidden to look and live, so also the Son of man must be lifted up

  — **10LtMs, Lt 57, 1895, par. 45** · Ellen G. White, Letter 57, 1895, printed as TM 92.2

- **Fix:** Before "God promised to write His law in our hearts", add one shot of the risen Christ pleading in the heavenly sanctuary, with Heb 8:1-2 as the text. `serpent` could open on the high priest at the mercy seat before the camp scene, which follows the order of par. 45.

## 3. `1888`: the third angel's message is named but never stated

- **Film:** "…when she was asked whether it was the third angel's message of Revelation 14, she answered: “It is the third angel's message in verity.”" The picture writes "the third angel · Revelation 14" under a trumpet angel. Nothing says what that message is.
- **Pioneer source:**

  > The third angel’s message is the proclamation of the commandments of God and the faith of Jesus Christ. The commandments of God have been proclaimed, but the faith of Jesus Christ has not been proclaimed by Seventh-day Adventists as of equal importance, the law and the gospel going hand in hand.

  — **5LtMs, Ms 24, 1888, par. 42** · Ellen G. White, Ms 24, 1888 (her account of the Minneapolis General Conference, par. 1)

  > And this is the Third Angel’s Message: “Here are they that keep the commandments of God, and the faith of Jesus.”

  — **GNT 256.4** · A. T. Jones, _The Great Nations of To-day_. There is no imprint year in the corpus. The same paragraph ran in ARSH October 23, 1900.

- **Fix:** Put Rev 14:12 on the angel's banner, or in one spoken line: "the commandments of God, and the faith of Jesus".

## 4. `within` / `justified`: obedience and the Sabbath after justification are left out

- **Film:** "God promised to write His law in our hearts. Imputed, then imparted." The law-circle settles into the heart as a gold ring. Obedience to the commandments is never said, and the Sabbath never appears. The letter behind "a most precious message" ties that message to obedience in the same sentence (par. 43, below). `sources.md` quotes that clause, but the script drops it.
- **Pioneer source:**

  > XVIII. That as all have violated the law of God, and cannot of themselves render obedience to his just requirements, we are dependent on Christ, first, for justification from our past offenses, and, secondly, for grace whereby to render acceptable obedience to his holy law in time to come.

  — **FP1889 150.6** · 1889 _Fundamental Principles_, Principle XVIII

  > It presented justification through faith in the Surety; it invited the people to receive the righteousness of Christ, which is made manifest in obedience to all the commandments of God.

  — **10LtMs, Lt 57, 1895, par. 43** · Ellen G. White, Letter 57, 1895, printed as TM 91.2

  > But I think anybody here can see that the third angel’s message is righteousness by faith; for the Sabbath is righteousness by faith; for by it a man comes into God’s works, and those works are perfect. Therefore he gets rest by faith.

  — **GCDB March 8, 1897, page 303.7** · E. J. Waggoner, "Studies in the Book of Hebrews. - No. 16" (sermon of Feb. 28, 1897; byline at page 297). The corpus author field names the Bulletin's editor, A. T. Jones.

- **Fix:** After "Imputed, then imparted", add one line: the same grace that forgives now keeps His law, the Sabbath rest included (Heb 4:9-10). The heart-ring could keep the two tables visible inside it rather than melting into a plain ring.

## 5. `robe`: the accuser, the Advocate and the day of atonement are missing

- **Film:** "The high priest stands in filthy garments, and God says, “Take away the filthy garments from him,” and, “I will clothe thee with change of raiment.”" `scenes/robe.ts` draws only the priest, the wind that takes the rags, and the loom. There is no Satan at his right hand, no Angel and no courtroom.
- **Pioneer source:** Ellen White's chapter "Joshua and the Angel" was first printed in 1885 in Testimony No. 32; 5T 3.1 dates No. 32 to 1885. It reads the vision as a courtroom at the close of the day of atonement.

  > Zechariah’s vision of Joshua and the Angel applies with peculiar force to the experience of God’s people in the closing up of the great day of atonement.

  — **5T 472.2** · Ellen G. White, _Testimonies for the Church_, vol. 5 (first printed 1885 as T32 228.2)

  > As Joshua humbly pleads for the fulfillment of God’s promises, Satan stands up boldly to resist him. He points to the transgressions of Israel as a reason why that people should not be restored to the favor of God.

  — **5T 468.1** · Ellen G. White, _Testimonies for the Church_, vol. 5 (first printed 1885 as T32 224.1)

  > Then the Angel, who is Christ Himself, the Saviour of sinners, puts to silence the accuser of His people

  — **5T 469.1** · Ellen G. White, _Testimonies for the Church_, vol. 5 (first printed 1885 as T32 225.1)

  > But Jesus our Advocate presents an effectual plea in behalf of all who by repentance and faith have committed the keeping of their souls to Him.

  — **5T 470.2** · Ellen G. White, _Testimonies for the Church_, vol. 5 (first printed 1885 as T32 226.2)

  > The filthy garments are to be removed, and Christ’s robe of righteousness is to be placed upon us. By repentance and faith we are enabled to render obedience to all the commandments of God, and are found without blame before Him.

  — **5T 472.1** · Ellen G. White, _Testimonies for the Church_, vol. 5 (first printed 1885 as T32 228.1)

- **Fix:**
  - Stage Zech 3:1-2. Satan stands at Joshua's right hand, pointing at the stains, and the Angel, Christ, silences him.
  - Only then are the filthy garments taken.
  - One line of narration can set the scene: this is the closing day of atonement, and Christ is our Advocate.
  - Using 5T 472.1 would also answer 4.4, because it puts obedience to "all the commandments" inside the robe scene itself.
  - The same chapter ends with "Their sins are transferred to the originator of sin" (5T 475.2), which is the scapegoat. Do not show the removed rags simply blowing away as if sin had no destination.

## 6. `exchange`: the rags pass to Christ right after "in the likeness of sinful flesh"

- **Film:** "Christ came all the way down to us, “in the likeness of sinful flesh.” He took our place, and gave us his."
  - In `scenes/exchange.ts` the descent runs from `came` to just after `flesh`.
  - The Rom 8:3 caption stays up until just before `took`.
  - The garment swap, rags onto Him, begins at `took`.
  - The cross shadow arrives only at `treated`.
  - A viewer can read the rags as the flesh He took rather than the sins He bore.
- **Pioneer source:** Rom 8:3 is quoted correctly, and Jones and Waggoner both argue from it. Where it leads, the sources pull in different directions:

  > the flesh which He assumed had all the weaknesses and sinful tendencies to which fallen human nature is subject

  — **CHR 26.2** · E. J. Waggoner, _Christ and His Righteousness_ (no date in the corpus; announced as "just issued" in GCDB March 19, 1891, page 165.1)

  > Thus in the flesh of Jesus Christ, — not in himself, but in his flesh, — our flesh which he took in the human nature, — there were just the same tendencies to sin that are in you and me.

  — **GCB February 21, 1895, page 266.9** · A. T. Jones, "The Third Angel's Message — No. 14", _General Conference Bulletin_ (1895)

  > In him was no guile or sinfulness; he was ever pure and undefiled; yet he took upon him our sinful nature.

  — **RH December 15, 1896, par. 7** · Ellen G. White, "The Importance of Obedience", _Review and Herald_

  > Never, in any way, leave the slightest impression upon human minds that a taint of, or inclination to corruption rested upon Christ, or that He in any way yielded to corruption.

  — **10LtMs, Lt 8, 1895, par. 16** · Ellen G. White, Letter 8, 1895, to "Bro. and Sr. Baker"

- **Fix:**
  - Start the rags-onto-Him swap at `treated`, under the cross shadow, so the rags read as our sins laid on Him (1 Pet 2:24, which the 1889 note cites).
  - Keep Rom 8:3 as spoken.
  - After it, add the counterweight "the example of a sinless life" (DA 48.6: "He came with such a heredity to share our sorrows and temptations, and to give us the example of a sinless life.").
  - Do not narrate either side's gloss as "the pioneer view".

## 7. `1888`: where the message was heading is left out

- **Film:** The beat ends on "in verity".
- **Pioneer source:** the same letter paragraph the film quotes for "most precious message" ends:

  > It is the third angel’s message, which is to be proclaimed with a loud voice, and attended with the outpouring of His Spirit in a large measure.

  — **10LtMs, Lt 57, 1895, par. 43** · Ellen G. White, Letter 57, 1895, printed as TM 91.2

  > we know by every evidence that now we are in the times of refreshing—the time of the latter rain. … And the blotting out of sins is exactly this thing of the cleansing of the sanctuary

  — **CWCP 124.2** · A. T. Jones, _The Consecrated Way to Christian Perfection_ (no imprint year in the corpus)

- **Fix:** Close the beat with one line: the message was to swell into the loud cry and the latter rain, when sins are blotted out (Acts 3:19; Rev 18:1).

## 8. `1888`: framing, pictures and dating

- **"two young editors"** is not supported by anything in `sources.md` or in this frame. The corpus does support "Minneapolis 1888": Ellen White went to "the General Conference held in Minneapolis" (5LtMs, Ms 24, 1888, par. 1). If the film wants a primary framing, it can use either of these:

  > The Lord has raised up Brother Jones and Brother Waggoner to proclaim a message to the world to prepare a people to stand in the day of God.

  — **8LtMs, Ms 61, 1893, par. 9** · Ellen G. White, Ms 61, 1893, printed as _1888 Materials_ 1814.4

  > Elder E. J. Waggoner had the privilege granted him of speaking plainly and presenting his views upon justification by faith and the righteousness of Christ in relation to the law. This was no new light, but it was old light placed where it should be in the third angel’s message.

  — **5LtMs, Ms 24, 1888, par. 25** · Ellen G. White, Ms 24, 1888

- **"a church where many had lost sight of Jesus"**, drawn as heads bowed over small tablets, sets the law against Christ. The source's complaint is that the two had been separated, not that the law was the problem:

  > This message of the gospel of His grace was to be given to the church in clear and distinct lines, that the world should no longer say, Seventh-day Adventists talk the law, the law, but do not preach or believe Christ.

  — **10LtMs, Lt 57, 1895, par. 44** · Ellen G. White, Letter 57, 1895, printed as TM 92.1 with "that" before "Seventh-day Adventists" and "teach" for "preach"

  Resolve the crowd into Christ and the tablets together (Rev 14:12).

- **Dating.** "A most precious message" is from 1895, seven years after Minneapolis. The on-screen cite "Testimonies to Ministers, 91" points to a 1923 compilation (TM ix.1). Cite the letter instead: "Letter 57, 1895 (TM 91)".

## 9. Openings the film can take without new material

- **`exchange` ties to the third angel's message.** In her own account of Minneapolis, Ellen White makes the substitution the content of "the faith of Jesus":

  > What constitutes the faith of Jesus that belongs to the third angel’s message? Jesus becoming our sin-bearer that He might become our sin-pardoning Saviour. He was treated as we deserve to be treated. He came to our world and took our sins that we might take His righteousness.

  — **5LtMs, Ms 24, 1888, par. 43** · Ellen G. White, Ms 24, 1888

  One on-screen echo in `1888` ("treated as we deserve") would tie the two beats together.

- **Cite originals, not compilations.** Two of the film's lines have earlier originals in the corpus. Both were verified word for word:
  - "Do not climb the pole, but only look. I present Christ to you. Look and live." is **7LtMs, Lt 85, 1891, par. 6**. The film cites _Manuscript Releases_, vol. 13, 150.
  - "The first is our title to heaven, the second is our fitness for heaven." is **RH June 4, 1895, par. 7**. The film cites _Messages to Young People_, 35.

## 10. Corrections to `script/sources.md`

- **GCDB March 8, 1897, page 308.10** (`sources.md` "p. 308.10": "The righteousness of Jesus Christ by faith is the loud cry of the third angel’s message") is credited to A. T. Jones. That is wrong. The paragraph belongs to the sermon headed "Righteousness by Faith. I. D. VAN HORN. (Sunday Evening, Feb. 28, 1897.)", which runs from page 305 to the next heading at page 309. Credit it to I. D. Van Horn, or drop it.
- **GCDB March 8, 1897, pp. 297–303.8** is E. J. Waggoner, "Studies in the Book of Hebrews. - No. 16". The corpus author field names the editor, A. T. Jones.
- **TM 91.2–92.2 is Letter 57, 1895, pars. 43–45** (10LtMs). The 1888 Materials index, which is editorial, gives it as a letter to O. A. Olsen of May 1, 1895.
  - `sources.md` gives the TM text. The letter reads "the world should no longer say, Seventh-day Adventists … do not preach or believe Christ". TM has "that" before "Seventh-day Adventists" and "teach" for "preach".
  - `sources.md` dates TM 91.2 "(1895)". That is the letter's year. The book is a 1923 compilation (TM ix.1).
