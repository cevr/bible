The person is the plain lead, the parable's "a certain man", exactly as his model sheet draws him: a small figure, a round head sitting right on a trapezoid body with no neck, his head nearly as wide as his hem, two short rounded stub legs under the hem; no hair, no ears, no nose, no shoes. One pale cool grey paper with a faint lilac cast for his head, hands, robe and legs (lit side #dbd6dc, shade side turned lilac-violet #b5a9d0, never a darker grey). A face of three marks: two short arched brows, two small dark oval dot eyes and a one-line mouth. For the wedding feast he wears his festive dress, the one thing the story changes: a longer robe of his own grey paper reaching to his shins, a quiet muted blue-green sash (#5f8f88) tied at the waist, and arms in sleeves of the same grey paper to the wrist, each round grey hand at the end of its sleeve, no thumbs. No pattern, trim, pockets or seams. His height and his width are his model sheet's: the sheet does not make him taller or slimmer. What he carries, and only where a pose names it: a small plain baby's bottle with a pale teat and no markings; a plain plate.
