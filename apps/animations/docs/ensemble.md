# The ensemble: a recurring cast in the background

Owner, 2026-10-13: "for the films in general, and to add to the skills that I think we should have an ensemble, a cast of characters, other than, you know, the main characters of each film, like a Jesus or something, that we kind of show in each film, so that there can be a sense of continuity."

Owner, 2026-10-15: "i actually liked more the non descriptive character in the room, let's make the recurring cast only background characters, and the main characters non descriptive so they can be better related".

So the cast is what the first word asked for: people other than a film's main characters, seen in film after film, so the films feel like one world. They stand in the background: at the next table, in the court's gallery, on the stair, in a crowd. A film's main characters are someone else: Bible figures, or plain figures a viewer can stand in (the plain lead, [character-design.md](../../../.claude/skills/film/reference/character-design.md) 2.3). BibleProject works the same way: one Everyman carries its Themes film, and its world reuses silhouettes and shots from film to film ([bibleproject-study.md](../../../.claude/skills/film/reference/bibleproject-study.md), V6).

This file is the cast bible. The film skill's **Ensemble** rule ([SKILL.md](../../../.claude/skills/film/SKILL.md)) points here. What the colours mean, how each role is designed, the plain lead, the minor figures and every character sheet are in [character-design.md](../../../.claude/skills/film/reference/character-design.md).

## The parable's figures

Everyone in the films' own world, outside the Bible's scenes, keeps these: the plain lead in front and the cast behind.

- **Parable figures.** "A certain man" and "a woman" of a parable. Jesus taught that way: "without a parable spake he not unto them" (Matt 13:34). Each appearance pictures a truth the film's text states. Nothing in their lives is a truth of its own.
- **Never real people, never history.** The narration never presents one as someone who lived, never tells a life as news or testimony ("Last week a man…"), and never claims an event happened. A parable scene is introduced the way the drafts already do it: "Picture a grown man at a wedding feast…" (theology 01). Fiction is avoided; allegory is allowed, framed openly as allegory (the owner's rule, 2026-10-13, carried in each series' `series.md`).
- **Never Bible people.** Jesus, Moses, Daniel and the rest stay the main figures of their own scenes. No parable figure plays a Bible person, and no Bible person joins the cast.
- **For every Christian.** No one carries a denominational marker: no church name or sign, no badge, no clothing, headcovering or habit that marks a group, no hymnal or book title in view. What they do is what the film's own text teaches, and nothing more ([series README](../series/README.md), "Voice and audience").
- **Christ's method on screen.** "The Saviour mingled with men as one who desired their good. He showed His sympathy for them, ministered to their needs, and won their confidence. Then He bade them, 'Follow Me'" (MH 143.3). A plain lead's need is one a viewer shares, and the film lets the viewer watch it met.
- **A parent pictures a likeness the text names.** The parenting pictures rest on texts that state the likeness, so a parent on screen illustrates, never invents (typology rule):
  - "Like as a father pitieth his children, so the LORD pitieth them that fear him" (Ps 103:13).
  - "Can a woman forget her sucking child … yet will I not forget thee" (Isa 49:15); "As one whom his mother comforteth, so will I comfort you" (Isa 66:13).
  - "I taught Ephraim also to go, taking them by their arms" (Hos 11:3).
  - "As a man chasteneth his son, so the LORD thy God chasteneth thee" (Deut 8:5); Heb 12:9–10, "for our profit, that we might be partakers of his holiness".
  - "If ye then, being evil, know how to give good gifts unto your children, how much more…" (Matt 7:11).
  - Luke 15:20, the father who "saw him, and had compassion, and ran".
    A parent never stands in for God the Father as a figure of God. God the Father is shown only as a hazy human form veiled in light (character-design 3.3).
- **One narrator.** No one in the parable's world speaks, sings or lip-syncs. Faces and whole bodies act ([CRAFT.md](../../../.claude/skills/film/CRAFT.md) rule 5; [rimworld-study.md](../../../.claude/skills/film/reference/rimworld-study.md) §2 rule 7); the narrator voices any question a face asks.

## The cast

Eight people: a family of five in a city flat, the grandmother in the hills, and two neighbours in the same building. Ages are their ages in every film (see Continuity). They are background figures (owner, 2026-10-15): never a film's lead, its accused, or the one whose need is the film's question. The one exception is a film's own "Picture a…" illustration when the owner casts a cast member as its figure (owner, 2026-10-16: "the grandmother should be one the background cast members, the old lady"): Rosa is the grandmother of RBF's `garden`.

The hue slots sit inside the band of [CRAFT.md](../../../.claude/skills/film/CRAFT.md) rule 12: every garment at HSV saturation 0.20–0.35, with scarlet the only accent, gold only for God's word and gift, and white only for the robe. The slots spread round the wheel about 40° apart and skip the reds and oranges from 330° to 50°: scarlet (357°), the chipboard world (about 25°) and gold (41°) live there. Each ramp shades toward the cool side under a warm key and lights toward the warm side, as rimproto C2 did; the papercut look's ramp step governs the final numbers. Heads and hands are the shared figure paper (open choice 2), and hair is cut chipboard as today, so a person is told apart by garment hue, silhouette and hair.

### Hugo, the father

- **Age and role:** 41. The father of the household; a builder by trade.
- **Temperament:** warm, loud, quick to laugh and quick to flare; just as quick to own it.
- **Habit:** in the wrong, one hand circle goes to the back of his head and he takes half a step back. Glad, he lifts a child high, the child rising beside him with his hand circles under them.
- **Silhouette:** the tallest (1.0) and broadest, square shoulders; short cropped hair and a short square beard, the only beard in the cast.
- **Slot 5, steel blue,** the darkest garment: a work jacket over trousers.
- **Seen:** at his workbench, on the stair with a child on his shoulders, at the head of the family's table.

### Clara, the mother

- **Age and role:** 38. The mother of the household. Rosa's daughter.
- **Temperament:** steady, observant, a planner with a quiet wit.
- **Habit:** she tilts her head and waits before she answers, "swift to hear, slow to speak" (Jas 1:19). Mila is often on her hip.
- **Silhouette:** middle height (0.92), slender; one long plait over the near shoulder, which reads from the front and the side.
- **Slot 4, teal:** a long dress or tunic to below the knee.
- **Seen:** at the table with a book open, Mila on her hip, at her mother's gate.

### Leo, the son

- **Age and role:** 15. The eldest child.
- **Temperament:** quiet, quick, dry under his breath, sceptical of anything he has not tested.
- **Habit:** he glances at the phone in his hand, then turns it face down.
- **Silhouette:** nearly his father's height (0.98) and the narrowest, with the longest legs; a tuft of hair standing up at the crown.
- **Slot 1, khaki:** a long, loose shirt.
- **Seen:** on the stair with the phone, at his father's bench, at the edge of the family group.

### Nina, the daughter

- **Age and role:** 7. The middle child.
- **Temperament:** strong-willed, fearless, loud, curious, "why?" about everything.
- **Habit:** she plants her feet, sets both hand circles on her hips and lifts her chin.
- **Silhouette:** small (0.62) and round; two bunches of hair sticking straight out, the widest head in the cast.
- **Slot 7, lavender,** light: a short smock over leggings.
- **Seen:** running ahead on the stair, at a parent's knee, hands on her hips at the gate.

### Mila, the little one

- **Age and role:** about 18 months. The baby of the family.
- **Temperament:** sunny, clinging, all reach.
- **Habit:** both hand circles up, reaching for whoever comes in, with a little bounce.
- **Silhouette:** the smallest (0.40), round, one curl on top; on Clara's hip the pair reads as one silhouette with two heads.
- **Slot 3, mint,** the lightest garment, a near neighbour of her mother's teal.
- **Seen:** on Clara's hip, reaching; on the floor with a cup. She grows in no film; the ledger keeps her small.

### Rosa, the grandmother

- **Age and role:** 71. Clara's mother, a widow, living alone in a cottage with a garden in the hills.
- **Temperament:** unhurried, merry, deep; hard to rattle and quick to notice.
- **Habit:** she presses something growing into another's hands: a seedling, a fruit, a cutting.
- **Silhouette:** short (0.82), a slight forward stoop; a shawl over the shoulders makes a triangle; outdoors, the widest brim in the cast, a garden hat. Silver hair in a bun, in a pale cool paper, never the robe's white.
- **Slot 8, plum,** dark: a long skirt, the shawl in a lighter neighbour of the slot.
- **Seen:** at her gate and in her garden in the hills, reading by the lamp; her husband's portrait on her shelf.

### Max, the neighbour across the landing

- **Age and role:** 29. He lives alone across the landing and works long hours at a desk in the city.
- **Temperament:** wry, tired, curious.
- **Habit:** a takeaway cup in one hand circle; sceptical, one brow up and his head tipped back.
- **Silhouette:** middle height (0.95), round-shouldered with his head forward; a hooded jacket with the hood down, a bump at the nape.
- **Slot 2, sage:** the hooded jacket.
- **Seen:** across the landing with a takeaway cup, at a desk in the city, on the stair late at night.

### Amara, the nurse downstairs

- **Age and role:** 46. A nurse on night shifts at the city hospital, living on the ground floor.
- **Temperament:** brisk, warm, unflappable at work, worn out at home.
- **Habit:** she lays a hand circle on a shoulder, or on a forehead to feel for fever.
- **Silhouette:** middle height (0.90), sturdy; a round crown of hair larger than the head; a square tunic hem at the hip over trousers.
- **Slot 6, periwinkle:** the tunic.
- **Seen:** home from a night shift with her bag over her shoulder, a hand on a neighbour's shoulder, at her door on the ground floor.

## Where the cast appears

- **In the background of the parable's scenes:** the cold open and the landing (the court's gallery, the next table at the feast, the street outside), the IDEA page behind its plain figures, a talk's cut-paper inserts. One or two at a time. A cast member is never featured: never the reason the camera moves, never given the close-up, never the one the narration is about.
- **Never the lead.** The lead of a parable scene is the plain lead, one figure in every film: a small figure of pale cool grey paper with a lilac cast, a round head with no hair, a plain trapezoid tunic, stub legs and circle hands (owner, 2026-10-15: "though this is what i mean by non descript"; [plain-lead.png](ref/plain-lead.png), his sheet [plain-lead-sheet.png](ref/plain-lead-sheet.png), character-design 2.3). The same figure stands for a parable's man or woman; a second plain figure in the same film differs by its paper or its height only. The cast stands behind. No film makes a cast member its accused, its seeker or the one whose need is its question, and none gives one a turn of faith or of life on screen.
- **The bridge.** A push-through from the parable's frame into the Bible scene ([CRAFT.md](../../../.claude/skills/film/CRAFT.md) rule 3): an open book on the page, and through its page into Berea.
- **Never inside a Bible scene.** No cast member stands in a STORY scene of Bible history: not in Capernaum's crowd, at the cross, at Sinai, in Babylon. No one from the parable's world plays a Bible person or a figure of Christ's own parables (the sower, the prodigal son and his father are drawn as that parable's figures). A plain lead may mirror a parable in the parable's frame after the parable is told: a father at the window as the narration closes on Luke 15:20.
- **Christ in the parable's frame.** Christ may come into the parable's world at the landing, where a stated text says He comes (Rev 3:20; 1 John 2:1), as the drafts already have Him: the host of the feast in 01, the Advocate beside the accused in 08. No one from the parable's world goes into His history. The Father appears only as a hazy, veiled human form, never in the parable's world (character-design 3.3).
- **Talks.** Education and health are talks: the owner on camera, cut with B-roll. Their everyday moments are short cut-paper inserts drawn and rendered by the film engine, so the faces are the same as in the films (open choice 6): a plain figure in front, the cast behind. The owner speaks; the figures act.
- **Shorts.** A short's hook frame (CRAFT § Shorts rule 2) carries the plain lead; a cast face may stand behind it.

### How often

- **Most films show the cast,** one or two at a time in the background of the cold open, the landing or both, as the owner's first word asked ("that we kind of show in each film"). The Bible's scenes carry the middle.
- **The talks and the country-living films** put the cast behind their everyday inserts: on the stair, in the street, at the next table, at Rosa's gate.
- **The cast before the crowd kit.** Where a background needs a person a viewer might know (a neighbour on the stair, a face in the gallery), a cast member stands there. A crowd of strangers takes the crowd kit (character-design 5), never cloned ([CRAFT.md](../../../.claude/skills/film/CRAFT.md) rule 12).

### Continuity

- **Fixed ages.** The cast does not age between films; a parable has no calendar.
- **Homes.** The city building: the family's flat on the third floor, Max's across the landing, Amara's on the ground floor, a shared stair and a rooftop (a film lands there only if it opened there, CRAFT rule 4). The hills: Rosa's cottage (her gate and her garden), and along the lane a cottage garden with a bench under a fig tree (Mic 4:4), a set where a plain lead sits (_Righteousness by Faith_'s garden; the country series' landing). These places are a shared shot library: a film reuses their layouts, so a viewer knows where they are at once.
- **Relationships.** Rosa is Clara's mother. Rosa's husband is asleep in Jesus: never cast, never shown alive in the cast's present, a paper portrait on her shelf. No romance, feud, accident or mystery between cast members: the cast has no plot of its own.
- **No names in speech.** The names are for scripts, sheets and this file. The narration never names a cast member, and a cast member is never the one it speaks of (open choice 1).
- **Look.** Minimal and expressive: circle hands of their own paper, no thumbs, no fingers, no arms; a face minimal but characterful, its kit the owner's pick (Kit A, three marks, 2026-10-13; [face-study.md](../../../.claude/skills/film/reference/face-study.md)); shade with colour, never black or grey; outlines may be dark ([CRAFT.md](../../../.claude/skills/film/CRAFT.md) rule 12). Faces default warm, not cross (the film skill's Gotchas: raise the inner brows, give the glad a smile).

### Recognition, not backstory

Owner, 2026-10-13: "im wondering if there needs to be a backstory, what im going for is recognizable cast of characters that gives a sense of continuity and familiarity."

No backstory beyond what each person's entry above already holds. A viewer comes to know a cast member the way they know a neighbour: by seeing the same person in the same places doing the same small things, never by being told a history. A parable's figure needs no past (The parable's figures). Familiarity is built from what shows on screen, film after film:

- **The same silhouette and one feature,** readable at quarter size (Before the first film, 2): Hugo's beard, Clara's plait, Nina's two bunches, Rosa's shawl triangle, Max's hood.
- **The same clothes in the same hue slot.** No outfit changes except what the scene needs (a coat outdoors).
- **One signature prop and one habit,** repeated so it becomes theirs: Amara's hand on a shoulder or forehead, Rosa's open book, Max's takeaway cup. A habit shows whenever the person is in frame.
- **The same places,** shot from the same layouts: the stair, the landing, the rooftop, Rosa's gate, the bench under the fig tree.
- **The same relationships, shown by staging.** Who sits by whom, who opens the door to whom, who runs to whom.
- **Small running gestures across films.** A detail that pays off for those who have seen earlier films and costs nothing to those who have not: Rosa's husband's portrait on her shelf, the bench under the fig tree, the rooftop at evening. Nothing in them ages or changes (Continuity, fixed ages).

Each person's entry is kept to these card facts. A fact that never reaches the screen is not added.

### The continuity ledger

A film's `script.ts` header names any cast member it puts in its background and the ledger state it assumes. The ledger is the cast's present. It changes only by a named turn, in the film named, with the owner's yes. No turn is planned: a background figure carries no story of its own (the owner's rule, 2026-10-15).

| State                     | Now (2026-10-15)                         | The turn that may change it |
| ------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- | --------------------------- |
| Where the household lives | The city flat; Rosa's cottage is visited | None (open choice 9)        |
| Who is living             | All eight; Rosa widowed                  | None (open choice 4)        |

Were a turn ever made, films before it would keep the old state and films after it the new one. A film that stands alone (every one should) never depends on the viewer having seen a turn.

## Before the first film uses them

1. **Owner's picks first.** The cast is drawn in the paper-cut look after the owner's rimproto picks (outline, ink, colour variety, head size); the slot table assumes C2, more hues.
2. **A cast sheet.** Each person as a rig with front, side and back facings ([rimworld-study.md](../../../.claude/skills/film/reference/rimworld-study.md) §2 rule 10), set in a lineup at full size and at quarter size. Done when, at quarter size, each person is named correctly from the silhouette filled dark (`--mode value`) and again from the hues blurred (`--mode squint`), and no two read alike.
3. **The two homes as layouts:** the city building (stair, landing, flats, roof) and Rosa's cottage (gate, garden, bench), each a reusable set.

## Open choices for the owner

1. **Names in speech.** Recommended: never spoken. A parable's "a certain man" lets every viewer stand in his place, and the one narrator never has to introduce anyone. The alternative, names spoken in talks only, makes continuity easier to follow at the cost of the parable's openness. The plain lead keeps the same rule, and more: no name in a brief either (character-design 2.3).
2. **Heads and hands.** Recommended: the one shared figure paper for every cast member, as every film draws people now, so the cast reads as anyone in any country, and rimproto C2's two-tone tan face is avoided. The alternative is three or four warm skin papers inside the band, each with its own ramp. **Answered (owner, 2026-10-13): the one shared figure paper.**
3. **The size of the cast.** Recommended: eight. The alternative is six, dropping Amara and Mila: a smaller cast is quicker to know, but fills fewer of the world's places (the ground floor, a parent's hip).
4. **Grief.** Recommended: Rosa is a widow, her husband's portrait on her shelf, and no cast member dies. Theology 12's grief with hope belongs to a plain lead (the owner's rule, 2026-10-15). The alternative, Rosa falling asleep in 12 and waking in 34, would make her a lead and is closed by that rule.
5. **Christ in the parable's frame.** Recommended: yes, at the landing only, where a stated text says He comes, as 01 and 08 already draw Him. The alternative keeps the parable's world and Christ always apart.
6. **Everyday inserts in the talks.** Recommended: every everyday moment in an education or health talk, its plain figure in front and the cast behind, is a cut-paper insert from the film engine, so the faces match; generated or filmed B-roll stays for places and nature. Bible scenes in the talks are generated illustrations in the sermon decks' painting style (owner, 2026-10-13), not cut-paper, so a talk never puts a Bible figure beside the cast.
7. **Recasting 08.** **Reversed (owner, 2026-10-15): "i actually liked more the non descriptive character in the room".** _Righteousness by Faith_'s accused stays a plain, unnamed figure: its grey everyman, kept as the Board drew him, is the plain lead (owner, 2026-10-15: "though this is what i mean by non descript"; character-design 2.3), not Max, and not the slate-blue man with dark hair of the first plain-lead concepts. (It had recommended Max, since rimproto C2 already wears his sage.)
8. **The family's schooling.** **Closed by the owner's rule (2026-10-15).** The education series' children are plain leads, and the cast's schooling is never shown. (It had recommended Nina at home and Leo at a day school, so both settings appear.)
9. **The ledger's turns.** **Closed by the owner's rule (2026-10-15).** The country-living finale's move belongs to its plain family ([`country/series.md`](../series/country/series.md)), and theology 09's baptism to plain leads; the cast's present does not change.
