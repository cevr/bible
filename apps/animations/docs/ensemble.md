# The ensemble: a recurring cast

Owner, 2026-10-13: "for the films in general, and to add to the skills that I think we should have an ensemble, a cast of characters, other than, you know, the main characters of each film, like a Jesus or something, that we kind of show in each film, so that there can be a sense of continuity."

Today each film invents its everyday figures. A viewer moving from _From Milk to Meat_ to the education talks, the health talks or the planned country-living series meets strangers every time. A small recurring cast lets them know faces, follow lives from film to film, and feel the doctrine through people they have come to care about. BibleProject does the same with its Everyman and its reused shots ([bibleproject-study.md](../../../.claude/skills/film/reference/bibleproject-study.md), V6).

This file is the cast bible. The film skill's **Ensemble** rule ([SKILL.md](../../../.claude/skills/film/SKILL.md)) points here. What the colours mean, how each role is designed, the minor figures and every character sheet are in [character-design.md](../../../.claude/skills/film/reference/character-design.md).

## What the cast is, and is not

- **Parable figures.** The cast are "a certain man" and "a woman" of a parable. Jesus taught that way: "without a parable spake he not unto them" (Matt 13:34). Each appearance pictures a truth the film's text states. Nothing in the cast's lives is a truth of its own.
- **Never real people, never history.** The narration never presents a cast member as someone who lived, never tells their life as news or testimony ("Last week Hugo…"), and never claims an event happened. A cast scene is introduced the way the drafts already do it: "Picture a grown man at a wedding feast…" (theology 01). Fiction is avoided; allegory is allowed, framed openly as allegory (the owner's rule, 2026-10-13, carried in each series' `series.md`).
- **Never Bible people.** Jesus, Moses, Daniel and the rest stay the main figures of their own films. No cast member plays a Bible person, and no Bible person joins the cast.
- **For every Christian.** No cast member carries a denominational marker: no church name or sign, no badge, no clothing, headcovering or habit that marks a group, no hymnal or book title in view. What the cast does is what the film's own text teaches, and nothing more ([series README](../series/README.md), "Voice and audience").
- **Christ's method on screen.** "The Saviour mingled with men as one who desired their good. He showed His sympathy for them, ministered to their needs, and won their confidence. Then He bade them, 'Follow Me'" (MH 143.3). Each cast member has a need a viewer shares, and the films let the viewer watch that person being met in it.
- **One narrator.** The cast never speaks, sings or lip-syncs. Faces and whole bodies act ([CRAFT.md](../../../.claude/skills/film/CRAFT.md) rule 5; [rimworld-study.md](../../../.claude/skills/film/reference/rimworld-study.md) §2 rule 7); the narrator voices any question a cast member's face asks.

## The household at the centre

A family in a city flat carries the education series and the emotional line of the theology series (theology `series.md`, "Parenting as pathos"). Its grandmother lives in the hills, which gives the country-living series its pull. Two neighbours in the same building carry the health series and the seeker's road.

The parenting pictures rest on texts that state the likeness, so the family illustrates, never invents (typology rule):

- "Like as a father pitieth his children, so the LORD pitieth them that fear him" (Ps 103:13).
- "Can a woman forget her sucking child … yet will I not forget thee" (Isa 49:15); "As one whom his mother comforteth, so will I comfort you" (Isa 66:13).
- "I taught Ephraim also to go, taking them by their arms" (Hos 11:3).
- "As a man chasteneth his son, so the LORD thy God chasteneth thee" (Deut 8:5); Heb 12:9–10, "for our profit, that we might be partakers of his holiness".
- "If ye then, being evil, know how to give good gifts unto your children, how much more…" (Matt 7:11).
- Luke 15:20, the father who "saw him, and had compassion, and ran".

A cast parent pictures a likeness the text names. The father never stands in for God the Father as a figure of God. God the Father is shown only as a hazy human form veiled in light (character-design 3.3).

## The cast

Eight people: a family of five, the grandmother, and two neighbours. Ages are their ages in every film (see Continuity).

The hue slots sit inside the band of [CRAFT.md](../../../.claude/skills/film/CRAFT.md) rule 12: every garment at HSV saturation 0.20–0.35, with scarlet the only accent, gold only for God's word and gift, and white only for the robe. The slots spread round the wheel about 40° apart and skip the reds and oranges from 330° to 50°: scarlet (357°), the chipboard world (about 25°) and gold (41°) live there. Each ramp shades toward the cool side under a warm key and lights toward the warm side, as rimproto C2 did; the papercut look's ramp step governs the final numbers. Heads and hands are the shared figure paper (open choice 2), and hair is cut chipboard as today, so a person is told apart by garment hue, silhouette and hair.

### Hugo, the father

- **Age and role:** 41. The father of the household; a builder by trade. He carries the father's side of "Parenting as pathos" and the cost of the country move.
- **Temperament:** warm, loud, quick to laugh and quick to flare; just as quick to own it. A believer who has stayed on milk longer than he should.
- **Habit:** in the wrong, one hand circle goes to the back of his head and he takes half a step back. Glad, he lifts a child high, the child rising beside him with his hand circles under them.
- **Silhouette:** the tallest (1.0) and broadest, square shoulders; short cropped hair and a short square beard, the only beard in the cast.
- **Slot 5, steel blue,** the darkest garment: a work jacket over trousers.
- **Arc:** from the grown man still holding the bottle (theology 01) to a father who corrects as the Father does. In education he learns never to correct in anger (14), governs by few, kept words (13) and teaches Leo his trade (18–19). In country living he sits down first and counts the cost (Luke 14:28), and builds the small house himself.

### Clara, the mother

- **Age and role:** 38. The mother and the home's first teacher. Rosa's daughter.
- **Temperament:** steady, observant, a planner with a quiet wit; under the steadiness, anxious for her children.
- **Habit:** she tilts her head and waits before she answers, "swift to hear, slow to speak" (Jas 1:19). Mila is often on her hip.
- **Silhouette:** middle height (0.92), slender; one long plait over the near shoulder, which reads from the front and the side.
- **Slot 4, teal:** a long dress or tunic to below the knee.
- **Arc:** from anxious control to trust. In theology she is the mother who cannot forget her child (Isa 49:15) and lifts the cup to the baby (01). In education she is taught first (5), then teaches at the table (10, 11) and gives a settled, kind answer (12, 13). In health her worry is met (47) and her kitchen shows a simple table (34).

### Leo, the son

- **Age and role:** 15. The eldest; the young person a parent is raising to go.
- **Temperament:** quiet, quick, dry under his breath, sceptical of anything he has not tested; wants his life to matter.
- **Habit:** he glances at the phone in his hand, then turns it face down. The arc is in that one motion: early films stop at the glance, later ones finish it.
- **Silhouette:** nearly his father's height (0.98) and the narrowest, with the longest legs; a tuft of hair standing up at the crown.
- **Slot 1, khaki:** a long, loose shirt.
- **Arc:** in education, screens and friends (23–25), grades against service (21, 22), a trade with his father (19), a calling (35) and the choice of a life partner (36), the last two shown as forward-looking IDEA pictures (Continuity). In theology he is the young person who chooses (Josh 24:15) and goes down into the water (09). In country living the phone comes along, and the country does not educate by itself.

### Nina, the daughter

- **Age and role:** 7. The middle child, strong-willed: the will to be won, not broken.
- **Temperament:** fearless, loud, curious, "why?" about everything; tender once won.
- **Habit:** she plants her feet, sets both hand circles on her hips and lifts her chin.
- **Silhouette:** small (0.62) and round; two bunches of hair sticking straight out, the widest head in the cast.
- **Slot 7, lavender,** light: a short smock over leggings.
- **Arc:** in education, when school should start (8), small and certain commands (13), the bedtime story instead of the scolding and why children lie (15), the strong will directed (16), and the knight of the giants allegory, framed as one (17). In theology she is the child at the slate who grows (01 `math`, its dissolves ending at her present age), and the will drawn out by goodness (07, Rom 2:4).

### Mila, the little one

- **Age and role:** about 18 months. The baby of the family: dependence, first steps, the milk.
- **Temperament:** sunny, clinging, all reach.
- **Habit:** both hand circles up, reaching for whoever comes in, with a little bounce.
- **Silhouette:** the smallest (0.40), round, one curl on top; on Clara's hip the pair reads as one silhouette with two heads.
- **Slot 3, mint,** the lightest garment, a near neighbour of her mother's teal.
- **Arc:** the baby's cup in theology 01; first steps with a parent's hands under her arms (Hos 11:3) in 11; the early years before books in education 7. She grows in no film; the ledger keeps her small.

### Rosa, the grandmother

- **Age and role:** 71. Clara's mother, a widow, living alone in a cottage with a garden in the hills. The elder: Scripture known by heart, faith tested by grief.
- **Temperament:** unhurried, merry, deep; hard to rattle and quick to notice.
- **Habit:** she presses something growing into another's hands: a seedling, a fruit, a cutting.
- **Silhouette:** short (0.82), a slight forward stoop; a shawl over the shoulders makes a triangle; outdoors, the widest brim in the cast, a garden hat. Silver hair in a bun, in a pale cool paper, never the robe's white.
- **Slot 8, plum,** dark: a long skirt, the shawl in a lighter neighbour of the slot.
- **Arc:** the grandmother whose faith "dwelt first" in her and then in her daughter (2 Tim 1:5; Deut 4:9), so she is the "teach them thy sons, and thy sons' sons" of education 5. Her garden is the second book (education 9; health 11, 14, 28). In theology she reads by the lamp (02), tends the tidy field beside an untidy one under the same sun (03), and grieves her husband with hope (12), a hope the resurrection and new-earth films answer (34, 37). In health she lives with a slow illness without giving up the means (49) and holds the eighth remedy, trust (44). In country living her cottage is where the family is drawn.

### Max, the neighbour across the landing

- **Age and role:** 29. The seeker. He lives alone across the landing and works long hours at a desk in the city. Outside the faith, honest about it.
- **Temperament:** wry, tired, curious, wary of church talk; he asks the plain question everyone else is too polite to ask.
- **Habit:** a takeaway cup in one hand circle; sceptical, one brow up and his head tipped back.
- **Silhouette:** middle height (0.95), round-shouldered with his head forward at first, straighter as his arc goes on; a hooded jacket with the hood down, a bump at the nape.
- **Slot 2, sage:** the hooded jacket.
- **Arc:** Christ's method across two series. The health talks meet his need first: stale air in a shut flat (6–8), irregular meals (17), late nights (21, 22), a chair all day (27, 30), borrowed energy (60), worry (47). The cup becomes a glass of water, then his hand is free. That wins his confidence, and the theology films make the call: he frowns at the open book and the expert's balloon covers it (02), meets the serpent's question as his own doubt (04), and walks Tier One's stones (07–09). Where he lands is a ledger turn (Continuity).

### Amara, the nurse downstairs

- **Age and role:** 46. A nurse on night shifts at the city hospital, living on the ground floor. A believer with deep, practical faith, who sees suffering every week.
- **Temperament:** brisk, warm, unflappable at work, worn out at home.
- **Habit:** she lays a hand circle on a shoulder, or on a forehead to feel for fever.
- **Silhouette:** middle height (0.90), sturdy; a round crown of hair larger than the head; a square tunic hem at the hip over trousers.
- **Slot 6, periwinkle:** the tunic.
- **Arc:** in health she is the caregiver as Christ's fellow worker (54): medicine is no lack of faith (50), the sick room's air (10), prayer for the sick without presumption (55). Her own need is met in rest (20, "Rest a while", and 25). In theology she carries the problem of suffering: where evil came from (04, 27) and the dead at rest (12). In country living she stays in the city by duty and serves where she is, so the counsel never reads as a verdict on those who remain.

## How the cast appears

### Where in a film

- **The cold open and the landing.** Theology's shared shape opens on "a Bible scene (STORY) or a plain human picture" and lands back in the cold open's layout. The plain human picture is a cast scene, and the landing returns to it, changed ([CRAFT.md](../../../.claude/skills/film/CRAFT.md) rules 3, 4 and 8).
- **IDEA beats.** The parchment's 1–4 flat figures are cast members wherever the beat needs an everyday person: the figure frowning at the book, the child at the slate, the farmer reading by the lamp.
- **The bridge.** A push-through from the cast's frame into the Bible scene (rule 3): Rosa's open book, and through its page into Berea.
- **Never inside a Bible scene.** No cast member stands in a STORY scene of Bible history: not in Capernaum's crowd, at the cross, at Sinai, in Babylon. No cast member plays a Bible person or a figure of Christ's own parables (the sower, the prodigal son and his father are drawn as that parable's figures). The cast may mirror a parable in its own frame after the parable is told: Hugo at the window as the narration closes on Luke 15:20.
- **Christ in the cast's frame.** Christ may come into the cast's world at the landing, where a stated text says He comes (Rev 3:20; 1 John 2:1), as the drafts already have Him: the host of the feast in 01, the Advocate beside the figure in 08. The cast never goes into His history. The Father appears only as a hazy, veiled human form, never in the cast's world (character-design 3.3).
- **Talks.** Education and health are talks: the owner on camera, cut with B-roll. Cast moments there are short cut-paper inserts drawn and rendered by the film engine, so the faces are the same as in the films (open choice 6). The owner speaks; the cast acts.
- **Shorts.** A cast member may carry a short's hook frame (CRAFT § Shorts rule 2): a face the viewer knows, moving on frame 0.

### How often

- **Theology:** every film shows at least one cast member, in the cold open, the landing or both. One cast member leads (the one whose need is the film's question); at most three are featured; the rest may appear in a crowd of the cast's own world. Their scenes take about a quarter of the film's time or less: the Bible's scenes carry the middle.
- **Education:** the family appears in most episodes: one or two inserts in a One Question short, two or three in an Episode, each 3–8 s.
- **Health:** Max, Amara and Rosa lead; the family appears where the subject is a home's (the table, the sick child, the walk after dinner). One to three inserts.
- **Country living:** the household's move is the spine, so the cast is in every episode.
- **No new everyday figure when a cast member fits.** A film invents an everyday person only when no cast member can stand there without breaking continuity (a crowd of strangers, a judge, a crowd at the city gate), and a crowd is never cloned (CRAFT rule 12).

### Continuity

- **Fixed ages.** The cast does not age between films; a parable has no calendar. A time-lapse (01's child growing at the slate) ends at the person's present age. A look forward (Leo at twenty choosing a calling or a wife) is an IDEA picture, framed as "picture him…", and changes nothing.
- **Homes.** The city building: the family's flat on the third floor, Max's across the landing, Amara's on the ground floor, a shared stair and a rooftop (08 already ends on a rooftop; the cast's roof is a recurring place; a film lands there only if it opened there, CRAFT rule 4). Rosa's cottage in the hills: a gate, the garden, a bench under a fig tree (Mic 4:4). These places are a shared shot library: a film reuses their layouts, so a viewer knows where they are at once.
- **Relationships.** Rosa is Clara's mother. Rosa's husband is asleep in Jesus: never cast, never shown alive in the cast's present, a paper portrait on her shelf; he appears only in the resurrection and new-earth films, the reunion the text promises (1 Thess 4:16–17). No romance, feud, accident or mystery between cast members: the cast has no plot of its own beyond what a film's truth needs.
- **What each knows.** Rosa knows Scripture best; Hugo and Clara are believers, still learning; Amara believes and asks the hard question; Leo tests; Nina asks why; Max knows no Bible words until a film gives them to him. A cast member never shows knowledge the film has not given its viewer.
- **No names in speech.** The names are for scripts, sheets and this file. The narration says "a father", "picture a grandmother" (open choice 1).
- **Look.** Minimal and expressive: circle hands of their own paper, no thumbs, no fingers, no arms; a face minimal but characterful, its kit the owner's pick (**OWNER QUESTION**: the four kits, each with one feature for each cast member, are in [face-study.md](../../../.claude/skills/film/reference/face-study.md)); shade with colour, never black or grey; outlines may be dark ([CRAFT.md](../../../.claude/skills/film/CRAFT.md) rule 12). Faces default warm, not cross (the film skill's Gotchas: raise the inner brows, give the glad a smile).

### Recognition, not backstory

Owner, 2026-10-13: "im wondering if there needs to be a backstory, what im going for is recognizable cast of characters that gives a sense of continuity and familiarity."

No backstory beyond what each person's entry above already holds. A viewer comes to know a cast member the way they know a neighbour: by seeing the same person in the same places doing the same small things, never by being told a history. A parable's figure needs no past (What the cast is, and is not). Familiarity is built from what shows on screen, film after film:

- **The same silhouette and one feature,** readable at quarter size (Before the first film, 2): Hugo's beard, Clara's plait, Nina's two bunches, Rosa's shawl triangle, Max's hood.
- **The same clothes in the same hue slot.** No outfit changes except what the scene needs (a coat outdoors, the baptismal water).
- **One signature prop and one habit,** repeated so it becomes theirs: Amara's hand on a shoulder or forehead, Rosa's open book. A habit shows in every film the person acts in.
- **The same places,** shot from the same layouts: the stair, the landing, the rooftop, Rosa's gate and bench.
- **The same relationships, shown by staging.** Who sits by whom, who opens the door to whom, who runs to whom.
- **One want each,** the need the films meet (Christ's method). It is the only inner life a viewer is given.
- **Small running gestures across films.** A detail that pays off for those who have seen earlier films and costs nothing to those who have not: Rosa's husband's portrait on her shelf, the bench under the fig tree, the rooftop at evening. Nothing in them ages or changes (Continuity, fixed ages).

Each person's entry is kept to these card facts. A fact that never reaches the screen is not added.

## Before the first film uses them

1. **Owner's picks first.** The cast is drawn in the paper-cut look after the owner's rimproto picks (outline, ink, colour variety, head size); the slot table assumes C2, more hues.
2. **A cast sheet.** Each person as a rig with front, side and back facings ([rimworld-study.md](../../../.claude/skills/film/reference/rimworld-study.md) §2 rule 10), set in a lineup at full size and at quarter size. Done when, at quarter size, each person is named correctly from the silhouette filled dark (`--mode value`) and again from the hues blurred (`--mode squint`), and no two read alike.
3. **The two homes as layouts:** the city building (stair, landing, flats, roof) and Rosa's cottage (gate, garden, bench), each a reusable set.

## Open choices for the owner

1. **Names in speech.** Recommended: never spoken. A parable's "a certain man" lets every viewer stand in his place, and the one narrator never has to introduce anyone. The alternative, names spoken in talks only, makes continuity easier to follow at the cost of the parable's openness.
2. **Heads and hands.** Recommended: the one shared figure paper for every cast member, as every film draws people now, so the cast reads as anyone in any country, and rimproto C2's two-tone tan face is avoided. The alternative is three or four warm skin papers inside the band, each with its own ramp.
3. **The size of the cast.** Recommended: eight. The alternative is six, dropping Amara (Max then carries health alone, and no one carries the caregiver) and Mila (Clara then carries a baby nobody knows).
4. **Grief.** Recommended: Rosa is a widow, so theology 12 grieves with hope and the resurrection films answer it, and no cast member dies. The alternative is Rosa herself falling asleep in 12 and waking in 34: the strongest pathos, but every film after 12 loses her, and films that stand alone would contradict one another.
5. **Christ in the cast's frame.** Recommended: yes, at the landing only, where a stated text says He comes, as 01 and 08 already draw Him. The alternative keeps the cast and Christ always apart.
6. **Cast inserts in the talks.** Recommended: every cast moment in an education or health talk is a cut-paper insert from the film engine, so the faces match; generated or filmed B-roll stays for places and nature. Bible scenes in the talks are generated illustrations in the sermon decks' painting style (owner, 2026-10-13), not cut-paper, so a talk never puts a Bible figure beside the cast.
7. **Recasting 08.** Recommended: when _Righteousness by Faith_ moves to the paper-cut look, its grey everyman becomes Max (rimproto C2 already wears his sage). The alternative leaves 08's unnamed figure as the one film without the cast.
8. **The family's schooling.** Recommended: Nina learns at home until about eight to ten, and Leo attends a day school in the city, so both settings appear and are judged by method, not building (education guide §12). The family's choice must not read as the series' verdict on any school.
9. **The ledger's two turns.** Recommended: Max and Leo go down into the water together in theology 09, and the household moves in the country-living finale (country 19, [`country/series.md`](../series/country/series.md)). Each turn is shown once, and later films assume it.
