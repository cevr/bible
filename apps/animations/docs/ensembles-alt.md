# Alternative ensembles

Owner, 2026-10-13: "try completely different ensembles with different character profiles."

Four whole casts to set beside the current eight in [ensemble.md](ensemble.md). Each has different people, a different household and different temperaments; none is the current family recoloured. This file is for choosing. It is not a cast bible. The ensemble the owner picks gets the full treatment in `ensemble.md`, and its sheets go in character-design §4.

**Superseded in part (owner, 2026-10-15):** "let's make the recurring cast only background characters, and the main characters non descriptive so they can be better related". Whichever cast is kept stands only in the background. The parts, arcs, turns and griefs each ensemble below gives itself in the series no longer apply: a film's main characters are plain leads (the film skill's character-design 2.3), and [ensemble.md](ensemble.md) says where a cast appears.

## What every ensemble keeps

These are the rules of [ensemble.md](ensemble.md) and the [series README](../series/README.md), unchanged for all four:

- **Parable figures.** "A certain man", never real people, never Bible people. No cast member appears in a STORY scene. Christ comes into the cast's frame only at the landing, where a stated text brings Him.
- **For every Christian.** No church name, badge, garment or book title in view. No insider terms. Diversity comes from who these people are, never from a quota.
- **Names never spoken.** All four keep the rule. Every role below can be said as "a grandfather", "a teacher" or "a boy who has been moved too often". Names are for scripts and sheets.
- **Names avoid the Bible's own.** No cast member has a name a KJV reader would take for a Bible person, so a cast scene is never mistaken for a STORY scene.
- **No cast death.** Grief comes through someone never cast: a portrait or a grave. Each ensemble names its own grief for theology 17.
- **Two turns on the ledger.** One faith turn is shown in theology 13: whoever goes down into the water wears their own plain clothes. One place turn is shown in country 19. Each ensemble names its own.
- **Fixed ages.** A look forward is an IDEA picture. Heights are given against the ensemble's tallest adult (1.0), with the head about ⅓ of an adult's height.
- **The look.**
  - K1 and its colour band: each garment at HSV saturation 0.20–0.35, and no slot between 330° and 50°.
  - The budget by role: main 0.28–0.35, supporting 0.22–0.30, minor 0.20–0.24 (character-design §2.2).
  - A garment carries identity only. It is never a type and never changes with an arc (§1.2, §1.5). Each colour note below gives its §1.3 reason: value, warm or cool, and how it groups.
  - Heads and hands use the one shared figure paper. Hair and headwear are cut chipboard and follow the Hair rules (character-design §2.1, branch castproto).

## A · The Orchard: three generations already on the land

**Premise.** A small orchard and market-garden farm two hours from the city, with three generations under one roof: the grandparents who planted it, their son and his city-born wife, their two children, and a burnt-out young man from the city hired for one harvest. Here the country series runs inside out. The family's question is not whether to leave the city, but whether to keep the land, and why.

- **Country living.** The question meets the text: a developer's offer for the lower field against "the land shall not be sold for ever: for the land is mine" (Lev 25:23, country 06), and the town spreading toward the farm (country 15). Lot's pull lives in the teenager, who wants the city (country 05; education 30). The city comes in through the mother and the hired hand.
- **Education.** It happens around the work: the grandfather's grafting knife is the trade (education 18–19), and the animals and seasons are the second book (education 9).
- **Health.** The farm does not keep the eight remedies by itself. The grandfather will not rest. The hand lives on energy drinks.
- **Doctrine.** It runs through a man who believes he must earn everything (theology 11, 25 and 27), and through a young man who has never opened the book (theology 02, 06, 11–13).

**Ledger turns:**

- Faith: Teo and Sami go down into the water together in theology 13.
- Place: country 19. The land is not sold. Sami builds a small house on the orchard's corner. Teo chooses for himself where he will live.

**Grief (theology 17).** Arturo and Luz's first child, born still fifty years ago, lies under the oldest pear tree: "I shall go to him, but he shall not return to me" (2 Sam 12:23). This needs the owner's yes, because it is the heaviest grief in any of the casts.

### Arturo, the grandfather

- **Age and role:** 73. Supporting; the main figure in _Dwell in the Land_.
- **Temperament, want:** gruff, few words, up before the light, gentle only with trees and animals. He wants the orchard to pass whole to his grandson.
- **Flaw or question:** he cannot rest. He believes a man earns every good thing, grace included.
- **Arc:**
  - Theology: he lays down dead works (11 and 25, Rom 4:5). In 27, "A Rest That Remains", he sits under the pear tree with empty hands.
  - Health: rest (20–25) and trust (44).
  - Education: he teaches Teo to graft (19), and lets Teo choose (33).
  - Country: in 06 he learns to hold the land as a steward. In 19 he hands Teo the knife.
- **Web:** Luz's husband of fifty years; Ivo's father, who still lives under his roof and his rule; Teo's adversary and hero; wary of Sami, then his teacher.
- **Silhouette and feature:** long-bodied, stooped forward from the shoulders; a flat cap.
- **Colour:** olive, 75° .30 .42, `#636b4b`. Dark and earthy, close to the orchard's ground in value, so he reads as part of the land.
- **Hair:** the cap hides the crown (rule 14). Two white tufts wedge out below it over the ears, lighter than the skin (rules 6, 13). A broad white moustache is one piece, and the mouth stays on skin.
- **Look line:** 0.98, long torso, short legs, stooped. Olive work coat to mid-thigh, chipboard trousers, walnut flat cap. Props: a grafting knife and a pruning ladder.

### Luz, the grandmother

- **Colour:** heather, 300° .26 .66, `#a87da8`. A light, cool-warm hue, the brightest elder in a warm kitchen.

### Ivo, the father

### The Orchard's places

1. **The farmhouse kitchen:** one long table for three generations, the window onto the orchard. The main bookend place.
2. **The oldest pear tree:** a bench, the small grave at its foot, and the grafting ladder against the trunk.
3. **The farm stand** at the edge of the market town. The town's new houses creep toward the lower field, and the developer's sign stands in it.
4. **The city:** a street of lit windows, as an IDEA, or Nadia's sister's flat. It is used only for Teo's weekend and Sami's past.

### Strengths and risks against the current cast

- **Better:**
  - Country living as a life with chores, debts and weather, not a dream.
  - Three generations under one roof is Deut 6:7 and 2 Tim 1:5 on screen.
  - The faith of works (Arturo) and the faith of a newcomer (Sami) are both sharp.
  - Staying or leaving is a genuinely open question for a teenager.
  - Lev 25:23 gives country 06 a living picture.
- **Loses:**
  - The series' core viewer, a parent in a city flat, sees a family already where the series points ("easy for them"). The call loses its stand-in.
  - The move, the current spine, becomes a quieter "kept, not sold".
  - No baby, so theology 01's cup and 15's first steps need a figure from outside the cast.
  - It can read as rural nostalgia, or as landowners' comfort.

## B · The Gallery: a single mother, the couple across the walkway, a building that becomes a family

**Premise.** The fourteenth floor of a city tower block, where the doors open onto an open-air walkway with the city below. A single mother who drives a city bus raises two children. Across the walkway live a retired couple: she has believed all her life; he is a lifelong, courteous doubter. The building's caretaker holds the place together.

The ensemble's spine is "A father of the fatherless … is God in his holy habitation. God setteth the solitary in families" (Ps 68:5–6). Christ's method happens literally here. The neighbours meet needs first: a child minded after school, a pot of soup, an inhaler run to the school gate. Words come later.

- **Country living.** It is the honest hard case. One income cannot simply move, so the counsel's comfort comes first: plant gardens where you are (country 16), pots on the walkway rail, and an allotment at the end of the mother's bus route (country 15). The finale is the old couple selling their flat so that two households can live under one roof on a town's edge (country 17, 19).
- **Education.** The mother is at work when school ends, and the retired teacher across the walkway becomes the after-school home.
- **Health.** Shift work, a child's asthma in a shut flat, the caretaker's diabetes, and an old man's daily walk.
- **Doctrine.** It comes through an old man asking Nicodemus' question, "How can a man be born when he is old?" (John 3:4).

**Ledger turns:**

- Faith: Walter goes down into the water in theology 13, with Milo watching. The boy's own choice is a forward IDEA picture.
- Place: country 19. The shared house.

**Grief (theology 17).** Rae's mother, who raised her in faith, is asleep. Her Bible with her handwriting in the margins sits on Rae's shelf, and she is never cast.

- **Hair:** a big round puff tied at the back of the head, breaking the circle (rule 9). The front piece is swept back from a clean hairline. The darkest value on the figure (rule 6).
- **Look line:** 0.95, upright, square shoulders. Navy driver's jacket with a stand-up collar over chipboard trousers, a lanyard. Props: keys, a thermos, and a child's school bag carried for him.

## C · The First Child: a young couple, the mentors who taught them, and the boy they foster

**Premise.** A young couple in a rented flat above the bakery where she works, in a market town, expecting their first child. Outside the town, the older couple who taught them years ago keep bees and an orchard on six acres. They are fostering a twelve-year-old boy who has been moved too often. ## Recommendation

**Ensemble B, The Gallery,** is the strongest whole alternative, and the only one that serves all four series about as well as the current cast while doing something it cannot.

1. **It puts the hardest viewer at the centre.** Country living's own source keeps "unnecessarily" and "many cannot", and the series must never read as a verdict on those who stay. A family that cannot simply move carries that comfort in its own life, and its finale shows a way through that does not need two incomes.
2. **Christ's method is the ensemble's daily life.** Needs are met across a walkway before anything is said: a child minded, a meal, a walk, an inhaler. That is MH 143.3 on screen, and the order every series already follows: health wins Walter and Rae's confidence before theology calls.
3. **The elder seeker is new and true.** Nicodemus' question in an old man's mouth (John 3:4) gives theology 13 more weight than a young seeker does. Ps 68:5–6 gives the parenting pathos a Father who stays, which reaches the many viewers without one.
4. **It keeps the parts the current cast does well:** a mother, two children of the right ages for the will and the screens, an elder of long faith, a health lead and a garden pull.

What it costs, plainly: no resident father, so the chastening and waiting-father pictures move to a grandfatherly Walter or stay with the text alone. The shared house must be seeded from the first country film (Elsie's spare room, the allotment) so that it reads as faith working, not a plot convenience.

If the owner keeps the current cast instead, C has the one idea most worth borrowing: an elder couple fostering a child, which gives adoption (Rom 8:15) and the won-not-broken will a face no secure child can.
