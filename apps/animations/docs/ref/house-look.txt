A 2D key frame from a narrated animated film, drawn in this art style: our own K1 paper-cut look made minimal, the house look, as the reference image shows it. Every figure and prop is a few flat cut-paper shapes. Every figure and prop has a thick warm dark-brown ink outline and a thin light inner edge just inside it, and is creased once by a single straight diagonal line at the light's angle, its far side a deeper hue of its own colour turned toward violet, never grey and never black. A head is ONE simple shape inside one single thick outline, and the light inner edge runs round that one outline. Garments are flat planes: no pockets, no buttons, no stitching, no seams, no cuffs, no fabric texture. Hands are plain round paper circles at the end of each arm in its sleeve, the sleeve running from the shoulder to the hand: never a hand floating free of the body, never a bare arm, no thumbs, no fingers. Faces are three marks only: two small dark ink oval eyes, short brows and a one-line mouth. One muted hue per piece. The light and the place: a warm, soft key light from the upper left that falls in soft, hazy beams; a real place behind the figures, built of simple flat paper planes with no ink outline, each plane further back paler and softer in a warm haze, so depth comes from haze and never from blur; a soft paper grain on every piece and every plane; soft violet cast shadows on the walls and the floor, never a darker brown; a muted, warm palette in which only the film's accents are loud. Keep it K1: no white sticker border round the figures, no felt or stitching style, no big glossy eyes, no realistic anatomy. The look is stylised, attractive, expressive and minimal.
