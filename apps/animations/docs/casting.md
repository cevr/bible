# Casting

Who has to be drawn for the films, and in what order. The rig artist works from this list. It covers two groups:

- **Part A:** the Bible figures that the scripts and series plans show or name.
- **Part B:** the plain leads and the supporting and background casts each series needs beyond the recurring eight.

Section 3 proposes three small cast prototypes to draw first. Section 4 lists every place a script disagrees with the character guide.

This file adds no design rules. Those live in two places:

- [character-design.md](../../../.claude/skills/film/reference/character-design.md), cited below as "the guide" with its section numbers;
- [ensemble.md](ensemble.md), which covers the recurring cast.

This file applies them figure by figure. Where the two disagree, the guide wins, and section 4 records the disagreement.

**Appearance terms.**

- **Shown:** on screen in a script.
  - "Film" means a cut-paper film scene.
  - "Talk" means B-roll in an education or health talk. The talk scripts call their Bible pictures generated "Illustration" B-roll. Ensemble open choice 6 makes only a talk's everyday moments (a plain figure in front, the cast behind) into cut-paper inserts. Whether talk Bible scenes need rigs at all is an owner question (section 4, item 11), so talk appearances are counted apart from film appearances.
- **Named:** spoken in narration, or written on a card, but not drawn.
- **Planned:** in a series plan with no script yet. Plans change, so a planned figure is drawn last.

**Priority tiers.** The most-used figure is drawn first.

| Tier | Rule                                                      |
| ---- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| P1   | shown in three or more films                              |
| P2   | shown in two films                                        |
| P3   | shown in one film                                         |
| P4   | shown only in talk B-roll (rig only if the owner says so) |
| P5   | planned, not yet scripted                                 |

Within a tier, more talk appearances and then more planned appearances rank higher.

**Roles** follow the guide's sections:

- §2.3 main;
- §2.4 supporting;
- §2.5 antagonist;
- §2.6 minor;
- §2.7 crowd;
- §2.8 allegorical.

**Heights** are measured against Hugo's 1.0. The guide's §4.3 heights are kept as they are. Every other height here is proposed, inside the ranges §4.3 already uses:

| Figure                                    | Height     |
| ----------------------------------------- | ---------- |
| Grown men                                 | 0.94–1.0   |
| Grown women                               | 0.88–0.92  |
| Youths                                    | 0.90–0.94  |
| A twelve-year-old                         | about 0.80 |
| A child of seven or eight                 | about 0.62 |
| Angels on earth ("a little taller", §3.5) | 1.04       |

**Faces and hair.**

- **Faces.** Every figure, Bible, plain lead or cast, takes Kit A, "three marks". The owner approved it for the cast on 2026-10-13. The face study says every face, of every people, takes the same kit and the same dignity.
- **Hair.** Hair is built the way the cast's newly approved hair is built. It is cut from walnut chipboard. Old hair is the pale cool paper, never robe white. Beards are allowed in Bible scenes (§3.1).

**Dress.** Bible figures dress in story cloth (§3.1). The base hexes:

| Cloth            | Base      |
| ---------------- | --------- |
| Linen (undyed)   | `#d1c6b4` |
| Walnut wool      | `#ab8163` |
| Indigo           | `#4b566b` |
| Olive            | `#768059` |
| Camel            | `#8c7762` |
| Royal purple     | `#62486b` |
| Priest blue      | `#5b708c` |
| Sanctuary purple | `#745380` |

**"Text silent."** Where Scripture does not describe a figure's look, the entry says "text silent" and gives the plain period default from §3.1. The default is a tunic, a mantle, a girdle, a headcloth outdoors and sandals, in two or three flat pieces.

**Scripture** is quoted from the KJV and checked against the local corpus with `bible verse`. The KJV's bracketed words, which the translators supplied, are printed without brackets.

**The scripts' "grey" figures.** Many scripts describe "grey" figures (grey Paul, grey Philip, grey travellers). That wording predates K1, which retires grey (§1.2). Read every "grey" as "in story cloth" for Bible figures and "in their slot" for the cast. For a plain lead it stays: the plain lead is the one figure K1 keeps in a pale cool grey paper with a lilac cast (§2.3; owner, 2026-10-15).

**Role:** main in T05; supporting or main by scene elsewhere. He never speaks on screen.

**Never**

- a glow in His earthly ministry outside the transfiguration;
- the white robe in earthly scenes (OQ5; owner, 2026-10-19: the seamless linen coat and a brown mantle on earth, the white robe and gold girdle only as Priest, Advocate and in glory);

## 2. Part B: the other casts

The recurring eight are in [ensemble.md](ensemble.md). The rules for everything else:

- **Plain leads in front.** The figure a parable scene is about (its lead, the one whose need is its question, the one who does its main action) is the plain lead (§2.3; owner, 2026-10-15: "though this is what i mean by non descript"), never a cast member: one figure, the same in every film. Where a scene needs a second plain figure, it differs by its paper or its height only (§2.3); the country series keeps one plain family and its plain grandmother through the series, told apart that way.
- **The cast behind.** Where a background needs a person a viewer might know (a neighbour on the stair, a face in the gallery), a cast member stands there (ensemble, How often), never featured (owner, 2026-10-15).
- **The minor set.** The guide's §5 set is used before any new figure: the neighbour, the teacher, the shopkeeper, the elder, the child friend, Leo's friend, the official and the stranger.
- **The crowd kit.** Six 0.20 ramps (79°, 120°, 188°, 226°, 263°, 299°) plus oat. Five bodies; five hats; never a hood; no two neighbours alike (§5).
- **Bible scenes draw their own** in story cloth. The story crowd kit (2.6) does for Bible crowds what §5's kit does for the cast's world.

"Shares the crowd kit" below means the row's figures are built from §5's crowd kit with no new parts.

## 4. Where the scripts conflict with the character guide

Each item gives where the conflict is and what the guide says. A recommendation is given where the guide or the ensemble already settles the conflict.

1. **Christ's earthly dress.**
   - Where: RBF (`roof`, `woman`, `centurion`) dresses His earthly ministry in a "white robe, gold sash".
   - The guide: §3.2 (OQ5) gives the earthly ministry the seamless undyed-linen coat (John 19:23), and keeps the white robe and golden girdle for Priest, Advocate and glory.
   - The owner chose the guide (2026-10-19). The theology drafts are restaged to the linen coat; RBF's scenes wait until RBF resumes (owner, 2026-10-19: RBF is paused).
2. **The seraphim's colour.**
   - Where: T05 draws them "flame-red and gold paper".
   - The guide: §3.5 makes them gold and cream; flame-red would put the scarlet accent on them.
3. **Wings on the angel host.**
   - Where: T05's Bethlehem host has "the same wings as the seraphim".
   - The guide: Luke 2:13 gives "a multitude of the heavenly host" and says nothing of wings or flight. §3.5 (OQ7) gives wings only where the text does.
4. **A portrait of Christ.**
   - Where: T05's `face` beat hangs "the portrait frame from 03" with Jesus' face in it.
   - The guide: §3.2 says never a framed portrait; restage it as His face in the story.
   - Related: T03 also floats a portrait frame over Christ's last panel, but it holds only light, which is fine.
5. **The parable's world in a STORY register.**
   - Where: T01 labels its cold open STORY, a village wedding with a grown man, and a mother and a baby at the cup: plain figures (§2.3), where the ensemble had cast Hugo, Clara and Mila until 2026-10-15.
   - The guide and ensemble: the cast is never STORY (§4.1), and no one from the parable's world stands in a Bible scene (ensemble, Never inside a Bible scene).
   - Recommendation: make the courtyard plainly the parable's own world, contemporary and not first-century; the cast may sit among the guests, in the background. Christ as host at the landing is allowed (ensemble open choice 5).
6. **The hood in T03's montage.**
   - Where: "a person in a city pulling their hood down".
   - The guide: the hood is Max's hook; §5 gives no one else a hood, and a plain figure wears none (§2.3). Max cannot play the shot: each figure in the montage is its shot's subject, so it is a plain figure.
   - Recommendation: a plain figure with no hood, who turns their face away and lowers their head. (Until 2026-10-15 the recommendation was to cast it as Max.)
7. **E52's named girl and period family.**
   - Where: the Abbott retelling names the girl "Mary" in speech and draws a 19th-century family with a chaise.
   - The ensemble: it put Clara in this episode (ep 12, "one mother, three ways") until 2026-10-15, when the owner moved the cast to the background; it never names the cast in speech.
   - Answered (owner, 2026-10-13): "lets go with period for education". A period illustration of the book's story, in the talks' generated-illustration style (item 11). The girl may be named in the retelling as the book names her; the cast stays out of it.
8. **RBF's judge.**
   - Where: a human judge with a head 1.7 times a person's sits behind the bench and gavels; at the landing "the judge's bench glows gold", and the narration makes the verdict God's.
   - The guide:
     - §5's official covers a parable judge.
     - §3.3 (owner, 2026-10-13) draws the Father only as a veiled, hazy human form with no features and no outline.
     - T13's plan makes Jesus the Judge.
