# Dwell in the Land: a series on country living

A playlist of narrated cut-paper films (apps/animations, the house style of _From Milk to Meat_) on country living: what it is, why God gives it, and where and how a family moves toward it. Drafted 2026-10-13. Nothing here is narrated, rendered or checked. The opening script is in `scripts/`; every citation, with its check against the local corpus, is in `sources.md`.

**Owner direction (2026-10-13):** "leaving the cities, I think is a very important topic that interleaves with education for many reasons. I think we can kind of even have a, maybe a whole series on country living in general, what, why, where, how, and that kind of have both doctrinal and educational content alongside with it. Adam's schoolroom, if we can use it, let's use it. Let's find the author. I think it's fun. But like I said, you know, we're not, we're not trying to overtly quote Adventist pioneers and whatnot. We want to reach a target, like a wide target audience and avoid prejudice."

**The frame (owner, binding):** country living is part of righteousness by faith. It is the life that faith works out, never merit and never a lifestyle add-on. Righteousness is God's gift, received by faith alone (Rom 4:3; Eph 2:8). Living faith then moves: "faith which worketh by love" (Gal 5:6), "for the obedience of faith" (Rom 16:26). Abraham is the series' proof. He was counted righteous by believing (Rom 4:3), and the same faith took him out of his country (Heb 11:8). "By works was faith made perfect" (Jas 2:22). The source behind this frame, paraphrased and never named, says that the same voice that told Abraham to leave his country told him to offer his son. Abraham was saved by faith in Christ as surely as any sinner today, and justifying faith always bears fruit (3SM 195.1–195.2). No episode says or pictures that a move earns anything. Every episode says, in its own way, that a move is what faith does when it believes God about its children.

## The name

**Recommended: _Dwell in the Land_** (Ps 37:3). The series line: "This is _Dwell in the Land_."

> "Trust in the LORD, and do good; so shalt thou dwell in the land, and verily thou shalt be fed." (Ps 37:3, KJV; the italic "so" is spoken)

- **It carries the frame in one verse.** Trust comes first, and the dwelling follows. The order is righteousness by faith: faith, then the life it works out, then God's care ("verily thou shalt be fed").
- **The source puts it at the centre.** The chapter on God's plan for Israel's land quotes this verse at its turning point (MH 189.2), straight after "The tilling of the soil, the employment that God appointed to man in Eden" (MH 189.1).
- **It runs to the end.** "The meek shall inherit the earth" (Ps 37:11) and "The righteous shall inherit the land, and dwell therein for ever" (37:29) are the series finale. The source reads Abraham's promise the same way: "not merely the land of Canaan, but the whole earth" (PP 169.4, which quotes Ps 37:11).
- **It is warm, not alarmed,** and it does not sound like a movement or a brand.

**Alternatives:**

- **_Under the Vine_** (Mic 4:4: "they shall sit every man under his vine and under his fig tree; and none shall make them afraid"). This is the most visual name. The grandmother's bench under a fig tree is already the cast's landing place (`apps/animations/docs/ensemble.md`, Homes). It answers fear with peace. Against it, it says nothing of faith, and "vine" can read as a vineyard or a wine channel.
- **_A Better Country_** (Heb 11:16: "now they desire a better country, that is, an heavenly"). It is honest about the goal: no earthly place is the point. Against it, it can sound like a series on heaven, and "country" can be heard as a nation.

## The arc, and why this order

The owner's four questions (what, why, where, how) become three arcs. The middle arc is the largest, because "why" carries the doctrine.

| Arc | Name                  | Episodes | The viewer's question                                     |
| --- | --------------------- | -------- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| I   | **What**: the pattern | 01–03    | What did God make a home to be?                           |
| II  | **Why**: the doctrine | 04–12    | Why does it matter where a family of faith lives?         |
|     | · the story           | 04–07    | Abraham, Lot, Israel's land, the carpenter from the hills |
|     | · the reasons         | 08–11    | Health, the children, the Sabbath, freedom of conscience  |
|     | · the hour            | 12       | Is there a time coming when it will matter more?          |
| III | **Where and how**     | 13–19    | When, how fast, where, with what work, and with whom?     |

19 films. At 4–5 minutes each, about 1½ hours in all.

Why this order:

- **Christ's method sets the order.** The README's rule (MH 143.3, verified) is to meet the need first, then win confidence, and only then call. So the series opens on beauty and longing: a garden, a home, a school with no walls (arc I). It moves to stories every Christian already loves (Abraham, Lot, Nazareth). Only then does it give the reasons a family would weigh today. The end-time layer (12) comes after eleven films of confidence, never first. The source counsels the same order for any hard truth: "Do not at the outset press before the people the most objectionable features of our faith" (Ev 141.2, verified; the theology series uses it the same way).
- **Doctrine rests on plain texts, with types laid over them.** Each "why" rests on narrative and didactic Scripture (Gen 2; 12–19; Lev 25; Luke 2; Heb 11; Luke 21). No doctrine rests on a figure (the typology rule, memory `feedback_typology_vs_spiritualizing`). Rev 18:4 completes the "come out" pattern (Gen 12:1; 19:14; Luke 21:21); it is not the ground of the move (episode 12).
- **The calm comes after the urgency.** Episode 12 is the most urgent film, and 13 ("Count the Cost") follows it at once. Its counsel is "not in a rush, in an excitement, or in a rash manner" (2SM 361.1), so no viewer leaves the series in panic.
- **The education series interleaves throughout.** Eden's school (01), the children (09) and keeping the education whole (18) are the education series' own subjects, told here from the angle of place. The shared episodes are listed below.
- **The household's move is the spine.** The recurring cast lives in a city flat; the grandmother's cottage is in the hills (ensemble). Each film moves the family one honest step. They long (arc I), weigh and decide (arc II), count the cost and plan (13–17), and move in the finale (19), which is the ensemble ledger's named turn. Amara stays in the city by duty and serves where she is, and Max stays too, so the series never reads as a verdict on those who remain.

## Audience

The audience is every Christian, of any church or none (the series README, "Voice and audience"). It is especially for the parent in a city flat who senses the noise is costing the children something, and does not know if anything can be done. No episode assumes the viewer has heard of "country living" as a teaching.

House rules for every episode (binding):

- **Speech quotes only Scripture,** in KJV, word for word, from the `bible` CLI. Ellen White and the pioneers are paraphrased without being named, and the credits name them. A plain idea needs no name on it.
- **No denominational vocabulary:** no "Country Living" as a book title, no "outpost centers", "institutions", "Battle Creek", "trades unions" or "the Sunday law" as a phrase in speech. Episode 12 says what Scripture says (Rev 13:16–17; 14:12) in plain words.
- **No fear-mongering and no date-setting.** Safety is a side effect, never the lead. Never a statistic without a primary source. Never "flee now".
- **No verdict on city families.** Keep "unnecessarily" (CL 9.4 = 22LtMs, Ms 115, 1907, par. 6) and "many cannot have their surroundings what they would" (PP 169.1). Every episode that calls also comforts: duty may place a family in the city, and God keeps the watchful there.
- **Quotations stay short:** one per beat, and 20% of the words or fewer. One narrator.
- **Fiction:** none, except allegory framed openly as allegory (memory `feedback_content_series_rules`). The cast are parable figures ("Picture a father…"), never presented as real people.
- **"The Almighty"** is not used as a title of Jesus. The series has no occasion for it.

## How each episode is built

The shape is the same as _From Milk to Meat_'s:

- **Cold open on the household** in the city flat, or at the grandmother's gate, ending on the viewer's question.
- **The Bible scene** that answers it (STORY), with one small dose of the honest objection, answered within seconds.
- **The deepest turn** at 55–65%: where the episode touches faith (trust, not merit).
- **The doctrine and the lesson side by side.** Each episode states one doctrinal truth from plain Scripture, and one educational truth: what it means for how children learn. The two columns below are the plan for that.
- **The landing** back in the cold open's layout, changed by one step. Then the thesis line of Scripture, about 30 s of music alone, and the credits.

**Series motifs:**

- **The seedling.** The grandmother presses something growing into another's hands (her habit). In 01 a seedling goes into a pot on the city windowsill. It grows through the series and is planted in the ground in 19.
- **The fig-tree bench** at the grandmother's cottage (Mic 4:4). It is the landing place of the series.
- **The window.** The flat's window shows walls and lit windows. Over the series a far line of hills appears in it, and in 19 the window is the cottage's.
- **The tent and the altar** (Gen 12:8; 13:18). Wherever the family is, the altar comes first (5T 320.3: "erect an altar to the Lord wherever you pitch your tent").

**Format:** films of 4–5 min (450–600 spoken words), like the theology films. Two of them (01 and 12) may need 5½.

| Doctrine                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          | Education                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Creation by design. Work was given before sin, as a blessing (Gen 2:15). Adam was "the son of God" (Luke 3:38). The garden's first lesson was trust: two trees, one word from God (Gen 2:16–17). The tree of life, lost, is restored in Christ's city (Rev 22:2). | The first school had a garden for a room, nature for a book, work for a lesson and God for the teacher (Ed 20.1). - **Support:** PP 81.1 (Cain, "gone out from the presence of the Lord", founded a city); PP 118.5 (Babel's city and tower "designed to prevent the people from scattering abroad"); Ed 33.3 (those who departed from God built cities; the faithful "dwelt among the fields and hills").

| Doctrine                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    | Education                                                                                                                                                                           |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| God is not against roofs and streets. He is building a city Himself (Heb 11:10; Rev 21:2). | Jesus gave His disciples a sign and told them to act on it (Luke 21:20–21). Those who believed lived (GC 30.2). The same Lord foretells a last test of worship, enforced by the market: "that no man might buy or sell" (Rev 13:16–17). God's people "keep the commandments of God, and the faith of Jesus" (Rev 14:12). Prudence foresees and hides (Prov 22:3). Faith is "moved with fear" and builds early (Heb 11:7). No one knows the day (Matt 24:36), and no one need be in the dark (1 Thess 5:4). | Noah's sons helped build the ark. A family that prepares together is ready together (education no. 94). Teach children why, not what to fear: the motive now is their souls (2SM 360.3, "especially families with children"). |

- **Cast:** the household at dinner, Leo asking what the news means; the landing on Rosa's bench, "none shall make them afraid" (Mic 4:4).
- **How to tell it (Christ's method):** open on Jesus' own warning and the Christians who lived because they listened. Give the reasons the texts give, and no more. Name no nation, church, law or year in speech. Present Rev 13's test as Scripture states it: a rival worship enforced by buying and selling, against the commandments of God. Theology films 30–33 (Come Out of Her; The Seal of the Living God; The Latter Rain; When the Door Is Shut) carry the full exposition; link them.
- **Rev 18:4** closes the episode as the last of the Bible's "come out" calls (Gen 12:1; 19:14; Luke 21:21). Its own subject is spiritual Babylon, false worship. It is not the proof that a family should buy land (guide §13, Beyond the source). The doctrine of the move rests on Gen 12–19, Luke 21 and the counsel. Rev 18:4 shows where the pattern ends.
- **Shares with education:** catalogue no. 94 (Noah's sons) and series-list ep. 37 (standing alone as a family).
- **Caution:** see `sources.md` §4 (overreach). Two errors are equally wrong. One is "flee now" panic. The other is "wait for the signal before doing anything", which 2SM 359.5 (1897, "making haste to get all things ready"), 2SM 360.1 (1903, "The time has come") and 5T 464.3 itself ("instead of seeking expensive dwellings here") rule out. No date. No "too late" timer: "those who wish to leave them will not be able" (2SM 142.4) is a forecast spoken about the church's city institutions in 1903, not a countdown. The owner rated this layer very important, so the episode is plain and warm, and it lands on peace.

### Arc III · Where and how: practical counsel

**13 · Count the Cost** (when, and how fast)

- **Question:** Should we move now, and how fast?
- **Core KJV:** Luke 14:28–30; Prov 19:2; Prov 21:5; Jas 1:5; Prov 3:6; Gen 19:16.
- **Support:** 2SM 360.1 ("as God opens the way"); 2SM 361.1–362.3 ("let it not be in a rush, in an excitement, or in a rash manner"; "Do nothing without seeking wisdom of God"; the builder who could not finish; "well-defined plans"); 2SM 363.1 (no "great loss or sacrifice made upon property" by "ardent, impulsive speeches"; Lt 45, 1893, par. 21); 2SM 364.2 ("Spread every plan before God with fasting").

| Doctrine                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           | Education                                                                                                                                                                         |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Faith is not haste: "he that hasteth with his feet sinneth" (Prov 19:2). Faith is not lingering either (Gen 19:16). God gives wisdom to anyone who asks (Jas 1:5) and directs the path of the one who acknowledges Him (Prov 3:6). The urgency is in the direction, and the care is in the method. | Plan as a family. The children's ages, the school, the work and the church are all weighed together (guide §13). Children learn how decisions are made by watching this one made. |

- **Cast:** Hugo sits down first with paper and pencil (Luke 14:28; his ensemble arc). Clara tilts her head and waits.
- **Shares with education:** series-list ep. 32 (catalogue nos. 118, 99).
- **Caution:** the 1893 letter (2SM 361–364) was written to Battle Creek. Use its principle in speech, never the place.

**14 · Prepare Thy Work Without** (work and livelihood)

- **Question:** How will we earn a living out there?
- **Core KJV:** Prov 24:27; 1 Thess 4:11–12; Eph 4:28; Prov 27:23; Eccl 5:9; Gen 3:19.
- **Support:** 2SM 362.2 (do not "enter into some business they know nothing about. This God does not require"); MH 185.3 (a trade for every son); 6T 178.2 ("If the land is cultivated, it will, with the blessing of God, supply our necessities"); FE 326.2 ("Fathers and mothers who possess a piece of land and a comfortable home are kings and queens"); MH 189.1–189.4; MH 192.2 (help families learn to make the land yield a livelihood).

| Doctrine                                                                                                                                                                                                                        | Education                                                                                                                                                                  |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| "Prepare thy work without, and make it fit for thyself in the field; and afterwards build thine house" (Prov 24:27). Work is God's gift since Eden (Gen 2:15), and honest labour keeps a believer free and generous (Eph 4:28). | Teach every child a trade (MH 185.3). Hugo's trade travels with him, and Leo learns it (education eps. 18–19). A family business the children share is a school in itself. |

- **Cast:** Hugo and Leo at a workbench; Rosa's eggs and vegetables.
- **Caution:** no promises of income, and no land-price claims. "Prepare … afterwards build" is the order. The garden is a school before it is a livelihood.

**15 · Near the Town, Not in It** (where)

- **Question:** How far out is far enough?
- **Core KJV:** Gen 13:18; Gen 23:10; Matt 5:14; Luke 4:16; Mark 1:35, 38.
- **Support:** Ev 78.1 (Lot "could have done" all his good work from "some distance away from the city"); 14LtMs, Ms 85, 1899, par. 21 ("As did Enoch, we must work in the cities but not dwell in them"); 2SM 360.1 ("as suitable a place as their means will allow … there should be land in connection with it"); 2SM 359.3 (where the Sabbath is easy to keep); 8T 244.3 (scattered "in the towns, cities, and villages, as lights"); 6T 198.1 (don't move just to be near a large school; serve where help is needed); MM 308.6 (places "just out from the large cities"). Research only, never a rule: the source's own homes were "four miles from the city, close under the shadows of the mountains" (2LtMs, Lt 19h, 1874, par. 1) and "two miles from the city" (7LtMs, Lt 98, 1892, par. 8).

| Doctrine                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               | Education                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Abraham lived at Mamre, by Hebron, and did his business "at the gate of his city" (Gen 13:18; 23:10). Jesus withdrew to pray and came back to "the next towns" (Mark 1:35, 38). Distance from the city is for living, not for hiding. A light is set where it can be seen (Matt 5:14). | Choose a place where the children can have land, a church, a school and friends of faith, and still serve the town. The question is "what will surround them?" (PP 169.1), not "how far?" |

- **Cast:** the family drives the road between the city and Rosa's hills, with a map; Max waves them off.
- **Caution:** no mileage rule (none exists in the sources). "Just out from the large cities" (MM 308.6) is said of institutions; use it only as a picture of the balance.

**16 · Plant Gardens Where You Are** (small steps for those who cannot move yet)

- **Question:** We can't move. Is there anything we can do?
- **Core KJV:** Jer 29:5, 7; Dan 6:10; Phil 4:11; Mark 6:31; Ezra 1:3.

| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |

Contentment and readiness are both faith (Phil 4:11). When the door opened, the question was who would go (Ezra 1:3). | Small steps now: a window box, a balcony of pots, a Sabbath in the park or the hills, screens off, a skill learned, money saved toward land. A day trip is a lesson (1T 514.3). Each step teaches the children where the family is heading. |

- **Cast:** Amara, who stays by duty, with a balcony garden; Nina's seedling now a small plant; the household's first weekend at Rosa's plot.

- **Caution:** the episode is comfort and readiness, not guilt. If the Babylon-exile frame borrows Haskell's _Story of Daniel the Prophet_ (SDP 163.2), credit Haskell, not Ellen White (see `education/sources.md` §6).
