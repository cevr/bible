## 2. Standing rules for every video

**Standing line.** Shown on screen for 0–3 s (small, inside the bottom safe area) and as the
first line of every description:

> _Principles from Scripture, not medical advice. Don't stop or change any treatment without your doctor._

In Medicine videos about treatment, it is also spoken once (see pilot 50, beat 8).

**Message rules** (from the films' audience rule, carried over by talk-shorts OWNER 1):

- The series is for every Christian. Scripture is quoted from the KJV, verbatim. The
  pioneers and Ellen White are paraphrased in the owner's own words, never quoted or named
  aloud, and credited in the description ("Further reading").
- No denominational history: no sanitarium stories, no "health message" history, no
  institution names. The "right arm of the gospel" passages are framed by the church's
  mission and institutions, so on camera use only their plain idea: Christ healed and
  taught together.
- Health claims stay with what the sources teach and with plain, well-established
  physiology. Each research note has a physiology section that says where the
  sources are of their time.
- Never individual advice. Never tell anyone to stop a treatment. The sources
  themselves say refusing every remedy is an error (2SG 135.1; 2SM 286.2; MH 231.3).
  Lean on that.
- The sources' own guardrails go into every diet video: no extremes "of indulgence
  and of restriction" (MH 319.1), diet is "not a test of fellowship" (9T 159.3),
  reform is "progressive" (MH 320.2), and no one sets the rule for another (MH 310.3; CD 491.3).

**Never on camera** (period language or claims the evidence won't carry):

- "Night air" fears, "miasma", air that "electrifies", "vital/magnetic" air, skin that "breathes".
- "Drugs never cure" as a general claim. Say instead what drugs the sources meant (Medicine 51).
- "Let medicine alone" (CH 55.3).
- "An hour before midnight is worth two after." Say "early and regular".
- "Nine tenths of disease" or "90%" figures. "Die of imaginary disease."
- "Tea is intoxicating" or "coffee is poison". Say "a stimulant you may not need".
- "Cancerous germs" in meat. Milk and egg warnings tied to their era's animal disease.
- Food-combining rules, "five hours between meals", "two meals for everyone".
- Col 2:21 ("touch not; taste not") as a command: Paul is rejecting those rules there.
- Gal 5:20 "witchcraft" (pharmakeia) as a verdict on medicine. Its sense is sorcery (Medicine 58).
- Isa 40:31 or 1 Tim 4:8 as exercise proof texts. 3 John 1:2 as a promise (it is a greeting; use it as an epigraph).
- 2 Kings 5 (Naaman) or John 9 (Siloam) as proof that "water heals". Both are faith and obedience.

## 3. The arc

1. **Frame (1–5).** Why God cares about the body, and the eight remedies as His. The
   opener's payoff ("The tree in the water didn't heal Marah. The LORD did. He just used a
   tree.") sets up the whole series: God heals, and He works through means.
2. **The eight, in the source's own order (6–49):** pure air, sunlight, abstemiousness,
   rest, exercise, proper diet, the use of water, trust in divine power. Each strand has
   the law itself, the "why" in Scripture and the sources, the practice, and the common
   questions. Trust comes last, as on the list, because it holds the other seven.
3. **Medicine (50–58).** Faith and means, what "drugs" meant, the physician's calling,
   praying for the sick, simple remedies, myths.
4. **Recurring themes (59–72):** temperance (appetite and stimulants), the mind and the
   body, dress.

**Release order (a proposal; cadence is the owner's).** Rotate strands so no two
neighbouring posts share a law, and alternate Q and E:
01 (E) → 06 (Q) → 50 (Q) → 20 (Q) → 33 (E) → 11 (Q) → 46 (Q) → 27 (Q) → 39 (E) → 37 (Q) → 15 (Q) → 44 (E).
That covers every law plus Medicine in the first twelve.

### Medicine (50–58)

**M0, for the owner.** Your message stopped at "whats very interesting is medicine…".
From what the sources contain, you most likely meant one of these. Each has a video,
and say which one you meant so the strand can lead with it:

1. "Drugs" in the sources meant named poisons of the day: mercury/calomel,
   strychnine, arsenic, opium and morphine in patent medicines. A medical student
   asked exactly this in 1893 and got exactly that answer (2SM 278.3–279.1). The
   warnings are sharper and narrower than people think. → **51**
2. The Greek _pharmakeia_ ("witchcraft", "sorceries") has the root "drug", and
   whether that means anything (it means sorcery; the line Scripture draws is
   occult healing, 2 Kings 1:3). → **58**
3. "Disease is an effort of nature to free the system" (MH 127.1) turns medicine
   around: symptoms as the body healing, and treatment as helping it rather than
   suppressing it. → **52, 57**
4. Scripture shows remedies alongside prayer (Hezekiah's figs, Christ's clay, Luke
   the physician, the Samaritan's oil and wine), so faith and medicine are not
   opposites. → **50, 53, 55**
5. Medicine caught up: physicians dropped calomel and bleeding, and by 1909 doctors
   were citing medical journals against the drugs of the day, while prevention-first
   care is now mainstream. It's told as medical history, never as church history. → **51, 54**
