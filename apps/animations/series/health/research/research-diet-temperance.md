# Research notes: PROPER DIET, ABSTEMIOUSNESS, and the TEMPERANCE theme

Strand of the eight-laws series (MH 127.2: "Pure air, sunlight, abstemiousness, rest, exercise, proper diet, the use of water,
trust in divine power—these are the true remedies."). Every refcode and quoted phrase below was copied from `q.ts` / API
output in this session; every KJV text from `bun src/main.ts verse`. On camera: quote KJV only, paraphrase the rest.

Note on refcodes: MH, CD, CH, Te, HL and CTBH overlap heavily (CD/HL/Te are compilations that reprint MH, CTBH, 2T-9T).
Where a passage exists in MH or a Testimony volume, that original is cited first and the compilation given as "= CD x".

## A. PROPER DIET

### A1. Core passages (EGW)

- `MH 295.3` * - "In order to know what are the best foods, we must study God's original plan for man's diet." - Start from
  Eden: Gen 1:29, then "the herb of the field" (Gen 3:18) after the fall. (= CD 81.1)
- `PHJ July 1, 1905, par. 5` (= MH 296.1, CD 81.2) * - "Grains, fruits, nuts, and vegetables constitute the diet chosen for
  us by our Creator. These foods, prepared in as simple and natural a manner as possible, are the most healthful" - The
  positive core: what to eat, and simply prepared.
- `MH 311.2` (= CD 374.2) - "In choosing man's food in Eden, the Lord showed what was the best diet; in the choice made for
  Israel He taught the same lesson." - Manna in the wilderness repeats Eden's lesson.
- `PP 378.1` - "a restriction was placed upon them for their good" ... "that they might enjoy the food originally provided
  for man" - Why Israel got manna, not flesh.
- `3SM 289.1` - "The flesh of dead animals was not the original food for man. Man was permitted to eat it after the Flood
  because all vegetation had been destroyed." - Flesh as a post-Flood permission, not the design.
- `MH 313.1` (= CD 313.2) - "Those who eat flesh are but eating grains and vegetables at second hand" - Why go "second
  hand" when you can eat what the animal ate.
- `MH 313.2` - "Flesh was never the best food; but its use is now doubly objectionable, since disease in animals is so
  rapidly increasing." - The health argument (see physiology note: historically situated in part).
- `MH 315.2` - "Think of the cruelty to animals that meat eating involves, and its effect on those who inflict and those
  who behold it." - The moral argument: tenderness toward God's creatures.
- `9T 159.2` (= CD 404.2) * - "We do not mark out any precise line to be followed in diet; but we do say that in countries
  where there are fruits, grains, and nuts in abundance, flesh food is not the right food" - Ideal held with a stated
  limit (abundance), no "precise line".
- `PP 562.1` - "The distinction between articles of food as clean and unclean was not a merely ceremonial and arbitrary
  regulation, but was based upon sanitary principles." - Lev 11 as health law, not ritual.
- `MH 313.3` - "The tissues of the swine swarm with parasites." - Swine (Deut 14:8) singled out; the case for Lev 11:7-8.
- `MH 300.1` - "The meals should be varied. The same dishes, prepared in the same way, should not appear on the table meal
  after meal" - Simplicity is not monotony; variety across meals.
- `MH 302.2` - "Scanty, ill-cooked food depraves the blood by weakening the blood-making organs." - Good cooking is part
  of the law; poor food is no virtue.
- `CH 450.1` (= CD 380.1) - "God is trying to lead us back, step by step, to His original design—that man should subsist
  upon the natural products of the earth." - Direction of travel, "step by step".

### A2. Pioneer passages

- `HBH 180.4` (J. N. Loughborough, Hand Book of Health, 1868) - Reasons "from the law of adaptation" that man was made for
  "a diet of fruits, grains, and vegetables"; cites Gen 1:29 and Israel's clamor for "the flesh-pots of Egypt".
- `BHY 169.1` (James White, Bible Hygiene, 1890) - Calls Gen 1:29 "the first grand hygienic rule" in the Bible.
- `ARSH July 24, 1900, page 467.4` (E. J. Waggoner) - KJV "meat" in Gen 1:29 is the old word for food of any kind; the
  Bible says "flesh" when it means animal flesh. Useful for reading the KJV aloud.
- `BHY 215.4` (James White, 1890) - The people put away swine's flesh and largely flesh generally "from a conviction that
  flesh is not the most nutritious or the most healthful food"; flesh stimulates rather than builds up.
- `ARSH March 1, 1898, page 140.3` (A. T. Jones) - Daniel's "pulse" stands for the whole plant diet, set against "the
  king's dainties".

### A3. KJV texts

- **Gen 1:29** (full) - "And God said, Behold, I have given you every herb bearing seed, which [is] upon the face of all
  the earth, and every tree, in the which [is] the fruit of a tree yielding seed; to you it shall be for meat."
- **Gen 3:18** - "Thorns also and thistles shall it bring forth to thee; and thou shalt eat the herb of the field;"
- **Gen 9:3-4** - "Every moving thing that liveth shall be meat for you; even as the green herb have I given you all
  things. But flesh with the life thereof, [which is] the blood thereof, shall ye not eat." (The post-Flood permission,
  with a limit at once. Cf. MH 312.1, blood drained.)
- **Lev 11:2-3, 7-8** - "These [are] the beasts which ye shall eat ... Whatsoever parteth the hoof, and is clovenfooted,
  [and] cheweth the cud ... And the swine ... he [is] unclean to you. Of their flesh shall ye not eat"
- **Num 11:4-6** - "the mixt multitude ... fell a lusting: and the children of Israel also wept again, and said, Who shall
  give us flesh to eat? ... But now our soul [is] dried away: [there is] nothing at all, beside this manna"
- **Ps 106:14-15** (full) - "But lusted exceedingly in the wilderness, and tempted God in the desert. And he gave them
  their request; but sent leanness into their soul." (Num 11:33-34 for the plague at Kibrothhattaavah: use carefully, it
  is about lusting against God, not a threat over a menu.)
- **Dan 1:8, 12, 15, 17** (full v.12) - "Prove thy servants, I beseech thee, ten days; and let them give us pulse to eat,
  and water to drink." v.8 "Daniel purposed in his heart that he would not defile himself with the portion of the king's
  meat, nor with the wine which he drank" v.15 "their countenances appeared fairer and fatter in flesh" v.17 "God gave
  them knowledge and skill in all learning and wisdom"
- **Isa 55:2** - "Wherefore do ye spend money for [that which is] not bread? ... eat ye [that which is] good, and let your
  soul delight itself in fatness." (Primarily spiritual invitation; use as an echo, not a proof text.)
- **1 Cor 10:31** - "Whether therefore ye eat, or drink, or whatsoever ye do, do all to the glory of God." (Used by the
  sources for diet: 9T 159.3, CH 443.1.)

## B. ABSTEMIOUSNESS (moderation, self-denial, regularity, not overeating)

### B1. Core passages (EGW)

- `MH 308.2` * - "Abstemiousness in diet is rewarded with mental and moral vigor; it also aids in the control of the
  passions." - The law defined by its fruit: clear mind, mastered passions.
- `Ed 205.2` - "overeating, even of the most wholesome food, is to be guarded against. Nature can use no more than is
  required" - Quantity matters, not only kind.
- `CTBH 51.1` (= CH 119.1, CD 131.4) - "It is possible to eat immoderately, even of wholesome food." - Discarding bad foods
  is not a license to eat without limit.
- `CH 160.1` - "Many professed health reformers are nothing less than gluttons." - Blunt: plant-based overeating is still
  intemperance.
- `MH 299.6` - "There should not be a great variety at any one meal, for this encourages overeating and causes
  indigestion." - Simplicity at a meal; variety across meals (MH 300.1).
- `MH 310.1` - "At each meal take only two or three kinds of simple food, and eat no more than is required to satisfy
  hunger." - Offered as a "suggestion" to try, for sedentary/mental workers.
- `MH 303.2` * - "Regularity in eating is of vital importance. There should be a specified time for each meal." - Then
  "take nothing more until the next meal"; nibbling comes from want of will.
- `CTBH 50.1` (= CH 118.2) - "Regularity in eating is very important for health of body and serenity of mind. Never should
  a morsel of food pass the lips between meals." - Eating between meals.
- `CD 173.1` - "Give this misused and much-abused organ some peace and quiet and rest." - The stomach's rest; "two meals
  better than three" offered as a trial (also HL 83.6).
- `MH 321.1` (= CD 176.2) - "The practice of eating but two meals a day is generally found a benefit to health; yet under
  some circumstances persons may require a third meal." - Two meals, not as a rule for all (cf. CTBH 58.2).
- `MH 235.2` - "In many cases of sickness, the very best remedy is for the patient to fast for a meal or two" - Abstinence
  as a remedy: relieve the overworked organs.
- `MH 310.3` * - "One person cannot lay down an exact rule for another. Everyone should exercise reason and self-control,
  and should act from principle." - Abstemiousness is self-government, not a rulebook.

### B2. Pioneer passages

- `HBH 195.4` (Loughborough, 1868) - Whether two or three meals, keep them well apart; "scrupulously" avoid "the least
  particle of aliment" except at regular meals.
- `HHTL 57.1` (EGW's chapter in James White, ed., Health, or How to Live, 1865) - Fashion and "morbid appetite" crowd rich
  cake, pies and puddings into the stomach; "the table must be loaded down with a variety". (Same text as CD 158.1.)
- `HHTL 7.3` (James White, 1865) - On 2 Pet 1:6: "The intemperate man cannot be a patient Christian"; intemperance in
  eating, "either in quantity, or in the rich quality", sours the temper.
- `ARSH June 13, 1865, page 11.16` (Review article) - "there are excesses from overeating this side of gluttony" - you
  need not be a glutton to overeat.
- `GCB July 1, 1900, page 184.1` (GC Bulletin talk) - Luke 21:34: "surfeiting, which is overeating"; appetite controlled
  "both as to the quantity and quality" and by the work one does.
- `ARSH October 27, 1896, page 679.4` (E. J. Waggoner) - Fasting clears a mind "beclouded by overeating", but not one who
  "habitually eats only according to his needs".

### B3. KJV texts

- **Prov 23:1-3** (full) - "When thou sittest to eat with a ruler, consider diligently what [is] before thee: And put a
  knife to thy throat, if thou [be] a man given to appetite. Be not desirous of his dainties: for they [are] deceitful
  meat."
- **Eccl 10:17** (full) - "Blessed [art] thou, O land, when thy king [is] the son of nobles, and thy princes eat in due
  season, for strength, and not for drunkenness!" (Regularity and purpose; used at Ed 206.4, Te 53.2.)
- **Prov 25:16** - "Hast thou found honey? eat so much as is sufficient for thee, lest thou be filled therewith, and vomit
  it." (Good food, limited quantity.)
- **Prov 23:20-21** - "Be not among winebibbers; among riotous eaters of flesh: For the drunkard and the glutton shall come
  to poverty: and drowsiness shall clothe [a man] with rags."
- **Luke 21:34** - "take heed to yourselves, lest at any time your hearts be overcharged with surfeiting, and drunkenness,
  and cares of this life" (Pioneers read "surfeiting" as overeating: GCB July 1, 1900.)
- **Phil 4:5** - "Let your moderation be known unto all men. The Lord [is] at hand." (Greek sense is gentleness/
  forbearance; use for the spirit of the reformer, not as a diet rule.)
- **Phil 3:19** - "whose God [is their] belly" (Pioneer usage: ARSH March 7, 1865.)

## C. TEMPERANCE (theme): appetite, stimulants, Eden and the wilderness

### C1. Core passages (EGW)

- `DA 117.3` * - "With Christ, as with the holy pair in Eden, appetite was the ground of the first great temptation. Just
  where the ruin began, the work of our redemption must begin." - The key frame for the whole strand.
- `3T 161.3` (= CD 70.1) - "As Adam fell through appetite and lost blissful Eden, the children of Adam may, through
  Christ, overcome appetite and through temperance in all things regain Eden." - Hope, not only warning.
- `CTBH 54.2` (= CD 167.1) - "Adam fell by the indulgence of appetite; Christ overcame by the denial of appetite." - The
  long fast measures the strength of the temptation.
- `Te 108.4` - "Christ fought the battle upon the point of appetite, and came off victorious; and we also can conquer
  through strength derived from Him." - Victory is borrowed strength.
- `MH 176.3` * - "A pure and noble life, a life of victory over appetite and lust, is possible to everyone who will unite
  his weak, wavering human will to the omnipotent, unwavering will of God." - The how: the will yielded to Christ.
- `PP 562.1` / `Te 138.2` * - "True temperance teaches us to dispense entirely with everything hurtful, and to use
  judiciously that which is healthful." - The definition: abstain from the harmful, moderate in the good.
- `Te 156.1` - "Daniel's clearness of mind and firmness of purpose ... were due in a great degree to the plainness of his
  diet, in connection with his life of prayer." - Diet plus prayer, not diet alone.
- `CTBH 22.3` (= CD 154.2) - "Daniel valued his human capabilities, but he did not trust in them." - Daniel's trust was
  God, not his menu.
- `MH 326.1` - "Tea acts as a stimulant and, to a certain extent, produces intoxication. The action of coffee and many
  other popular drinks is similar." - Stimulants: borrowed energy.
- `MH 326.3` - "Tired nerves need rest and quiet instead of stimulation and overwork." - Stimulants mask fatigue, then
  demand more.
- `MH 327.1` - "Tobacco is a slow, insidious, but most malignant poison." - "It excites and then paralyzes the nerves."
- `MH 330.2` - quotes Prov 20:1 and Prov 23:29-32 on wine; `MH 331.1` - the drunkard as "souls for whom Christ died, and
  over whom angels weep" - Compassion toward the addicted.
- `CD 436.3` - "Moderate drinking is the school in which men are educated for the drunkard's career." - Why abstain, not
  moderate, with alcohol.
- `MH 335.3` - "Those who attempt to leave off these stimulants will for a time feel a loss and will suffer without them.
  But by persistence they will overcome the craving" - Honest about withdrawal; nature "will again rally".
- `MH 316.3` - "When the use of flesh food is discontinued, there is often a sense of weakness, a lack of vigor." - Same
  transition pattern for leaving flesh.

### C2. Pioneer passages

- `BHY 205.1` (James White, 1890) - The wilderness temptations "represent the leading temptations to which the fallen race
  is exposed"; the first "relates to appetite"; Christ's victory is "an example full of encouragement".
- `BHY 199.3` (James White, 1890) - For liquor, tobacco, tea and coffee, "Total abstinence is the only remedy."
- `BHY 256.6` (James White, 1890) - Testimony of a man who gave up tea and coffee after a sleepless night following a
  tea-party; calls them "poison".
- `ARSH May 17, 1898, page 315.23` (A. T. Jones) - Tea, coffee and alcohol on one ladder: each "shades into" the next and
  "leads on to an appetite for it".
- `GCB May 30, 1909, page 217.3` (GC Bulletin health talk) - Daniel's strength to refuse Babylon's wine "may be in part
  attributed to the nature of the food of his choice"; "Babylon's meat called for wine".
- `GCB May 31, 1909, page 233.11` (GC Bulletin health talk) - Objects to "all narcotic stimulants, including alcohol,
  tobacco, tea, and coffee. Neither of these articles is a real food."

### C3. KJV texts

- **Matt 4:2-4** (full) - "And when he had fasted forty days and forty nights, he was afterward an hungred. And when the
  tempter came to him, he said, If thou be the Son of God, command that these stones be made bread. But he answered and
  said, It is written, Man shall not live by bread alone, but by every word that proceedeth out of the mouth of God."
- **1 Cor 9:25-27** (full v.25) - "And every man that striveth for the mastery is temperate in all things. Now they [do
  it] to obtain a corruptible crown; but we an incorruptible." v.27 "But I keep under my body, and bring [it] into
  subjection"
- **2 Pet 1:5-6** - "add to your faith virtue; and to virtue knowledge; And to knowledge temperance; and to temperance
  patience; and to patience godliness;"
- **Gal 5:22-23** - "But the fruit of the Spirit is love, joy, peace, longsuffering, gentleness, goodness, faith, Meekness,
  temperance: against such there is no law." (Temperance as fruit of the Spirit, not of willpower alone.)
- **Prov 20:1** (full) - "Wine [is] a mocker, strong drink [is] raging: and whosoever is deceived thereby is not wise."
- **Prov 23:29-32** - "Who hath woe? who hath sorrow? ... They that tarry long at the wine ... Look not thou upon the wine
  when it is red ... At the last it biteth like a serpent, and stingeth like an adder."
- **1 Cor 6:19-20** - "know ye not that your body is the temple of the Holy Ghost ... therefore glorify God in your body,
  and in your spirit, which are God's."
- **Rom 12:1** - "present your bodies a living sacrifice, holy, acceptable unto God, [which is] your reasonable service."

### C4. KJV checked and handled with care / dropped

- **Col 2:21** "(Touch not; taste not; handle not;" - The sources borrow this phrase for stimulants (MH 335.3, CD 428.1),
  but in Colossians Paul is quoting and rejecting "the commandments and doctrines of men" (v.22-23). Do NOT quote it on
  camera as a command; paraphrase the counsel instead.
- **Rom 14:3, 17, 21** - Context is conscience over disputed foods among believers. Fits the "do not judge / not a test"
  section (v.3 "let not him which eateth not judge him that eateth: for God hath received him"; v.17 "the kingdom of God
  is not meat and drink"). Not a text about clean/unclean or about health; use only for the non-judging point.
- **Num 11:33-34** (plague) - Verified; keep off camera unless the Ps 106:15 point needs it. Too easily heard as threat.
- **Isa 55:2** - Kept above as an echo only.

## D. AGAINST EXTREMES, NOT A TEST, PROGRESSIVE CHANGE (the sources' own guardrails)

Put this in every episode where diet comes up. The sources themselves insist on it.

- `MH 319.1` * - "Those who understand the laws of health and who are governed by principle, will shun the extremes, both
  of indulgence and of restriction." - Two ditches, not one. "they do not urge their views offensively".
- `9T 159.3` (= CD 404.3, CH 133.3) * - "We are not to make the use of flesh food a test of fellowship" - Explicitly not a
  test; influence, not enforcement.
- `MM 279.5` - "While we do not make the use of flesh-meat a test, while we do not want to force anyone to give up its use"
- `CD 210.1` - "May the Lord keep us from human tests and extremes." - Extremes force corrections that others then use to
  excuse indulgence.
- `MH 319.2` (= CD 198.3) * - "no one should criticize others because their practice is not, in all things, in harmony
  with his own. It is impossible to make an unvarying rule to regulate everyone's habits" - "Not all can eat the same
  things."
- `CD 491.3` (Letter 127, 1904) - "I do not hold myself up as a criterion for them." ... "I bind no one else's conscience by
  my own." - Her own household: family members eat differently; "to me beans are poison".
- `MH 318.4` - "Those who have but a partial understanding of the principles of reform are often the most rigid" - Rigidity
  is a sign of partial understanding, and drives people away.
- `MH 318.2` - "they adopt an impoverished diet. Their food does not supply the elements needed to make good blood." - The
  restrictive extreme harms health; `MH 321.3`: "an impoverished diet produces poverty of the blood".
- `CD 202.4` - "Health reform becomes health deform, a health destroyer, when it is carried to extremes." - Warning to a
  worker whose own diet was too restricted.
- `MM 286.2` - "because some are far behind, you must not, in order to be an example to them, be an extremist."
- `2T 374.3` (= CH 161.2) - caution "those that are extremists not to raise a false standard and then endeavor to bring
  everybody to it."
- `MH 320.2` (= CD 365.2) - "The diet reform should be progressive." - The compilers list this first among the principles
  for reading CD (CD 7.2, CD 481.2).
- `3T 20.3` (= CD 468.1) - "We must go no faster than we can take those with us whose consciences and intellects are
  convinced" ... "It is slow work to obtain a reform in diet."
- `CD 460.3` - "we are to meet the people where they are." - And only present advanced steps once people can cook
  palatable, nourishing, inexpensive food.
- `CD 466.3` - "We did not say to the people, You must not eat meat." - In sickness they gave a family flesh food "when we
  thought it essential". Context over rule.
- `MM 260.4` - "this reform cannot be made all at once. The change must be made gradually."
- `MH 323.3` - "The narrow ideas of some would-be health reformers have been a great injury to the cause of hygiene." -
  Reform judged by its table: make it attractive.
- Pioneer: `SPCR 1.3` (James White, Review, March 17, 1868) - Strong appeals push a few to extremes; reproofs to them are
  taken by the tardy "as an excuse to remain too far behind." Explains why both kinds of counsel exist in the writings.
- Also note `CD 481.3` (compilers' appendix, not EGW): general principles were given; details "must sometimes be determined
  by experimentation, and by the best scientific conclusions available."

## E. Physiology note (honest calibration)

Plain and well-established today: a diet built mainly on whole grains, legumes, fruits, vegetables and nuts is associated
with lower rates of heart disease, type 2 diabetes and some cancers; processed and red meat carry measurable cancer and
cardiovascular risk; overeating and excess weight drive chronic disease, so intemperance in quantity as well as quality (Te 162.2) is
mainstream; tobacco is lethal in every form (MH 327.1 has been fully vindicated); alcohol has no health-protective "safe
dose" for cancer risk and dependency usually begins with "moderate" use; caffeine is a real stimulant with tolerance,
sleep disruption and withdrawal headache, and stimulants do mask fatigue (MH 326.3); quitting stimulants involves a real
but temporary withdrawal (MH 335.3); simple, regular meals and a stomach not working through the night (late eating)
are sensible and widely advised. Historically situated or stronger than modern consensus: "tea ... produces intoxication"
and "poison" language for tea and coffee (moderate coffee is neutral or even associated with some benefits in current
studies; say "stimulant you may not need", not "poison"); "tuberculous and cancerous germs" in meat (bovine TB from meat and
milk was real before inspection and pasteurization; cancer as a germ transmitted by meat is not established); warnings to
leave milk and eggs because of animal disease (tied to that era's disease, framed by her as future and progressive);
specific rules like "five hours" between meals, two meals as better than three, food-combining (fruit and vegetables at
one meal), cereal coffee and zwieback. Meal-timing research is active but not settled; present regular meals and no
grazing as wise practice, not proven law. The sources' own warning against an "impoverished diet" matches modern advice
that a fully plant-based diet needs planning (e.g. vitamin B12). Never individual advice; never tell anyone to drop a
prescribed diet or treatment.
