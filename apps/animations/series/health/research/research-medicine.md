# Research notes: MEDICINE (drugs, true remedies, the physician, prayer and means)

Scope: what EGW and the pioneers wrote on medicine; what "drugs" meant in their day; the passages that keep
this from being read as "refuse all medicine". Every refcode and quote below came from q.ts or the KJV CLI in
this session. Quotes are verbatim (page-break numbers inside a paragraph are dropped and marked [pb]).

Standing guardrail for every script in this strand: we teach principles; we never tell a viewer to stop,
refuse or replace a treatment. The sources themselves forbid "no remedies at all" (2SM 286.2, 2SG 135.1).

---

## 0. The one-paragraph map

The sources make four moves, and a video that keeps only the first misreads them:

1. **Diagnosis**: disease is mostly the body's effort to throw off what broken law left behind (MH 127.1);
   remove the cause, assist nature.
2. **Verdict on the "drug medication" of their time**: the named targets are mercury/calomel, strychnine (nux
   vomica), opium/morphine, arsenic, heavy quinine dosing, and patent nostrums (4aSG 138-139, 2SM 278.3-279.1,
   MH 126.2-4). "Educate away from drugs; use them less and less" (CH 261.2).
3. **Balance**: using means is not a denial of faith; Hezekiah's figs, Christ's clay, Luke the physician,
   surgery, "well-qualified physicians"; extremism opposed (MH 231-233, 2SM 284-287, 2SG 135.1, PK 211.1).
   She herself took 23 X-ray treatments and (per her secretary) was vaccinated (2SM 303).
4. **Calling**: Christ the Great Physician; the physician as educator and gospel worker; medical missionary
   work the "right hand of the gospel" (MH 125.1, MM 120.3, 5T 443, 7T 59.1).

## 1. Core EGW passages

### 1a. Disease, drugs and the true remedies

- - `MH 127.1` - "Disease is an effort of nature to free the system from conditions that result from a violation of the laws of health." - Seek the cause, change conditions, then assist nature; restorative power is "not in drugs, but in nature" (= CH 90.1, Te 85.4).
- - `MH 126.3` - "Health is recovered in spite of the drug. But in most cases the drug only changes the form and location of the disease." - Drugs "sometimes afford present relief"; recovery comes from the body's own vital force.
- `MH 126.2` - "So they resort to patent nostrums, of whose real properties they know little" - The target is "the free use of poisonous drugs" to silence pain without changing habits.
- `MH 126.4` - "act a part in laying the foundation of the liquor habit, the opium habit, the morphine habit" - Patent medicines and some prescribed drugs built addictions (the nostrums of the day were often alcohol/opiate based).
- - `CH 261.2` (= 2SM 281.4, Te 85.3, HL 246.4 dated 1888) - "Drug medication, as it is generally practiced, is a curse. Educate away from drugs. Use them less and less" - Note the qualifiers: "as it is generally practiced", "less and less", "Drugs need seldom be used" - a direction of travel, not an absolute ban.
- `2SM 283.3` (Letter 3, 1884) - "True, drugs may not be as dangerous wisely administered as they usually are, but in the hands of many they will be hurtful" - Concedes that administration and dose matter.
- `2SM 283.4` - "discarding almost entirely the use of drugs" - Purpose of their institutions: hygienic treatment; "almost entirely", not "entirely".
- `2SM 283.6` (= MM 229.2) - "When you understand physiology in its truest sense, your drug bills will be very much smaller" - The ideal: understanding the body shrinks dependence on drugs.
- `5T 443.1` (= 2SM 287.3) - "There are many ways of practicing the healing art, but there is only one way that Heaven approves." - God's remedies: "Pure air and water, cleanliness, a proper diet, purity of life, and a firm trust in God".
- `7T 76.2` - "Connected with water treatment, they are more effective in restoring health than all the drug medication in the world." - The things of nature are "given to the well to keep them well and to the sick to make them well."
- `CH 59.1` (= MM 13.4) - "God will not work a miracle to keep those from sickness who have no care for themselves" - Prayer without changed habits; faith "made perfect by works".
- `HR September 1, 1866, par. 1` - "If he is an honest physician, and declines to give the child medicine because he is wise enough to know it will be for its hurt" - Parents dosing children for every upset; the honest doctor is the one who declines.

### 1b. What "drugs" meant then (the sources name them)

- - `2SM 278.3` (a medical student's letter, 1893) - "Does it refer only to the stronger medicines as mercury, strychnine, arsenic, and such poisons, the things we medical students call ‘drugs,’" - The exact question: what does "drug" mean?
- - `2SM 279.1` (her reply, Letter 17a, 1893) - "Drug poisons mean the articles which you have mentioned. The simpler remedies are less harmful in proportion to their simplicity" - Also: "it would not be wisdom to engage in controversy over this subject." She still names no doctor-compounded medicine as "perfectly harmless".
- `4aSG 138.1` (1864) - "Upon it was written, Nux vomica, strychnine. Beneath was written, No antidote." - Strychnine as a "tonic" in small doses.
- `4aSG 139.1` - "Nux vomica, or strychnine, and opium have killed their millions" - Opium users likened to the drunkard and tobacco slave.
- `4aSG 139.2` - "Mercury, calomel, and quinine have brought their amount of wretchedness, which the day of God alone will fully reveal." - Mercury effects listed: ulcers, bone pain, "loss of teeth, loss of memory, and impaired sight".
- `2SM 447.2` (calomel case) - "His lips were dark and swollen. His gums were inflamed." - Then swollen tongue and heavy salivation: a textbook picture of mercury poisoning.
- `4aSG 134.1` (= 2SM 451.1) - "Drugs never cure disease. They only change the form and location. Nature alone is the effectual restorer" - 1864 wording of what MH 126.3 says more carefully in 1905.
- `15MR 276.2` (Ms 22, 1887) - "so thoroughly drugged with quinine, morphine, or some strong health- and life-destroying medicine" - Drugging so that nature "may never make sufficient protest" and habits continue.
- `2SM 277.2` (compilers' note, White Estate) - "There were certain physicians on both sides of the Atlantic who deplored the absence of adequate diagnosis" - Others also attacked the poisonous drugs; practice changed after modern scientific medical education.
- `2SM 277.5` (compilers' note) - "it was justifiable and necessary to employ even medications that were known to be poisonous." - The compilers' reading of her own practice: she held up the ideal but allowed for special situations. (Context, not her own words.)

### 1c. Prayer and means; against extremes (the guardrails)

- - `MH 231.3` (= 2SM 286.1) - "It is not a denial of faith to use such remedies as God has provided to alleviate pain and to aid nature in her work of restoration." - "We should employ every facility for the restoration of health"; pray, then work "with all the more energy".
- - `MH 232.1` - "Now, one word from God would have healed Hezekiah instantly; but special directions were given" - Quotes Isa 38:21: "We have the sanction of the word of God for the use of remedial agencies."
- `MH 233.1` - "While He did not give countenance to drug medication, He sanctioned the use of simple and natural remedies." - Christ's clay and Siloam (John 9:7).
- `MH 227.2` - "In many cases of prayer for the healing of the sick, that which is called faith is nothing less than presumption." - Prayer for healing is "a most solemn act".
- `MH 227.4` - "labor lost to teach people to look to God as a healer of their infirmities, unless they are taught also to lay aside unhealthful practices" - Healing prayer and habit change go together.
- `MH 228.1` - "the violation of God’s law, either natural or spiritual, is sin" - Natural law has moral weight.
- `MH 229.2` - "If, therefore, it is for Thy glory and the good of the afflicted ones, we ask, in the name of Jesus" - Pray in submission to God's will: "we know not what we should pray for as we ought" (Rom 8:26).
- `MH 233.2` - "whatever the outcome of the case, let us not lose faith in God." - Bereavement is a cup held by "a Father's hand"; the healed owe gratitude (the ten lepers).
- - `2SG 135.1` (1860) - "Some have taken the strong ground that it was wrong to use simple remedies. We have never taken this position, but have opposed it." - "In some cases the counsel of an earthly physician is very necessary. This position we have always held."
- `2SM 286.2` (Lt 182, 1899) - "The idea which you hold, that no remedies should be used for the sick, is an error." - God does not heal "without the aid of the means of healing which lie within the reach of man".
- `2SM 284.4` (Ms 67, 1899) - "It is our privilege to use every God-appointed means in correspondence with our faith" - "it is not a denial of faith to have the operation performed."
- `2SM 286.4` - "The God of nature directs the human agent to use natural remedies now." - Hezekiah again: why figs when God turned back the sun?
- `2SM 346.2` (Ms 31, 1911) - "It is no denial of faith to use rational remedies judiciously." - Full text (25LtMs Ms 31, 1911, par. 17): herbs "in harmony with the exercise of faith".
- `2MCP 513.7` (Lt 37, 1892) - "This is not a denial of faith; it is in strict harmony with the plans of God." - Same letter: "I have seen so much of carrying matters to extremes, even in praying for the sick" (7LtMs, Lt 37, 1892, par. 6).
- `PK 211.1` - "instead of trusting in the power of the living God and the skill of well-qualified physicians." - Context 2 Kings 1 (Ahaziah to Baal-zebub): the alternative to spiritist healers is God AND qualified physicians.
- `Ev 594.3` - "Should this be done, great fanaticism would arise in our ranks." - Answer to "why not just pray for miraculous healing instead of building sanitariums?"
- `6LtMs, Lt 88, 1889, par. 8` - "except the one method of allopathy which they have learned. I cannot, I will not, fall into line with this thing." - She also resisted a monopoly of drug-only medicine; same letter: "let the drugs fall into disuse and practice upon hygienic principles."

### 1d. Her own practice (useful against both extremes)

- - `2SM 303.7` (Letter 30, 1911) - "For several weeks I took treatment with the X-ray for the black spot that was on my forehead." - 23 treatments: "For this I am very grateful."
- `2SM 303.6` (D. E. Robinson, her secretary, 1931 - secondhand) - "she herself was vaccinated and urged her helpers, those connected with her, to be vaccinated." - During a smallpox epidemic; also "she did not refer to them in any of her writings" (303.5).
- `2SM 302.2` - "once since on this side I took it as a medicine when I was sick and vomiting." - Tea, refused as a beverage, used as a medicine; coffee likewise during sickness (2SM 302.7).
- `2SM 281.4` (bracketed compilers' lead-in) - "Mrs. White’s counsel when asked concerning the use of quinine in the treatment of malaria" - W. C. White's report that follows is NOT in the corpus text (the next indexed paragraph is 2SM 282.1). Do not quote it until checked in print.

### 1e. The physician and the calling

- - `MH 125.1` - "The true physician is an educator." - "He stands as a guardian of both physical and moral health."
- `2SM 282.2` (= MM 221) - "The first labors of a physician should be to educate the sick and suffering in the very course they should pursue to prevent disease." - Prescribing drugs is the easy road for those who will not teach.
- - `MM 120.3` - "Christ is the Great Physician, not only of the body, but of the soul." - "Physicians are to act in Christ’s stead."
- `5T 443.3` - "The physician should know how to pray. In many cases he must increase suffering in order to save life" - Prayer gives "the sick an abiding confidence".
- `5T 443.4` - "Satan is the originator of disease; and the physician is [pb] warring against his work and power." - The physician fights on God's side; mind and heart troubles behind much disease.
- `2SM 284.2` (= MH 118) - "Before performing a critical operation, let the physician ask for the aid of the Great Physician." - Surgery presented as normal, prayerful work.
- `2SM 286.3` - "Luke is called the beloved physician. He trusted in the Lord to make him skillful in the application of remedies." - "There were physicians in Christ’s day and in the days of the apostles."
- `RH December 17, 1914, par. 3` (= LLM 339.1) - "doing double service as a physician and a gospel minister. He was indeed a medical missionary" - Luke at Philippi; medical skill opened hearts.
- `CH 34.1` (= 4T 225.2) - "The Saviour devoted more time and labor to healing the afflicted of their maladies than to preaching." - His last injunction: lay hands on the sick.
- `7T 59.1` (= CH 219.2) - "Medical missionary work is the right hand of the gospel." - "God’s methods of treating disease will open doors".
- `MH 135.2` - "A man that is feeble or diseased cannot endure the wearing labor incident to the physician’s calling." - The physician must keep his own health ("Physician, heal thyself").

### 1f. Simple remedies, herbs, charcoal, water

- - `2SM 294.1` (Lt 82, 1897) - "These old-fashioned, simple herbs, used intelligently, would have recovered many sick who have died under drug medication." - "some simple herbs of the field that at times are beneficial" - note "some", "at times", "intelligently".
- `2SM 290.2` (Ms 86, 1897) - "There are herbs that are harmless, the use of which will tide over many apparently serious difficulties." - Ends: "An ounce of prevention is worth a pound of cure."
- `2SM 288.1` (Ms 73, 1908) - "great efficacy for healing lies in a proper use of water. These treatments should be given skillfully." - Herbs whose effect differs from drugs "that poison the blood".
- `2SM 298.5` (Lt 100, 1903) - "For some forms of indigestion, it is more efficacious than drugs." - Charcoal; eucalyptus-wood charcoal compress for inflammation.
- `22LtMs, Lt 158, 1907, par. 1` - "the use of charcoal as a powerful agency for removing poison from the system" - "it has often brought relief when every other means had failed."
- `2SM 300.1` (Ms 29, 1911) - "It would be well to treasure this prescription which the Lord ordered to be used, more than we do." - Hezekiah's fig poultice.
- `2SM 298.6` (Lt 100, 1903) - "Always study and teach the use of the simplest remedies" - Means "within the reach of the common people".
- `2SM 292.5` (compilers' note) - "except for the brief mention of the “lump of figs” for Hezekiah’s boils, and a fleeting allusion to the ineffectual use of “simple herbs”" - In 50 years of published health writing she named almost no specific herbal remedy; the herb counsel is in letters. Keep the "herbs" theme modest.
- `23LtMs, Lt 348, 1908, par. 1` - "I cannot advise any remedy for her cough better than the eucalyptus and honey." - A sample of her homely counsel; not for scripts as a prescription.

## 2. Pioneer passages

- - `HBH 204.6` (J. N. Loughborough, Hand Book of Health, 1868) - Drugs cannot do what the body needs and are "in itself an evil", yet "there are some cases of disease in which medicine, to some extent, is indispensably necessary to the salvation of human life" - a "necessary evil"; "That physician who uses the least medicine is the best friend to man’s physical interests." Best pioneer balance statement.
- `HHTL 155.2` (R. T. Trall, from Water-Cure for the Million, reprinted in HHTL 1865; credit per HHTL 129.4) - "all curative virtue is inherent in the living organism"; remedies only supply favorable conditions. HHTL 155.3: hygienic medication "adopts whatever nature appropriates, and discards only what nature rejects."
- `HHTL 158.3` (HHTL 1865, quoting Prof. Jos. M. Smith, M.D., of a New York medical college) - "drugs do not cure disease; disease is always cured by the vis medicatrix naturae." and "Digitalis has hurried thousands to the grave." - the critique came from inside medicine too. (Digitalis later became a standard heart drug at controlled doses: dose was the issue.)
- `HHTL 390.5` (HHTL 1865) - a printed list of what passed as "medical advisement": "calomel, jalap, opium, Dovers’ powder, morphine, arsenic, quinine, podophyllyn, lobelia, strychnine" plus whiskey, brandy, gin, blisters and burning. Shows what the word "drugs" called up for a 1865 reader.
- `HHTL 177.2` (EGW, "Disease and its Causes" ch. 3, in HHTL 1865) - the slightly ill mother "might have recovered by abstinence from food for a short period, and a cessation from labor" but sends for a physician who is "either too ignorant ... or too anxious to obtain a fee."
- `3SG 27.3` (introduction to Spiritual Gifts vol. 3, 1864; the intro is likely James White's - confirm authorship before crediting) - "The sick were not always healed by faith in Paul’s day." Cites Trophimus left sick (2 Tim 4:20) and Timothy's "little wine" (1 Tim 5:23).
- `GCB May 31, 1909, page 219.6` (Dr. J. D. Shively, General Conference health session) - "It is probable that drugs will never be entirely discarded." To what extent are they necessary? "Just to that extent that we have no remedy more effective." Also names anesthetics and disinfectants in surgery.
- `GCB June 1, 1909, page 236.3-236.4` - Dr. W. A. George asks that the discussion "avoid any radical position"; Dr. G. H. Heald cites a JAMA article (May 15, 1909) "Pharmacologic Fetishisms" showing many drugs of the day "worse than useless". 236.7 (Dr. D. H. Kress): "The Lord tells us to educate away from drugs."
- `ARSH October 17, 1899, page 668.7` (A. T. Jones) - "please do not ask nor expect him to take away the disease while you continue the cause of that disease."

### The pharmakeia question (G5331), handled honestly

`bun src/main.ts concordance G5331`: φαρμακεία (pharmakeia), gloss "medication ("pharmacy", i.e. (by extension)
magic(literally or figuratively) — sorcery, witchcraft." The tool lists 2 verses: Gal 5:20 "witchcraft" and
Rev 18:23 "by thy sorceries were all nations deceived". (Rev 9:21 "sorceries" is not listed under G5331 by
this tool; related G5332 pharmakeus Rev 21:8 "sorcerers", G5333 pharmakos Rev 22:15 "sorcerers".)

- What it means: the root is "drug/potion", but in the New Testament the sense is sorcery: magic potions and
  spells, listed next to idolatry among "the works of the flesh". The KJV translators rendered it "witchcraft"
  and "sorceries", not "medicine".
- What it does NOT mean: it does not say that taking medicine, or pharmacy, is witchcraft. Saying so is word
  root fallacy, and the sources themselves would not support it (surgery, X-ray, herbs, quinine counsel).
- The honest bridge: Scripture does condemn seeking healing from occult powers (2 Kings 1:3; PK 211.1 and
  5T 193.2 name "electricity, magnetism, or the so-called “sympathetic remedies”" of spiritist healers).
  That is the line, and it is about the source of power, not about chemistry.
- Recommendation: use only in a "myth check" episode, if at all.

## 4. Physiology note (honest)

Plain and well established: much illness comes from habits and conditions (diet, alcohol, tobacco, inactivity,
poor sleep, bad air), and changing them prevents and often reverses disease; fever and inflammation are partly
the body's own defense and repair; the body does the healing and treatment mostly removes obstacles and
supports it. The drugs the sources name were in fact poisons as used: calomel is mercury (her description in
2SM 447.2 of inflamed gums, swollen tongue and heavy salivation matches mercury poisoning), strychnine was given
as a "tonic", opium and morphine were in unregulated patent medicines and built addictions, arsenic was in
"tonics", and quinine was given in large doses for everything. Charcoal: activated charcoal swallowed is still a
recognized emergency treatment for some poisonings (it binds toxins in the gut); poultice use has little
evidence. Water treatments, rest and nursing are real support. What modern physiology qualifies: "disease is an
effort of nature" fits fevers and many acute illnesses, but not infections that overwhelm the body, cancers,
or genetic and autoimmune disease. The germ theory came after the 1860s statements. "Drugs never cure; they only
change the form" was true of most remedies of 1864, but not of antibiotics, insulin, vaccines or the
quinine-type drugs used today against malaria, which are lifesaving. The line between "herb" and "drug" is not
chemical: quinine itself comes from cinchona bark, digitalis from foxglove, morphine from the poppy. Dose,
purity, evidence and need matter more than origin. The sources' own wording ("as it is generally practiced",
"less and less", "seldom", "wisely administered", "rational remedies judiciously", surgery, X-ray, vaccination)
fits this reading. 1 Tim 5:23-type specific remedies and the "night air" kind of 19th-c. advice belong to their
setting.

## 6. What the owner's cut-off line ("whats very interesting is medicine...") may have pointed to

1. That "drugs" in the sources meant named poisons (mercury, strychnine, arsenic, opium), as a student's 1893
   question and her answer show (2SM 278.3, 279.1), so the warnings are sharper and narrower than people think.
2. That pharmakeia, the Greek word behind "witchcraft/sorcery", has the root "drug", and whether that means
   anything (it means sorcery; the honest line is occult healing, 2 Kings 1).
3. That "disease is an effort of nature" (MH 127.1) turns medicine around: symptoms as the body healing, and
   treatment as assisting it rather than suppressing it.
4. That Scripture itself shows remedies alongside prayer (Hezekiah's figs, Christ's clay, Luke the physician,
   the Samaritan's oil and wine), so faith and medicine are not opposites.
5. That medicine later caught up: physicians dropped calomel and bleeding, and even the 1909 Conference doctors
   cited JAMA against "pharmacologic fetishisms", while she herself took X-ray treatment and was vaccinated.
   Prevention-first medicine is now mainstream.

  before using it.
- "BHY (1890)": in this corpus it is CTBH (Christian Temperance and Bible Hygiene). No drug passage surfaced from
  it except CTBH 113.1 ("It is not safe to trust to physicians who have not the fear of God before them").
- HHTL 155 "DRUGS" is by R. T. Trall (HHTL 129.4), not James White. 3SG 27.3 sits in the vol. 3 introduction;
  authorship should be checked before crediting.
- Compilers' notes (2SM 277.2, 277.5, 278.1, 292.5, 303.3-6) are White Estate editorial text: use as context, not
  as her words.
