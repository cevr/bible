## 1. REST

### Core EGW passages

- `MH 236.1`* - "Some make themselves sick by overwork. For these, rest, freedom from care, and a spare diet, are essential to restoration of health." - The chapter is titled "Rest as a Remedy". It prescribes the country, fields, woods and birdsong for people worn out by brain work.
- `DA 360.2`* - "They had been putting their whole souls into labor for the people, and this was exhausting their physical and mental strength. It was their duty to rest." - Mark 6:31. Rest is a duty, and Christ was the one who prescribed it.
- `MH 58.2` - "To His toil-worn workers today as really as to His first disciples He speaks these words of compassion, “Come ye yourselves apart, ... and rest awhile.”" - Christ did not urge "ceaseless toil".
- `RH November 7, 1893, par. 5` (also `ChS 249.2`) - "The Christian life is not made up of unceasing activity, or of continual meditation." - Constant strain injures "the powers of mind and body". Christian life needs both work and stillness.
- `Ed 205.4`* - "Since the work of building up the body takes place during the hours of rest, it is essential, especially in youth, that sleep should be regular and abundant." - Regular hours. The body is built up during sleep.
- `7T 247.1` (= `GW 422.3`) - "Proper periods of sleep and rest and an abundance of physical exercise are essential to health of body and mind." - It warns against robbing nature of her hours of rest. "Vigor of mind depends largely upon vigor of body."
- `7MR 224.3` - "sleep is worth far more before than after midnight. Two hours’ good sleep before twelve o’clock is worth more than four hours after twelve o’clock" - The counsel to sleep early, given "for brain workers". See the physiology note.
- `2MCP 388.1` (from Youth's Instructor, May 31, 1894) - "Irregular hours for eating and sleeping sap the brain forces." - Regularity matters as much as the amount of sleep.
- `PH043 38.3` - "God has given us night as one of his greatest blessings, bringing quiet and repose to overworked bodies and minds." - Night is a gift, and cutting sleep short brings "weariness and want of every power".
- `1T 514.1` (= `CH 195.1`) - "Sabbathkeepers as a people labor too hard without allowing themselves change or periods of rest." - Recreation is needful, and more so for people whose work is mainly mental. Do not use the list of disapproved games that follows; it is tied to its period.
- `Ed 207.1` (= `MYP 362.1`) - "Recreation, when true to its name, re-creation, tends to strengthen and build up." - Amusement "absorbs the energies that are required for useful work". This is the recreation-versus-amusement distinction.
- `DA 207.2` (full sentence read via `ML 231.4` / `RRe 331.4`) - "man is to leave the occupations of his daily life and devote those sacred hours to healthful rest, to worship, and to holy deeds" - The Sabbath "is not intended to be a period of useless inactivity".
- `2T 583.1` - "the mind cannot be refreshed, enlivened, and elevated by being confined nearly all the Sabbath hours within walls" - Sabbath rest includes nature and fresh air.
- `GW 245.2` - "he should turn aside and rest awhile, not for selfish gratification, but that he may be better prepared for future duties" - It also warns that the enemy presses his "fiercest temptations" on an overstrained mind.
- `DG 162.2` (= `2MCP 375.4`) - "It is not work but over work, without periods of rest, that breaks people down, endangering the life forces." - Work itself is good; overwork without rest is what breaks people.
- `3MR 368.1` - "It is not work that kills; it is worry. The only way to avoid worry is to take every trouble to Christ." - This bridges rest and trust.
- `RH November 14, 1893, par. 6` - "They needed rest of mind, they needed sleep; “for so he giveth his beloved sleep" - EGW applies Ps 127:2 to overburdened workers. (`7LtMs, Lt 41, 1892, par. 10` adds: "Life is too precious in His sight to be imperilled".)
- `MH 247.1` - "Abiding peace, true rest of spirit, has but one Source." - Matt 11:28 and John 14:27. Deep rest is found in Christ himself.

### Pioneer passages

- `HHTL 334.1` (Health, or How to Live, 1865, health rules list) - "Sleep is the best restorer of the Nervous system." and "One hour of sleep before midnight is worth two after it." Ventilated bedrooms and early sleep.
- `HHTL 356.2` (1865) - Invalids generally "do not sleep enough". Sleep is when "the structures are repaired". Rule for invalids: retire early. See the physiology note on "assimilation only during sleep".
- `HHTL 387.1` (1865) - "those who think most, who do most brainwork, require most sleep."
- `HBH 59.2` (Loughborough, Hand Book of Health, 1868) - "Sleep is the grand restorative after severe muscular exertion". Wind down gradually after exertion; "After work we need repose."
- `HBH 115.2` (1868) - People with studious or indoor work need more sleep. The dietary claims in the same paragraph (two meals, vegetarians need less sleep) are historically situated.
- `ARSH October 27, 1859, page 182.30` (Uriah Smith) - "All need full, liberal appropriation of time for rest." Constant fatigue "unfits the mind to act, to reason".
- `AMS January 30, 1896, page 35.17` (A. T. Jones) - "It is not true that the object of the Sabbath is physical rest." A pioneer guardrail: do not sell the Sabbath as a health technique.

### KJV texts

- **Psalms 127:2** (full) - "It is vain for you to rise up early, to sit up late, to eat the bread of sorrows: for so he giveth his beloved sleep."

- Hebrews 4:9-10 (optional, spiritual rest) - "There remaineth therefore a rest to the people of God. For he that is entered into his rest, he also hath ceased from his own works, as God did from his."

### Physiology note (rest)

These claims are plain and well established: the body repairs itself and the brain consolidates during sleep; growth-hormone release and deep slow-wave sleep come mostly in the first part of the night; a regular sleep-wake time supports the circadian rhythm; chronic short sleep impairs mood, judgment, immunity and metabolism; and mental overwork without breaks lowers performance. The "two hours before midnight are worth four after" counsel (`7MR 224.3`, `HHTL 334.1`) is not literally true by clock time. What science supports is that early, regular sleep lined up with darkness is the most restorative, and that deep sleep is concentrated in the early cycles whenever sleep begins. Present it as "go to bed early and regularly", not as midnight arithmetic. `HHTL 356.2`'s "assimilation only takes place during sleep" overstates it, because tissue building happens around the clock. The sleep-and-diet links in `HBH 115.2` and the counsel to ventilate even with "night air" (`HBH 116.2`) belong to their period. The Sabbath is God's gift and command. Its health benefit is real but secondary (A. T. Jones explicitly denies physical rest is its object), so do not claim longevity statistics for it.

## 2. EXERCISE

### Core EGW passages

- `MH 238.1`* - "Inactivity is a fruitful cause of disease. Exercise quickens and equalizes the circulation of the blood" - In idleness the blood does not circulate freely. The skin and lungs part of this paragraph is dated (see the note).
- `3T 78.2`* (= `CH 200.2`) - "Walking, in all cases where it is possible, is the best remedy for diseased bodies, because in this exercise all the organs of the body are brought into use." - Walking is the best exercise.
- `MH 240.2`* - "a short walk after a meal, with the head erect and the shoulders back, is a great benefit." - Exercise aids digestion, but not hard study or violent exertion right after eating.
- `MH 238.4` - "Ministers, teachers, students, and other brain workers often suffer from illness as the result of severe mental taxation, unrelieved by physical exercise." - Desk workers need "a more active life".
- `4T 264.3` (= `CH 193.2`) - "A few hours of manual labor each day would tend to renew the bodily vigor and rest and relax the mind." - Physical work rests the mind.
- `RC 147.4` (= `10MR 57.2`) - "When physical exercise is taken, the circulation is quickened." "Exercise gives new life and strength to every part of the body." - The heart and lungs work harder.
- `DG 173.5` (cf. `RC 147.6`) - "If physical exercise is regarded as drudgery, the mind takes no interest in the exercise" - Exercise works best when the mind takes part in it, joyfully.
- `MM 106.3` (from Health Reformer, May 1873) - "Manual labor quickens the circulation of the blood." "If work is performed without the heart being in it, it is simply drudgery"
- `PP 50.1`* - "God appointed labor as a blessing to man, to occupy his mind, to strengthen his body, and to develop his faculties." - Eden was given work to do: "to dress it and to keep it."
- `Ed 214.1` - "At the creation, labor was appointed as a blessing. It meant development, power, happiness." - After the curse, work remains "a source of happiness" and "a safeguard against temptation".
- `CTBH 101.2` - "More people die for want of exercise than from overwork; very many more rust out than wear out." - Good line for a hook.
- `3T 156.3` (= `CH 181.1`) - "The impression that work is degrading to fashionable life has laid thousands in the grave who might have lived." - Labor and brain work both need balance.
- `Ed 278.2` - "Outdoor exercise, especially in useful labor, is one of the best means of recreation for body and mind" - This links exercise and recreation.
- `CH 52.2` - "all who can possibly do so ought to walk in the open air every day, summer and winter." - Dress suitably. A walk does more than "all the medicine the doctors may prescribe" (period comparison; see note).
- `CH 200.1` - "should practice exercising out of doors in walking or working in the garden. They will become very much fatigued, but this will not injure them" - Start where you are.
- `DG 172.1` - "Walk with a cheerful mind. And as you walk, look at the beauties of nature" - The source pairs exercise with trust and joy. "Your sleep would be sweeter."
- `COL 345.2` - "He was the Son of the infinite God, yet He worked at the carpenter’s trade with His father Joseph." - Christ honored physical work.
- `MM 77.2` (= `CE 183.4`) - "The health cannot be preserved unless some portion of each day is given to muscular exertion in the open air." - Balance the strain on mind and body.

### Pioneer passages

- `HBH 58.2` (Loughborough, 1868) - "The muscles, to be kept healthy, should be used." Exercise moves the blood through the muscles. Open-air exercise is "imperatively necessary" for students and indoor workers, and should be taken free from care.
- `HBH 59.2` (1868) - Exercise is better endured "if it is done gradually, than if a violent exertion is made". After work comes repose.
- `HBH 202.2` (1868) - "If man takes too little voluntary exercise, he suffers; if his exercise is too excessive, he also suffers." Inactivity does more harm than excess. "Temperance in this matter".
- `HBH 155.1` (1868) - Deep-breathing and arm-swing exercises several times a day for sedentary people, "commenced carefully".
- `HHTL 302.1` (1865, a compiled article) - People are suffering for want of open-air exercise. Note: it says "walking is not sufficient exercise", which disagrees with 3T 78.2. Use it only to show that the pioneers valued whole-body exercise.
- `GCB June 4, 1913, page 271.10` - Recreation "may be found at the wood pile, in the garden, or in walking". Exercise relieves the mind and keeps the body fit.

### KJV texts

- **Genesis 2:15** (full) - "And the LORD God took the man, and put him into the garden of Eden to dress it and to keep it."
- **Genesis 3:19** (full) - "In the sweat of thy face shalt thou eat bread, till thou return unto the ground; for out of it wast thou taken: for dust thou art, and unto dust shalt thou return."
- **Ecclesiastes 5:12** (full) - "The sleep of a labouring man is sweet, whether he eat little or much: but the abundance of the rich will not suffer him to sleep."
- Proverbs 6:6, 9-11 - "Go to the ant, thou sluggard; consider her ways, and be wise:" ... "How long wilt thou sleep, O sluggard?... Yet a little sleep, a little slumber, a little folding of the hands to sleep: So shall thy poverty come..."
- 2 Thessalonians 3:10 - "...that if any would not work, neither should he eat." (Paul's point is diligence; use it lightly, not as health shaming.)
- Ecclesiastes 9:10 - "Whatsoever thy hand findeth to do, do it with thy might..."
- Isaiah 40:31 - "...they shall run, and not be weary; and they shall walk, and not faint." (This is about spiritual strength. Do not present it as an exercise text; use it only as a bridge to trust.)
- 1 Timothy 4:8 (the objection text) - "For bodily exercise profiteth little: but godliness is profitable unto all things, having promise of the life that now is, and of that which is to come." Paul ranks bodily training below godliness without forbidding it. Godliness has promise for "the life that now is" too. In the corpus EGW cites the verse against theatrical gestures in preaching (`VSS 423.1`), not against physical exercise.

### Physiology note (exercise)

These claims are plain and well established: inactivity is a major independent risk factor for heart disease, diabetes, depression and early death; exercise raises cardiac output and breathing and improves circulation and insulin sensitivity; walking is accessible and strongly protective; a 10-15 minute walk after meals lowers the rise in blood sugar after eating; hard exertion straight after a large meal can upset digestion; exercise improves mood, sleep and cognition; and fitness should be built gradually (`HBH 59.2`). "Equalizes the circulation" is fair language. The skin part of `MH 238.1`/`CTBH 101.2` (skin "inactive", "little mouths in the skin, through which the body breathes, become clogged", impurities expelled through the skin) reflects 19th-century physiology. The skin does not meaningfully breathe for the body, and sweat is a minor route of excretion, so leave it out. "Movement cure" (`3T 78.2`) was a period therapy. "Better than all the medicine" (`CH 52.2`) compares exercise with 19th-century drugging. Never present it as advice to drop a treatment. Present the "labor not gym" emphasis as "move in useful, outdoor ways", not as a ban on structured exercise.

### Physiology note (trust)

These are plain and well established: chronic stress, anxiety and unresolved guilt raise stress hormones and sympathetic arousal and affect sleep, digestion, blood pressure and immunity; social support, a sense of meaning, gratitude practices and religious coping are linked in studies (mostly observational, modest effects) to better wellbeing and recovery; and expectation shapes symptoms in both directions (placebo and nocebo; compare `MH 241.2`, "the evil effect is produced because it is expected"). Do not say "nine tenths" or "ninety per cent" as a statistic (`5T 443.4`; `BHY 218.2` attributes 90% of despondency to the stomach). They are rhetorical and of their period. Never imply that illness or depression proves weak faith, and never imply that faith replaces treatment. `MH 231.3` and `2SG 135.1` explicitly join prayer with remedies and physicians.

## 4. THEME: THE MIND AND THE BODY ("Mind Cure", MH 241-259; Ed 197; 3T/4T/5T)

### Core EGW passages

- `MH 241.1`* - "The relation that exists between the mind and the body is very intimate. When one is affected, the other sympathizes." - "Grief, anxiety, discontent, remorse, guilt, distrust, all tend to break down the life forces".
- `MH 241.3`* - "Courage, hope, faith, sympathy, love, promote health and prolong life. A contented mind, a cheerful spirit, is health to the body and strength to the soul." - Cites Prov 17:22.
- `MH 241.2` - "Disease is sometimes produced, and is often greatly aggravated, by the imagination." - "the evil effect is produced because it is expected" (the nocebo effect). Do not use the next line, "Many die from disease the cause of which is wholly imaginary", on camera.
- `Ed 197.2`* - "The influence of the mind on the body, as well as of the body on the mind, should be emphasized." - It runs both ways. It names the will, self-control, and the "life-giving power" of cheerfulness, unselfishness and gratitude. The same section cites Prov 16:24 ("health to the bones"; see `RRe 274.4`).
- `4T 60.2` (= `CH 28.2`, `CTBH 13.3`) - "If the mind is free and happy, under a consciousness of rightdoing and a sense of satisfaction in causing happiness to others, it will create a cheerfulness" - This causes "a freer circulation of the blood and a toning up of the entire body".
- `3T 183.2` (= `CH 349.3`) - "But few realize the power that the mind has over the body." "Heart sickness makes many dyspeptics, for mental trouble has a paralyzing influence upon the digestive organs." - (Same in `HL 233.2`.)
- `5T 443.4` - "Sickness of the mind prevails everywhere." - It names home trouble and "remorse for sin" as things that weaken "the life-forces". Do not quote the "nine tenths" figure.
- `MH 244.1` - "A sore, sick heart, a discouraged mind, needs mild treatment." - "It is through tender sympathy that this class of invalids can be benefited." The helper first gains confidence, then points to the Great Healer.
- `MH 257.1` - "Good deeds are twice a blessing, benefiting both the giver and the receiver of the kindness." - "The consciousness of right-doing is one of the best medicines". Serving others heals the server.
- `1T 702.1` (via `HL 232.5`) - "Nothing is so fruitful a cause of disease as depression, gloominess, and sadness." - Use it with compassion and pair it with MH 244.1. Never use it as blame.
- `2MCP 734.4` (Well-directed physical exercise) - "When invalids have nothing to occupy their time and attention, their thoughts become centered upon themselves, and they grow morbid and irritable." - The body helps the mind: useful activity lifts it.

### Physiology note (mind and body)

This is solid ground: mind and body interact through the nervous, endocrine and immune systems; chronic stress, grief and anxiety have measurable effects on the heart, gut and immunity; depression often comes with physical illness and makes it worse; the gut-brain axis is real (compare `3T 183.2`, `HBH 122.4`); expectation shapes symptoms (placebo and nocebo); kind words, social connection, purpose and serving others are linked to better health outcomes; and exercise is an established treatment support for mild-to-moderate depression (compare `2MCP 734.4`, `CH 627.3`). These are dated or need softening: "electric power of the brain... vitalizes the whole system" (`Ed 197.2`, the brain is electrochemical, but present this as a figure of speech); the "nine tenths of diseases" and "90 per cent" figures; "many die from disease wholly imaginary"; and anything that could sound like "your illness is in your head". Depression and anxiety are real illnesses. The sources themselves call for "tender sympathy" (`MH 244.1`). Treatment and counseling sit alongside faith, not against it.
