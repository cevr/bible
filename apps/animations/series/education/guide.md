# Taught of the Lord: a parents' guide

This is the core understanding behind the education series, in one place. It answers the questions parents actually ask: what education is for, who teaches first, when school should start, how to govern and correct, what to do about screens, books and friends, which school, where to live and whether to move, and how to send a young person out. Compiled 2026-10-13.

**What it is built from.** Everything the series already holds, organized by the parent's question rather than by speaker:

- Ellen G. White's education counsel, read in the local corpus (`sources.md` §2, §6, §8);
- the Bible, KJV, printed by `bible verse`;
- the three old child-training books (Abbott, _Gentle Measures_, 1871; Kellogg, _Studies in Character Building_, 1905; Byrum, _Happy Hours at Home_) and the SonLight booklets (`sources.md` §1);
- Scott Ritsema's seminar (`ritsema-review.md`, `transcripts/ritsema/`);
- Joshua White's six playlists (`white-review.md`, `transcripts/white/`);
- the 127 draft topics in `series.md`, which this guide groups into the episode sequence in `series-list.md`.

**How to read the witnesses.** Every EGW reference below was printed by `bible egw lookup` from the local corpus; every KJV reference by `bible verse`. Short phrases in quotation marks are her exact words. A reference that is a reprint is given once with its first or trade-book form (for example CG 300.3, Pacific Health Journal 1897). Where a speaker's popular use of a passage goes beyond the passage, the section says so under "Beyond the source".

**Series rules this guide keeps** (owner, 2026-10-13; `../README.md`, "Voice and audience"):

- **Audience: every Christian parent, Adventist or not.** Each principle below is written for a parent from any church. Ellen White is cited by refcode in the witnesses, as research; in a talk she is paraphrased and credited in the description. Each section ends with a line, "For a parent outside our tradition", naming what a talk must explain, bridge or leave out. Only a talk on Adventist history speaks to Adventists as insiders.
- **Persuade, by Christ's method** (MH 143.3): meet the parent's need, win their confidence, then call. Reason, feeling and evident trustworthiness together; warm, earnest language, never an insider's shorthand.
- Fiction is avoided, but allegory is allowed, as a parable carries a Bible truth (_The Pilgrim's Progress_; GC 252.2).
- Typology is fine; spiritualizing is not. A doctrine must rest on plain Scripture, and a type is its illustration, never its proof.
- Jesus is not called "the Almighty" except to clarify an Old Testament text read in the New.
- Country living is part of righteousness by faith: the life that faith works out, for the sake of the soul and the children. It is never a lifestyle add-on (§13).

## 1. What education is for

**The principle.** Every parent wants a child who will do well. God wants more for your child than that, and so, deep down, do you. Your child was made in God's image, and sin has marred it; education, in the Bible's sense, is the slow, glad work of restoring that likeness. That makes teaching a child and saving a child one work. The lessons, the chores, the corrections and the bedtime prayers all serve one end: a character fit for heaven and useful on earth. A child can be well schooled and still badly educated, and a child with few diplomas can be educated indeed. And the root of it is love: the law that asks a child to love God with heart, soul, strength and mind, and his neighbor as himself, is the law that grows every power he has, and service to others is where those powers grow best.

**Witnesses**

- **Gen 1:27; Col 3:10.** Made in His image; "renewed in knowledge after the image of him that created him".
- **PP 595.2** (= CE 63.3): "The true object of education is to restore the image of God in the soul."
- **Ed 30.2:** "In the highest sense the work of education and the work of redemption are one." **DA 330.2:** "Redemption is that process by which the soul is trained for heaven."
- **Ed 13.1:** it "has to do with the whole being, and with the whole period of existence possible to man"; "the harmonious development of the physical, the mental, and the spiritual powers". **COL 332.3:** a character "formed according to the divine likeness is the only treasure that we can take from this world to the next."
- **Ed 16.1–16.2** (Luke 10:27; Matt 22:39): "Love, the basis of creation and of redemption, is the basis of true education"; "Through unselfish service we receive the highest culture of every faculty … We are fitted for heaven, for we receive heaven into our hearts." **CT 505.3:** "The Christian aims to reach the highest attainments for the purpose of doing others good." **Ed 309.1:** "the greatest joy and the highest education are in service." **COL 330.3:** "the training of body, mind, and soul for divine service."

**Practical counsel**

- Judge every plan (a school, a schedule, a move, an activity) by one question: does it help form the character God wants in this child?
- Knowing God is the core subject (John 17:3; COL 114.2), and thinking is the core skill: "thinkers, and not mere reflectors of other men's thought" (Ed 17.2). Memory without thought leaves a child "an easy prey to deception" (Ed 230.3).
- Teach dependence. An education that hides the child's need of God hides the Healer (White's frame; Prov 3:5–7; Jer 17:5, 7).
- Learning is meant to be used. Years of "you'll use it someday" train a hearer, not a doer (James 1:22; MH 449.5 = CT 387.4).

**Beyond the source / disagreements**

- Ritsema and White both cite CT 13.1 as "without God you lose eternal life". The paragraph's stronger line is real ("it is certain that man will lose eternal life", CT 13.1) but it is about the knowledge of God, not about which school.
- "Truth not lived is not truth" is White's own line, not a quotation. "The very foundation of true education is in the fear of the Lord" compresses two sentences of a manuscript (Ms 25, 1898); the citable form is CT 505.2: "Piety and religious experience lie at the very foundation of true education" (`transcripts/white/verification.md`).
- Do children need to meet evil to resist it? The source says the knowledge of evil "was in love withheld" (Ed 23.2), and mingling evil with good confused the mind (Ed 25.3). Paul agrees: "simple concerning evil" (Rom 16:19). This is protection, not ignorance of danger.

**For a parent outside our tradition.** The aim itself is common Christian ground (Col 3:10; Rom 8:29), so start there, from Scripture. Name Ellen White only in the credits. Avoid "true education" as a brand name; say "education as the Bible sees it".

**Draft topics:** 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 88, 89, 90, 106, 109, and new nos. 124, 126.

## 2. The home is the first school, and the parents are its first students

**The principle.** You are your child's first and most lasting teacher, whatever school they attend. From the beginning God's plan was a family: a home was the first school, and fathers and mothers were its teachers. Every family is still a home school, at least after the school bell. And here is the hopeful part: before God asked Israel's parents to teach, He taught the parents. Moses' charge has two steps. First, "these words … shall be in thine heart"; then "thou shalt teach them diligently unto thy children", sitting, walking, lying down and rising up (Deut 6:6–7). Israel kept the information and lost that way of passing it on, and within one generation the children "knew not the LORD, nor yet the works" (Judg 2:10). The faith of a child is mostly caught from a parent who has it.

**Witnesses**

- **Deut 6:6–7:** "these words … shall be in thine heart: And thou shalt teach them diligently unto thy children, and shalt talk of them when thou sittest in thine house, and when thou walkest by the way, and when thou liest down, and when thou risest up."
- **Ed 34.1:** "The parents themselves needed instruction and discipline." **Ed 276.3:** education will never do all it should "until the importance of the parents' work is fully recognized, and they receive a training for its sacred responsibilities."
- **CT 107.1:** the family is "the greatest of all educational agencies … Here is his first school." **PP 592.2:** teaching the children was a duty "not to be delegated to another".
- **Ed 187.1:** "Our instruction to them will have only the weight of influence given it by our own example and spirit." **PK 245.1:** the captive girl in Naaman's house is "a strong witness to the power of early home training."
- **FE 95.3:** in Israel "The home and the school were one. In the place of stranger lips, the loving hearts of the father and mother were to give instruction." **ST March 16, 1891, par. 1:** "The king upon his throne has no higher work than has the mother." **Luke 1:17:** the work before the Lord comes is "to turn the hearts of the fathers to the children".

**Practical counsel**

- Start with yourself: your own Bible study, your own self-control, your own prayer. The child learns what the parent is before what the parent says (Kellogg pp. 6–12; FE 58.2 for teachers).
- Teach in the flow of the day, not only in a lesson: at the table, on the road, at bedtime, at work (Deut 6:7; PP 592.2, "associated with all the events of daily life").
- Tell the family's story with God, and answer when the child asks "What mean ye?" (Ex 13:14; Deut 6:20–21; Ps 78:4–7). God's lessons came "as inquiry was made" (Ed 41.2).
- Relationship carries the rules. Children who lack sympathy at home "will seek it from sources that may endanger" (FE 156.1); be at times "a child among children" (FE 18.2 = 3T 134.2). Live _with_ your children, not just _for_ them (Kellogg p. 14).
- Your own voice, not a recording. A parent reading, singing and talking with a child does what no screen or audio Bible can (White, E2).
- A mother's home is not a lesser field: "You may have at home a little school, of which you shall be the teacher … He will lead you, not away from your own home, but back to it" (Lt 28, 1890, par. 11).
- A household where the children are actually needed, and that builds together, is ready together (Noah's sons, Heb 11:7; PP 95.1, 98.1; White's frame).

**Beyond the source / disagreements**

- Schools came later, "as an aid to parents" where homes were failing (Ed 46.1). Ritsema reads that as "schools were never God's plan"; the text says they were His provision to meet "this growing evil". Both are true: the first plan was the home, and the school is a God-given helper, not a replacement.
- **AH 246.1** (Lt 28, 1890) was written to one mother being drawn into church work away from her little children. White (F5) widens it to all employment and adds animal-separation studies about working mothers; both go beyond the source and would wound many parents. Speak the principle (the children come first), never a verdict on a family's work.
- The GCDB 1899 line "not only Adam's dwelling, but his schoolroom" is filed as EGW but its reprint is signed "—P.C." Use Ed 20.1.

**For a parent outside our tradition.** Deut 6 and Judg 2 carry the whole argument; no other authority is needed. "The Deuteronomy method" is White's label: credit it, or just say "Moses' two steps". Don't present home education as a denominational program; present the parent as teacher in every kind of family.

**Draft topics:** 7, 9, 12, 13, 14, 79, 92, 93, 94, 97, 98, 18, 26, and new nos. 123, 127.

## 3. The early years and when to start school

**The principle.** Earlier is not always better. The first years lay the foundation for a sound body and a sound character, and books can wait for both. An old and wise counsel puts it simply: until about eight or ten, the garden is the best schoolroom, the mother the best teacher and nature the best lesson book. The point is not just to delay school. It is to spend those golden years on what is hardest to lay later: obedience, self-control, usefulness, wonder, and a child's first knowledge of God. Hannah had Samuel for three years; Moses' mother had him for perhaps twelve. What they sowed then held for life.

**Witnesses**

- **Ed 208.1:** "For the first eight or ten years of a child's life the field or garden is the best schoolroom, the mother the best teacher, nature the best lesson book." **CT 80.1:** "The only schoolroom for children until eight or ten years of age should be in the open air."
- **CG 300.3–300.4:** for the first six or seven years attention goes to physical training "rather than the intellect"; parents "should not educate from books" but answer the child's questions about nature.
- **Ed 107.2:** "Children should not be forced into a precocious maturity." **Eccl 3:1; Mark 4:28** ("first the blade, then the ear").
- **CG 197.1; PP 244.1:** Hannah's first three years with Samuel, Jochebed's twelve with Moses. "The lessons learned at his mother's side could not be forgotten."

**Practical counsel**

- Outdoors, every day; real work alongside you; plain answers to every "why" (CG 119.3; Abbott ch. 19). Don't rush a child into books (Kellogg p. 166).
- Lay the habits now: obedience, truthfulness, gratitude, finishing what is started (§5–§7). "If you only had twelve years…" (White's hook, PP 243.4–244.1) is the right urgency.
- A restless child is usually an active child with nothing useful to do. Channel the energy; don't dam it (Abbott ch. 14; Kellogg pp. 165–176).
- Keep a little child's life quiet and simple: "the more free from artificial excitement … the more favorable" (DA 74.3; Ed 107.2).

**Beyond the source / disagreements**

- **"Eight for girls, ten for boys"** (White) is not in the text. It says "eight or ten".
- **FE 21.2 is from 1872** (= 3T 137.2), not late counsel.
- **"Through seven"** (White, E2) is not in CG 300.3; it says "six or seven". White's rule that CG 103.5 names every area "except early childhood, so academics can come later" (F5) is his own reading.
- **CT 80.3** ("You may bend a young tree … It will ever be a crooked tree") is about disposition and habits. White (F2) applies it to forcing early academics; use it for habits, as written.
- **The 1904 exception** (3SM 214.5–218.2): where homes were not training children, the counsel wanted a lower department for children of seven to nine, for order and obedience rather than academics. The ideal stays the home; the exception shows the ideal is about the child's good, not a rule for its own sake.
- **Brain science** (myelination, myopia, reading at five vs seven) is used by both speakers as proof of the counsel. Present the counsel as counsel; the studies are leads, never the ground (`sources.md` §8).

**For a parent outside our tradition.** The start age comes from Ellen White, not from a Bible verse, so present it honestly as wise counsel that many families have proved, with Scripture's principle beside it (Eccl 3:1; Mark 4:28; Luke 2:40, 52). Many parents meet it as a shock; meet the worry about "falling behind" first, with Jesus' own quiet years (Luke 2:51–52).

**Draft topics:** 10, 11, 15, 16, 35, 54, 70, 105.

## 4. The Bible as the textbook, and how a family studies it

**The principle.** The Bible is the best schoolbook a child will ever have, and God's creation is the second. Not the only books, but the first: the Bible is the textbook of principles that every other subject is measured by, and because its themes are the highest, it stretches the mind more than any other study. A child who learns to dig in it for himself, asking "How readest thou?" (Luke 10:26), learns to think. And family worship should be the sweetest part of the day, something the children look forward to, not a duty they endure.

**Witnesses**

- **CT 108.3:** "true education is religion. The Bible should be the child's first textbook." **MH 401.4:** "The Bible should be made the foundation of study and of teaching."
- **Ed 124.2:** "As a means of intellectual training, the Bible is more effective than any other book, or all other books combined." **Ps 119:99–100, 130.**
- **Ed 189.1:** search "not for proof to sustain our opinions, but in order to know what God says." **Acts 17:11; Luke 10:26** ("How readest thou?").
- **Ed 186.2:** the hours of worship "should be the sweetest and most helpful of the day." **CG 521.4:** "make the hour of worship intensely interesting."

**Practical counsel**

- Study one word or one passage at a time with a concordance and a notebook, verse by verse, "as the miner digs" (Ed 189.2–189.4; White's "one word, twenty minutes").
- Worship: a few verses, one question, a story, a song, a short prayer, brief and varied (Ed 186.2; CG 521.4; White's pattern). Let the children tell what they found (CSW 115.2). Aim for interesting, not entertaining.
- Keep a Bible within reach for spare minutes (Ed 191.4). Give God the first thought of the morning (OHC 116.2; Ps 5:3).
- Make the Sabbath a delight (Isa 58:13; 6T 359.2): outdoors, together, with the best food and the best stories of the week.
- Teach nature from nature, not from a book about nature (Ed 100.2–101.1; CT 185.1).
- Old school readers set a higher bar than many modern ones (Ritsema's lead; needs a primary source before it is spoken).

**Beyond the source / disagreements**

- "The Bible only" is not the counsel. A. T. Jones's _Place of the Bible in Education_ (PBE 150.1–2) says the Bible is the textbook of principles, and the schools of the prophets also taught music, history, mathematics and trades (PBE 83–88; Ed 46–47).
- White's claim that the Bible gives "not a single example" of age-separated teaching overstates: the assemblies did include the little ones (Deut 31:12–13; Josh 8:35; 2 Chr 20:13; Joel 2:16), but Neh 8:2 has "all that could hear with understanding".

**For a parent outside our tradition.** This is shared Protestant ground (2 Tim 3:15–17; Acts 17:11). The Sabbath delight (no. 75) needs its text, Isa 58:13 and Ex 20:8–11, stated plainly and gently; most viewers keep Sunday, so teach the day from Scripture without church history, and invite rather than accuse.

**Draft topics:** 29, 39, 40, 41, 75, 83, 84, 101, 102, 103, 104, 111, 113, 114.

## 5. Authority: governing by love

**The principle.** Children feel safest under authority that is settled and kind. Not tricks, not bargaining, not shouting: a few clear words, said calmly and kept every time. Gentleness does not weaken a parent's authority; it is the surest way to build it. The Bible warns against both ditches. Eli was a kind man who never said no, and he lost his sons (1 Sam 3:13); and fathers are told not to provoke their children, "lest they be discouraged" (Col 3:21). God governs us by love, and He asks parents to govern the same way.

**Witnesses**

- **Gen 18:19:** Abraham "will command his children and his household after him". **Eph 6:4; Col 3:21:** "provoke not your children to wrath … lest they be discouraged."
- **PP 579.1:** "There is no greater curse upon households than to allow the youth to have their own way." (Eli, 1 Sam 3:13.)
- **CG 175.2:** many err on the side of indulgence; "others go to the opposite extreme and rule their children with a rod of iron. Neither of these follow out the Bible directions."
- **7T 47.4–48.1:** when the mother's will meets the child's, "by stern compulsion, great harm may be done"; she gives "no loud-voiced commands", "God is her Helper; love, her power."

**Practical counsel**

- Few commands, a quiet voice, and always follow through (Kellogg pp. 39–40; Abbott ch. 4). Never let disobedience pay, and make no empty threats.
- Certainty, not severity: a small consequence every time beats a large one sometimes (Abbott pp. 21–23, 33–34; Eccl 8:11).
- When you say no, mean it: a request "firmly denied" so as to remove all hope stops the teasing (CG 283.4). Hear the request first, then decide (Abbott ch. 18).
- Give reasons at another time, never as the price of obeying (Abbott pp. 11, 43–44). As the child grows, enlist his reason "on the side of obedience" (Ed 287.2), and request rather than command where you can (Ed 290.1).
- Train obedience like a skill, with many small, pleasant commands (Abbott ch. 7). Be generous with every harmless wish ("Yes, and let me help"; Abbott pp. 125–130).

**Beyond the source / disagreements**

- Abbott's "kind, settled authority" and the counsel agree closely. Abbott's theology (ch. 16, 21, 23) does not, and stays out.
- Isaac's obedience at Moriah was "trusting", not blind (PP 152.2): that is the obedience to aim for.

**For a parent outside our tradition.** Common ground with any Christian parent; Abbott (a Congregationalist) is the main practical voice here. No insider terms needed.

**Draft topics:** 48, 49, 52, 55, 108.

## 6. Correction: how and when

**The principle.** Correction is not for the parent's relief; it is for the child's turning. The goal is a child who sees the wrong himself and wants to make it right, because only then has the correction done its work. So correct calmly, privately, and soon, and always leave the way home open. And before you deal with the child, settle the matter with God and with yourself: an angry parent teaches anger, whatever words he uses (Jas 1:20).

**Witnesses**

- **Heb 12:11:** chastening "yieldeth the peaceable fruit of righteousness unto them which are exercised thereby." **Jas 1:19–20:** "the wrath of man worketh not the righteousness of God."
- **CG 246.1:** "The parent who, when correcting a child, gives way to anger is more at fault than the child." **CCh 197.3:** "Do not let them have their own way until you get angry, and then punish them."
- **Ed 287.2:** "The object of discipline is the training of the child for self-government." **CG 223.2** (= Ed 291): "The true object of reproof is gained only when the wrongdoer himself is led to see his fault and his will is enlisted for its correction."
- **CG 251.2:** before any correction, "defer any settlement of the difficulty until you have settled the case with yourselves. Ask yourself, Have I submitted my way and will to God?"

**Practical counsel**

- Wait until you are calm (Abbott pp. 30, 34; Kellogg pp. 93–95). If you are angry, the correction is about you.
- Correct in private, never before others (Kellogg pp. 85–87; Byrum p. 51).
- Separate the deed from the child, and restore the child quickly (Kellogg pp. 28, 105).
- Instruct before you punish: many faults are immaturity (Abbott ch. 13). Punish disobedience, not the accident.
- Correct without a lecture: a story at bedtime, advice moved into the future, a game (Abbott pp. 4–9, 53–60).
- Lying: under about five, fact and fancy blur; train accuracy, find the cause, keep your own promises (Kellogg pp. 74–83; Abbott ch. 16; CG 151.3).
- Natural consequences teach best, and the child can help design his own correction (Abbott pp. 34–39; Kellogg pp. 96–99).
- Notice nineteen good acts before the twentieth bad one (Abbott p. 77).

**Beyond the source / disagreements**

- **The rod.** The counsel allows it as a last resort, never in anger: "Whipping may be necessary when other resorts fail, yet she should not use the rod if it is possible to avoid doing so" (CG 250.2); "not in passion, but with love and prayer" (CG 276.1). Abbott reads the rod as an emblem (ch. 21), Kellogg says "last resort, if at all" (pp. 103–104), and Byrum whips a two-year-old to "conquer" his will (pp. 51–53). The films leave this alone (`series.md` §5); the guide states the counsel as written.
- Byrum's whipping and the 1905 methods (tying, taping, locking up, Kellogg pp. 45, 97, 99–100) are not counsel to follow.

**For a parent outside our tradition.** Common ground. The rod is where Christian parents divide most sharply; a talk that took it up would need to meet both sides with care, which is why the films leave it alone.

**Draft topics:** 46, 50, 51, 53, 56, 63, 64.

## 7. The will and character

**The principle.** Your strong-willed child is not a mistake. The will is the power to choose, and it is God's gift; one day your child will need every ounce of it to say no to wrong when you are not there. So the parent's work is not to break the will but to win it, until the child freely places it on God's side (Josh 24:15). Character is built the same way, from small habits: faults that begin small grow into giants unless they are named early, fought in God's strength, and crowded out with something better.

**Witnesses**

- **Ed 288.1–288.2:** "The effort to 'break the will' of a child is a terrible mistake." "The will should be guided and molded, but not ignored or crushed. Save the strength of the will; in the battle of life it will be needed." **Ed 289.1:** "The will is the governing power in the nature of man … Everyone may place his will on the side of the will of God." (Josh 24:15.)
- **MH 396.2:** "God's word does not repress activity, but guides it aright."
- **CT 61.2:** "The great work of parents and teachers is character building." **Rom 12:21; Song 2:15** (the little foxes).
- **PP 53.2:** had Adam and Eve "steadfastly repelled his first insinuations", they would have been safe: never yield the first time.

**Practical counsel**

- Put good in the place of evil: "Do" instead of "Don't", "something better" instead of a bare refusal (Kellogg pp. 22–28; Ed 296.6).
- Teach the child to recognize his own faults by name and to fight them in God's strength. The knight-and-giants allegory (Byrum, after A.L.O.E.) does this for children, framed openly as an allegory (§10).
- A strong-willed child is not a problem to crush but a strength to direct (Kellogg pp. 44–46; MH 396.2). "Every youth needs to cultivate decision"; shun "Pliable" (ML 73.4).
- Truthfulness, gratitude, courage (Caleb's "other spirit", Num 14:24) are trained in small things first (Byrum; Kellogg ch. 9; Abbott ch. 22).
- The foundation nobody sees matters more than the grades and recitals everyone sees (Luke 6:48–49; White's parable).

**Beyond the source / disagreements**

- The counsel also says obedience is to be taught "in babyhood" (CG 82.1–82.3). That is not breaking the will; it is training it early, gently (7T 47.4–48.1).
- Byrum's theology implies final victory over sin by the will alone (pp. 4, 48). The guide's ground is the will placed "on the side of the will of God" (Ed 289.1).

**For a parent outside our tradition.** Some Christian parenting books teach "breaking the will". Meet that parent's real concern (a defiant child) before showing a better way, and ground it in Scripture (Prov 16:32; Josh 24:15) before any other writer. The knight allegory must be named an allegory.

**Draft topics:** 57, 58, 59, 60, 61, 62, 65, 66, 67, 109, 110.

## 8. Work, money and health

**The principle.** Work is not the curse. God gave Adam a garden "to dress it and to keep it" before sin ever entered (Gen 2:15), and the Son of God spent most of His years at a carpenter's bench (Mark 6:3). Useful work is part of a child's education, not a break from it: it builds the body, steadies the mind and trains the character. Bread before algebra; a trade before a title; accounts before advanced mathematics. And health is the ground everything else stands on.

**Witnesses**

- **Ed 215.2:** "life means earnest work, responsibility, care-taking." **CT 307.2:** every youth should learn "some branch of manual labor by which, if need be, he may obtain a livelihood."
- **CT 289.2:** "If one must be neglected, let it be the study of books." **Ed 216.1:** bread before French and algebra; boys keep house, girls use tools (Ed 216.3).
- **Ed 238.6:** let every child "keep an accurate account of his own income and outgoes."
- **Ed 207.2:** "The whole body is designed for action." **Ed 219.1:** "No line of manual training is of more value than agriculture." **Ed 195.1:** "both mental and spiritual vigor are in great degree dependent upon physical strength and activity." **Dan 1:8, 12, 15** (the ten-day test).

**Practical counsel**

- Build a household in which the children are actually needed (White's line; Lam 3:27; Kellogg pp. 171–176).
- Every child learns a trade (Acts 18:3; Paul the tentmaker). Manual work is not beneath the clever child (Mark 6:3).
- Prefer useful work and outdoor recreation to games and sports pursued for their own sake (Ed 210.2; CT 274.3; FE 418.3).
- Money: a fixed allowance, outside earning, accounts kept; don't pay for ordinary chores (Kellogg pp. 109–111; Abbott ch. 20).
- Keep the series to Scripture's account on diet; no health claims in speech.

**Beyond the source / disagreements**

- AH 90.2 is often quoted for chores; it was written to a young woman preparing for marriage.
- Ritsema and White both make health "the first study" (Ed 195). The series avoids health claims and keeps health as the base, not a separate program.

**For a parent outside our tradition.** Common ground. Avoid "health reform" and diet doctrine; the ten-day test is told as Scripture tells it.

**Draft topics:** 8, 20, 25, 27, 30, 31, 32, 33, 34, 36, 37, 38, 81, 82.

## 9. Motives: praise, grades, display and service

**The principle.** Most of what drives children at school is the wish to beat someone: the grade, the prize, the ranking. Jesus turned that upside down: "whosoever will be great among you, shall be your minister" (Mark 10:43). Teach your children to work for God's approval rather than the crowd's, to help the slower one rather than outshine him, and to serve real people now, alongside you, without being put on show.

**Witnesses**

- **Ed 225.4:** "To what motive is appeal most often made? To self-seeking." **2 Cor 10:12; Mark 10:43–44.**
- **Ed 285.2:** "Let the older assist the younger, the strong the weak."
- **Ed 237.3:** "It is not wise to give them special notice, or to repeat before them their clever sayings." **DA 74.3:** "Jesus shunned display."
- **Ed 268.3:** "It is in the water, not on the land, that men learn to swim." **Ev 109.1:** young workers go "in company with a worker of experience." **Ed 269.2:** "It is acquaintance that awakens sympathy."
- **MH 401.3:** "service for Christ's sake in the little things of everyday experience has power to mold the character … To awaken this spirit … is the parents' and the teacher's work."

**Practical counsel**

- Praise the effort and the character, not the cleverness; never compare siblings.
- Use cooperation at home: older children teach younger ones (Ed 285.2).
- Rewards are thanks afterwards, never a price beforehand (Abbott pp. 40–42; Kellogg pp. 107–109).
- Let children serve real people now, with you, before they are "ready" (Mark 6:7; Luke 10:1). Don't only teach them how to share their faith; give them someone to share it with (White, D3).
- When a young person gives his heart to God, give him a task: "Lay some special responsibility upon them. Make them feel that they are expected to do something" (CE 223.1 = GW 278–279).
- Begin in little things: a neighbor's errand, a younger child taught, a sick friend visited (MH 401.3).

**Beyond the source / disagreements**

- **CT 177.1's "grading"** means class levels, not marks, and the published text says "sometimes a hindrance". Ritsema reads the stronger manuscript (2MR 215.3). Quote the published form.
- **"No junior Levites"** (White) overstates: Samuel "ministered before the LORD, being a child" (1 Sam 2:18). The reconciliation is Ev 109.1: serve now, alongside, not on show.
- The _Credits_ booklet's "papal degrees" claim is polemic; leave it out.

**For a parent outside our tradition.** Common ground. Speak to parents whose children are in graded schools without asking them to leave; the principle (motive) applies inside any school.

**Draft topics:** 21, 42, 43, 44, 45, 46, 85, 86, 105, 107, and new no. 125.

## 10. Screens, books and stories

**The principle.** We become like what we look at. Paul says that by beholding the glory of the Lord we "are changed into the same image" (2 Cor 3:18), and the law works the other way too: what a child takes in, day after day, slowly becomes what he loves. So guard the doors of the mind, but above all fill the house with something better: your company, real work, the outdoors, good books, the Bible read aloud. Stories written only to excite are best left alone; but a story told openly as an allegory, the way Jesus told parables and Bunyan wrote _The Pilgrim's Progress_, can carry Bible truth to a child's heart.

**Witnesses**

- **Phil 4:8; Ps 101:3:** "I will set no wicked thing before mine eyes."
- **GC 555.1:** "The mind gradually adapts itself to the subjects upon which it is allowed to dwell."
- **CT 134.2:** "love stories and frivolous, exciting tales … are a curse to everyone who reads them, even though the author may attach a good moral." **MH 446.1:** fiction "creates a distaste for life's practical duties."
- **GC 252.2:** _The Pilgrim's Progress_ is Bunyan's "wonderful allegory", which has "guided many feet into the path of life."

**Practical counsel**

- Take away, then replace with yourself: your time, real work, the outdoors, good books, the Bible read aloud (Ed 296.6; _Better Readers_ ways 1–9).
- Fill the soil before the weeds: "The best way to prevent the growth of evil is to preoccupy the soil" (CT 136.2).
- Keep screens out of the early years and out of the evenings; hold the evenings at home (Byrum pp. 10–11, 90–92; Kellogg pp. 125–126).
- Stories: true history, Bible biography, and openly framed allegory. Ask of any story what it makes the child love.
- Even "Babylon" can come into a country home in a pocket (White's phone illustration). A screen is a tool, not a toy; whatever is that hard to put down needs firm limits, and the question to ask is whether what holds the child's attention is good for him (White, E3, F3).

**Beyond the source / disagreements**

- **Ritsema and White on fiction.** Ritsema argues "total abstinence"; White extends "light reading" even to true stories read for entertainment (A7). The owner's rule (2026-10-13): fiction is avoided, allegory allowed. The line in the source is the kind of story, not a moral attached (CT 134.2; CT 384.1 names "fairy tales, myths, and fictitious stories").
- The C8 imaginative Jochebed story, the "Evangelist Anderson" skit and the Animal School fable are fiction. Use the fable only as an openly framed allegory.
- None of the speakers' media statistics has a primary source. The series speaks the principle, not the numbers.

**For a parent outside our tradition.** The fiction counsel will surprise many Christian parents who love C. S. Lewis and Narnia. Meet that honestly: give the principle (what a story makes the child love) and the allegory exception before the restriction, and don't name or condemn popular books.

**Draft topics:** 34, 41, 57, 65, 68, 69, 72, 111.

## 11. Companions and influence

**The principle.** Children become like the people they spend their days with. Solomon said it in one line: "He that walketh with wise men shall be wise" (Prov 13:20). The pull of friends is never stronger than in the school years, and that, more than any subject, is the real question behind "socialization" and school choice. The healthiest social school is a mixed-age one: family, church, neighbors and work, where the older help the younger.

**Witnesses**

- **Prov 13:20:** "He that walketh with wise men shall be wise: but a companion of fools shall be destroyed." **1 Cor 15:33:** "evil communications corrupt good manners."
- **FE 297.1** (above).
- **CT 173.1:** the danger in the public school is association with children left "to obtain a street education".
- **PP 168.1:** Lot "permitted his children to mingle with a corrupt and idolatrous people".

**Practical counsel**

- Children learn to be adults from adults: give them more hours with grown-ups who love God than with a crowd of one age (White, E and F1; Ed 285.2).
- Know your children's friends and their friends' homes; make your home the place they want to be (Byrum pp. 8–13, 90–92).
- Keep your children's confidence early: trust started young is a barrier against evil (Kellogg pp. 14–20).
- Mixed-age company (family, church, neighbors, work) is the normal social school (Ed 85.3; Ed 285.2; Titus 2:3–4).

**Beyond the source / disagreements**

- "Socialization is a lie" (White) and Ritsema's homeschool percentiles are not series register; the percentiles need a primary source (Shyers 1992 is a checkable lead).
- CT 332.1 ("crowds of one age") is about large boarding schools.

**For a parent outside our tradition.** Common ground. Avoid making it homeschool against school; every parent can apply it.

**Draft topics:** 71, 72, 87, 93.

## 12. Public school, church school, home school

**The principle.** The question is not first "which building?" but "which place will best form the character God wants in this child?" Public, private, church or home: each family weighs the same things, the companions, the teachers' own lives, the motives the school appeals to, the time it leaves for home and work, and whether God's word is honored there. The early years belong at home. After that, the wisest writers on Christian education urge a school built on Scripture wherever families can make one, and families uniting to make one where none exists. But no school replaces the parent, and moving the same worksheets to the kitchen table only changes the location. Method matters more than setting.

**What the counsel actually says**

- **CT 205.1:** "parents should realize that it is no longer safe to send them to the public school, **and should endeavor to send them to schools where they will obtain an education based on a Scriptural foundation**." The alternative it names is a Christian school.
- **CG 305.1** (Ms 100, 1902): some "permit their children to attend the public schools, where they mingle with those who are corrupt in morals … Christian parents, you must make provision for your children to be educated in Bible principles." The fuller form of the same manuscript goes on (3SM 211.3): "do not rest satisfied merely with having them study the Word in the church school. Teach the Scriptures to your children yourselves."
- **CG 307.5:** "As far as possible, all our children should have the privilege of a Christian education. To provide this we must sometimes establish home church schools": several families uniting to employ "a humble, God-fearing teacher".
- **6T 199.1:** where the law compels school attendance, a church should start a school "if there are no more than six children".
- **6MR 369.3** (a 1904 interview report): "If they prefer to send their children to the public school, let them send them. But these many studies is a great fallacy." Said in a church-school setting, of parents comparing the two.
- **3SM 214.5–217.3** (1904): home until "eight, or nine, or ten" if mothers can teach; otherwise a lower department for the seven-to-nine-year-olds whose homes are not training them.
- **CT 162.3:** "Neither the church school nor the college affords the opportunities for establishing a child's character building upon the right foundation that are afforded in the home." **CT 158.2:** for young children "the well-ordered Christian home … is the best place for them." **CT 167.1:** the church school is "an aid to the parents"; **CT 161.1:** "Let fathers and mothers co-operate with the teacher."
- **Scripture:** the commands go to the home (Deut 6:6–7; Eph 6:4). And Scripture's own examples include youth schooled by pagans who stayed true, because the heart was settled at home: Moses ("learned in all the wisdom of the Egyptians", Acts 7:22), Daniel (Dan 1:8, 17, 20), Joseph (Gen 39:2–4).

**Practical counsel**

- Whatever the school, you are the first teacher. Teach the Scriptures yourself (CG 305.1's context; CT 107.1).
- Choose by character: the companions, the teachers' own lives (FE 58.2), the motives appealed to (§9), the time left for home, work and nature, and the number of studies ("cut off some of those studies", 6MR 372.3–372.4; 6T 130.1).
- If your child is in a school, treat it as help, not a handover: know the teachers, work with them, and keep the home's own teaching running (CT 161.1; CT 167.1). White's picture (D2): the school is a push from behind; the family is the engine.
- If no Christian school exists, families can unite for one (CG 307.5; 6T 199.1).
- Before twelve, a child's home and heart decide more than the school; Daniel's training came "before the scenes of the captivity" (FE 96.1; PK 428.1).

**Quotes cut short** (verified, but used against their own context):

- **CT 205.1** is read without its second half ("schools … Scriptural foundation") to make a home-only case.
- **CG 304.3 + 305.1 + 315.1** (Ms 100, 1902) are spliced as one quotation; the address was urging a church school.
- **The "danger signal"** (Ms 155, 1903 + Lt 70, 1906, spliced) is about the Battle Creek Sanitarium, not a school.
- **CG 307.5** is misquoted as "all our children should have the privilege of one of these schools of the prophets"; it says "As far as possible … a Christian education".
- **FE 312.2–313.1** ("All this is a false education") is about siting a school away from a city's holiday amusements in 1894.
- **6T 208.1** ("as homelike as possible") is about thrift and plain buildings.

**Where the sources disagree**

- **Ritsema:** public school is "insane"; opt out, don't reform; the system was "conceived in iniquity". **White:** milder in his own words ("only changed the location"; the 1904 interview), but A8 and C14 use "send our children to Caesar" and the "insanity" line. **The series:** principles for every family, no verdict on families. His own balance lines are worth keeping: "we can't control the way schools run, but we can control the way our family runs" (F1); "don't point fingers … start within our own homes" (E3). His condemning lines ("Babylonian schools, pretty much all"; universities "a dangerous place") stay out. His earliest recorded series (D2, 2018) is the balanced one: the home is God's first plan and the school a merciful second, "an aid to parents" (Ed 46.1). His sanctuary analogy ("in pity for their weakness", Ed 35.1) is his own; the paragraph is about the tabernacle, not schools.
- **Daniel 1:** an indoctrination camp to opt out of (Ritsema; White in C12), or a test passed inside Babylon's school by youth trained at home (the series, Haskell, White in A6/B2)? Both halves are in the text (Dan 1:4–7 the program; 1:8, 17–20 the four inside it). Haskell himself allows youth "rooted and grounded in the Scriptures, to enter worldly universities" and come forth "unsullied" (SDP 26.2). The series' reading stands.
- **History** of compulsory schooling (Mann, Dewey, the General Education Board) is a lead only, and several of Ritsema's facts are wrong (`ritsema-review.md` §4.6).

**For a parent outside our tradition.** "Church school" here means a school a church runs, not Sunday school; say "a Christian school". Most of the counsel's school passages were written for Adventist church schools and institutions (Battle Creek and others): use the principle, never the institutional history, and never shame a family's choice. Daniel, Moses and Joseph carry the case for every Christian.

**Draft topics:** 17, 19, 22, 23, 24, 28, 73, 78, 80, 91, 96, 100, 112, and new no. 120.

## 13. Where to live: country living as the life of faith

**The principle.** Where we live is not a neutral choice, and for a family of faith it is not finally a financial one. Righteousness is God's gift, received by faith alone. But living faith always moves: it takes God at His word and acts on it. "By faith Abraham, when he was called to go out … obeyed" (Heb 11:8); "by faith Noah … prepared an ark to the saving of his house … and became heir of the righteousness which is by faith" (Heb 11:7). The owner holds country living to be part of righteousness by faith in exactly this sense, and the sources bear it out: it is the life that faith works out, for the sake of the soul and the children. Lot chose his home by what his eyes saw and his purse wanted, and it cost him his family; Abraham chose by what God said, and his children kept the faith. God's first home for His children was a garden, and when His own Son came, the Father chose for Him a quiet home in the hills. So the question "where should we live?" is a question of trust: whom do we trust with our children, and what will we let decide? The move earns nothing. It is what faith does when it believes God about our children.

The owner's frame rests on these texts and paragraphs. "The faith that justifies always produces first true repentance, and then good works, which are the fruit of that faith" (3SM 195.2). And of Abraham: "When God told him to offer his son as a sacrifice it was the same voice that had spoken telling him to leave his country … Abraham was saved by faith in Christ as verily as the sinner is saved by faith in Christ today" (3SM 195.1). "The gospel is a wonderful simplifier of life's problems … This lesson is needed by those upon whom rests the responsibility of selecting a home" (MH 363.1).

**The ground in Scripture** (plain narrative and didactic texts; no doctrine rests on a type):

- **Eden.** "The LORD God planted a garden eastward in Eden; and there he put the man" (Gen 2:8), "to dress it and to keep it" (2:15). The first home was a garden, and the first school was in it (Ed 20.1). The first city was built by Cain, who "went out from the presence of the LORD" (Gen 4:16–17).
- **Abraham.** "Get thee out of thy country" (Gen 12:1). "By faith Abraham, when he was called to go out … obeyed; and he went out, not knowing whither he went. By faith he sojourned in the land of promise, as in a strange country, dwelling in tabernacles … For he looked for a city which hath foundations" (Heb 11:8–10). "Abraham believed God, and it was imputed unto him for righteousness" (Jas 2:23), and "by works was faith made perfect" (Jas 2:22).
- **Lot.** Lot chose by sight: he "lifted up his eyes, and beheld all the plain of Jordan, that it was well watered … Lot dwelled in the cities of the plain, and pitched his tent toward Sodom" (Gen 13:10, 12). He stayed "just" (2 Pet 2:7–8), but "while he lingered" the angels had to take him by the hand (Gen 19:16), and his wife looked back (Gen 19:26). "Remember Lot's wife" (Luke 17:32).

**Witnesses** (EGW)

- **PP 169.1:** "In choosing a home, God would have us consider, first of all, the moral and religious influences that will surround us and our families." **PP 168.4:** many "look more to the temporal advantages they may gain"; their children "form associations that are unfavorable to the development of piety." **PP 169.2:** "Like Lot, many see their children ruined, and barely save their own souls."
- **PP 126.2–126.3:** "Abraham's unquestioning obedience is one of the most striking evidences of faith to be found in all the Bible … the happiest place on earth for him was the place where God would have him to be." **PP 126.4:** "Many are still tested as was Abraham. They do not hear the voice of God speaking directly from the heavens, but He calls them by the teachings of His word and the events of His providence."
- **MH 363.1; MH 365.2–367.1:** "the home on earth is to be a symbol of and a preparation for the home in heaven"; "It was not God's purpose that people should be crowded into cities"; the Father chose for His Son "a secluded home in the Galilean hills" (MH 365.3); "Go where … you can give your children your companionship, where you can teach them to learn of God through His works" (MH 367.1).
- **2SM 355.1–356.5:** "Fathers and mothers, how do you regard the souls of your children? … How will ease, comfort, convenience, compare with the value of the souls of your children?" (Ms 76, 1905). "'Out of the cities' is my message for the education of our children" (2SM 355.2). "By beholding the scenes of nature, the works of the Creator … imperceptibly you will be changed into the same image" (2SM 356.1, Ms 85, 1908; 2 Cor 3:18). "Get out of the cities as soon as possible, and purchase a little piece of land, where you can have a garden, where your children can watch the flowers growing" (2SM 356.5).
- **Ed 33.3:** "Those who departed from God built for themselves cities … But the men who held fast God's principles of life dwelt among the fields and hills … they learned of God and taught their children of His works and ways."
- **CL 9.4** (Ms 115, 1907): "those who remain in them **unnecessarily** do so at the peril of their soul's salvation." **5T 320.2:** "You moved your families into places of temptation … and the Lord has not worked a miracle to deliver your children from temptation."

**Should we move? When, and how**

The counsel answers this in its own words, and it holds two things together: urgency of direction and care in method.

- **Why.** For the souls and characters of the children, and your own walk with God (2SM 355.1; MH 363.1). Not for profit (PP 168.4), and not in panic.
- **When.** "As soon as possible" (2SM 356.5), but "as God opens the way" (2SM 360.1) and "as the way opens before them to do so" (2SM 360.3). "Let it not be in a rush, in an excitement, or in a rash manner" (2SM 361.1). "Do nothing without seeking wisdom of God" (2SM 361.2).
- **How.** "Let everyone take time to consider carefully; and not be like the man in the parable who began to build, and was not able to finish … everything weighed" (2SM 362.1; Luke 14:28–30). Have "well-defined plans as to what you will do" (2SM 362.3). Don't "enter into some business they know nothing about. This God does not require" (2SM 362.2).
- **Where.** "As suitable a place as their means will allow. Though the dwelling may be small, yet there should be land in connection with it, that may be cultivated" (2SM 360.1). "God will help His people to find such homes" (2SM 360.2). Consider where it will be easy to keep the Sabbath and to serve (2SM 359.3). Don't move simply to cluster around a large church institution; a family can "do better service for the Master by remaining where they are" and helping a small church start a school (6T 198.1).
- **Until then.** "So long as they remain, they should be most active in doing missionary work, however limited their sphere of influence may be" (2SM 360.3). "Many cannot have their surroundings what they would; and whenever duty calls us, God will enable us to stand uncorrupted, if we watch and pray" (PP 169.1). Take Jesus with you, and "erect an altar to the Lord wherever you pitch your tent" (5T 320.3).

**Practical counsel**

- Decide by the first question of PP 169.1, together, in prayer, with the children's ages in view.
- Count the cost: work, income, church, school, family nearby. Make the plan before the move (Luke 14:28; 2SM 362.1–362.3).
- A garden first. A little land, work the children share, and the works of God in view (2SM 356.5; Ed 111.1).
- The country does not educate by itself. The Deuteronomy method must move with you, and the phone can bring the city along (White's illustration; §2, §10).

**Beyond the source / disagreements**

- **Fear-based framing.** Some talks lead with crime, prices or crisis. The counsel leads with the children's souls and with God's original plan (MH 363.1, 365.2); safety is a side effect (White, C12: "the right environment for our children … a really nice side effect is it's a safer place").
- **The fear-driven version** (White, E4): the Jews of Shushan "shouldn't have been there"; a villager's word that persecution fell mostly on the cities; the persecution decree (PK 600.1) applied to city families now. He reads no country-living text for it, and it drops CL 9.4's "unnecessarily". His own better framing is in E1, E5, D1 and F5: an environment chosen for the children's education, and the faith of Noah and Abraham, who looked foolish to their neighbors. The series keeps that one.
- **"Leave your family and friends to isolate your children"** (White, F5) goes beyond PP 126.4, which is about God's call to a work, not about isolating children.
- **Ed 33.3** is read by him without its cities clause (D2, E8). The clause is the paragraph's contrast: those who departed from God "gloried in the splendor, the luxury, and the vice that make the cities of today the world's pride and its curse", while the faithful "dwelt among the fields and hills".
- **"Unnecessarily"** (CL 9.4) is often dropped. Keep it. PP 169.1 allows that duty may place a family in a trying position.
- **Rev 18:4** ("Come out of her, my people") is about spiritual Babylon. It is not the ground of country living; Gen 12–19, Heb 11, Ed 33.3 and the counsel are. Using Rev 18:4 as a geographic proof-text would be the spiritualizing the series avoids.
- **Lot's family "lost to the city"** (PP 161.1) is used by C7 as "get out of the city". The paragraph is about Lot's dimmed faith as he pleaded for Zoar; PP 168.1–169.2 is the right witness.
- **A second, later layer.** The counsel also describes a crisis signal for leaving the large cities (5T 464.3, the siege of Jerusalem as type; 2SM 359.2–359.5). That is a prophetic application, outside the series' audience rule; the motive for moving now is the children (2SM 360.3: "especially families with children").
- **Nazareth.** MH 365.3 calls it "a secluded home in the Galilean hills", but Luke 2:39 calls Nazareth "their own city". It was a small hill town, not a farm; say "a quiet home in the hills", not "the country".
- **Land prices and safety statistics** in the talks need primary sources and are not spoken.

**For a parent outside our tradition.** This is the section most likely to sound strange, so it most needs Christ's method: meet the parent's worry (the children, the noise, the screens, the pace) before any call to move, and build on Abraham, Lot and Eden, which every Christian knows. Say "righteousness by faith" in plain words ("faith that acts"; "the obedience of faith", Rom 16:26). Leave out "Country Living" as a book title, the institutional passages ("our institutions", outposts), the Sunday-law crisis, and any hint that a city family is less Christian. Say "the Sabbath" only with its text.

**Draft topics:** 7, 11, 94, 95, 99, and new nos. 115–118 (`series.md`).

## 14. Sending them out: calling, leaving, marriage, vocation

**The principle.** From the day they are born, we are raising our children to leave: to leave home for their own work, and to leave the world's ways for God's. When the door home to Jerusalem opened after seventy years, most of the exiles stayed in Babylon; they had been raised to be comfortable there (Ezra 2:64). A calling is found by doing the nearest duty faithfully and watching for God's leading, not by a test or a ranking. And after the choice to follow Christ, the choice of a husband or wife is the largest a young person will make.

**Witnesses**

- **PK 598.1; PK 612.2:** "nearly fifty thousand" returned from "hundreds of thousands"; to Ezra the number was "disappointingly small. Many who had acquired houses and lands had no desire to sacrifice these possessions."
- **Ed 267.3:** "To do our best in the work that lies nearest, to commit our ways to God, and to watch for the indications of His providence—these are rules that ensure safe guidance in the choice of an occupation." **Acts 9:6; Prov 3:5–6.**
- **Ed 262.1–262.2; Ed 264.2:** the noblest aim; "many a lad of today, growing up as did Daniel in his Judean home … will yet stand in legislative assemblies"; not "a life of respectable conventionality, a life professedly Christian, but lacking His self-sacrifice" (Ed 264.2). **Ed 264.3:** "Many a father and mother, denying their children to the cross of Christ, have learned too late that they were thus giving them over to the enemy of God and man."
- **Ed 269.3:** "Many of these have had little opportunity for education; but Christ sees in them qualifications … If they put their hearts into the work, and continue to be learners, He will fit them." **1 Kgs 19:18:** the seven thousand Elijah didn't know of; a faithful family is rarely as alone as it feels.
- **Ed 265.1:** life wrongly split into "the period of learning and the period of doing"; graduates "out of touch with life". **Ed 271.1:** of youth in ministry, "what 'university course' can equal this?"
- **MH 358.1, 358.3, 359.1–359.2:** "Early marriages are not to be encouraged"; "It is only in Christ that a marriage alliance can be safely formed"; "weigh every sentiment and watch every development of character"; "If you are blessed with God-fearing parents, seek counsel of them." **2 Cor 6:14.**

**Practical counsel**

- Raise children for the call, not for comfort: a trade, practical skill, the habit of service, and the courage to be different (Ed 215.2; Ed 57.3; Dan 1:8).
- Guidance by the three rules of Ed 267.3, in that order. "Lord, what wilt thou have me to do?" comes before career tests.
- Send them to serve with an experienced worker (Ev 109.1).
- Keep the young adult's confidence so they bring you their hopes and plans, including about marriage (MH 359.2; Kellogg pp. 14–20).

**Beyond the source / disagreements**

- **"Called to leave, educated to stay"** is White's frame; Haskell (SDP 163.2) supplies "homes should have been schools". His prophecy charts and 1844 parallels stay out.
- **University.** White calls it "extremely dangerous" (C14). The counsel's own measure is the principle of §12 (the most favorable place for character), and Daniel's "ten times better" came from inside Babylon's school.
- **"Ezra … none of the Levites followed"** is wrong (Ezra 8:18–20).
- **"Respectable conventionality"** (White, D3 and F). Ed 264.2 is a question about a Christian life "lacking His self-sacrifice". White turns it into a verdict ("God condemns this form of education") and lists a Christian school, good grades and a Christian marriage under it. Keep the question; the fault it names is a life without service, not those goods.

**For a parent outside our tradition.** Common ground. "Called to leave" must stay with the Bible's exiles; White's application of the decrees to 1844 and the "Laodicean message" is Adventist history and stays out.

**Draft topics:** 25, 27, 31, 43, 47, 74, 99, and new nos. 119, 121 (`series.md`).

## 15. The school that never ends

**The principle.** Parents are students too. "In the school of Christ, students are never graduated" (CT 51.1), and heaven is a school (Ed 301; AH 547.4). The promise behind the whole series is Isaiah's: "all thy children shall be taught of the LORD; and great shall be the peace of thy children" (Isa 54:13; John 6:45). And the strongest argument for the gospel a family can give is itself: "a well-ordered, well-disciplined family" (AH 32.2).

**Witnesses**

- **Isa 54:13; John 6:45.**
- **CT 51.1** (above). **Ed 57.3:** "The greatest want of the world is the want of men … who will stand for the right though the heavens fall."
- **AH 32.2** (= 4T 304).
- **Mal 4:6:** the hearts of the fathers turned to the children, and the children to the fathers.

**For a parent outside our tradition.** Common ground.

**Draft topics:** 73, 76, 77, 113, 114, and new no. 122.
