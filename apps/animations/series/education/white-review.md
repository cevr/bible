## 1. What he is worth to the series

**Strongest contributions**

1. **The "Deuteronomy method."** Deut 6:6–7 read as two steps: first the parent's own heart, then all-day relationship. Israel kept the information and lost the method, so "another generation" knew "not the LORD, nor yet the works" (Judg 2:10). Backed by Ed 34.1 (the parents needed the instruction first), Ed 187.1 ("the weight of influence given it by our own example") and PP 592.2 (the duty "was not to be delegated to another"). It strengthens nos. 12–14 and gives new no. 92.
2. **One frame for the first school.** A table that runs from Eden through the Fall to the patriarchs: classroom, activity, subject, unit, teacher (no. 7). Also Ed 23.2: the knowledge of evil "was in love withheld" (new no. 88).
3. **Bible students formed young**, each with a checkable text:
   - Daniel "chapter zero": FE 95.3–96.1, PK 428.1, and Josiah's reading to "all the people, both small and great" (2 Kgs 23:1–2). New no. 98, and script 19 is strengthened.
   - Jochebed's twelve years (PP 243.4–244.1) and Hannah's first three (CG 197.1). Nos. 15 and 16; "If you only had twelve years…" is the strongest hook he offers.
   - The girl in Naaman's house (PK 245.1, no. 97); John in the desert (DA 101.3, no. 96).
   - "Called to leave, educated to stay": the exiles who could leave Babylon and didn't (PK 598.1, 612.2). New no. 99.
4. **Method for the Book.** Bible study by one word (Ed 189.1–189.4, nos. 101–102), family worship children look forward to (CG 521.4; Ed 186.2 = CG 522.1; no. 104), and guidance by Ed 267.3's three rules: the nearest work, commit your ways, watch His providence (no. 47).
5. **Assemblies that included the children** (Deut 31:12–13; Josh 8:35; 2 Chr 20:13; Ezra 10:1; Joel 2:16), from his book _Gather the Children_. It supports no. 85 from Scripture, not only from the counsel.
6. **"Do kids need to see evil to resist it?"** (Ed 23.2–25.3): a sharp question the series lacked (no. 88).
7. **Accurate reading.** 721 quotation rows were checked across six playlists and 95% are verified, better than Ritsema's 93%.
8. **The aim as love and service** (D3, F4, F7): Ed ch. 31 read almost whole; Ed 16.1–16.2, Ed 309.1, MH 401.3. It gives the series' "what is it for?" its warmest form (§6).
9. **Home first, school as mercy** (D2): his most balanced word on school, and the series' own position (CT 162.3, 161.1, 167.1; Ed 46.1).

**Main liabilities** (all now under "Steer around" in `series.md` §5):

- **Insider language and denominational history:** "the Spirit of Prophecy", "remnant", Ellen White by name, Battle Creek and Madison. B7 is wholly denominational history (1844, 1863, 1872, 1888). He also uses prophecy charts.
- **Science used as proof of the counsel:** brain, eye and hormone claims, and ADHD medication. Several are wrong or overstated (§4.8).
- **Culture-war and lineage material:** A3 ("boys will be girls"), and a Reformation → Jesuits → Prussia → United States lineage that is milder than Ritsema's.
- **Rhetoric that condemns families' choices:** "socialization is a lie", the "insanity" line and "send our children to Caesar" (A8), video games (C5), and university "extremely dangerous" (C14).
- **Haskell read as Ellen White** (SDP 49.1, 163.2).

## 4. Where he and the series disagree

### 4.1 Daniel 1: home school, or indoctrination?

- **White, two ways.** In A6 and B2 he takes the series' reading: Daniel was educated at home before the captivity, accepted the name and the university, and drew the line at the diet. In B8 and C12 he calls Babylon's course "a course of indoctrination … exactly what it was" (C12 0:44:37), Ritsema's reading.
- **The pioneer he reads.** In C10 he extends Haskell's SDP 26.1 into 26.2 but leaves out its first sentence. That sentence says such principles would fit youth "rooted and grounded in the Scriptures, to enter worldly universities" and "come forth unsullied", like the Waldenses.
- **Verdict.** The series' reading stands (`ritsema-review.md` §4.1): both halves are in Dan 1:4–8 and 1:17–20, and the heart was settled at home. SDP 26.2 is a balance note for no. 19.

### 4.2 Public school

- **White** is milder than Ritsema in his own words. Moving the desk home "only changed the location" (A4, B7), and the 1904 Taylor interview he reads (6MR 369–372, a report of a conversation) says, "If they prefer to send their children to the public school, let them send them."
- **But** he reads CT 205.1 and Ms 100, 1902 short of their continuations: schools with a "Scriptural foundation", the church school, and "Teach the Scriptures to your children yourselves". A8 and C14 carry the "Caesar" and "insanity" rhetoric, and the "danger signal" letters are about a sanitarium, not a school.
- **Verdict.** The series' restraint stands, and the 1904 interview supports it. No. 91 ("Where, or how?") turns his best line into a question for every family.

### 4.3 Fiction

- **White is stricter than the owner's rule.** In A7 he extends "light reading" from novels to "even good and true stories" read for entertainment.
- **He is also looser.** C8 opens with a read-aloud imaginative story of Jochebed and Moses by an unidentified author. A5 and A6 tell imagined scenes (Noah's last nails, Josiah's appeal) as narrative. C2 uses the Animal School fable.
- **The owner's rule (2026-10-13).** Fiction is avoided, but an allegory that carries a Bible truth is allowed. It rests on GC 252.2 (_The Pilgrim's Progress_, "his wonderful allegory") and CT 134.2 (the kind of story, not a moral tacked on). It is now in `series.md` §5 and `sources.md` §2.
- **Verdict.** Use the Animal School only as an openly framed allegory (no. 44). Don't retell the C8 story. Any imagined scene must be said to be imagined (no. 98).

### 4.4 Age-grading

- **White:** there is "not a single example" in the Bible of age-separated teaching (A5, 0:31:24). In C9 and in _Gather the Children_ he builds family-based Sabbath school on the same claim.
- **Scripture:** the assemblies did include the little ones (Deut 31:12–13; Josh 8:35; 2 Chr 20:13; Ezra 10:1; Joel 2:16). But Neh 8:2 has "all that could hear with understanding", which some read as a threshold.
- **Verdict.** No. 85 gains the assembly texts, and the series does not say "not one example".

### 4.5 When to start school

- **White** agrees with no. 10 (FE 21.2; CT 80.1), and his best turn is that the years are for character work, not only for delaying books.
- **But** he splits the start age by sex (girls about 8, boys about 10), and the counsel says "eight or ten" with no split. He also dates FE 21.2 to the late 1880s; it is 1872 (= 3T 137.2).
- **Verdict.** No disagreement on substance. Say "eight or ten".

### 4.6 Serve now, or "no junior Levites"?

- **White** (A2): spiritual fruit comes late too. There were no "junior Levites", and Jesus waited until thirty, so children should not be put on display.
- **The series** (no. 86, from Ritsema): you learn to swim in the water.
- **Scripture:** Levites served "from the age of twenty years and upward" under David (1 Chr 23:24), and Samuel "ministered before the LORD, being a child" (1 Sam 2:18). White concedes Samuel.
- **Resolution:** Ev 109.1 sends young workers out with "a worker of experience". Youth serve now, alongside others and not on display. That reconciles both, and it is now in nos. 86 and 105.

### 4.7 Joseph

- **White** (C1): Joseph "had no worldly education".
- **The series** (no. 22): Joseph learned in Potiphar's house and Pharaoh's court (Gen 39:2–4; Ps 105:22). In C14 White himself praises Joseph's faithfulness as a slave (Ed 51.3, 52.4).
- **Verdict.** The series stands.

### 4.8 History and science

None of it is spoken unchecked (`sources.md` §8 lists the checkable leads). Wrong or overstated as stated:

- "male hormone levels dropped 50% in 50 years" (Travison et al. 2007 found about 1% a year);
- Alaska bought and made a state together (1867 vs 1959);
- "94% of Waldorf graduates go to the Ivy League" (the usual claim is about college);
- "100% of mass shootings by video gamers";
- Hitler "voted in by a majority";
- brain maturity "at 30" or "22", and glasses as a cause of myopia, given as settled;
- "none of the Levites followed" Ezra (Ezra 8:18–20 records 38 Levites and 220 Nethinim).

### 4.10 Respectable conventionality (D3, F4, F7)

Ed 264.2 asks a question: can we educate our children "for a life of respectable conventionality, a life professedly Christian, but lacking His self-sacrifice"? He turns it into a verdict ("God condemns this form of education"), lists a Christian school, good grades, a Christian marriage and mission trips under it, and glosses Rev 3:16 as "I'd rather you weren't a Christian at all". He also stops Ed 264.3 before its warning to parents. **Verdict:** keep the question and the paragraph whole; the fault it names is a life without His service, not those goods (no. 121; `guide.md` §14).

### 4.11 Mothers and work (F5)

AH 246.1 (Lt 28, 1890) was written to one mother drawn into church work away from her little children. He widens it to all employment ("that is not the Lord's calling") and adds animal-separation studies. **Verdict:** both go past the source. The series speaks the principle (the children come first; "not away from your own home, but back to it") and never a verdict on a family's work (`guide.md` §2).

### 4.12 Country living: two framings (D1, E1, E4, E5, F5)

His usual framing fits the owner's: country living chosen for the children's education, with Abraham's faith as the model (PP 126.3–126.4). E4 is the exception: Mordecai's Jews "shouldn't have been there", persecution "mostly in the cities", and the decree of PK 600.1 applied to city families now, with no country-living text read and CL 9.4's "unnecessarily" dropped. In F5, "leave your family and friends to isolate your children" goes beyond PP 126.4. He reads Ed 33.3 without its cities clause (D2, E8). **Verdict:** the series keeps the child-centred, faith-shaped version and leaves E4 out (`guide.md` §13).
