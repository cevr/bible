# Taught of the Lord: plan

These are talk shorts. The owner speaks on camera, cut with B-roll; they are not cut-paper films. They follow the two formats in the talk-shorts style notes:

- **Question** (One Question): 45–65 s, about 140–160 words at 155 wpm with its marked breaths and holds.
- **Episode**: 2:15–2:50, about 330–410 words at 155 wpm with its marks.

The audience is every Christian:

- **Scripture** is quoted in KJV, word for word, from the `bible` CLI.
- **Ellen G. White and the pioneers** are paraphrased and never named in speech; they are credited in the description.
- **Old child-training books** (Abbott, Kellogg, Byrum) are retold and credited the same way.
- **No denominational history**: no church history and no denomination names.

Companion files:

- `guide.md`: **the parents' guide**, the core understanding compiled from every source below and organized by the parent's question (what education is for, the home, start age, the Bible, authority, correction, the will, work, motives, media, friends, school choice, where to live and whether to move, sending them out).
- `series-list.md`: **the series list**, a proposed sequence of 36 episodes in 9 arcs that walks a parent through the guide. Each episode names its guide section and the draft topics below it folds together. This file stays the catalogue.
- `sources.md`: every PDF with author, year, public-domain status, summary, chapters and page references, plus the EGW and pioneer references.
- `scripts/`: five pilot scripts (nos. 1, 17, 19, 44, 52).
- `ocr/`: full text of the seven PDFs.
- `transcripts/ritsema/`: Scott Ritsema's seven-part seminar (transcripts, `notes.md`, `verification.md`). `ritsema-review.md` lists what it added where.
- `transcripts/white/`: Joshua White's six true-education playlists (49 talks: Bogenhofen 2026, Autumn Leaves NZ, Trinidad 2023, the 2018 _True Education 3 Part Series_, _True Education_ and the Uchee Pines seminars), with `notes.md` and `verification.md`. `white-review.md` lists what they added where and where he and the series disagree.

**+R** in a Sources cell marks an addition from the Ritsema seminar (2026-10-12). Every EGW ref after **+R** was checked in the local corpus; Ritsema's own illustrations are credited in `sources.md` §6 and are never spoken as the owner's. Rows marked _(new, from Ritsema)_ are topics he covers that the series lacked.

**+W** marks an addition from Joshua White's playlists (2026-10-13). The same rule holds: every EGW ref after **+W** was checked in the local corpus, and his illustrations and leads are credited in `sources.md` §8, never spoken as the owner's. Rows marked _(new, from J. White)_ are topics he covers that the series lacked.

## 4. Rules for writing the remaining 72

- **Opening and hook:** the first word lands within 0.1 s. The hook is either 2–6 words pointing at an object or one claim against expectation in 20 words or fewer. A typed viewer question sits at the top of the frame for 2–4 s.
- **Question structure:** hook, then the common belief, then 2–3 evidence beats, the turn, and a payoff line that restores the hook's object.
- **Episode structure:** hook, series line, story 1, turn, story 2, payoff.
- **Ending:** no spoken call to action. Cut no more than 0.6 s after the last word.
- **Scripture:** quote only from fresh `bible verse` output, and drop `[ ]` and `‹ ›`. A partial verse is a continuous run of words, and the caption shows only those words. Say the book name in speech and leave chapter and verse on screen.
- **Other sources:** EGW, the pioneers and the old child-training books are paraphrased and never named in speech. List them in the description as "Ideas paraphrased from: …" with page numbers. Retell stories in fresh words; don't read the book's dialogue aloud.
- **Generated images:** any image of a historical scene is labeled "Illustration".

## 5. Steer around (audience rule: every Christian)

- **Fiction (owner rule, 2026-10-13).** Fiction is avoided as a rule (CT 134.2, 383.2), but a story may be used allegorically, the way a parable is, to carry a Bible truth. Ellen White commended _The Pilgrim's Progress_ as "his wonderful allegory", which has "guided many feet into the path of life" (GC 252.2, 1911; first in 4SP 174.2, 1884). It "portrays the Christian life so accurately, and presents the love of Christ so attractively" that thousands were converted through it (HS 151.2, 1886; RH May 30, 1912 par. 11). She used its characters herself: Pliable, to warn the young (RH May 9, 1899 Art. A par. 6 = ML 73.4), and the man with the muck-rake (COL 253.2). Her full words are in `sources.md` §2. The line she draws is the kind of story, not a moral tacked on: "love stories and frivolous, exciting tales" are out "even though the author may attach a good moral" (CT 134.2), while an allegory that pictures the Christian life is in. So no. 57's knight-and-giants allegory stays, framed openly as an allegory, and no. 68 teaches the rule and its allegory exception.

- **Denominational history:** no church history, school-founding stories or institutional names.
  - The Kellogg book's Battle Creek setting is not mentioned in speech.
  - The Credits booklet's "papal degrees" claim (pp. 8–9) is left out.
  - The Daniel booklet's example of a historical church group (p. 23) is left out.
- **Corporal punishment.** Owner, 2026-10-13: keep the last-resort counsel, stated as written: whipping only "when other resorts fail", and avoided wherever possible (CG 250.2); "not in passion, but with love and prayer" (CG 276.1); a rod of iron is as wrong as indulgence (CG 175.2). Neither Abbott's "the rod is an emblem" (ch. 21) nor Byrum's "conquer the will by whipping" (pp. 51–53) gets a short. A topic that carries it puts authority, calm and consistency first and the last resort last, as the counsel orders them.
