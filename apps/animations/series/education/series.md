# Christian education talk series: plan

These are talk shorts. The owner speaks on camera, cut with B-roll; they are not cut-paper films. They follow the two formats in the talk-shorts style notes:

- **Question** (One Question): 45–65 s, 170–200 words.
- **Episode**: 2:15–2:50, 450–550 words.

The audience is every Christian:

- **Scripture** is quoted in KJV, word for word, from the `bible` CLI.
- **Ellen G. White and the pioneers** are paraphrased and never named in speech; they are credited in the description.
- **Old child-training books** (Abbott, Kellogg, Byrum) are retold and credited the same way.
- **No denominational history**: no church history and no denomination names.

Companion files:

- `sources.md`: every PDF with author, year, public-domain status, summary, chapters and page references, plus the EGW and pioneer references.
- `scripts/`: five pilot scripts (nos. 1, 17, 19, 44, 52).
- `ocr/`: full text of the seven PDFs.
- `transcripts/ritsema/`: Scott Ritsema's seven-part seminar (transcripts, `notes.md`, `verification.md`). `ritsema-review.md` lists what it added where.

**+R** in a Sources cell marks an addition from the Ritsema seminar (2026-10-12). Every EGW ref after **+R** was checked in the local corpus; Ritsema's own illustrations are credited in `sources.md` §6 and are never spoken as the owner's. Rows marked _(new, from Ritsema)_ are topics he covers that the series lacked.

## 2. The arc

The series runs in ten parts, moving from why, to where, to whom, to hands, mind, motive, method and heart, and ending with heaven as the last school.

## 4. Rules for writing the remaining 72

- **Opening and hook:** the first word lands within 0.1 s. The hook is either 2–6 words pointing at an object or one claim against expectation in 20 words or fewer. A typed viewer question sits at the top of the frame for 2–4 s.
- **Question structure:** hook, then the common belief, then 2–3 evidence beats, the turn, and a payoff line that restores the hook's object.
- **Episode structure:** hook, series line, story 1, turn, story 2, payoff.
- **Ending:** no spoken call to action. Cut no more than 0.6 s after the last word.
- **Scripture:** quote only from fresh `bible verse` output, and drop `[ ]` and `‹ ›`. A partial verse is a continuous run of words, and the caption shows only those words. Say the book name in speech and leave chapter and verse on screen.
- **Other sources:** EGW, the pioneers and the old child-training books are paraphrased and never named in speech. List them in the description as "Ideas paraphrased from: …" with page numbers. Retell stories in fresh words; don't read the book's dialogue aloud.
- **Generated images:** any image of a historical scene is labeled "Illustration".

## 5. Steer around (audience rule: every Christian)

- **Fiction (owner rule, 2026-10-13).** Fiction is avoided as a rule (CT 134.2, 383.2), but a story may be used allegorically, the way a parable is, to carry a Bible truth. Ellen White commended _The Pilgrim's Progress_ (find and cite her words in `sources.md`). So no. 57's knight-and-giants allegory stays, framed openly as an allegory, and no. 68 teaches the rule and its allegory exception.

- **Denominational history:** no church history, school-founding stories or institutional names.
  - The Kellogg book's Battle Creek setting is not mentioned in speech.
  - The Credits booklet's "papal degrees" claim (pp. 8–9) is left out.
  - The Daniel booklet's example of a historical church group (p. 23) is left out.
- **Corporal punishment.** Abbott ch. 21, Kellogg pp. 103–104 and Byrum pp. 51–53 each take a position that splits Christian audiences: either "the rod is an emblem" or "conquer the will by whipping." Neither gets a short. Gentle-measures topics speak of authority, calm and consistency, and leave the rod alone.
- **Dated methods and science:**
  - tying, taping or locking up children (Kellogg pp. 45, 97, 99–100);
  - fake "illness" as a cure (Kellogg pp. 133–134);
  - diet-causes-temper claims (Kellogg pp. 53, 71);
  - the energy-physics digression (Abbott pp. 91–96);
  - "savages" language (Abbott pp. 39, 147; Kellogg p. 34);
  - servants and gender assumptions.
- **Adult content:** Byrum's court-room chapter (pp. 83–89) and Kellogg's purity chapter. The purity chapter can feed one general line about knowing who watches your children; it gets no standalone short.
- **Contested theology:** Abbott ch. 23 on human nature and judgment, and ch. 16 on "deceiving by appearances." Use the parts that stand alone (Bible stories first; say "we," not "you"). Leave out the claims.
- **The Ritsema seminar's framing** (use its sources, not its frame):
  - the 1844 / 1872 / 1888 / 1890s "parallel" timeline, and every institution he names (Battle Creek, Walla Walla, Red Bluff, campus videos, CognitiveGenesis, Raymond Moore as "ours");
  - the Jesuit → Prussia → Rockefeller lineage told as a plot, the Nazi line, "New World Order" quotes (Pierce 1973, the London _Times_ 1919), and Rev 13 or plague applications to school choice;
  - "sending kids to public school is insane", "they should never have become parents", and "opt out, don't reform". The series speaks to every family;
  - history he states wrongly: Massachusetts was the _first_ state with a compulsory law (1852) and Mississippi the last (1918); Mann died in 1859, so no "1867" Mann quote; the "perfect docility" line is F. T. Gates for the General Education Board, not "Rockefeller himself"; "destroy free will" is Russell's paraphrase of Fichte; the "behave as they do not behave" line is Ruskin's;
  - statistics with no primary source: the creativity curve (98% to 2%), homeschool percentiles, the dropout-genius list, "television is hypnotic".
- **Unconfirmed claims, never spoken:**
  - dates for Daniel's captivity (Haskell gives both 606 and 607 B.C.);
  - meanings of the Hebrew names;
  - the speaker of the 1899 bulletin talk;
  - the original year and publisher of _Happy Hours at Home_.
