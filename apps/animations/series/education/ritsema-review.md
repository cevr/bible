## 1. What he is worth to the series

**Strongest contributions**

1. **A close reading of the EGW education counsel**, with the references. 424 quotation rows were checked and 93% are verified. That gives the series many paragraphs it lacked, especially on:
   - Jesus' school of twelve (Ed 84–96);
   - schools as an aid to failing homes (Ed 45–46);
   - practical learning (Ed 216, 234, 238);
   - service while studying (Ed 268);
   - fiction (CT 134–136, 383–384);
   - the teacher's posture (FE 58, CT 436).
2. **Fresh framings of Bible texts:**
   - Daniel 1 as Babylon's program to remake young minds (Dan 1:4, last clause);
   - "sons of the prophets", a family word for a school;
   - the Twelve as a mixed-age family school that reached the world in one generation.
3. **History of modern schooling in its planners' own words.** Mann's _Seventh Report_ (1844), Dewey's "My Pedagogic Creed" (1897), the General Education Board's _Occasional Papers_ No. 1 (1913), Harris and Ross. These are leads only: most of his wording comes second-hand through Gatto, and several facts are wrong (§4).
4. **Illustrations for shorts**, all credited, and none spoken as the owner's:
   - the "game of school";
   - Washington's Farewell in old readers;
   - "ten reasons to justify indoor education?";
   - the vitamin analogy ("home-deficient");
   - Helen Todd's 1913 _McClure's_ survey.

**Main liabilities**

- **Adventist institutional history.** He uses the 1844/1872/1888 parallels and names Battle Creek, Sutherland, Red Bluff and CognitiveGenesis.
- **A conspiratorial frame.** He traces a Jesuit → Prussia → Rockefeller lineage and makes Rev 13 and plague applications to school choice.
- **Rhetoric that splits audiences.** He calls public school "insane" and says "opt out, don't reform".

The series' audience rule excludes all three. `series.md` §5 now lists them under "Steer around".

## 4. Where he and the series disagree

### 4.1 Daniel 1: an indoctrination camp, or a test passed inside the school?

- **Ritsema's reading.** Babylon's three-year program is the template for modern schooling, a state shaping captive minds through literature and diet. The lesson is to opt out.
- **The series' reading** (no. 19). Daniel studied Babylon's learning diligently, refused only what would defile him, and came out "ten times better". The heart was settled at home.
- **What the sources support.** Both halves are in the text.
  - Dan 1:4–7 states the program: "the learning and the tongue of the Chaldeans", new names, the king's food. PK 480.3 says the names honored Babylon's gods.
  - Dan 1:8 and 1:17–20 show the four inside the school, refusing defilement, not learning. God "gave them knowledge and skill in all learning".
  - Acts 7:22 (Moses "learned in all the wisdom of the Egyptians") and Ed 54–55 point the same way.
  - The script now carries his half (beat 8) without dropping the series' half.
- **Verdict.** Scripture supports the series' reading, and Ritsema's adds a true point about Babylon's aim.

### 4.2 Public school: "insane", or a choice the series leaves to parents?

- **Ritsema's position.**
  - Sending children to public school is "an absolutely insane thing to do".
  - Opt out rather than reform.
  - The system was "conceived in iniquity".
- **The series' position.** It is for every Christian, so it teaches principles and lets parents apply them.
- **What the sources support.**
  - EGW urges parents to place children where they will not be "bewitched by a false education" (FE 470.1) and in "the most favorable situation" (CT 156.1).
  - She also allows that "as far as possible" Christian education be provided (CG 307.5).
  - Scripture gives faithful youth who were schooled by pagans (Daniel, Moses), and its strongest commands go to the home (Deut 6:6–7; Eph 6:4).
  - Ritsema's own Ep 4 line, "every family is a home-school family", is closer to the series than his rhetoric.
- **Verdict.** The series' restraint is a rule about audience, not a doctrinal disagreement. It stands. Episodes 79, 85 and 87 present the principles without condemning any family.

### 4.3 Fiction: the series retells stories; the source he reads forbids "exciting tales" even with a moral

- **Ritsema's position.** "Total abstinence" from fiction, including realistic and historical novels. He cites CT 134.2 ("even though the author may attach a good moral") and CT 136.2, which names _Robinson Crusoe_ and _Uncle Tom's Cabin_.
- **The series' position.** No. 68 asks whether fiction is harmless. Yet the series itself retells:
  - the A.L.O.E. knight-and-giants allegory (no. 57);
  - Byrum's and Abbott's illustrative stories (nos. 51, 52, 58, 59);
  - Kellogg's home stories.
- **What the sources support.**
  - The texts are strong (CT 134.2, 136.2, 383–384.1), and the series should not soften them in no. 68.
  - The series' stories are given as true or illustrative examples told by a parent, like Christ's parables (Matt 13:34). EGW's targets are "love stories and frivolous, exciting tales" and "fairy tales, myths" (CT 384.1).
  - The knight allegory sits closest to the line.
- **Recommendation.** The owner should decide whether no. 57 stays. If it stays, frame it openly as an allegory that explains Bible truth, the way a parable does.

### 4.4 Age-grading: "a hindrance", or "sometimes a hindrance"?

- He reads the 1903 manuscript (2MR 215.3), which says "is a hindrance". The published CT 177.1 says "is _sometimes_ a hindrance".
- **Verdict.** The published wording is what the author released. No. 85 uses it.

### 4.5 When to start school

- **Ritsema** agrees with no. 10 (home until 8–10; FE 21.2, CT 80.1). He adds the 1904 exception (3SM 214–218).
- **Verdict.** No disagreement. The exception is now no. 10's counter-argument. It strengthens the series' "present as counsel, not as brain science".

### 4.6 History

The series makes no history claims yet. The new no. 78 does, so it is gated on primary documents. Where his history is wrong as stated (the primary or standard sources support the correction):

- Massachusetts was the _first_ state with a compulsory law (1852), not the last. Mississippi was last (1918).
- No Mann quotation can be dated 1867 (he died in 1859).
- "Perfect docility" is F. T. Gates for the General Education Board, not Rockefeller.
- "Destroy free will" is Russell paraphrasing Fichte.
- "Behave as they do not behave" is Ruskin's line.
- Mann's report was the _Seventh_.
- The 98%-to-2% creativity curve, the homeschool percentiles and the "dropout geniuses" list (Einstein was a good student) are unsourced or wrong. None should be spoken.

### 4.7 Smaller context stretches

These are his stretches, and the series follows the source:

- 6T 208.1 "homelike" is about thrift.
- CE 12.3 is about overstudy, not Prussia.
- Ed 265.1 is about training for service.
- FE 312–313 is about a school site in 1894, not American cities.

Each is noted where the series uses the passage.
