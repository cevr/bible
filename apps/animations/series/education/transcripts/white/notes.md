**Thesis**

- True education is "redemption through knowing God resulting in transformation". Education and redemption are one (Ed 30.2), and "to know God" (John 17:3; COL 114.2) is its core.
- Knowing God comes in four stages: through the parents (infancy), through His works (childhood, outdoors), through His word (from about 8–10), and through fellowship with Him in service (youth). The goal is harmonious physical, mental and spiritual development, with every subject made practical "missionary training".
- Noah's ark is the frame: he followed God's exact plan, built it with his sons, and his faith was seen in what he did.

### A2 · Early Growth, Late Fruit: How to Lay a Good Foundation in Childhood (OSev4eaaSo8) · 1:30:43 · speaker: Joshua White

Transcript: `bogenhofen-2026/02-early-growth-late-fruit.txt` (ytt --json)

**Thesis**

- The counsel "parents the only teachers until eight or ten, nature the textbook, the open air the schoolroom" is backed by development: brain, eyes, movement, speech, emotion, social life, memory and reading. Early academics do harm, and late starters catch up and pass early starters.
- The counsel's real point is positive. The early years are for character, and that work takes all of a parent's time.
- Spiritual fruit should come late too. There were no "junior Levites", and God's servants (Joseph, Moses, Joshua, David, Elisha, Jesus at 30) grew in obscurity. Children should not be put on display.

**Outline**

- 0:01:10–0:06:40 Fruit-tree grafting: rootstock, scion, about 3 years to bond. A first-year fruiting spur breaks the graft. Weakened for life (true horticulture used as an object lesson).
- 0:06:40–0:10:46 CG/MH 380.2 (early habits outweigh later training). FE 21.2 + CT 80.1. A Q&A point: "only textbook" vs "most familiar textbook". A "scientific article": no evidence that earlier is better.
- 0:11:20–0:21:54 Brain: myelination schedule; limbic system to 4½; "gestalt" to 7 (girls) and 9 (boys); logic areas after. Forcing early routes learning through "lower" systems (Healy, _Endangered Minds_). The word-problem "blank stare". Learn by doing, then paper.
- 0:22:27–0:35:59 Eyes: binocular focus to 7–9; myopia from near work plus too little time outdoors. US ~50%, East Asia ~90%, Seoul 96%, Africa 10–15%; the Alaska Inuit study; retinal dopamine; 3 hours at 10,000 lux vs a classroom's ~500. Anecdote: a friend's daughter outdoors 2 h a day stopped progressing. Claim: glasses worsen myopia, which "can heal".
- 0:35:59–0:42:15 "Sit still, be quiet": movement → BDNF → myelination; "free as lambs" (CT 79.1), with parents nearby like the ewe. Outer speech; Hannaford: silent reading is ineffective before 7.
- 0:42:15–0:47:31 Emotional security as a survival need (the lost-hiker "time to brush up on algebra" joke). Raymond Moore, _School Can Wait_. Socialization: "a lie"; Shyers and Smedley studies; mixed ages vs same-age peers.
- 0:47:31–0:50:48 Memory: unschooled peoples (Aboriginal song cycles, herds). His Tanzania Maasai cattle anecdote.
- 0:50:48–0:59:04 Reading: language first; talk, answer questions, interesting books. RIA 5 vs 7 study (the late group catches up by 9 and is ahead at 11); Montessori advantage gone by 12; a "zero reading instruction" district study.
- 0:59:04–1:04:42 ADHD: a Stanford study on delayed entry (−73%); 300 late starters; a staircase example of math repetition vs a 20-minute lesson at the right age.

Closing: Mark 4:28, corn needs water early and dryness late (Ed 106.3–107.2). "Consider moving to an area that makes it easier."
