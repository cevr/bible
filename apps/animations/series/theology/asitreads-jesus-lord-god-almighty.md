## 1. The argument

**Thesis.** "JESUS CHRIST REALLY IS THE LORD GOD ALMIGHTY! No, I am not saying that He is the same Person as the Father but what I am saying is that He has this same title like the Father." The Son is a different individual from the Father, and the Father is His Head. But the Son shares the Father's name and royal titles, "Lord God Almighty" among them. He laid that status down at the incarnation, and He resumed it at His exaltation.

The steps, in the article's order:

1. **Granted: "Jesus Christ is a different individual than God."** "God" most often names the Father (John 3:16). The Son "is God in His nature but He is not the same Person as God Himself". Witness: YI December 16, 1897, par. 5.
2. **Granted: "God has no God."** The Father is the supreme authority. The Son had the Father as His Head even before the incarnation, and ruled "under God" (Ms 43b, 1891). The article adds a caveat: the Son is "begotten of God and is not a creation", so He is not under God "just as we are" (Gal 4:4, "made under the law", applies only to His time on earth).
3. **Disputed: "Jesus Christ is not God Almighty."** The article denies this.
   - **(A) The Son has the Father's name.** YHWH is in Him (Ex 23:21) by inheritance (Heb 1:4). He also has names the Father does not: Michael, Jesus.
   - **(B) The Son has the Father's titles.** "King of kings, and Lord of lords" belongs to the Father (1 Tim 6:15) and to the Lamb (Rev 17:14; 19:16). He also has titles the Father does not: Prince, Captain of the host, Archangel (Dan 12:1; 8:25; 8:11; Josh 5:14; Jude 9).
   - **(C) "The Almighty."** The article claims that "the Lord" who said "I am the Almighty God" to Abram (Gen 17:1) was the pre-incarnate Son. It grants that an exegesis of Rev 1:8 "does seem to support" the Father, then appeals to EGW: Ms 64, 1895 ("It was He that revealed Himself to John … the Almighty") and RH March 12, 1908 ("The angels bow before him … Holy, holy, holy, Lord God Almighty").
   - **(D) The objection, Ms 140, 1903** ("The man Christ Jesus was not the Lord God Almighty"). The article answers by the verb's tense: _was_, not _is_. At the incarnation the Son "gave back into his Father's hand the scepter" (YI May 27, 1897) and laid down "almighty power" (DA 336.1). He "was publicly reinstated" at the ascension (ST May 10, 1899, par. 16–17), so He carries the title again.
4. **Disputed: "worshiping more than one God … is the very center of Babylonian doctrine."**
   - The Father is the Source and the Son the channel (DA 21.2).
   - The non-Trinitarian also worships the Son (Matt 2:11; 14:33; John 5:23; Heb 1:6).
   - So the two positions are "so similar that I do not think there should be any trial". Here the article cites James White (RH October 12, 1876), 3SG 37.3 (Satan would obey God but not Christ), a modern writer (Eugene Prewitt), and forum correspondents. It ends by commending "3 living persons of the heavenly trio" with "flexibility regarding the 2nd and 3rd Persons of the Godhead".

**What the series can use.**

- Steps 1–3 (A, B, D): the Son bears the Father's name and titles; He laid down His glory and power; He was restored to it. These are within the god-christ frame, and the corpus supports them strongly (section 4).
- Step 3C, "the Almighty", is where the pioneers disagree (section 5).
- Step 4 is an intra-church Godhead debate. It is outside this series, and the frame forbids it on screen: no Trinity words, no settled account of the Son's origin, no picking a side.

## 5. Where the sources differ (dated, both sides)

1. **Who is "the Almighty" of Rev 1:8, and the "Lord God Almighty" of Rev 4:8?**
   - **The Father:**
     - G. W. Amadon, ARSH September 24, 1861, page 136.1–136.9, signed "G. W. AMADON" (the corpus tags the volume to James White): "We do not believe that Christ is at all meant by the phrase, the Almighty". He also reads Rev 4:8, 11:17 and 16:7 of the Father.
     - An unsigned Review editorial on Revelation 1, ARSH June 3, 1862, page 4.18 (the writer reports Uriah Smith's Bible class in the third person, "we choose to be alone responsible"; the corpus tags James White; writer not established): "The same, in verse 11, calls himself the Almighty, a name never given to Jesus Christ." At 4.27, the speaker in Rev 1:8 is determined by the terms used, and "The last two are especially applicable to the Father."
     - Uriah Smith, DAR 335.2 (1897), and the same at DAR1909 362.4: "“Which is, and which was, and which is to come,” … referring exclusively to God", with Barnes' manuscript note that "God" may stand for "Lord".
   - **The Son:**
     - J. M. Stephenson, ATO 149.1 (= ARSH November 21, 1854, page 113.13): "It is the testimony of the faithful witness himself … “I am the first and the last,” “I am Alpha and Omega,” “the Lord,” “the Almighty.”"
     - Ellen White, Ms 64, 1895, par. 5 = RH February 18, 1896, par. 5: "It was He that revealed Himself to John on the Isle of Patmos, and proclaimed, “I am Alpha and Omega …, the Almighty.”"
     - RH March 12, 1908, par. 4: the angels cry "Holy, holy, holy, Lord God Almighty" before Christ.
   - **Qualifying both:** Ms 140, 1903, par. 28: "The man Christ Jesus was not the Lord God Almighty, yet Christ and the Father are one."
   - **What the series does:** no narration and no on-screen line calls Jesus "the Almighty" or "the Lord God Almighty", and Rev 1:8 and 4:8 are not used. The script uses only texts where the KJV itself names Jesus as the speaker: Rev 1:17–18 and 22:13, 16, where "Alpha and Omega" is "I Jesus". The credits carry EGW's statements; they are not voiced.
2. **Whom did Isaiah see (Isa 6; John 12:41)?**
   - All the sources below agree that John means Christ's glory. They differ on the "LORD of hosts" of verses 3 and 5.
     - J. H. Waggoner, AERS 170.1 (1884; ARSH 1863): verse 10's speaker is "Adonai, the Son, and not … Jehovah". Verses 3 and 5 are "Jehovah".
     - E. J. Waggoner, SITI July 7, 1887, 407.2: "This, says John, was Jesus whom Isaiah saw in glory". He quotes verses 1–3 whole.
     - SITI November 26, 1885, 711.3 (unsigned Sabbath-school note, tagged Jones; byline not confirmed): "that one of whom these seraphim said, “Holy, holy, holy, is the Lord of hosts” … —that was Christ our Lord".
     - EGW, RH September 18, 1894, par. 3 and Ms 71, 1897, par. 22: the whole vision is Christ's glory.
   - **What the series does:** it quotes Isa 6:1 ("the Lord", Adonai, which every source here gives to the Son) and John 12:41 verbatim. The seraphim's cry is pictured and not narrated.
3. **In what sense is the Son "God"?**
   - AERS 153.1 (1884): the title is used "in a sense subordinate".
   - CHR 22.1 (by 1891): "of the very substance and nature of God".
   - LUJ 10.2 (1898): "equal with the Father".
   - RH April 5, 1906: "God essentially, and in the highest sense".
   - Ms 140, 1903, par. 27: "Equal with the Father".
   - **What the series does:** the script says what PP 34.1 and YI December 16, 1897 say (one with the Father in nature, character and purpose; two, yet almost identical). It quotes 1 Cor 8:6 verbatim and paraphrases John 14:28. It glosses neither side.
4. **The Son's origin.** This is unchanged from the frame (CHR 21.2; LUJ 10.1; DA 530.3). The script does not touch it.
