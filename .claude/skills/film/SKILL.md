# Film

A film lives in `apps/animations/src/films/<film>/`. The engine is the `@bible/film` package (`packages/film`). Its API, the film folder layout and every command are in `apps/animations/README.md`: read it before the first edit. This skill is the workflow and the gotchas the README cannot show. Three references sit beside it:

- **[frame/README.md](frame/README.md)** is the doctrine: the 1889 Fundamental Principles verbatim, and a source-checked topic file for each doctrine, with what a film must show and must not say or show. A film's doctrine comes from these texts, never from memory.
- **[CRAFT.md](CRAFT.md)** is how a film carries its argument: text on screen, quoting, the STORY and IDEA registers, human scale, the shape in time, pauses, sound and the look. It was measured from BibleProject films, and it sets the tone too: informative, curious, hopeful.
- **DIRECTION: [reference/director-vision.md](reference/director-vision.md)** is how a film looks, moves and sounds (a paper theatre lit from behind), with the reasons and numbers behind CRAFT's look, camera and sound rules. Read it before a film's colour script, its first scene, or any change to the paper look.

Every ElevenLabs call and every render needs `dangerouslyDisableSandbox: true` (the CLI's login sits in the Keychain on macOS; Chromium sits in `~/Library/Caches/ms-playwright` on macOS, `~/.cache/ms-playwright` on Linux).

## Corpus, not memory (paramount)

Generate against the corpus, never against training data. A film's doctrine, its argument's shape, every quote, reference, date and attribution, and every picture that teaches come from the frame ([frame/README.md](frame/README.md) and its topic files) and from what the `bible` CLI printed in this session (`bible egw study <subject> --pioneers --export <file> --full`, verse and EGW lookups). Training data carries mainstream framings (for example, justification as a verdict only, with change coming later) and misremembered wording; the corpus is the pioneer record.

- **Fetch, then write.** Each line a beat speaks or shows as a quote is in `sources.md` and `quotes.jsonl`, verbatim with its refcode, and passes the frame's verifier.
- **Frame from the corpus.** The spine of the script (what the doctrine is, what it includes, in what order) is read from the topic file and the pioneers in context, never from a familiar summary. Where they differ, follow the corpus and name the difference in the topic file.
- **A gap stays a gap.** A claim the corpus does not support is cut or marked unverified; memory never fills it.

## Steps

0. **Tools.** `bun run doctor` (with `dangerouslyDisableSandbox: true`) says whether headless Chromium, the logged-in `elevenlabs` CLI and `ffmpeg` (x264 for a software render's share copy; takes load and encode in-process) are there, and how to fix each that is not. `narrate` and `score` check the `elevenlabs` login before spending a credit.

1. **Frame and sources.**
   - Find the film's topic in [frame/README.md](frame/README.md). Read its 1889 principle and the topic file whole.
   - For anything the topic file lacks, build a corpus with `bible egw study <subject> --pioneers --export <file> --full`.
   - Copy each quote you will use into `src/films/<film>/sources.md`, verbatim and with its refcode. Add each one as a record to `src/films/<film>/quotes.jsonl`, and run the frame's verifier over that file (see **Verify** in the frame).
   - Done when every quote the script will speak or show verifies, and you can name, for the topic, what the film must show and what it must not say or show.

2. **Script.** Write `script.ts`: ordered beats `{ id, say, cite, picture }`. Put a `{mark}` before each word a picture must hit. Marks are stripped before speech, so adding one never re-records.
   - Write to [CRAFT.md](CRAFT.md) rules 2–4, 6, 7 and 11: one quotation per beat at most, each `picture` opening with its register, the motifs listed with their payoffs, the viewer's question at each turn, the landing at 70–84%, and the problem in small doses.
   - In the header beside the motifs, list 3–5 tent-pole frames, each with its act's lighting (CRAFT rule 11), and the question that opens each act: those become the chapters.
   - Hold every line against the topic's Film direction.
   - `bun run dev` plays undrawn beats as storyboard cards at estimated timing.
   - Done when the storyboard reads as the argument, start to finish, and quoted words make up 20% of the script or less.

3. **Voice.** ElevenLabs stages the voice; the owner's own recordings replace it beat by beat.
   - **Stage.** `voice.ts` names one reader (one entry, no `{@name}` turns): a film has one narrator, who asks the viewer's question too ([CRAFT.md](CRAFT.md) rule 6). `bun run narrate <film>` records stale beats and transcribes each take back. A take over 8% word error fails the run with `TakeMismatch` and is not kept: re-record it with `--only <id>`, or keep it with `--accept-mismatch <id>` when the transcript is wrong and the take is right (it names its beats; bare, only with `--only`). The check reads numbers, years, scripture references and `Mrs.`-style abbreviations as said; a name the transcriber spells another way goes in `script.ts` as `export const heardAs = { Ellet: ['Elliot'] }`. `--dry-run` lists every beat as `recorded` (a person read it), `staging` (ElevenLabs) or `stale` (with why) without recording.
   - **Settings stay at stability 0.5, similarity 0.75.** A sweep of eleven_v4 over stability 0.3/0.5/0.7 and similarity 0.5/0.75/0.9 on three beats (2026-09-29) moved pace by at most 6 wpm, sentence-end pauses by 0.06 s and word error by one word at most: the settings do not fix a take. A take that reads rushed, flat or mis-stressed is fixed in the line (a comma or a full stop where the breath belongs, a shorter sentence) and re-recorded with `--only <id>`.
   - **Print the sheet.** `bun run script <film> --sheet` writes `out/<film>/script-sheet.md` and `script-sheet.html` (print it): per beat, the file to save the take as (`<beat>.wav`), the line with its marks stripped, each quotation set apart with the source `quotes.jsonl` verifies it in, and `/` for a breath at each sentence end.
   - **Record, then import.** The owner records each beat into its own file (WAV or FLAC; M4A or MP3 import with a `takes.lossy` warning; any rate) and `bun run takes import <film> <folder>` (with `dangerouslyDisableSandbox: true`: the speech-to-text call) makes each a take: trimmed to the staging takes' padding (0.07 s lead, no tail), levelled to their speech loudness by one clean gain (−17.1 dBFS speech level, pauses left out; less when the peak would pass −1 dBFS; no limiter; clicks after the line trimmed, 5 ms fades), kept as a 24-bit FLAC master (`<beat>.<hash>.flac`: the final voice, never lossy after the recorder; the original file stays beside it in `attempts/`), transcribed and timed by the words heard, then the film remixes. One file imports as the beat it is named for, or as `--only <id>`; `--whole` takes one recording of the whole script and cuts each beat at the quietest point of the silence around it (a flubbed line read again keeps the reading that finished it; a beat it cannot find fails `BeatUnplaced`, naming it: record that beat alone). The same `TakeMismatch` and `--accept-mismatch` hold. Every recording is kept under `narration/attempts/<beat>/` (git-ignored); only the take the timings name is committed. The lab serves the same import as a studio at `/lab/<film>/studio/*` (packages/film README, Lab → Studio): record in the lab's **Studio** section. Pick the beat, read it off the teleprompter, R records after a 3 s count-in (the meter warns at −1 dBFS), Space stops, play it back, K submits; the status line shows what was heard and the word error, or the server's refusal, and a `TakeMismatch` offers Accept anyway (K). Each beat's attempts play again and Keep makes one the take. A kept take reloads the lab at the same T, playing the new take. The capture is raw PCM (no echo cancelling, noise suppression or gain control) posted as a 24-bit WAV at the microphone's rate; the mic picked is remembered per browser. Recording to files and `takes import` still work.
   - **Staging never overwrites a person's take.** `narrate` skips a recorded beat (`narrate.skip`), even under `--force` or `--only`. When its line changes, the take goes stale (`check` fails `TakeStale ... recorded take`) and `narrate` refuses to stage over it (`narrate.refused`) unless `--replace-recorded`: record the line again and import it instead. A change of staging voice leaves recorded takes current.
   - **Tighten.** Then check, cues and mix as for any take, and read the seams from `bun run cues <film>` (`seam=` on each line; [CRAFT.md](CRAFT.md) rule 9; `check --static` warns `SeamLong` on one over 0.6 s that no `lead`, `tail`, or `min` long enough to stretch the scene declares) and tighten each scene's `lead` and `tail`; that re-times the film without re-recording. Done when every beat is recorded with no mismatch and every seam is 0.6 s or less, except the pauses the script means.

4. **Scenes.** One `Drawing` per beat in `scenes/`, drawn to [CRAFT.md](CRAFT.md) rules 1, 3, 5 and 8: one text element at a time and never the narration, the beat's register, a face at human scale, and a callback in its earlier scene's layout. Pin every motion to a mark (`f.mark`, `f.spoken`), never to a hand-timed second. A moment anything else reads (a sound, another cue) is declared once as a named cue: `drawing({ timeline: { slam: { mark: 'fiction', offset: 0.9, dur: 0.35 } }, draw })`; a motion that must reach a word ends on it with `until: 'mark'` in place of `dur`, never a hand-sized `dur`; a motion that must land on its moment in a set time `ends` there, `{ mark: 'true', dur: 0.5, ends: true }`, never `offset: -0.5, dur: 0.5` (`film/span-ends-on-anchor` refuses it); a beat on a word with no mark is a word pin, `{ mark: 'gift', word: 'faith', dur: 0.6 }` (the first word at or after the mark that reads it, in any case, apostrophes dropped and a hyphenated part on its own; a line that never says it fails with `WordMissing`, and `check` warns `WordPinFar` when it lands more than a sentence past the mark), never `mark + seconds` (`bun run lint` refuses a literal second in a draw and a span offset over 1 s from its mark or the scene's start: `film/no-hand-timed-seconds`; a pause the script means is a named cue off `speechEnd` or another cue, its reason in a comment); read with `f.at('slam')` (eased by the span's `ease`, data like the rest of the span; `f.at` takes no ease), `f.keys('slam', [[0, 1.2], [0.6, 0.97, 'outCubic'], [1, 1]])` (keyframes in fractions of the cue, so a `dur` edit stretches them; a key naming no ease takes the span's), `f.stagger('drop', i, n)` (item `i` of `n` across a span with `stagger: 0.8`, the share of the cue their starts spread over; never `start + i * 0.15`) or `f.cue('slam')`; a set spread by place reads `f.staggerAt('green', field.reach)`. A part of a cue (a fill over its last tenth) is its own cue, `{ after: 'into', dur: 0.1, ends: true }`, never `clamp((f.at('into') - 0.75) / 0.25)`, and a cue's progress is never eased again, `ease.inCubic(f.at('plunge'))` (`film/no-ease-on-cue`): another curve is its own cue or `f.keys`. Never repeat its time elsewhere. A position or angle a review may tweak is a knob: `knobs: { palm: [960, 800] }`, read with `f.knob('palm')`; read it under the transform it is drawn with, so the lab's handle lands on it. A framing is a point and a zoom knob, `face: [800, 610], faceZoom: 1.22`, made a camera in the draw with `knobCamera(f.knob('face'), f.knob('faceZoom'))` (`@bible/film/canvas`; a film's kit re-exports it); only the unmoved frame and a derived framing stay constants; a callback that frames what another scene framed reads that scene's knob there, `f.knobsOf(thesis)('city')` (`film check` warns `KnobRepeated` on a point knob copied from another scene). Beats pair with drawings by id in `scenes/index.ts`, `scenesOf(script, { drawings, light, card })` (the script is `defineScript([...])`, so a drawing keyed by an id the script lacks does not compile; `check` warns `Storyboard` for each beat still a card), and a light that moves on a scene's own cue is that drawing's `light: (f) => …`, typed by its cues. For many scenes, fork agents in parallel: each gets its own `scenes/group-N.ts` registry and `props-N.ts`, may not edit `packages/film/` (the engine), `kit.ts` or `scenes/index.ts`, and reports engine bugs rather than working around them. For depth, draw every STORY shot as 3–5 `multiplane` planes (the wall far, the people near), their distances roughly 1 : 3 : 7, rather than one flat layer; a camera move then shows parallax. Every drawn scene breathes once by `DRIFT` (a 3 % push and a 24 px slide that peak mid-scene and settle back, so cuts and callbacks still meet as framed): through its outermost `camera` or `multiplane`, or around its whole draw when it frames neither. A shot's `drift` is the share of the breath it takes: pass `drift: 0` (a `multiplane` depth's `drift`, or `camera`'s last argument) only where the script means stillness; a whole scene that must not breathe (an end card) sets `drawing({ drift: 0, … })`, its own breath in place of `DRIFT`, and `driftHeld(f.at(cue))` to ease into it. The backdrop plane carries the paper's fibre in its own space, so a pan slides the grain with the paper (`depth.fibre`, 0 for none); the screen grain is only a faint film layer, so never add grain of your own. Every piece of paper names its role, `piece(ctx, shape, color, hand, { role: 'figure' | 'scenery' })`: a figure is cut and outlined, scenery torn with no ink; a made thing in the scenery (furniture, a card, a tablet) adds `kind: 'cut'`, a prop's letters `kind: 'ink'`. The role also sets the boil: a figure's edge and line crawl (at most 0.3 px a tick) and scenery's hold still. A raw `stroke` boils as ink, redrawn every tick, so give one that is part of a person, creature or what they hold `boil: 'crawl'`, and one that is part of the world (stems, panels, folds, paths, smoke) `boil: 'none'`. A stroke round a closed path (a ring, a panel's edge) adds `closed: true`, so it meets itself with no notch; `piece` outlines already do. A standing `person` casts its own contact shadow (the engine's `ground`), fading as it sits; give it `ground: 0` when nothing is under the feet (flying, on the cross, in bed), and lay `ground(ctx, x, y, w)` yourself only under furniture and props. Hands come from the kit's arms only, and a glow sits on a plane behind the figure, never as fog in front of it. A camera that moves through several framings is one `shotPath(REST, [[f.at('push'), FACE], [f.at('back'), REST]])`, never nested blends. A push is a stop to a knob camera with `pushInto`, `[f.at('plunge'), knobCamera(f.knob('page'), f.knob('pageZoom')), pushInto]`, and a held close-up that keeps pushing in is `pushOn(cam, f.knob('pushOn'), f.at('hold'))`. Hands float and no arm is ever drawn: a person's `far` or `near` hand at work is `{ to, reach, grip }`, its target a point in the person's units (what the hand holds, lifts or reaches for), its `reach` a named cue (`f.at('grasp') * (1 - f.at('letGo'))`, a hand held from the cut `{ at: 'start', dur: 0 }`), never a threshold, a switch or a hand-timed second; with no gesture the hand floats at rest beside the body, bobbing with the breath. `handOf(p, side, hand)` says where the hand is, so what it holds rides it. A big hand is `handCloseUp`, pushed or cut to from the figure just seen and matched back (CRAFT rule 12): the figure's own hand first turns palm up on a short named cue (`{ ..., turn: f.at('palmUp') }`, the close-up's shape at the mitten's size), and `pushedHand` grows the same shape out of it and back. Settle the look across scenes with `bun run lookbook <film>` (Lab loop, step 0) before refining any one. Done when no beat plays as a storyboard card.

5. **Review.** `bun run check <film>` first: it samples every mark, cue edge and 60% point and fails on text over text (captions included), text cut off by the frame, a stroke through a line of text (`InkOverText`), a text plate the frame crops (`PlateOffFrame`), a line that runs off the plate it is declared on (`TextOffPlate`), cues past their scene, unknown sound cues, stale takes or sound, and an audio master that is missing, not the film's length or mixed for another plan (a score pick, a re-take or a moved effect since the last `mix`). It warns `StaticHold` (scene, from–to) where a drawn scene's voice speaks for more than 4 s with no cue running and nothing moving for over 4 s of it (probed a boil tick apart; captions and boil aside, at any zoom): pin a motion to a mark there, or cut; a storyboard card is exempt, `--static` cannot see it, and a colour change or drawing the probe cannot see reads as still, so look first. The look pass (every scene at 2 fps, 64×36 grey) warns `HeldShare` where a spoken scene holds still for over 40 % of its seconds, boil counted as still (the default drift is too gentle for its 64×36 thumbnails to count, so slide a plane or push on the turn); `FaceSmall` where no face the kit declares (`probeFace` in its person) reaches a third of the frame's height; `HandJump` where a floating hand (`floatingHand`, the kit's one hand, declares its own when given its body, `HandBody`) moves more than 1.5 of its length about its shoulder, or changes size by over a quarter, in one frame (every frame across each hand's travel is drawn; a pan, zoom or scaled figure is no jump); `HandFar` where a hand is sent to a target past its figure's reach; `HandHidden` where a hand at work sits inside its own body and is drawn behind it; and `ColourScript` where an act of `film.ts`'s `look` measures outside its luma, saturation or dark target. It warns `EndShort` under 20 s after the last word or a 5 s end card, and fails `DeadAir` where the mix, rendered in memory as `mix` would make it now, sits under −60 dBFS for over 1.5 s that no cue declares `silence: true`. `--scene id,id` probes just those scenes; a misspelt id fails with `UnknownScene`. Fix each finding in the scene, or, when the scene is right and the finding is wrong, tighten the detector in `packages/film/src/tools/check.ts` (never add an exception for one film). A text on a plate declares the plate with `probePlate(ctx, shape, () => write(…))`: the shape the plate was drawn with, and every line on it drawn inside (one plate per card, however many lines). Then, for the look: `bun run render <film> --scene <id> --contact 1.5 --variant <name>` (`out/<film>/scenes/<id>/<name>/contact.jpg`) and full-size `--stills <t>` at the marks; read the images. To have the owner review scene by scene: `bun run project render <film> --scale 0.33` renders each scene on its own into `out/<film>/scenes/<id>/main.mp4`, skips a scene whose render is still current (its stamp's key matches its sources now, and it carries the mix the film makes now) and re-muxes one stale by its sound alone (after a score pick, a level or a kept take, then `mix`: its sound cut again from the master, nothing drawn); `bun run project <film>` lists each scene current, stale, stale:sound or missing, its approval and its comments; the owner approves with `bun run project approve <film> --all` (every current scene) or `--scene id`, and comments with `bun run project comment <film> "text" --scene <id>` (or `--act <name>`; neither, and it is said of the film). A re-render or a re-mux leaves an approval stale, never deleted. A stroke that marks a line on purpose (underline, swash, ring, strike) declares the lines it marks in its style, `marks: [f.hand('<key>')]`: the very hand that wrote each line. Ink drawn on the raw context (`ctx.stroke()`, `fillText`) is invisible to the check; `bun run lint` refuses it (`film/no-unprobed-ink`): draw it with the kit, or wrap texture that never crosses text in `unprobed(ctx, () => …)`. The check samples moments, not every frame: a ring sweeping through a quote between two marks is still found by eye. Last, hold each contact sheet against the topic's MUST NOT SAY-OR-SHOW, because a picture teaches as loudly as a line does: for example, Christ's work closing at the cross, or a soul rising at death. Done when `check` is green, every `StaticHold` and `HeldShare` is fixed or meant, every contact sheet reads well, and no frame shows what the frame forbids.

6. **Sound.** No film is done without a `sound.ts`: the score is not optional ([CRAFT.md](CRAFT.md) rule 10). Declare the score in it (`score: { play, under, alone, options }`: each option a whole score in its own musical language, in `movements` of 3–120 s that start at scenes (the score's own turns, not the film's acts); `play` names the one the mix plays; `under` about −18.5 and `alone` about −6, dB against the voice) and the effects, then `bun run score <film> --dry-run` (each option's movements and credits), then `bun run score <film> --cap n --tally file`. `bun run mix <film> --score <option>` plays another option; the generated files stay out of the repo (`sfx push`/`pull` keep them in the private store). An effect that hits a picture event references the scene's cue, `{ scene, cue, edge: 'start' | 'end' }`, never a `mark + offset` copy of the scene's arithmetic; one whose loudest moment is the event (a thud, a page landing) adds `sync: 'hit'` so each take's recorded hit lands on the cue, never an `offset: -0.3` sized to one take's lead-in (`bun run sfx describe` records takes kept before the lock carried it); a sound on a word takes `{ scene, mark }`, a bed that hands over to the next scene ends at its voice, `{ scene, at: 'speech' }` (never `offset: 0.4`, a copy of its `lead`), and a scene-start sound (page, slide) takes that landmark, `{ scene, at: 'start', offset }`. A point names one anchor, as a short's does. `bun run cues <film> --sound` prints every placement's film time. Place effects on the story's concrete nouns, not the medium's page turns ([CRAFT.md](CRAFT.md) rule 10). Balance by the `mix.levels` lines `bun run mix <film>` logs (each bus's mean and peak dBFS over the film). `--stems` writes each bus the film's length, to hear alone. Done when `bun run check <film>` finds no `DeadAir`, `MasterLoudness` or `EffectHot` (an effect whose loudest 50 ms comes within 3 dB of the voice around it: lower its level, or re-roll a take whose thud is the problem): a silence the script means is a cue with `silence: true`.
   - The bed sits 17 to 20 dB under the voice wherever anyone speaks, short gaps included.
   - Music comes up to about 6 dB under the voice only where no one speaks: the landing's 25 to 35 s, and an optional open.
   - The low end swells on the problem and the climax. Generated music carries little under 70 Hz, and a style like "sub bass swell" barely moves it: ask by instrument (bowed basses on the lowest notes, a bass pedal tone swelling, a soft low timpani roll), then measure the band per act from the `music.<option>` stem.
   - The mix masters the summed track to −18 LUFS by one gain (`MASTER`; `mix.master gain=` logs it), capped so it never pushes a peak into the limiter; a `MasterLoudness` finding therefore means a hot peak held the lift back.
   - The last act is composed 6 s past the film's end (`MUSIC_TAIL`), so a composed ending that decays to silence lands after the cut, not in the end screens as `DeadAir`.
   - Done when the stems measure in range and the score transcribes as instrumental (`elevenlabs speech-to-text convert --model-id scribe_v1`).

7. **Render.** `bun run render <film> --no-captions` (the captions ship as the `.vtt`; [CRAFT.md](CRAFT.md) rule 1) writes the master `out/<film>/film/main.mp4`, a share copy `main.share.mp4`, and `main.vtt` captions beside it, plus `main.chapters.txt` (the YouTube chapters `look.acts` name, declared once in the film's `acts.ts`; `bun run chapters <film>` prints them) when the film declares a look. One part renders or checks by its address: `--act name`, `--scene id,id` or `--short id`, one at a time; each lands in its address's folder (`acts/<act>/`, `scenes/<id>/`, `shorts/<id>/`) and is recorded in `out/<film>/catalogue.json`, which is what the review lists. `--variant <name>` renders beside `main`. The page chooses one H.264 encoder before drawing and every page uses it; the render logs the choice (`render.encoder kind=hardware|software workers=… encoders=… cores=…`), and the `encoder` line of `bun run doctor` says which one this machine gets. On the Mac it is the hardware encoder and nothing else: about two minutes for a six-minute film, the share copy (about 160 MB) encoded in the same pass; a Mac whose GPU encoder fails stops with `EncoderMissing` rather than silently changing the look (`--encoder software` renders there in software on purpose). On a Linux box it is Chromium's software encoder, with the share copy made after the join by x264 from the master (`render.share by=x264 secs=…`): about 5 minutes for righteousness-by-faith (457 s) on the 16-core Workbox, 2.4 of them the pages and join at 8 pages and 2.5 the x264 pass; the master is about 765 MB and the share about 126 MB (`packages/film/README.md`, "Encoders"). With no allowed encoder, the render stops with `EncoderMissing` before it draws. It takes its audio from `narration/full.wav`; if a render stops with `AudioMissing` or `AudioStale` (a mix cut short, or made before a re-timing), run `bun run mix <film>` (free). Ctrl-C is safe: the render's scope closes every page, the browser and the server. For multi-language audio, keep the dry voice and the music-and-effects stems beside it: `bun run mix <film> --stems`. Write a contact sheet with `bun run render <film> --contact 6` (`out/<film>/film/main/contact.jpg`, drawn by the same pages), read it, then `open` the MP4 for the user. Done when the sheet shows every scene and the file plays.

8. **Shorts.** Cut vertical shorts from the finished film; never draw them again. Declare each in the film's `shorts.ts`: `{ id, title, hook, spans: [{ scene, from, to }] }` (the `hook` is the narrator's own question or claim, typed above the picture for the first 2–3 s; set its font in the film's `short` style), each point a `{ mark }`, a named `{ cue }` or a landmark (`{ at: 'speech' }`, `'speechEnd'`, `'start'`, `'end'`), never a second; a span opening on a mark or the speech opens 0.1 s before its word is heard, so a late hook or a stalled loop is fixed by the span's marks, never by the rule. Register its pages in `src/films/index.ts`: spread `shortPages('<film>', load, shorts)` into `pages` (the player's), never into `films` (one key per film folder). `bun run cues <film> --short <id>` prints each span's film time and the length; a scene, mark or cue the film lacks fails naming what it has. `bun run render <film> --short <id>` writes `out/<film>/shorts/<id>/main.mp4` and `main.vtt` at 1080×1920, the track cut to the same spans, the captions burned in below the picture two to four words at a time with quoted words marked gold as they are read (the `.vtt` carries the same phrases; the long film's captions do not change); `--contact 2` and `--stills <t>` take the short's seconds. Run `bun run check <film> --short <id>` (add `--zone ads` for a paid cut): text under the platform's buttons (`ShortUnsafeText`), an open that does not hook (`ShortHook`: first word heard after 0.3 s, its voice measured from the take, nothing moving by 0.5 s, the title card first), a loop that shows its seam (`ShortLoop`, a warning) and a length over 90 s (error) or outside 45–75 s (warning); a film's own text in the band is only a warning, fixed by choosing another span, never by redrawing the scene. Read the contact sheet, then `open` the MP4. Done when the short plays whole, every span starts and ends on a word boundary, and the check has no errors.

## Lab loop

The user reviews in the lab and the agent answers each note with the frame after the change.

0. **Look-book first, for a new film.** Before building a new film's scenes (and again once the first few exist), `bun run lookbook <film>` writes `out/<film>/film/main/lookbook.jpg`: the palette as swatches, then each scene's row of stills at every cue's start (▸) and end (◂) and its 60% point, labelled; it then prints each scene's and each declared act's mean luma, dark share, saturation, top colours, held share and largest face, against the act's target. Read the sheet as an image and the numbers against the colour script, and settle the look (palette, figure scale, type, where text sits) across scenes before refining any one of them. The lab's **Look-book** link (panel header, `?film=<film>&lookbook`) composes the same sheet live from the code as it is now; a click on a still opens that frame in the lab. `--captions` burns captions in.
1. **Open it.** `bun run lab <film>` (background, `dangerouslyDisableSandbox: true`) prints `http://127.0.0.1:4401/lab?film=<film>` (the lab is its own page, Solid panels around the preview; the render page never loads it; it listens on the loopback interface only, and its API answers only its own page: same-origin JSON writes); hand the URL to the user. It runs until Ctrl-C and hot-reloads scenes as they change; a line fixed in the script or scenes while it runs reaches the studio's sheet and takes, and a cue's answer, at the next read (the lab reads them in a fresh process: `film read voice|cue`), with no restart. A film not mixed yet plays silent and the time line says `no narration` (run `bun run mix <film>`). If the page loses the server (a restart), its notes panel says `notes offline: …; trying again` and reconnects every 2 s on its own: no reload needed.
2. **Watch.** List first, then watch from the cursor the list printed, so nothing made between the two is lost:
   - `bun run notes <film>` (in `apps/animations`) prints each open note, then `cursor seq=<N>` as its last line.
   - Start a Monitor on `bun run notes <film> --watch --since <N>`. Its first line is `watch since=<N>`; then each new note and each user reply arrives once as a line, and every line carries its change number: `note id=n3 seq=3 status=open scene=hand T=230.38 frame=6911 cue=topple:end mark=hand box=760,560,400x400 … still=/…/stills/n3.png text="…"`, `reply id=n3 seq=5 by=user …`.
   - **Resume** a Monitor that stopped (or the session that ran it) with `--since <the last seq= it printed>`: nothing is printed twice and nothing made while it was down is skipped. `--since 0` replays everything.
3. **Read the note.** Read the `still` as an image; the box (canvas pixels, a pin is `w`×`h` 0) and the nearest cue and mark say where and when. A timing note is a cue's `offset`/`dur` (or `until`)/`ease`; a position is a knob. If the value is still a constant, promote it to a knob or a cue first (a one-line change that draws the same frame).
4. **Change it.** Timing and position are data, and the lab writes data to source:
   - **Cues**: drag a cue on the strip under the timeline (body = offset, edges = start/end; a bar too short for edges is all body, alt-drag for its end; snaps to words, marks and frames, shift for free; an `until` cue keeps ending on its mark unless its right edge is dropped off it; Esc during a drag puts it back) or set `offset`, `dur` and `ease` in the inspector (each ease's curve is drawn).
   - **Knobs**: number inputs, and a handle on the frame for a point knob. The handle follows the knob through the canvas transform it was read under (inside `at(...)`, scaled, tilted) or, for a knob read before a camera (a camera's target, a pole the shot pushes in on), through that camera, and a drag maps back into the knob's own units, so drag it where it is drawn. A camera's target (`face` beside `faceZoom`, as `knobCamera` reads them) is a reticle: while the camera sits on it, dragging moves the picture with the pointer, so the target moves the other way. A knob read inside a transition's layer, or under two transforms in one frame, gets numbers only; the inspector says why.
   - Each release rewrites the literal in the scene's `.ts` file (oxfmt'd, read back) and the page reloads at the same `T` and selection, with speed, loop, onion, compare and play as they were. The lab refuses a value that is not a literal, or a timing the scene's timeline would not resolve with, and names it; **Undo** (⌘Z) puts the newest write back and **Redo** (⇧⌘Z) makes it again, over the last 50 writes; neither runs over a change made since (say, an edit in your editor). A write the server has not answered in 20 s is refused, naming the timeout: check `git diff` before writing again. One write is out at a time: a drag, a field or an Undo while one is in flight is not taken, so wait for the status line to say what landed.
   - Review every write with `git diff -- src/films/<film>/scenes/`; the diff is the record of what changed.
   - Anything else (drawing, layout, a new element): edit the scene code yourself.
5. **See the motion, not just the frame.** In the panel's **Motion** section: **Onion** ghosts the frames around the one shown (± count, every N frames; warm before, cool after) to judge an arc or spacing on a paused frame; **0.25× / 0.5×** slow the clock (narration mutes off 1×); **loop cue** loops the cue selected on the strip (a very short cue gets padding either side), **A / B** loop any range (B after A plays from A; a B before A waits for a new B). Use them for any timing or easing note before and after the change.
6. **Compare with HEAD.** The panel's **Compare** section draws the same frame as HEAD declared the scene's timeline and knobs: **wipe** puts HEAD left of a draggable divider and now to its right, **blink** flips between them; when HEAD cannot give the scene the section shows the server's reason, and when HEAD's timeline names a mark today's narration lacks it says so and draws no layer. It compares data only; when the scene's code changed since HEAD the panel says so, and for a code change render stills instead.
7. **Check after every edit.** The lab runs `film check --static --json` after each write (the film's files only: no mix, no browser) and lists its findings in the panel, each with its address (scene, film second). After a sound change, `bun run check <film> --sound` adds the mix (`DeadAir`, `MasterLoudness`, `EffectHot`) without the browser. After editing code, run `bun run check <film> --scene <id>` (the layout leg: `TextOverlap`, `TextOffFrame`, `InkOverText`, `PlateOffFrame`, `TextOffPlate`, and the `StaticHold`, `HeldShare`, `FaceSmall`, `HandJump`, `HandFar` and `HandHidden` warnings) and fix what it finds before answering.
8. **Prove it.** Render the note's frame: `bun run render <film> --stills <T> --variant lab-<id>`, and read the after-still as an image. It must show the note's problem gone, at the note's `T`.
9. **Answer.** `bun run notes reply <film> <id> "what changed" --still out/<film>/film/lab-<id>/stills/t0230.38.png` (a still is named by its time, zero-padded to seven characters with two decimals: 8.14 s is `t0008.14.png`, 230.38 s is `t0230.38.png`). The lab shows the reply and the still at once; the note becomes `replied`. After the reply's own line it prints what the user said since your previous reply (new notes, user replies, each with `seq=`), then `cursor seq=<N>`: answer those too before you stop. The user resolves it in the lab (or replies, which reopens it, and the Monitor hands it back). `bun run notes resolve <film> <id>` only when the user said OK.

## Options loop: make options, compare, pick

When the owner is choosing between ways a film could go (a look, a score, a take of a sound, a voice take, a level), make the options, put them side by side in the review, and let the owner pick there. The pick lands in source, in exactly one place.

1. **Make the options.**
   - _A look_ declared in `palette.ts`'s `looks` (`ground`: `now`, `light`, `lighter`; `play` names the one drawn) is a choice point; to see a level, set `play` to it, render `--variant <level>` (the name only labels the output), and set `play` back.
   - _A staging_ (no pick in source): render each variant of the same span with `--variant <name>`: the catalogue lists them as one set at their address (`main` first). A montage folder of files made by hand is listed only with a `review.json` that names its files (`videos`, `images`, `docs`, and per set each variant, by default `<clip>.<variant>.mp4`) and adds a title, the order, labels, a tag and a verdict per variant, notes (markdown), a start and moments; a variant that lies elsewhere is pulled in with its `file`.
   - _A score_: declare each option in `sound.ts`'s `score.options` (one musical language each, the same movements), `bun run score <film> --dry-run`, then `bun run score <film>` (paid: only with the owner's go-ahead). `play` names the one the mix plays.
   - _A sound's takes_: `bun run sfx plan <name>`, then `bun run sfx make <name> --yes` (paid: only with the owner's go-ahead). The takes wait as candidates.
2. **Serve them.** The review runs as the box's unit (`film-review.service` on port 8229: every checkout's `out/`, and the scratchpad's montages) or by hand: `bun run review` (loopback 8229; `REVIEW_HOST=0.0.0.0` with `FILM_REVIEW_HOSTS=<host:port,…>` to reach it from a phone). Hand the owner the link: a set is `/?folder=<ref>&set=<point id>`, a film's choices `/?film=<film>`, its scenes to approve `/?project=<film>`. A new render shows on the next **Refresh**; a hand edit to `sound.ts`'s options or to a prompt in `sounds/library.ts` shows on the next read (the review reads each film, and keeps, unkeeps and rejects takes, in a fresh process: `film options list <film> [--check]` prints what it sees, `film options take` changes the lock).
3. **Compare.** A set plays every variant on one clock: all of them, the first against one other (`&view=pair&other=<id>`), every variant's frame at the moments, or the notes. A film's page plays its newest render with the sound heard over it: 🔊 on a score option hears the film's whole mix with it; 🔊 on a take hears the film's mix with only that take at every placement, and a placement's time jumps there. Each take also plays alone.
4. **Pick.** The owner picks on the page, or you do on the owner's word through the same routes (`POST /lab/<film>/choices/pick` `{point, variant, verb}`, `…/knob` `{point, value}`): **Pick** writes `play` in `sound.ts` (score) or `palette.ts` (look); on a take, **Pick**, **Unpick** and **Reject** run the library's own keep, unkeep and reject on `library.lock.json` in a fresh process (`film options take <film> --point --variant --verb`, as `bun run sfx keep|unkeep|reject` does); on a voice attempt it keeps the take (`film options keep-voice`); a level's knob writes its one literal in `sound.ts`. Every write is formatted, verified, checked (`check --static`, shown under the buttons; after a pick or a knob, `check --sound` too) and undoable: **Undo** puts the file back byte for byte while it is as the write left it. Approve and comment on any variant; a render is approved, never picked. Scenes are approved in the project view (`bun run project approve <film> --scene id | --act name | --all`, or "Approve all current" on the page).
5. **Review the pick.** `git diff -- src/films/<film>/sound.ts src/films/<film>/palette.ts sounds/library.lock.json` is the record of what was picked. Then `bun run mix <film>` and `bun run check <film>`, and commit.

The review writes only a film's `sound.ts` (`play`, levels), `palette.ts` (a look's `play`), its timings (a voice kept), its catalogue (approvals, comments) and the library's lock; scene edits stay in the lab (`bun run lab <film>`, loopback only). A take is picked for its whole sound, never for one placement: a moment that needs its own take is its own library sound.

## Speed

Speed lives in the framework (`@bible/film`), never in one film's scenes. A change to the draw path or the render claims its speed with a before and after on one machine under the same load (other renders running at once move the numbers): the render's own log (`render.done` frames and wall time, `dangerouslyDisableSandbox: true`; each `render.progress` line and `render.done` also split a frame's page time into `draw_ms`, `encode_ms` and `transfer_ms`), or a scratch script that times the draw alone over sampled frames through the export handle's `drawTimes` (`Pages`, `tools/pages.ts`). A change is reviewed by its diff: the code says what a frame draws. Renders are for the owner reviewing new scenes, looks and scores. A render runs one encoder a page (two on the Mac with its in-page share copy), and its budget is the chosen encoder's: hardware (the Mac) at most 14 at once (past 14 it hangs), default 6 pages; software (Linux) at most one per core, default 8 pages or half the cores on a smaller machine (8 pages 98 fps against 84 at 6 and 104 at 10, `--no-share`, 16 cores). Past the budget `render` fails with `TooManyEncoders` before a frame is drawn, naming the encoder and the `--workers` that fits. On Linux a render with the share copy on also runs the x264 pass after the join; pass `--no-share` to time the pages alone.

## Gotchas

- **Parallel renders share the encoder.** `TooManyEncoders` counts one render's pages only (one encoder a page, two on the Mac with the share copy, at most 14 on hardware, one per core on software). On the Mac two renders at once can pass it and still hang together: keep their pages within 14 in sum, or run them one after the other. On Linux they only slow each other.
- **Linux encodes in software.** The GPU launch flags (`--enable-gpu`, `--use-angle=metal`) go to macOS only (`launchArgs`, `tools/browser.ts`); stills are pixel-identical either way. Software H.264 (OpenH264) refuses quantizer rate control, so the master takes a bitrate (37 Mbps), each chunk opens on two dropped pre-roll frames (its first key frame would otherwise be coded at a fixed, flat quantizer: the paper's grain blinked every 8 s), and the share copy is x264's (CRF 22, slow, tune grain) from the joined master, not the page's: the page kept the grain only at 24 Mbps, 1.16 GB. Don't swap in `@mediabunny/server` for it: its libx264 runs a fixed `qp` with no CRF or tune, and kept far less grain at the same size.
- **`film doctor` with the player down** prints `missing encoder … the player server did not start` and still prints every other line.
- **Playwright browser missing**: `render` fails with `BrowserMissing`, whose message is the exact install command for the installed playwright-core (the cache in `~/Library/Caches/ms-playwright`, or `~/.cache/ms-playwright` on Linux, was wiped). Run it with `dangerouslyDisableSandbox: true`.
- **Sound effects return 401** under the CLI's OAuth login, which covers speech, speech-to-text and music only. Effects need `ELEVENLABS_API_KEY` in the environment or in the Keychain under that service name; `score` skips them without one. The OAuth token also cannot create keys.
- **Music plans:** `music_v2` and `music_v2_5` take `{ chunks: [...] }`; a v1 `sections` plan fails with "Invalid type of composition_plan".
- **Timing changes:** changing a scene's `lead`, `tail` or `min` moves every later scene. Remix with `bun run mix <film>` (no API cost); the score goes stale and `score` regenerates it.
- **`math` (pmndrs) stays behind the kit.** It is pinned and reached only through engine and kit functions (the normals of `stroke` and `cutout` over `vec2.normalize`), never from a scene. Its `spring` is stepped by a delta, so it has no place in a draw, which must be a pure function of `f.t`; the kit keeps its own `random.ts`, which every committed frame depends on.
- **A held wide with only boil moving reads as still.** Line boil and a small figure's gesture inside a locked wide do not register across a room: the camera drifts or a plane slides, or the shot cuts in.
- **Faces read as cross by default.** Level brows on a small grey head look stern; a face meant curious, hopeful or tender raises the inner ends (`browTilt` > 0 in a film kit's `person`), and a glad one smiles (`smile` −1..1; `eyes` 1..0 shuts them, a happy crescent past `smile` 0.5). Check faces at full size (`--stills`), not only on the contact sheet.
- **Shell loops:** zsh does not word-split `$var`; run loops over time windows with `bash -c`.
- **Commits:** the pre-commit hook lints and formats the staged files (under 5 s) and runs the repo guards (sound licence, no renders) on them. The gate (`bun run guard`: lint, format check, the repo guards over every tracked file; then typecheck, build, tests, perf) runs in CI (`.github/workflows/gate.yml`, one run per commit on main) and by hand between units: `bun run gate`. After a push, `bun run ci` waits for the commit's run and prints its verdict.
