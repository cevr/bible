# "real life" (@ilove-reallife): editing breakdown

Primary: `3ug4AxXk__M` "why liking things is embarrassing" (4:28.88, 1280x720, 60 fps, uploaded 2026-10-06).
Secondary: `xip4z6_fsls` "why old clothes cost more" (5:20.30, 1280x720, 60 fps, uploaded 2026-10-09).

Measured 2026-10-11 for the owner, who named this channel as the films' editing reference ("how immediately it enters into the story… the punchy full screen text"). It is the source of [CRAFT.md](../CRAFT.md) rule 1's full-screen cards. The contact sheets and media it mentions were working files and are not kept.

## How I measured this, and how sure each kind of claim is

- **Frame-accurate (±1 frame at 60 fps):** cut times, card start and end frames, and word-entry frames. Cuts were found from a per-frame mean-absolute-difference spike (diff > 3 and > 2.5× its neighbours). I kept a spike if more than 12 % of pixels changed by more than 30 levels. I checked the whole open and every ambiguous cut by eye on frame strips, and added transitions the detector misses by hand (slides, irises, inset expansions). Inside 0–45 s every cut was checked by eye. Outside it the list is detector-plus-spot-check, so treat the counts as ±3.
- **Card colours:** sampled from 720p frames. **Font family:** a guess from the letter shapes.
- **Spoken words:** come from YouTube's English auto-captions, whose word times run about ±0.1–0.2 s. Some words are missing from them, marked `[…]`. The primary has no manual subtitle track.
- **Audio:** from ebur128, silencedetect, band-energy analysis and spectrograms. I could not listen to it. Any statement about what a sound _is_ (piano, riser, which song) is an inference and is marked as a guess.

## 3. Full-screen text cards (primary)

**Common properties** (exceptions are noted in the table):

- **Palette (three colours per video).** Ink #0E1010, cream #F2E9D7 and light blue #A7D7E8. Black cards use cream text, with light blue as the accent word colour. Blue cards use ink text. The **same blue is the highlight colour on every document** and the sky in the final shot.
- **Typeface.** A neo-grotesque sans. My guess is **Helvetica Neue**: two-storey "a", straight-tailed "y", angled terminals on "t" and "f", tight default tracking. Bold is used for the main lines; a lighter Roman or Medium is used for some lead-ins ("thought the reason…", "told slate", the tiny "once admitted…"). Quotations get curly quote marks. There are no serifs in the primary (the secondary has some, see §5).
- **Case.** **All lowercase, including proper nouns**: "barry manilow", "black sabbath", "creed's", "slate", "yougov", "americans", and the title.
- **Layout.** Flush left at x ≈ 108–112 px out of 1280 (an 8.6 % margin), ragged right. Blocks often grow **from the bottom and push older lines up** ("music came up…", "lowered his opinion…"), or **scroll older lines off the top** ("reminded people…").
- **Entry.** A **hard cut** to the card (once, a highlight flood). Each **word pops in on its spoken syllable**: a 3-frame rise through a mask (≈50 ms) at 60 fps, effectively a pop with a tiny upward slide. There is no fade, scale-in, typewriter or shake. Measured: "guilty" partial at f2735, settled by f2737.
- **Exit.** A **hard cut** on the end of the sentence, ≈0.4–1.5 s after the last word lands. Three cards instead hand off by **expanding an inset** (circle or square) into the next shot.
- **Voice.** The voice **always doubles the card**. Every card is a verbatim subset of the narration, revealed at speaking speed (about 3 words/s). No card replaces narration. The only silent text is the title, and labels such as list chips and stat captions.
- **Sound.** There is **no per-card SFX.** The only clear hits are the click at 17.23 (Bieber lands) and the silence before 47.27.

### The rules the cards follow (inferred from all 21)

1. **The card is the narration, not a summary of it.** The text is a verbatim fragment of the voice-over, revealed on each spoken word. The voice never stops for a card.
2. **One sentence or clause per card**, cut on its last word. A long sentence becomes two cards (C10→C11, C22→C23) or scrolls (C6).
3. **6 words median, 12 maximum, about 3 s on screen.** The word that lands last stays up for ≈0.4–1.5 s.
4. **Each card gets exactly one payoff word**, made bigger (2–3×) and given one treatment: the accent colour, an image fill, a highlight box, a redaction bar, a strike-through, a rebus image, a numeral window, a number ticker or a unit chart. The lead-in words stay small and plain.
5. **Size is tone of voice:** whisper-small for "quietly", huge for the punchline.
6. **Lowercase, one sans family, flush left, two grounds and one accent.** The accent colour also links cards to their sources: documents are highlighted in the same blue, and once that highlight literally floods into a card (43.93).
7. **Receipt, then quote.** 16 of the 21 cards come straight after the source (article, paper, tweet, clip) they quote, with the phrase highlighted there first.
8. **Hard cuts in and out. No whooshes, no shakes, no fades.** The motion is all _inside_ the card: pops, scrolls, morphs and swaps.

## 4. Other editing devices

- **Subtitles.** There are no running burned-in subtitles. The cards act as selective subtitles. Apart from them, caption text over picture appears only three times: the one-word track at 81.8–83.7, the label chips in C21, and the stat caption in C14.
- **Punch-ins and zooms.** Card elements punch in (Bieber chip 17.67, "black sabbath" on C11, the "jingle." pop, the "3%" pop at 222.6). Documents get **slow push-ins and pans plus highlight sweeps** (the Christgau page pan at 158.4–161, the Facebook ticker scroll). **Every 3D shot has a moving camera** (cranes, push-ins, orbits); there are no locked-off 3D shots.
- **Transitions other than cuts.** Horizontal slides in the open (6.40, 8.40). A zoom-out where the 3D frame shrinks into the Facebook mock (32.1). **Screen-to-world pull-outs**: the iTunes screenshot zooms out into a 3D monitor at 117.4. **Highlight flood into a card** (43.93). **Insets that expand into the next shot**: circles at 72.8 and 190.67, a square at 200.4. Each happens once or twice; the default is a hard cut.
- **Freeze frames.** None.
- **Cuts to black.** One, of 6 frames (132.27–132.37) before the "embarrassed…" card. There are no fades to black anywhere, including the end.
- **Silence as punctuation.** Below −50 dBFS there are only five drop-outs, each just before a turn in the story:
  - 46.97–47.27: after "guilty pleasures." and the redaction, before "Now Spotify writes the excuses".
  - 148.50–148.88: end of the Barry Manilow chapter, before "ABBA won Eurovision".
  - 234.97–235.38: before "You don't have to like ABBA."
  - 250.07–250.67: after the song plays, before "Who knows?"
  - 252.42–252.70: before the final music hit and the outro.
- **Inserts.** No memes and no stock footage. Real material is used briefly and at high speed: a **film clip** (_High Fidelity_, four cuts at 101.9–108.3), archive photos and **record sleeves** (152.3–154.2 at 0.4–0.9 s each), period TV and interview clips (183.6 inside a 3D TV; Benny Andersson at 193.0), and many **screenshots of the actual sources**. Interfaces are **mocked**: the Android-Auto-style car screen, the 2011 Facebook page with invented names, and the phone UI.
- **Recurring visual motifs.** The **imagined judge**: a boy in glasses seen in the rear-view mirror at 99.5–100.2, 125.3–127.6 and 217.07, which turns "the judge we picture" into a character. The **car screen** as the "song" prop: in the open (Dancing Queen → Clair de Lune) and at the end (Dancing Queen stays).

## Summary of findings

1. **No preamble.** Frame 0 is the 3D story scene and the voice starts at 0.48 s (0.80 s in the secondary), in the second person. There is no logo until a small wordmark about 8 s before the end.
2. **A fixed open template.** Scene (0–11 s), a highlighted real source and a card (11–18 s), the question (~19–20 s), then a **silent ~3.9 s title built in 3 chunks over a 3D camera move** (~23–29 s), then a chapter that starts with a date.
3. **The narration is acted out literally** in the scene: the volume slider rises on "turn it up", and Dancing Queen flips to Clair de Lune on "the song is gone".
4. **Cards say exactly what the voice says.** Each word pops on its syllable in 3 frames, and the card cuts out on the sentence's last word. Median 6 words, maximum 12, about 3.1 s per card.
5. **One payoff word per card**, made 2–3× bigger and given a single treatment: accent colour, image fill, highlight, redaction, strike-through, rebus image, ticker or unit chart.
6. **A three-colour system per video** (dark, light, one accent), all lowercase and set in one Helvetica-like sans. The accent colour is also the source highlight, so the source shows the phrase in the accent and the next card repeats it ("receipt, then quote": 16 of 21 cards).
7. **Fast cutting tied to speech.** Median shot 2.0 s in the body; cuts land about 5 frames before the next phrase. There is almost no transition SFX.
8. **Silence is used as punctuation**: only five true drop-outs, each just before a turn in the story. A continuous low bed runs everywhere else, and it is filtered under the title.
9. **Bookended ending.** The story returns to the opening scene at about 81 % of the runtime, the last line reverses the first scene, the music comes up at full level, and the camera rises to an empty sky for 13–16 s.
10. Real archive and screenshots appear only in fast inserts (0.4–2 s). The interfaces are mocked, and the 3D cast carries 44 % of the screen time.
