# Long-form explainer films: research for a director's vision

The evidence behind [director-vision.md](director-vision.md), whose (#n) point at the findings here. The files it names (the scripts, `bench/`, `stills/`, the per-source notes) stayed in the research folder outside the repo; the findings carry their sources and numbers.

Research date 2026-09-28, same evidence standard as the shorts research. **measured** = pulled today with yt-dlp + ffmpeg (metadata, transcripts, 1 fps contact sheets, the shared scripts `bench/measure.py`, `bench/motion2.py`, `measure.py`); **official** = the platform, the studio, or the artist in their own words (interview, making-of, book); **secondary** = journalism quoting them; **weak** = vendor/SEO/fan page without a dataset; **unverified** = no source found. View counts are as of today.

Our own film (the first cut of `righteousness-by-faith`, HEAD f1acdd14, before the single-narrator voice) was measured the same way.

## Findings

### Q1. Benchmarks, measured

Motion classes (from `bench/motion2.py`, 64×36 grey at 2 fps, per second the smaller of its two frame differences): **held** < 2 (paper grain alone scores 0.5–2), **moving** 2–20, **big** ≥ 20. "Motion runs" = ≥ 3 consecutive moving seconds; this includes character animation, not only camera.

Three conclusions:

1. **Stillness is our biggest gap.** CRAFT rule 5 says "hold a shot 5 to 10 s and move the picture inside it. Cut only to change place." We kept the first half and lost the second. The measured shots run 23 s at the median, and 79 % of seconds barely change. Every benchmark that cuts rarely makes up for it with continuous camera travel (BP uib2, LEMMiNO) or character animation. The fix is not more cuts. It is **continuous, motivated motion within the long take**: slow drifts, parallax, and a push that arrives on the turn.
2. **The colour script is too flat and too bright.** Every benchmark that tells a salvation-shaped story darkens its middle act and recovers: BP uib2 and b54d, Lost Sheep, and TED-Ed's rising accent. Ours sits at luma 167–191 in every act, which breaks CRAFT's own 140–150 target. CRAFT rule 11 ("bright") was measured on 2015–2017 BP films. The 2025–26 films keep the average hopeful but allow one real low act. _Extend CRAFT 11:_ mean 140–150, with one act allowed down to about 90–110.
3. **The ending is the channel's signature now.** BP's 30–36 s music-alone landing with credits is exactly what CRAFT rule 7 prescribes, and our film reserves the time (`thesis` `min: 32`) but plays it in silence. The score is missing.

Also confirmed:

- Our pace (171 wpm), title placement (5 %) and text budget are in range. At 171 wpm we are fast for the tone, at the top of BP's band.
- The runtime question has no single answer: views follow series and topic (Spearman ≈ 0.2). Length is a format choice. BP's 5:55–6:09 and our 7:17 are both in band.

### Q2. What makes cut paper read as crafted

The sources agree on these points, each drawable in Canvas 2D (costs in `art-direction.md`):

- **Depth is built from separated planes and veils, not blur.** Reiniger layered transparent paper; Ocelot's set was "several levels"; Norstein fogged with tracing paper; the Ocarina example uses 3 planes at 1 : 3 : 7. Our engine already does this (`multiplane` with haze and `thickness`), but only 8 of 18 scenes use it (#21, 23, 29, 31).
- **The evidence of paper is at the edges and underneath:**
  - Tearaway puts it in "the tiny shadows under the paper … the torn edges, the tabs".
  - Three Brothers gives near planes crisp shadows and far planes soft ones.
  - Our kit's `piece()` turns the torn rim off (`rim: 0`) and nearly off (`torn: 1.4`). It then draws a 3 px black boiling ink outline over every shape, which reads as a vector cartoon with a paper filter. Our film never calls the engine's `raised()`, which gives nearer layers longer shadows (#26, 28).
- **Texture is fixed to the object, not the screen.** Paperman carried its texture with the motion, and Three Brothers tracked the grain to camera. Our `grain()` is a screen overlay that shifts every tick. That is the "dirt on the screen" Framestore avoided (#25, 26).
- **The line is alive but not boiling.** Paperman avoided boil and kept "one line intact through time". Moore varies line weight with emotion. Our 12 fps boil on every outline is the opposite choice (#24, 25).
- **Limited palette per world, one accent.** Wolfwalkers' town allowed only black and red; Tearaway limited itself to "art-shop" colours; BP gives each series its own palette with one accent. Our `palette.ts` has 33 swatches, and scarlet (sin) and gold (the word) are already the natural accents (#3, 24, 28).
- **Faces minimal; hands and posture act.** Hibon: emotion through "hands and heads and body positions". Ocelot uses profile staging. Framestore gave no eye whites. BP's Redemption hides faces until the "home world". Our dot-eye faces fit this, but the hands vary. Some are discs on rods (the centurion). Others are giant grey mittens drawn as props (message, look, daily), a second design at a different scale (#18, 23, 26).
- **Stylised but true motion** (Reiniger). A rod-puppet pivot high on the body plus slight jitter reads as paper (#22, 32).

### Q3. The grammar that follows

- **Emotion before continuity** (Murch 51 % vs 4 %). A push-in is identification (Katz), so it belongs on the turn and on the vulnerable close-up, not on every sentence.
- **Cut on the finished thought.** For us, the unit of the cut is the narrator's sentence, which our marks already expose.
- **Plot visual intensity against the argument** (Block). Our CRAFT already names the doses (problem ≤ 20 %, hope ≥ 1.5×). The colour script and camera should rise and fall with them: tent poles (Romano) at the cold open, the cross, the robe and the landing.
- **Motifs paid off in light** (Feinberg's WALL-E eye): our hollow/solid stamp is exactly this. Apply the same grammar to the heart glow.
- **Visual rhymes and matched compositions** (BP "make the visual composition of each story parallel"; Kon's match cuts; Lost Sheep's sun disc) are CRAFT rule 8 made into cuts. The benchmarks show it done with **match cuts and bookends** (TED-Ed's reverse replay, Kurzgesagt's return to the opening image, LEMMiNO's scale pull-back).
- **Sound is synchresis plus room.** Hit the noun on the frame (Chion); linger after key lines so sound can creep in (Thom); compose to picture, not wallpaper (Zhou, Kurzgesagt). Our effects-on-concrete-nouns rule (CRAFT 10) is right. The missing piece is the score itself.

### Q4. Long-form YouTube for a teaching film

- **The first 30 s are what YouTube measures** (#42). Our cold open (hook at 0:00, question by 0:20) already fits. Keep logos and title cards out of the first 5–10 s (#52).
- **Chapters are a discovery tool** (#43), and BP uses them on teaching series. For a 7-min argument, 4–5 chapters named as the viewer's questions ("Is it a cover-up?", "Where does righteousness come from?") map straight onto the narrator's question at each turn.
- **Packaging is tested on watch time, not clicks** (#45, 47). Ship three title/thumbnail pairs. The strongest candidates are our own hooks: the courtroom paradox and "You can't wash your face with a mirror".
- **Reserve the last 5–20 s for end screens** (#44). BP's credits-and-music landing gives that room. Our 3 s end card does not.
- **TV and languages:**
  - TV is now the main screen (#50), which favours readable silhouettes and big type.
  - Multi-language audio is cheap reach (#51). It needs a clean dry narration stem plus M&E stems, which our mixer already writes as `--stems`.
- **No ideal length exists** (#49, 54). Set length by argument: 5–8 min for one doctrine, the band where BP's teaching films sit.
