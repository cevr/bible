## 1. What colours convey

### 1.1 Three layers of meaning

A colour's meaning is read in this order. A lower layer never overrides a higher one.

1. **Scripture.** Where the text names a colour, the text's meaning wins: sins "as scarlet" made "white as snow" (Isa 1:18).
2. **The house palette.** Three colours carry one fixed meaning in every film ([CRAFT.md](../CRAFT.md) rule 12):
   - **gold** `#e6b347` is God's word, gift and verdict, and only gold glows (glow `#fbefc8`);
   - **scarlet** `#ce0914` is sin, only on the person or cloth that carries it, and the blood that answers it;
   - **robe white** `#fcfefc` is the robe of righteousness.
3. **Colour psychology.** Mood, attention and depth. It tunes a scene and never carries doctrine (1.3).

Every other colour (every garment of the cast, the world's chipboard, the skies) means nothing by itself. It tells people apart, sets the hour and the mood, and leaves the meaning to the three.

### 1.2 The colour key

#### White: righteousness given

- **Scripture.** "Though your sins be as scarlet, they shall be as white as snow" (Isa 1:18); "wash me, and I shall be whiter than snow" (Ps 51:7). "He hath covered me with the robe of righteousness" (Isa 61:10). "He that overcometh, the same shall be clothed in white raiment" (Rev 3:5); Christ counsels us to buy of Him "white raiment, that thou mayest be clothed" (Rev 3:18). "The fine linen is the righteousness of saints" (Rev 19:8). The multitude "washed their robes, and made them white in the blood of the Lamb" (Rev 7:14). Heaven's holy ones wear white (Dan 7:9; Matt 28:3; Rev 15:6), and at the transfiguration Christ's "raiment was white as the light" (Matt 17:2).
- **Ellen White.** "The white raiment is the righteousness of Christ, the wedding garment which Christ alone can give" (RH April 1, 1890, par. 10). "This robe, woven in the loom of heaven, has in it not one thread of human devising" (COL 311.4). Eden's pair wore "a robe of light", "a symbol of their spiritual garments of heavenly innocence" (COL 310.4).
- **House.** The robe: righteousness given, never grown.
- **Under K1.** The only pure white in a frame. Nothing else reaches `#fcfefc`: no garment, no hair, no paper, no sky. Undyed cloth is oat (`#d1c6b4`); old hair is a pale cool paper (Rosa, [ensemble.md](../../../../apps/animations/docs/ensemble.md)). The robe shades toward the open robe shade above, never toward grey.
- **Never.** White as a costume colour, as "the good one", or as a church garment (a choir robe, a baptismal gown). In theology 09 the two who go down into the water wear their own plain clothes.

#### Scarlet: sin, and the blood that answers it

- **Scripture.** Sins "as scarlet … red like crimson" (Isa 1:18). Blood that atones: "it is the blood that maketh an atonement for the soul" (Lev 17:11); "without shedding of blood is no remission" (Heb 9:22); Moses sprinkled the book and the people with blood, "scarlet wool, and hyssop" (Heb 9:19). The blood "for a token upon the houses" (Ex 12:13); the "line of scarlet thread in the window" (Josh 2:18). Babylon "arrayed in purple and scarlet colour" (Rev 17:4; 18:16); "a great red dragon" (Rev 12:3).
- **House.** Sin, only on the person or cloth that carries it ([CRAFT.md](../CRAFT.md) rule 12), and the blood that answers it.
- **Under K1.** The only saturated garment colour, and never a garment's identity. It is spent in small areas: a stain, specks, a thread, a cup.
- **Scarlet moves.** Sin's scarlet sits on the one who carries it. At the answer it leaves them (Isa 1:18; Zech 3:4; Rev 7:14). Blood's scarlet is given, never worn: the doorpost, the altar, the mercy seat (Lev 16:14), the cross. The two meanings meet on one person only at the cross, where Christ "his own self bare our sins in his own body on the tree" (1 Pet 2:24).
- **Never.** Scarlet as decoration (a red scarf, a red door, a red sky). Scarlet on Christ as a stain ([nature-of-christ.md](../frame/nature-of-christ.md), MUST NOT: "No stain on Christ"). Scarlet as a cast member's slot.
- **Dyed scarlet that is neither sin nor blood** (a household "clothed with scarlet", Prov 31:21; Belshazzar's robe on Daniel, Dan 5:29; the curtains' thread, Ex 26:1) needs its own muted swatch, so the accent stays reserved. **OWNER QUESTION 2** (1.6).

#### Gold: God's word, gift and verdict

- **Scripture.** Faith "much more precious than of gold … though it be tried with fire" (1 Pet 1:7); "when he hath tried me, I shall come forth as gold" (Job 23:10); "buy of me gold tried in the fire" (Rev 3:18). The judgments of the LORD are "more to be desired … than gold" (Ps 19:10). The ark overlaid "with pure gold" (Ex 25:11), where God says "there I will meet with thee" (Ex 25:22). The risen Christ "girt about the paps with a golden girdle" (Rev 1:13). The city "pure gold, like unto clear glass" (Rev 21:18).
- **Ellen White.** The gold of Rev 3:18 "is the gold of faith and love, that has no defiling substance mingled with it" (RH April 1, 1890, par. 10). The tabernacle's gold gave the building "the appearance of solid gold" (PP 347.1).
- **House.** God's word, gift and verdict. Only gold glows (director-vision, Palette logic).
- **Under K1.** No figure's identity colour is gold. Gold sits only where the text makes a thing God's: the Word, a gift given, the ark and its furniture, Christ's girdle (Rev 1:13), the high priest's gold (Ex 28:6, 36), the sword of the allegory's knight, "which is the word of God" (Eph 6:17).
- **God's gold and the world's.** Babylon's "golden cup" (Rev 17:4), a king's crown, the serpent's "brilliancy of burnished gold" (PP 53.4) take gold's hue at band saturation, as brass (`#b2a474`), and never glow. The counterfeit borrows the look and cannot give light.
- **Never.** Gild a joint or a hand (director-vision, What we never do). Gild a believer to show that they have faith.

#### Blue: the commandments remembered

- **Scripture.** "Put upon the fringe of the borders a ribband of blue … that ye may look upon it, and remember all the commandments of the LORD, and do them" (Num 15:38–39). The high priest's "robe of the ephod all of blue" (Ex 28:31); "a cloth wholly of blue" spread over the ark (Num 4:6); under God's feet "a paved work of a sapphire stone, and as it were the body of heaven in his clearness" (Ex 24:10; Ezek 1:26).
- **Ellen White.** The ribbon "would keep in their memory the high claims Jehovah had upon them" (T12 39.1).
- **House.** No fixed meaning. A cool shadow is blue because of the shading rule; that is light, not doctrine.
- **Under K1.** A band hue on the cast (Hugo's steel, Amara's periwinkle) that means nothing. Blue means "remember the commandments" only where a film shows what the text shows: the fringe, the priest's robe, the ark's cloth, the sapphire pavement.
- **Never.** Blue as "the law" on a person the text gives no fringe.

#### Purple: royalty, honest or proud

- **Scripture.** "Purple raiment that was on the kings of Midian" (Judg 8:26); Mordecai "in royal apparel of blue and white … and with a garment of fine linen and purple" (Esth 8:15). The soldiers "put on him a purple robe" to mock Him (John 19:2). The rich man "clothed in purple and fine linen" (Luke 16:19); the woman of strength, "her clothing is silk and purple" (Prov 31:22). Babylon in "purple and scarlet" (Rev 17:4; 18:16).
- **House.** Royalty and wealth. Its context says whether it is honour, mockery or pride.
- **Under K1.** Muted purples are cast hues (Amara, Nina, Rosa) and mean nothing. A king's purple sits at the band's top and dark (`#62486b`, 285° .33 .42), never saturated.
- **Never.** Purple as "evil" or as "holy" by itself.

#### Green: life the word gives

- **Scripture.** The righteous "like a tree planted by the rivers of water … his leaf also shall not wither" (Ps 1:3; Jer 17:8); "green pastures" (Ps 23:2); "a green olive tree in the house of God" (Ps 52:8); "every green herb for meat" (Gen 1:30); "the tree of life … for the healing of the nations" (Rev 22:2). And what fades: "all flesh is grass … The grass withereth, the flower fadeth: but the word of our God shall stand for ever" (Isa 40:6, 8); fig leaves sewn into aprons (Gen 3:7).
- **House.** "What grows (only what the word makes grow, and the fig leaves we sew)" (the first film's palette). Leaf `#86b86a` (shade `#5f9451`); dry leaf `#9a7b52`.
- **Under K1.** Band greens on the cast (Max's sage, Mila's mint) mean nothing. Green in the world means life given; green that browns means a covering of our own making, or life cut off.
- **Never.** Green on a person to say they are growing in grace.

#### Brown and chipboard: dust, and the covering God made

- **Scripture.** "The LORD God formed man of the dust of the ground" (Gen 2:7); "unto Adam also and to his wife did the LORD God make coats of skins, and clothed them" (Gen 3:21).
- **House.** The world's ground (board `#ab8163`) and cut chipboard for what people wear and carry: hair, headcloths, tools (the "cut" swatches).
- **Under K1.** Hair, props, and the wool and leather of Bible figures. A cast slot is never chipboard.

#### Black and night: the one black moment

- **Scripture.** "From the sixth hour there was darkness over all the land unto the ninth hour" (Matt 27:45); "the sun became black as sackcloth of hair" (Rev 6:12); "woe unto them that … put darkness for light" (Isa 5:20).
- **House.** Night `#1b150d` only for the one black moment and at most one IDEA stretch. A fully black frame only at the cross (director-vision, Palette logic).
- **Under K1.** Ink `#2b2622` for outlines and features. A line may be black. A shadow never is.

#### Grey: retired

Grey is no colour of Scripture. A grey or dimmed copy of a colour "reads dead and muddy" (director-vision, Shadow colour). Under K1 grey is no longer a shade, and no figure's body but one: the plain lead's pale cool grey paper with its faint lilac cast, whose shade turns to violet (owner, 2026-10-15: "though this is what i mean by non descript"; 2.3). The first film's grey everyman is that plain lead, kept in his own paper. The Canvas kit's other grey figures and the first film's grey accuser (`#5f5a55`) take hued ramps when they are next drawn (2.5).

#### Teal: a light, not a sin

BibleProject's Holiness film paints "gold/warm = God's presence, life; cold teal = impurity, death" ([style-research.md](style-research.md), Palette). The owner's earlier summary was teal = sin, gold = grace, white = the robe. Our own rules have since given teal to the opening and the landing, the brightest and most saturated act ([CRAFT.md](../CRAFT.md) rule 11), and Clara wears teal. So teal cannot mean sin here.

Recommendation: sin's **mark** is scarlet; sin's **state** is a light, not a hue: cold, flat and desaturated (BibleProject's slate `#486868`), worn by the problem act's light and lifted by the answer. Gold stays grace, white the robe. **OWNER QUESTION 1** (1.6).

#### The sanctuary's colours

- **Scripture.** The curtains and the veil: "fine twined linen, and blue, and purple, and scarlet: with cherubims of cunning work" (Ex 26:1, 31). The high priest's ephod: "gold, of blue, and of purple, of scarlet, and fine twined linen" (Ex 28:6). The pomegranates of "blue, and … purple, and … scarlet" on his robe's hem, "and bells of gold between them" (Ex 28:33). The gold plate, "HOLINESS TO THE LORD" (Ex 28:36). On the day of atonement the high priest wears only "the holy linen coat … these are holy garments" (Lev 16:4).
- **Ellen White.** The walls "were overlaid with gold, giving to the building the appearance of solid gold"; the innermost roof curtain was "fine twined linen, and blue, and purple, and scarlet" (PP 347.1).
- **Under K1.** The sanctuary is the one set where every colour of the key appears together. Linen takes robe white (the text calls fine linen righteousness, Rev 19:8). The gold glows. Blue and purple sit at the band's top (`#5b708c` priest blue, `#745380` sanctuary purple). The threads' scarlet waits on OWNER QUESTION 2; the blood on the mercy seat (Lev 16:14) is the accent.
- **Draw, don't decode.** Exodus lists the colours and gives them no meanings. A film may draw every thread. It may not narrate a doctrine for each one unless a source does (1.5). The day-of-atonement linen is a literal change the text makes, so a film may show it.

### 1.3 Colour psychology: the third layer

General practice in colour design, not Scripture. It sets attention and mood, never a claim.

- **Saturation draws the eye first.** That is why accents are rare: in any frame the eye finds scarlet, gold and the robe before anything else.
- **Value carries the read.** A figure reads by its value against its ground before its hue does. Check with `--mode value`.
- **Warm advances, cool recedes.** Lead figures and what they hold sit warm under the key; the far planes cool through the haze.
- **Warm light reads as home and safety;** cool light as distance, night and loss; a rising light as hope.
- **Low saturation reads as fatigue, memory or loss;** high saturation as life and presence. That is why the landing is the most saturated act.
- **Red alarms.** One scarlet speck pulls the eye from across the frame, so it is spent only where the text puts sin or blood.

### 1.4 The colour script carries the argument

Each act has a light ([director-vision.md](director-vision.md), Colour script per act; [CRAFT.md](../CRAFT.md) rule 11). The light carries the argument's mood. The accents carry its claims.

| Act                 | Light                                          | What the light says                     | What the accents do                                                            |
| ------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Cold open           | a cool, even page, no sun                      | a question with no answer yet           | gold appears hollow: the thing the question is about, empty                    |
| Problem             | peach afternoon toward dusk                    | the weight comes on, in small doses     | scarlet appears on the one who carries it                                      |
| Answer (the valley) | sunset, the one black moment, dawn             | the cost, then the turn                 | the cross is the one black frame; scarlet is given there; gold returns at dawn |
| Power and life      | early gold morning, warm cream                 | God's gift at work inside a life        | gold glows from inside (the heart lit), not only on the surface                |
| Landing             | teal day, the opening's layout, most saturated | the answer lived in the opening's world | the robe is white, the scarlet is gone, the hollow gold lands solid            |

Rules:

- **Light changes; garments don't.** A figure's garment hue is the same in every act. The light on it changes: its ramp shades toward the act's complement and lights toward the act's key.
- **One valley.** The film goes down once and comes back ([CRAFT.md](../CRAFT.md) rule 11). A cast scene is never darkened on its own to say a person is lost.
- **The accents keep their budget in every act.** A dusk act does not get more scarlet to feel heavier; it gets the scarlet the text puts there.

### 1.5 Colour never asserts a doctrine

Owner rule: typology, a text-authorized reading from literal to literal, is fine; spiritualizing, asserting a doctrine from a non-literal reading, is not. A colour is a claim the viewer cannot argue with, so it is held to the same rule as a line of narration.

**Yes: the text makes the change.**

- Filthy garments taken away and a "change of raiment" put on (Zech 3:3–4): stains off, robe white on.
- Sins "as scarlet" made "white as snow" (Isa 1:18).
- Blood on the doorposts (Ex 12:13); the scarlet line in Rahab's window (Josh 2:18).
- The fringe's blue where Num 15:38–39 is read.
- Babylon in purple and scarlet with a golden cup (Rev 17:4), the gold not glowing.
- Eden's pair in a robe of light, then fig leaves, then coats of skins (COL 310.4; Gen 3:7, 21).
- Moses' face shining as he comes down (Ex 34:29): a glow the text puts on a man.

**No: the colour would add a claim.**

- A doctrine named for each curtain thread (Ex 26:1). Draw the threads; don't decode them.
- An altar or a slain lamb at Gen 3:21. The text says God made coats of skins, and no more.
- A cast member's hue as a spiritual type. Max's sage is not "less saved" than Clara's teal.
- A garment changing hue to show growth. The robe given is the only garment change that means a change of standing, and it is shown given (Zech 3:4), never grown.
- Gold on a believer to show faith, or a glow the text does not give.
- A cast scene darkened to say someone is lost.

## 2. Designing by role

### 2.1 The grammar every figure shares

- **K1, as above.** Outline with a light core, head about ⅓, circle hands floating with no arm, shading by colour.
- **Built from a kit.** A figure is a few parts: body, head, hair or hat, two hand circles, a garment tint from a ramp ([rimworld-study.md](rimworld-study.md) §2 rules 3 and 5). Body types: slight, broad, tall, child, elder. A new person adds a silhouette, not new parts.
- **Faces.** Today a face is two dot eyes, a brow and a small mouth, warm by default: raise the inner brows, give the glad a smile (the film skill's Gotchas). The owner wants faces "more interesting", RimWorld-style: four face kits, eight expressions, five ages and one feature per cast member are in [face-study.md](face-study.md). Answered (owner, 2026-10-13): Kit A, three marks. The kit itself is defined in face-study.md, not here; where a role below speaks of the face, it speaks of range and use, not construction.
- **Movement.** Whole body first: a hop, a lean, a tilt, then the face confirms (rimworld rule 7). Travel by a glide, not a walk cycle (rule 8). Hands travel on soft arcs within their reach, never a straight line and never a pop (director-vision, Hands). Everyone breathes.
- **Turning.** Front, three-quarter, side and back are drawings swapped on a cue, not a rotation (rimworld rule 10). Each side is its own traced drawing, never a mirror, so the crease and crescent stay on the side the light comes from (owner, 2026-10-18, ask a9d4570003a).
- **The quarter-size test.** Every figure is checked in a lineup at quarter size: named from its silhouette filled dark (`--mode value`) and again from its hues blurred (`--mode squint`) ([ensemble.md](../../../../apps/animations/docs/ensemble.md), Before the first film uses them).

### 2.3 Main characters: the plain lead

Owner, 2026-10-15: "i actually liked more the non descriptive character in the room, let's make the recurring cast only background characters, and the main characters non descriptive so they can be better related".

The lead of a film is one of two:

- **A Bible figure** whose story the STORY scenes carry (Isaiah in theology 05, the Bereans and Paul in 02, the paralytic, Joshua in Zech 3). Section 3 draws them, with the text's own details.
- **The plain lead:** the parable's "a certain man" (Luke 10:30) or "a woman" (Luke 15:8), whose need is the film's question. "Picture a young man in a courtroom" (theology 08), "picture a grown man at a wedding feast" (theology 01). The recurring cast is never the lead, the accused or the one whose need is the question: its eight stand in the background ([ensemble.md](../../../../apps/animations/docs/ensemble.md)).

**Why plain.** A parable gives its man no name and no face, so every hearer can stand where he stands. A figure with marks of its own is someone else; a plain figure can be the viewer. Comics found the same: the simpler a face, the more people it stands for (Scott McCloud, _Understanding Comics_, 1993, ch. 2).

**The plain lead is one figure** (owner, 2026-10-15: "though this is what i mean by non descript", with a picture of him). The picture is the RBF Board's `word.church` try 5, kept as [plain-lead.png](../../../../apps/animations/docs/ref/plain-lead.png). It is v2's everyman as the Board drew him, and every film's plain lead is this figure. His model sheet, [plain-lead-sheet.png](../../../../apps/animations/docs/ref/plain-lead-sheet.png), draws him from the picture in the house look: front, three-quarter, and front beside a doorway for his height. Every concept try and Board try of him takes the sheet as a reference.

- **Build.** A small figure: a round head sitting right on a trapezoid tunic, with no neck. The tunic has rounded shoulders and widens to a straight hem. Two short rounded stub legs stand under the hem, and two hand circles float beside him. He has no hair, ears, nose, neck, arms or shoes, and no clothes detail. Measured on the sheet's front view, the picture in brackets:
  - head height, crown to where it meets the tunic: 0.375 of his height (0.379), so he is about 2.6 heads tall;
  - head width: 0.41 of his height (0.425);
  - tunic width at the hem: 0.43 of his height (0.435), so his head is nearly as wide as his hem;
  - legs, hem to soles: about 0.17 of his height;
  - a hand circle: about 0.38 of the head's width.
- **One paper.** His head, hands, tunic and legs are one pale cool grey paper with a faint lilac cast, the one figure paper K1 keeps near neutral (1.2, Grey). Lit side: `#cccbd1` on the picture, `#dbd6dc` on the sheet (saturation 0.03 or less, value 0.82–0.86). Shade side, past the crease: `#a9a2c6` on the picture, `#b5a9d0` on the sheet (hue 252°–258°, saturation 0.18–0.19, value 0.78–0.82), turned toward violet, never a darker grey. The head reads a touch warmer where the light is nearest (`#e1d6d0`).
- **Line and light.** The house's thick warm dark-brown outline, about 1/40 of his height, with a thin light inner edge. One straight diagonal crease from the upper left on the head and on the tunic, the far side turned violet. A soft lilac cast shadow.

  - **its paper,** a second quiet paper of the same value (a warm oat, `#d9cfc0` lit, its shade turned violet), for a second adult: RBF's grandmother, who must never be taken for the accused;

  Never hair, clothes detail or a mark.

- **No marks.** Nothing that tells who he is: no badge, bag, jewellery, hood, shawl, hat or hair style that names someone; no name in speech or on screen ([ensemble.md](../../../../apps/animations/docs/ensemble.md), open choice 1); no habit gesture of his own, since a habit is a cast member's mark. Script briefs call him by his part: "the accused", "the grandmother". Being bald is his plainness, not a mark, and he reads as any age.
- **He reads first anyway.** By staging, not by marks: the near plane, at least ¼ of frame height in a wide, a face at ⅓ of the frame once per beat ([CRAFT.md](../CRAFT.md) rule 5), the widest value step against his ground, and the accent the beat is about (the stain, the seal, the seed).
- **One design.** He is one design from a film's first frame to its last, and from film to film, so the bookend reads as one person, changed ([CRAFT.md](../CRAFT.md) rule 4).
- **The garment changes only where the story changes it.** The robe white given at the landing (Zech 3:4; 1.2), a coat for outdoors. Never a hue that changes to show growth (1.5).
- **Movement.** The most acting in the film: whole-body impulses, the hand that reaches.
- **Staging.** One screen side for the whole film; the near plane; the line crossed once, at the turn (director-vision, Camera and staging 1).
- **At quarter size.** Read as "a man", "a woman", "a child": by role and staging, never by a mark.

A Bible lead keeps the text's details (Joshua's "fair mitre", John's camel hair): they are the text's, not marks we add (3.1). Its colour sits at the top of the band, with the widest value step against its ground.

### 2.4 Supporting characters

Those who share the lead's frame and answer it: a disciple beside Christ, a mother beside a child, Silas beside Paul.

- **Silhouette.** A different height class and one different shape from the lead.
- **Size.** About the lead's scale, a plane behind or across.
- **Colour.** One value step from the lead, a little less saturated, never the lead's hue family in the same frame.
- **Face.** Reactions: a reaction follows an idea ([CRAFT.md](../CRAFT.md) rule 5).
- **Movement.** Smaller impulses. They react after the lead's beat, never on the same frame, so the eye reads cause and then effect.
- **Staging.** The mid plane, the other side or behind; never between the lead and the camera.
- **At quarter size.** Reads as "with the lead": grouped by spacing, apart by value.

### 2.5 Antagonists, by Christ's method

The antagonists are Satan (the serpent, the accuser, the dragon), the powers of prophecy, the people who oppose God in the Bible's stories, temptation itself, and the giants of the allegory. They are drawn by seven principles.

1. **Real, without glamour or horror.** Scripture names evil plainly (Gen 3:1; Zech 3:1; Rev 12:9–10) and wants us "wise unto that which is good, and simple concerning evil" (Rom 16:19); "think on these things" (Phil 4:8). Ellen White: "We must not talk and think and magnify the power of Satan, but talk of the confidence we have in Jesus Christ" (7LtMs, Ms 25, 1891, par. 2). The education series' own source says the same to children: speak of God's power, never the devil's strength (Byrum, _Happy Hours_, p. 35; [education sources.md](../../../../apps/animations/series/education/sources.md)).
2. **Fallen, not monstrous.** Lucifer was made beautiful, "every precious stone was thy covering" (Ezek 28:13), "the anointed cherub that covereth" (Ezek 28:14), and fell by pride in it: "Thine heart was lifted up because of thy beauty" (Ezek 28:17; Isa 14:12). Ellen White saw him after the fall: "He still bears a kingly form. His features are still noble, for he is an angel fallen", yet "his frame was large, but the flesh hung loosely about his hands and face" (EW 152.3). So: a large, upright, kingly frame that sags; nobility emptied out. No horns, tail, pitchfork, red skin, claws or fangs. None is in Scripture, and the folk devil turns evil into a cartoon to laugh at or to fear.
3. **Temptation is attractive but hollow.** The tree was "pleasant to the eyes" (Gen 3:6). "Bread of deceit is sweet to a man; but afterwards his mouth shall be filled with gravel" (Prov 20:17). "Stolen waters are sweet … But he knoweth not that the dead are there" (Prov 9:17–18). "Broken cisterns, that can hold no water" (Jer 2:13). "The pleasures of sin for a season" (Heb 11:25). "Satan himself is transformed into an angel of light" (2 Cor 11:14). Paper can show this as no other medium can:
   - **Sheen, not light.** A tempting thing may shine, a burnished band of highlight, but it never glows. Only gold glows.
   - **Hollow when turned.** Front-on it looks full. Turned side-on it is one sheet, a sliver with nothing behind it; or the camera finds the hole cut where its heart should be, and the light falls straight through.
   - **It costs the one who takes it.** The holder's light dims a step when they take it. It never adds.
4. **Shown by what it does to people.** The close-up goes to the tempted face and to the answer, never to the tempter's. A shot never lingers on the tempter's beauty.
5. **Never a caricature of a real group or faith.**
   - The people who oppose God in the stories (Pharaoh, the rulers, a mob) are drawn in the same figure grammar as everyone else, of their own people, with no exaggerated features.
   - Christ and His disciples were Jews (Heb 7:14). His opponents are never drawn as "the Jews" set against Him.
   - Prophecy's powers are drawn as the text's own symbols: a beast "dreadful and terrible" with "great iron teeth", a little horn with "eyes like the eyes of man" (Dan 7:7–8), a beast from the sea (Rev 13:1). Never as clergy, a church building, a living nation's people or an ethnic type. The frame names the powers in history ([prophecy-chains.md](../frame/prophecy-chains.md)); the picture shows the symbol and the history and mocks no one.
6. **Answered in the frame.** "The LORD rebuke thee, O Satan" (Zech 3:2); "the accuser of our brethren is cast down" (Rev 12:10). When the answer comes, the antagonist shrinks, steps back and loses its sheen, as the first film's accuser does.
7. **A human antagonist keeps a human moment.** "Father, forgive them; for they know not what they do" (Luke 23:34). A scoffer or a soldier is someone Christ's method would reach: flat brows, never a snarl, and one beat of stillness or doubt where the text allows.

By the seven role labels:

- **Silhouette.** Tall, narrow, angular or sagging: fewer curves than the people. Nothing pointed grows from the head. Satan's kingly frame sags (EW 152.3).
- **Size.** Tall in its scene, but never higher than the light and never larger than the Advocate. It shrinks on the answer.
- **Colour.** Saturation at the band's floor, 0.20; the darkest value of any figure (about 0.30–0.36); a cold hue off the cast's slots; no accent of its own. The scarlet belongs to the sin he points at, not to him. Exception: where the text colours him, the text wins ("a great red dragon", Rev 12:3).
  - Accuser ramp, proposed: base `#444152`, shade `#242430`, light `#666070` (250° .20 .32), replacing the first film's grey `#5f5a55`.
  - Temptation's sheen, proposed: brass `#b2a474`, shade `#6e6380`, light `#dbd39a` (46° .35 .70). It sits beside gold on purpose, and never glows.
  - **OWNER QUESTION 3** (2.10).
- **Face.** A narrow range: level or lowered brows, watchful eyes, a closed mouth. Never a grin of horror: Ellen White saw a smile that "grows horrible" (EW 152.3), and we do not draw it. See the face study (coming).
- **Movement.** Little breath. Slow and deliberate. It presses toward others rather than acting itself. One pointing hand (the first film's accuser has "the film's one pointing hand"). It recoils on the answer.
- **Staging.** Where the text puts him: "Satan standing at his right hand to resist him" (Zech 3:1). Otherwise the far plane or the frame's edge. Never the centre, never above the light, never on the Advocate's side.
- **At quarter size.** A cold, tall, dark shape set apart from the warm people, and never mistaken for the Advocate: opposite side, opposite value (the Advocate light, the accuser dark).

### 2.6 Minor and background figures

The people who fill a world: a shopkeeper, a passer-by, a teacher in a doorway. Section 5 is the reusable set.

- **Silhouette.** From the kit, one hook each.
- **Size.** Mid and far planes.
- **Colour.** Saturation 0.20–0.24, middle values that sit toward the ground. No accent unless the text puts one on them.
- **Face.** Small, or turned away.
- **Movement.** Breath and one small gesture, between the lead's beats, never on them.
- **Staging.** Never across the lead's eye line.
- **At quarter size.** Part of the setting: people, not anyone in particular.

### 2.7 Crowds

- **Never cloned** ([CRAFT.md](../CRAFT.md) rule 12): vary height, silhouette and hat. Build from the kit's bodies, section 5's crowd ramps and its hats, so no two neighbours share all three.
- **Every people.** "Of all nations, and kindreds, and people, and tongues" (Rev 7:9; 5:9); "one blood all nations of men" (Acts 17:26). Vary build, hair and headwear. Skin follows [ensemble.md](../../../../apps/animations/docs/ensemble.md) open choice 2.
- **Colour.** Saturation 0.20, values grouped so `--mode squint` shows the crowd as one mass and the lead as another. No white unless the text robes them (the multitude "clothed with white robes", Rev 7:9). Scarlet only on the one the text marks.
- **Movement.** Breath out of phase, figure to figure. A reaction runs through a crowd as a ripple, staggered, never all on one frame. A crowd turns as one shape only when the text makes them one (Acts 2:2–3).
- **Staging.** A crowd is a plane. The lead is cut out of it by value and by a gap.
- **Pity, not menace.** Christ "saw the multitudes" and "was moved with compassion on them, because they fainted, and were scattered abroad, as sheep having no shepherd" (Matt 9:36). A crowd is people to be pitied, never a faceless threat. A hostile crowd is still people.

### 2.8 Allegorical figures

The owner's rule: fiction is avoided; allegory is allowed, framed openly as allegory, the way a parable is ([education series.md](../../../../apps/animations/series/education/series.md) §5). Ellen White called _The Pilgrim's Progress_ "his wonderful allegory" (GC 252.2) and used its figures herself (the same section lists them).

- **Its own page.** An allegory lives on a storybook page inside the parable's frame: a child's bedtime story (education 15, 17), pushed into and pulled back out of. The page is the parchment (IDEA), torn at its edge like a book's. A viewer never mistakes it for the cast's world or for a Bible scene.
- **The knight** is "the Christian" (the father's reading in Byrum, after A.L.O.E.; [education sources.md](../../../../apps/animations/series/education/sources.md)). His armour is the text's: "the whole armour of God" (Eph 6:11, 13), the loins "girt about with truth", "the breastplate of righteousness", feet shod "with the preparation of the gospel of peace", "the shield of faith", "the helmet of salvation", "the sword of the Spirit, which is the word of God" (Eph 6:14–17).
  - Steel at the band, `#8fa4b2` (shade `#6d7591`, light `#b4cad1`; 205° .20 .70).
  - The sword is the one gold thing: the text calls it the word of God.
  - The breastplate may take robe white: the text calls it righteousness.
  - A child's proportions (a large head), because the knight is the child who fights.
- **The giants** are our faults: Selfishness, Untruth, Hate and Pride (Byrum, after A.L.O.E.'s _The Giant-Killer_, 1856). The antagonist principles hold (2.5).
  - Each giant's shape comes from its fault and the book's props: **Selfishness** round and grasping, at its feast; **Untruth** with a mask, a cloak lined two ways and a magnifying mirror; **Hate** hunched, its edge torn rough; **Pride** the tallest, chin up, a false knight who cannot read "I repent. I am grateful".
  - **Scale is the meaning.** Each begins the size of a child and grows when it is left alone, the book's thesis that faults begin small and grow into giants; and Scripture's own order, "when lust hath conceived, it bringeth forth sin: and sin, when it is finished, bringeth forth death" (Jas 1:15).
  - Drained ramp `#49565c` (shade `#2c313b`, light `#69787a`; 200° .20 .36), plus one prop each. No scarlet on a giant: the giant is the fault; sin's scarlet sits on the child who gives in.
  - Not frightening. A child watches (education 17 is a child's). Their weight is size and posture, never teeth or gore.
- **Pilgrim's Progress-style figures** (Pliable, the man with the muck-rake) are parable figures with one emblem prop that names their trait. The narrator names them. No label on screen ([CRAFT.md](../CRAFT.md) rule 1).

### 2.9 Personifications: Sin and Death

Scripture personifies both. "Sin lieth at the door. And unto thee shall be his desire, and thou shalt rule over him" (Gen 4:7). "Sin hath reigned unto death" (Rom 5:21). "The wages of sin is death" (Rom 6:23). "Sin, when it is finished, bringeth forth death" (Jas 1:15). "The last enemy that shall be destroyed is death" (1 Cor 15:26); "O death, where is thy sting?" (1 Cor 15:55). Death rides "a pale horse" (Rev 6:8), and "death and hell were cast into the lake of fire" (Rev 20:14).

Recommendation: **draw neither as a person or creature.**

- **Sin.** "Sin is the transgression of the law" (1 John 3:4): an act and its record. Temptation comes when a man "is drawn away of his own lust" (Jas 1:14). A creature called Sin would double Satan, the one being behind sin ("him that had the power of death, that is, the devil", Heb 2:14), and would teach that sin attacks us from outside. Show sin as the house shows it: the scarlet stain or the weight on the one who carries it. For Gen 4:7, sin "at the door" may be the person's own shadow, crouched at the threshold: the text's image, cast by the person, not a beast.
- **Death.** The frame shows death as sleep, still and silent, and forbids souls, ghosts and the dead watching the living ([state-of-dead.md](../frame/state-of-dead.md)). No reaper, skeleton or hooded figure in any story or cast scene. Death's end is drawn as the grave opened (1 Cor 15:55) and "no more death" (Rev 21:4).
- **Revelation's rider** (Rev 6:8) is a prophetic symbol. If a prophecy film reads the fourth seal, draw the text's symbol only: small, a faceless silhouette on the IDEA page, never in a cast scene. **OWNER QUESTION 4** (2.10).

Christ, the Father, the Spirit and the angels are in section 3.

## 3. Bible figures

The people, beings and places of the Bible's own stories. They live in STORY scenes; the cast and the plain lead (2.3) never enter them ([ensemble.md](../../../../apps/animations/docs/ensemble.md)). The frame files' MUST NOTs bind every picture here: a picture teaches as loudly as a line.

**Drawn once, kept for every film.** Every Bible person is a cast member, drawn once and kept in the library to be used again (owner, 2026-10-18: "all characters of the bible are cast members, if they are ever mentioned, they shoudl be drawn once and kept in the library to be used again"). The first film that names a person makes that person's concept, traces it and locks it. Every later film reuses that one figure. Age, dress for a role and expression are variants of the one figure, never a new drawing. One person keeps one figure under every name the text uses (Simon, Peter and Cephas; Saul and Paul). An unnamed person an account tells of is a Bible person too, kept under a descriptive id, such as the paralysed man of Capernaum (`paralytic-capernaum`; Matt 9:2, Mark 2:3, Luke 5:18) or the woman of John 8 (owner, 2026-10-18, ask a9d4570003a). The roles of Christ's parables are not Bible people: they come from the crowd kit (2.7) or the ensemble.

**One height band for the cast.** Adults stand within one band of height, set by the court canon (Christ about 3.36 heads, as court.12 draws Him; owner, 2026-10-18, ask a5fc41dfe7b), with only natural variation between them, and Christ at its top (owner: "lets also make sure every character has similar heights (other than natural variability - or characters that are desdribed as being tall like Goliath) - Jesus should not be short compared to others"). Christ is never shorter than the adults beside Him. A height the text gives is a named variant that cites its verse: Goliath's six cubits and a span (1 Samuel 17:4), Saul higher than any of the people from his shoulders upward (1 Samuel 9:2), Zacchaeus little of stature (Luke 19:3). A figure outside the band without such a variant is drawn wrong. A concept is an image-generated sketch of the design, not a measure of height (owner, 2026-10-18: "since concepts use image generation, we can use them as rough design sketch. i like the design more, but we dont need to take too much stalk on heights sicne its hard to constrain, use it only as a general design"). A figure keeps its concept's shapes, face, dress and shading, and takes its height from the band; a concept that draws a figure taller or shorter than the band is not followed in that.

**A concept's head angle and pose are design, not height.** The rough-sketch rule above is about height alone. A concept that turns a head three-quarter, tilts it or raises a hand is drawing the figure's design, and the figure is drawn so: a front, level head where the concept turns one is drawn wrong (owner, 2026-10-17, on the RBF farmer: "the farmer looks nothing like the concept, face proportions and angles are off"). The match flags a differing turn or roll and never reads it as the sketch's slip; the critique names the pose. A raised hand is the house hand raised, floating free: never an arm, though the concept draws a sleeve to it. Before a figure is put to the owner, `film review` takes its 1:1 crops and a fresh critique, and every finding is fixed or answered (SKILL.md, "Review before an owner look"). After he revises a figure, its lesson and guard go in [docs/lessons.md](../../../../apps/animations/docs/lessons.md) before it is redrawn.

### 3.2 Christ

The frame shows the Son freely; the Father is shown only as a veiled, hazy human form (3.3; [god-christ.md](../frame/god-christ.md)): "No man hath seen God at any time; the only begotten Son … he hath declared him" (John 1:18); "he that hath seen me hath seen the Father" (John 14:9). Ellen White: Christ alone is the express image of the Father (PP 45.2). So Christ is drawn, with His face. How He is drawn is held to four texts.

- **An ordinary man.** "He hath no form nor comeliness; and when we shall see him, there is no beauty that we should desire him" (Isa 53:2). "Made in the likeness of men" (Phil 2:7); "in all things it behoved him to be made like unto his brethren" (Heb 2:17). The disciples on the road did not know Him (Luke 24:16); Mary took Him for the gardener (John 20:15); Nazareth knew Him as "the carpenter" (Mark 6:3). Ellen White: men "could not see, beneath the disguise of humility, the glory of the Son of God" (FH 183.2). So He is not the fairest or the most saturated figure in the crowd. He stands at the top of the cast's height band: other adults may reach His height, none passes it (owner, 2026-10-18, ask a9d4570003a). He is "of Juda" (Heb 7:14), a Jewish man of His time.
- **Known by what He does.** In a crowd the eye finds Him by staging (He moves toward people, others turn to Him) and by light (2.3), not by costume or a halo. No halo, no nimbus, no glow on His person in His earthly ministry except where the text lights Him: the transfiguration, "his face did shine as the sun, and his raiment was white as the light" (Matt 17:2; Mark 9:3).
- **His dress follows the text.** In His earthly ministry, the plain coat "without seam, woven from the top throughout" (John 19:23) in undyed linen, a walnut mantle and a headcloth outdoors. As Priest and Advocate, and risen in glory, the robe white and the "golden girdle" (Rev 1:13): the drafts' white robe and gold sash, and the first film's Advocate. As Priest He also wears the breastplate (owner, 2026-10-13: "Christ - breastplate"), the type's sign that He bears His people's names "upon his heart … for a memorial before the LORD continually" (Ex 28:29). It is drawn as twelve small stones in four rows on gold (Ex 28:17–21), and appears only when He ministers as Priest, never in His earthly ministry. In Rev 1's vision, "his head and his hairs were white like wool" (Rev 1:14). **OWNER QUESTION 5.**
  - **One look in a film where the owner keeps it.** _Righteousness by Faith_ keeps Christ in the white robe and gold sash throughout, as its approved Board shows (owner, 2026-10-18, ask a14290aca0a: "One look throughout: the white robe and gold sash, as the approved Board shows; update rule 3.2 for this film"). Another film follows this rule unless the owner keeps one look for it too.
- **One body, frame and face.** Christ is one cast member, with the same body, height in heads, frame and face in every beat and every film (owner, 2026-10-18: "ensure Christ has a consistent body, frame, face"). His dress and pose are variants of that one figure, never a redrawn Christ.
- **The full human range.** "Jesus wept" (John 11:35). He looked on them "with anger, being grieved" (Mark 3:5). "My soul is exceeding sorrowful, even unto death" (Matt 26:38). He was "moved with compassion" (Matt 9:36). He received children (Mark 10:14) and ate with sinners. He is never a still, blank, beatific mask.
- **Never a devotional portrait.** He is always acting in the story: walking, reaching, speaking, breaking bread. Never a framed, held, front-on portrait offered to be looked at for its own sake: the second commandment forbids "the worship of the true God by images or similitudes" (PP 306.1; Ex 20:4). Theology 05's `face` beat, which puts His face in a portrait frame, should be restaged as His face in the story. **OWNER QUESTION 5.**
- **Never stained.** "In all points tempted like as we are, yet without sin" (Heb 4:15). No scarlet on Him, except the sins laid on Him at the cross (1 Pet 2:24; [nature-of-christ.md](../frame/nature-of-christ.md)).
- **The cross.** Drawn at the film's one black moment, in silhouette against the dark, with the sins laid on Him as scarlet. No gore and no wounds in close-up. The mocking robe takes the cloth the text names: purple (John 19:2; Mark 15:17) or Matthew's "scarlet robe" (Matt 27:28) in the cloth crimson of OWNER QUESTION 2, since the soldiers' robe is cloth, not sin. Risen, He shows "his hands and his side" (John 20:20, 27): small ink cuts on the circle hands, not scarlet. The one place blood marks His garment is Rev 19:13, "a vesture dipped in blood", where the text puts it. **OWNER QUESTION 6.**
- **He does not speak on screen.** The films have one narrator ([CRAFT.md](../CRAFT.md)); His words are quoted by the narrator from the KJV, and His mouth moves only as an action (blessing, calling), never lip-synced.
- **Staging.** As Advocate, screen-right of the accused, on the light side, never below the accuser (director-vision, Camera and staging). His face reaches ⅓ of the frame at the answer.

### 3.3 The Father

**Owner, 2026-10-13: "the father should be hazy, with a definite human form."**

- **A definite human form, veiled.** The Father is a person with a form ("a personal, spiritual being", [god-christ.md](../frame/god-christ.md)). The text gives that form on the throne: "the Ancient of days did sit, whose garment was white as snow, and the hair of his head like the pure wool" (Dan 7:9); "the likeness as the appearance of a man above upon it" (Ezek 1:26); "one sat on the throne" (Rev 4:2).
  - Draw a clear human silhouette, seated: head, shoulders, the robe's fall, the hands' places.
  - Wrap it all in a haze of glorious light: "who coverest thyself with light as with a garment" (Ps 104:2).
- **Hazy, never sharp.**
  - No face, no features and no ink outline: "Thou canst not see my face" (Ex 33:20, 23).
  - The form reads through soft gold-white light: feathered edges, the robe white at the centre (Dan 7:9), the light brightest where the face would be.
  - It is the brightest glow in the film.
  - He is never a cut-paper figure like the rest, and never an old man with a beard drawn in detail.
- **Round the throne,** what the text puts there: the fire (Dan 7:9), the rainbow "in sight like unto an emerald" (Rev 4:3), the lamps of fire (Rev 4:5), the host (Dan 7:10).
- **With the Son:** the Son is drawn plainly beside Him, the one "express image of his person" (Heb 1:3). The two read as two distinct persons.
- **No emblem.** No triangle, no three-in-one sign, no eye ([god-christ.md](../frame/god-christ.md)).
- **In a scene, a man, indistinct** (owner, 2026-10-13: "He should be pictured as a man, just an indistinct figure"). Wherever the text puts God present in a scene, the same veiled form stands or walks in it:
  - the judge's seat in a judgment scene: "the Ancient of days did sit … the judgment was set" (Dan 7:9–10). The Son comes "near before him" (Dan 7:13) and is drawn plainly, so the two read apart;
  - the presence in Eden "walking in the garden in the cool of the day" (Gen 3:8): a man's shape walking in light between the trees, with no face.
  - Never a cut-paper judge with a face or a beard. The gold of a verdict comes from His light, not from a gavel.

### 3.4 The Holy Spirit

- **No figure.** No body, no face, no third figure on the throne ([holy-spirit.md](../frame/holy-spirit.md)).
- **The text's signs only.** At Pentecost "a sound from heaven as of a rushing mighty wind" and "cloven tongues like as of fire" (Acts 2:2–3): the wind as motion in paper (mantles, leaves, the page), the tongues as small gold flames that glow, since they are God's gift. At the Jordan, "in a bodily shape like a dove" (Luke 3:22), the one time a dove is drawn. **OWNER QUESTION 8.**
- **In a life.** The heart lit from inside (the power-and-life act, 1.4), never a ghost or a beam entering a body.

### 3.5 Angels

- **They appear as men.** Abraham saw "three men" (Gen 18:2); "some have entertained angels unawares" (Heb 13:2); "a young man … clothed in a long white garment" (Mark 16:5); "two men stood by them in white apparel" (Acts 1:10). Ellen White: "angels in human form enter the homes of men" (6T 342.3). So a messenger on earth is a figure in the human grammar, in white (Matt 28:3; Rev 15:6), a little taller than the people, with no wings.
- **Wings only where the text gives them.** The cherubim "stretch forth their wings on high" over the mercy seat (Ex 25:20); the seraphim "each one had six wings" (Isa 6:2); Gabriel "being caused to fly swiftly" (Dan 9:21); the angel "fly in the midst of heaven" (Rev 14:6). **OWNER QUESTION 7.**
- **White, and never glowing on their own.** Angels' white is the robe white of holy beings. The glow is the light of the One they come from: At Bethlehem "the glory of the Lord shone round about them" (Luke 2:9): draw the glory round them, not from them.
- **They say "Fear not".** "Fear not" (Matt 28:5; Luke 1:30; 2:10); "the angel of the LORD encampeth round about them that fear him" (Ps 34:7). Never frightening, never armoured warriors, never cute cherubs.
- **Never above Christ.** Never brighter, larger or more central than Christ in a shared frame. Never worshipped: "See thou do it not … worship God" (Rev 22:9; Col 2:18).
- **Seraphim** are gold and cream paper, not flame-red: theology 05's draft "flame-red and gold paper" would put the accent on them. Their six wings: "with twain he covered his face, and with twain he covered his feet, and with twain he did fly" (Isa 6:2).
- **The Rev 14 angels** are flying messengers with the gospel; never drawn as 1840s preachers or any historical people (theology 29).

### 3.6 Satan

Section 2.5's principles apply in full. He is drawn by the text's forms, each in its own scene:

- **Lucifer before the fall:** a covering cherub (Ezek 28:14), "every precious stone was thy covering" (Ezek 28:13). His beauty is real and reflected: the throne's gold light falls on him; he glows with none of his own. His pride is shown as the turn of his head from the throne toward himself (Ezek 28:17; Isa 14:12).
- **The serpent in Eden:** "more subtil than any beast of the field" (Gen 3:1). Ellen White saw it winged, "having the color and brilliancy of burnished gold" (PP 53.4): brass sheen, never glowing. After the curse, on its belly (Gen 3:14). **OWNER QUESTION 9.**
- **The accuser:** "Satan standing at his right hand to resist him" (Zech 3:1); "the accuser of our brethren" (Rev 12:10). The kingly frame that sags (EW 152.3), in the accuser ramp (2.5), the one pointing hand, shrinking when rebuked (Zech 3:2).
- **The dragon:** "a great red dragon, having seven heads and ten horns, and seven crowns upon his heads" (Rev 12:3), whose "tail drew the third part of the stars" (Rev 12:4). The text makes him red, so here scarlet is allowed: it is the text's colour, on a prophetic symbol, on the IDEA page or a prophecy STORY scene. Not frightening: flat, cut, symbolic.
- **As an angel of light:** "Satan himself is transformed into an angel of light" (2 Cor 11:14). At the counterfeit coming Ellen White saw "a majestic being of dazzling brightness" (GC 624.2). Draw him in brass sheen that lights nothing round him: no shadow cast by his light, no gold glow on the faces he faces. The real coming lights everything.
- **His end:** "cast into the lake of fire" (Rev 20:10), "I will bring thee to ashes upon the earth" (Ezek 28:18); never a ruler of a burning hell ([hell-destruction.md](../frame/hell-destruction.md)).
- **Never** horns on his head as a person, a tail, a pitchfork, red skin, or a grin of horror (EW 152.3's smile is not drawn).

### 3.7 Places that shape figures

- **Eden.** The pair wore "a robe of light" (COL 310.4), "both naked … and were not ashamed" (Gen 2:25): their figures in the robe white with a soft gold glow, the glow the gift. After the fall the glow goes out, they sew fig leaves (Gen 3:7: leaf green that browns to the dry leaf), and God makes "coats of skins" (Gen 3:21: chipboard). No altar, no slain lamb (1.5). They hid "amongst the trees" (Gen 3:8): faces turned away.
- **The heavenly sanctuary.** The original of which Moses made the copy (Heb 8:2, 5). Two apartments ([sanctuary-judgment.md](../frame/sanctuary-judgment.md)); built of light (backlit gold paper), never a cathedral or a church building. The ark gold with the cherubim facing the mercy seat (Ex 25:20). The host in rows, "thousand thousands" (Dan 7:10; Rev 5:11), as small white figures, never a crowd of portraits.
- **The earthly sanctuary.** The full palette of 1.2's sanctuary entry. The high priest in his garments of glory and beauty (Ex 28:2), and on the day of atonement in plain linen (Lev 16:4).
- **The new earth.** Faces seen: "they shall see his face" (Rev 22:4); "no more death, neither sorrow, nor crying" (Rev 21:4). The saints in white (Rev 7:9), every people.

### 3.8 Owner questions in this section

**Answered (owner, 2026-10-13: "lets go with your recs"):** every question below is settled as recommended.

- **OWNER QUESTION 5. Christ's look.** An ordinary Jewish man, beard (Isa 50:6), no halo, full expressive range, never a devotional portrait (restage theology 05's `face` beat). His earthly coat plain undyed linen (John 19:23); the robe white and gold girdle as Priest, Advocate and in glory (Rev 1:13). Answered in part (owner, 2026-10-13): as Priest He also wears the breastplate (Ex 28:29). Why: Isa 53:2 and FH 183.2 hide His glory under humility, and the white robe in the earthly scenes would say what the text says only of the transfiguration. The alternative is the drafts' white robe and gold sash throughout, which makes Him easy to find in a crowd.
- **OWNER QUESTION 6. The cross.** Silhouette at the black moment; scarlet as the sins laid on Him; no wounds in close-up; the marks in the risen hands as ink cuts; Rev 19:13 the one text-blood exception on His garment.
- **OWNER QUESTION 7. Angels' wings.** Wings only for cherubim, seraphim and angels the text says fly; the messengers on earth appear as men in white. Seraphim gold and cream, not flame-red.
- **OWNER QUESTION 8. The dove.** The Spirit drawn as a dove only at the Jordan (Luke 3:22), never elsewhere.
- **OWNER QUESTION 9. The serpent.** Winged with a burnished, non-glowing brass sheen before the curse (PP 53.4), on its belly after. Why: it is Ellen White's detail beyond the text, though consistent with it; the alternative is a plain serpent, subtle by posture.

## 5. Minor characters

The owner: "for minor characters just pick what makes sense". These are the picks, chosen by the rules above.

**Rules for the set.**

- **The plain lead in front, the cast behind.** A film's main figures are plain leads (2.3), and the cast stands in the background ([ensemble.md](../../../../apps/animations/docs/ensemble.md)). These are for the parts between: a shop needs a shopkeeper, a school a teacher, a court a judge.
- **Parable figures.** Like the cast, never real people, never named in speech, never Bible people.
- **Plain contemporary dress,** no logo, no lettering, no uniform or headcovering that marks a group.
- **Quieter than the cast.** Saturation 0.20–0.24, against the cast's 0.22–0.33.
- **Between the cast's hues.** The cast's eight slots sit about 40° apart (58°, 100°, 140°, 168°, 208°, 245°, 282°, 316°) and skip 330°–50°. The minor set takes the midpoints (79°, 120°, 188°, 226°, 263°, 299°), each at a value unlike both cast neighbours, so no minor figure reads as a cast member in a squint. The stranger and the shopkeeper's apron take oat, the undyed neighbour of the world's chipboard.
- **One hook each** that no cast member has: no beard (Hugo's), no plait (Clara's), no crown tuft (Leo's), no bunches (Nina's), no single curl (Mila's), no shawl or wide brim (Rosa's), no hood (Max's), no round crown of hair (Amara's).
- **Ramps** follow the cast's rule: the shade turns about 20° toward the cool side and darker; the light turns about 10° toward the warm side and lighter. Warm neutrals (oat) shade to a dusky violet, as director-vision says parchment does.
