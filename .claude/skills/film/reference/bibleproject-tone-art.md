## 1. Tone and voice

### Informative, curious, hopeful: the moves

**The hook is a concrete oddity or a question anyone has, never a verdict.**

- "If you were a preying mantis, it would be socially acceptable to devour your mate." (A14T 0:04) … "Yeah, why is that? Why do humans care so much about justice?" / "The Bible has a fascinating response to that question." (A14T 0:18–0:28)
- "Why do I hear Christians say, "I've been redeemed by the blood of the Lamb?"" / "Yeah, that's a really dense phrase. Let's unpack it." (uib2 0:00–0:05)
- "…the word "covet" in English buries something that I think is really interesting that I want us to see." (185K 0:19–0:28)

**The problem is named without heaviness.** The speaker says "we"; sin is a mechanism ("redefining", "default setting", "hijacked", "enslaved"); the turn to hope follows within seconds; a light aside takes the weight off.

- "It shows how we are constantly redefining good and evil to our own advantage at the expense of others." / "Yeah, self preservation." (A14T 1:00–1:07)
- "So, we all participate in injustice…" / "We're all the guilty ones." / "So this is the surprising message of the biblical story." (A14T 4:15–4:23): the verdict and the turn are 2 s apart.
- "It's like the default setting of our hearts is opposed to God's law." (3BGO 3:46)
- "And so paradoxically these rules made Israel even more guilty. But Paul says that paradox is the point." (0SVT 3:25–3:30)
- "…and it's going to kill you. Which is, of course, not good." (185K 1:56–1:57)
- Judgment and justification share one verb: "declaring them to be "rashah", guilty" (A14T 3:36) → "God declared Jesus to be the righteous one when he rose" (4:39) → "declared righteous before God" (4:48).
- Warnings are defused aloud: "They are not there to make you afraid, they are there to show you that rejecting Jesus is foolish because He's so awesome." (1fNW 7:46–7:58)

**The second voice asks in the viewer's own words.** Sixteen sampled questions run 2 to 18 words, median 7.5, in five forms:

- **The echo:** "Beautiful feet?" (xmFP 0:59); "Whoa, he thwarts the wicked?" (A14T 3:10).
- **"What does that even mean?"**: "Doing righteousness, that's a Bible word I don't really use. But, what comes to mind is being a good person. What does that even mean, "being good"?" (A14T 1:37–1:43)
- **"So X?"**, restating to check: "So justice and righteousness is a big deal to God?" (A14T 3:23)
- **"Okay, but… / Okay, so…?"**: "Okay, so where does the blood of the lamb fit into all of this?" (uib2 1:33)
- **The fair objection:** "But wait, you said death has no claim over the life of this lamb. So why does it die?" (uib2 3:03)

**A Hebrew or Greek word has one formula:** name it; give two or three glosses joined with "or even"; tie it to a word the viewer knows; say what it "most basically" means; show 2–4 uses in stories; apply it to God, then Jesus, then us; from then on use it as an English word.

- "It's the Hebrew word "emet," which can be translated as "faithfulness" or even "truth." It's related to another word you've probably heard before, "amen,"…" (HCLu 0:21–0:32); later "Abraham he'emins God; he considers God trustworthy to open a way forward." (HCLu 2:07)
- "To be justified, or literally, "to be declared righteous". It is a rich Old Testament term for Paul." (vmx4 2:54–2:57)
- A translation is corrected gently: "Well, that translation is kind of confusing because…" (3BGO 0:32)

**"So" lines wrap the point in one plain sentence**, often from the second voice: "Okay, so to go back in is like being in the house that is covered by the blood of the lamb, protected from death, belonging to God." / "Yes." (uib2 3:19–3:26)

**Lightness is small and frequent, never at the text's expense:** the animal cold open (A14T 0:04–0:18); "Which is a weird thing to do if you are trying to become king." (xmFP 3:20); Moses in modern speech (3BGO 2:21–2:25); the second voice role-plays: "Ah, I've lost my freedom." (uib2 0:55); casual words: "really, really old", "tons of", "totally worth the effort".

**Wonder is said out loud, about the text:** "fascinating" (A14T 0:28); "this astounding claim" (1fNW 2:25); "Ah, I get it." (uib2 1:09).

**The pivot to hope is a "But" that names a surprise, then a gift:** "But the story doesn't end there." (A14T 1:24); "God's response to humanity's legacy of injustice is to give us a gift: the life of Jesus." (A14T 4:23–4:32); "And here's how:" (0SVT 3:47).

**The landing is short and uses the film's key word:** "We belong to God, and nothing can change that. This is what it means to be redeemed by the blood of the lamb." (uib2 5:25–5:29). **The close invites:** "And as with any gift, all one has to do is receive it." (ABPV 3:40); "we are invited to take that same risk and join Abraham…" (HCLu 4:24–4:35).

The problem takes a median of about 16% of the speech, in short doses each answered quickly (uib2 has five, none over 27 s). Only the Law film inverts the ratio, and its own second voice says "And that's kind of a downer." (3BGO 4:45). The largest share is explanation: the word, the story, the pattern. That is where the curiosity lives.

### Rhythm

- **Openers:** "So", "And", "But", "Yeah", then "Now", "Well", "Right", "Okay". Hinges: "Now, when we turn to…", "Here is the backstory:", "In other words,", "Or, in his words:".
- **Contractions** 1.6–2.6 per 100 words. **"We/us/our"** 0.6–1.6 per 100 words; "I" almost never; "you" only in the hook, a word note, a tip and the invitation.
- **Sources by person or book, almost never by verse:** "the words of the ancient prophet Micah" (A14T 5:31), "according to the apostle Paul" (ABPV 3:15).
- **Hedges:** "kind of", "so to speak", "crystal clear".

### How two voices divide the work

- **Law (labelled):** Jon asks, restates and voices the viewer's reaction ("And that's kind of a downer."); Tim explains and corrects gently, opening "Well," or "No,".
- **Redemption (2025):** the second voice asks at almost every turn, role-plays, objects and wraps; the first answers "Right." "Yes." "Yes, exactly."
- **Desire (2026):** full of one-word affirmations ("- Okay." "- Desire. - Desire." "- Mhm."); the second voice also reads the Scripture aloud (185K 1:27–1:38).
- Galatians, Romans, Hebrews, Faithful and Gracious are one narrator (as captioned; **unchecked** by ear), holding a conversation inside one voice with "Now," and "Like when…".

Exchanges worth imitating:

1. "It can refer to retributive justice." / "Like if I steal something, I pay the consequences." / "Exactly." (A14T 2:04–2:09)
2. "But he thwarts the way of the wicked." / "Whoa, he thwarts the wicked?" / "Yeah, in Hebrew the word "wicked" is "rashah"." (A14T 3:07–3:12)
3. "- [Jon] So that seems pretty easy. I mean, we all want to love." / "- [Tim] Well, we think we want to love, but Jesus showed how love is far more demanding than we realize." (3BGO 4:07–4:13)
4. "- [Jon] And that's kind of a downer." / "- [Tim] But where Israel failed, Jesus brought this story to its fulfillment." (3BGO 4:45–4:48)
5. "But wait, you said death has no claim over the life of this lamb. So why does it die?" / "Yes, exactly. It's not fair." (uib2 3:03–3:08)

### Their plain words for our terms (register only; doctrine comes from the frame)

- **Righteousness:** "an ethical standard that refers to right relationships between people" (A14T 1:50); set against "what comes to mind is being a good person" (A14T 1:40).
- **Justified:** "declared righteous before God, not because of anything they have done, but because of what Jesus did for them" (A14T 4:48); "not just as a new status, but as a power that changed their lives" (A14T 4:56–5:02).
- **Faith:** "most basically it means "to consider someone trustworthy," or "to trust."" (HCLu 1:40); "what is true of Him becomes true of them" (vmx4 3:41).
- **Grace:** "a gift given with delight or favor" (ABPV 0:59); "kharis, which means gracious gift" (ABPV 2:58).
- **High priest:** "to represent Israel before God and to offer sacrifices that atoned for or covered over for the sins of the people" (1fNW 4:56); "He's the ultimate priest." (1fNW 5:18).
- **Law:** "like a magnifying glass" (vmx4 5:21; 0SVT 3:48); "But the laws, good as they are, they did not give Israel the power to obey them." (vmx4 7:03).

### The looks

**A14T Justice (2017): 2D sketch figures in a cardboard diorama, the closest match to a cut-paper engine.**

- **Colour script:** ink animals on cardboard tiles → teal day for "image of God" → rose-brown with darker corners for "how the world really works" → peach to yellow for Abraham, the words and Israel → sunset, then a black silhouette at the cross → dawn relight at the tomb → teal/mint to yellow for the landing, which returns to the opening sky.
- **Figures:** flat light-grey (#b1a89c–#b7b7b2) bean bodies with a sketchy dark outline of varying weight; tapering legs and handless arms; round heads with a cross guideline for facing and no features. Emotion by posture only: hands on hips, hand to chin, curled on the steps, head bowed, running with arms flung up.
- **Light and depth:** soft contact shadows; warm sky light with a rim at sunset; miniature depth of field (soft foreground boards and far towers). Backgrounds are clean 2–3-stop vertical gradients, no clouds.
- **Camera:** aerial wide descending to eye level; close-ups carry the "power" beat.
- **Transitions:** a word morphing into its Hebrew on a gradient; a red ribbon sweeping across the lens as a wipe; sunset → silhouette → dark tomb → dawn relight.
- **Type:** a rounded heavy sans in cream; Hebrew in a brush serif on a ribbon; Scripture as white condensed caps on grey slabs lying in the city like monuments.

**1fNW Hebrews (2015), vmx4 Galatians (2016): an ink poster on parchment.** Luma 186–201, saturation about 0.18; small comic ink characters with strong brows and dot eyes; drawn light (radiating ink lines); one continuous shot over a poster that grows; hand-lettered caps, the key word reversed white on black ("JUSTIFIED" → "DECLARED RIGHTEOUS", vmx4 2:58).

**HCLu Faithful (2021): flat vector in a square frame.** The square (450×450 in an 854×480 matte) opens to 16:9 as the kingdom collapses (≈3:30) and returns at 3:58. Tall robe figures with small heads and disc hands; faces are one brow line and closed-eye strokes, no mouth. Green petal god-rays around the king on a stepped hill (3:22); stepped green hills as the ground people stand on; floating islands when trust fails. Tall condensed hand-drawn caps with a tiny figure above the word (1:42).

**185K Desire (2026): a risograph zine collage, the current look.** Pink-to-purple washes on dot-grid paper; torn cards and taped sticker icons; detailed hatched ink faces; one metaphor object that changes (the eye's pupil is a magnet, 1:10 → a compass, 2:34); Scripture typed on a card with one word highlighted and the reference on label tape.

### How they picture abstractions

- **Righteousness:** a white banner lettered צדקה arcing over one thoughtful figure (A14T 1:50), then over two figures clasping hands (1:58).
- **Guilt:** a red banner רשע (A14T 3:14) becomes a sash binding a figure (3:26), then red ribbons stream across Egypt and the whole city (3:42–3:58). Galatians draws "old humanity" as a black blob with the vices inside (vmx4 7:24).
- **Law:** Moses with tablets under ink rays; a magnifying glass over a golden-calf scene labelled FAILURE / SIN (vmx4 5:22); a strict schoolteacher, "STAY IN LINE" (5:46); Scripture slabs as monuments (A14T 2:42–3:10).
- **Faith and trust:** friends hold up Moses' arms, "HIS HANDS WERE EMET" (HCLu 0:54); a bench tips over on "stability"; people stand on a stepped hill; the ground breaks into floating islands when trust fails (3:26–3:46).
- **Union:** "what's true of him becomes true of them": a cross and tomb with figures whose raised arms give off radiating lines (vmx4 3:42–3:58).
- **Gift:** a white cloth handed over at the tomb (A14T 4:46–4:55), then handed on through a crowd (5:12–5:22).
- **High priest:** a diagram only: people → "FLAWED PRIEST" → altar with a SIN cloud ⇄ a rayed GOD cloud; below, Jesus as "ULTIMATE PRIEST KING / FLAWLESS / ETERNAL" ⇄ GOD (1fNW 4:54–5:58). No STORY-register priest (robe, breastplate, veil) appears; the Royal Priest and Temple films are **unchecked**.

**The exchange, A14T's own sequence (4:24–5:24):**

1. "He did righteousness and justice": a kneeling figure washes the feet of a seated figure in a red sash; the red comes off, and a white cloth passes between them.
2. "died on behalf of the guilty": the red ribbon goes onto the cross; dip to a black silhouette.
3. "declared Jesus to be the righteous one when he rose": the dark tomb relights to dawn.
4. "so that they, too, can be declared righteous": a figure wrapped in white hands white cloth to a grey figure; the red strip lies on the ground.
5. "not just as a new status, but as a power": close-ups as the white ribbon streams and wraps the figure.
6. "the only reasonable response": the white is handed on to others.
7. The landing: two figures share one scarf under the teal-to-yellow sky.

### In Canvas 2D

**Reproduces well:** 2–3-stop gradient skies that change per beat; chipboard blocks (a lighter top face, a darker side, grain, jittered edges, parallax with blurred foreground and background); featureless grey bean figures with a wobbly outline and a contact shadow, emotion by posture; ribbons and sashes as Bézier strips with a darker underside, wrapped with clipping or flown along a path; one word card on a gradient or on the ribbon itself; the silhouette dip and dawn relight; petal god-rays with `'lighter'` compositing; stepped hills and floating islands; a square frame opening to 16:9 as an animated clip; flat close-up faces (a brow line, closed-eye strokes, disc hands); radiating joy lines; small edge tags.

**Not worth attempting:** photoreal miniature sets with real paper curl; a continuous ink poster with hundreds of hand-lettered labels (it also breaks the text budget); hatched ink faces and etched animals; riso collage texture; hand-drawn 3D Hebrew; cloth simulation (keep ribbons as scripted curves).

## 3. Recommendations for a salvation explainer

1. **Make the exchange one colour-coded object.** Guilt is a red sash; righteousness is a white robe or sash. It binds, is carried to the cross, the frame dips to black, dawn shows the red on the ground and the white handed over, the white works as power, then is passed on.
2. **Dose the problem:** at most 20% of the speech, in 2–4 doses of 5–40 s, each answered within seconds; hope totals at least 1.5 times the problem; the final hope stretch starts at 75–85% and runs 40–90 s.
3. **One question at each turn, in the five forms** (2–18 words, median 7.5), and let the same voice wrap: "Okay, so…".
4. **Name sin in "we", as a mechanism, then take the weight off lightly;** put judgment in the verb "declared" so it can flip to "declared righteous".
5. **A hopeful colour script:** mean luma about 140–150, at most 5% of frames darker than 60, one black moment (the cross), teal day for the opening and the landing, peach for explanation, sunset at the cross, dawn at the answer; the night palette for at most one IDEA stretch.
6. **Explain words with the formula,** one word card per word, then use the word as a verb.
7. **Figures after A14T** (grey beans, sketch outline, emotion by posture); close-ups after HCLu (brow line, closed-eye strokes, disc hands).
8. **The law as a magnifying glass** that shows the stain but cannot remove it; it pairs with a mirror.
9. **Faith as steady ground and being held** (a stepped hill; ground that breaks where trust fails; light gathering on the faithful one).
10. **Union as figures raised beside the cross and tomb, with radiating lines.**
11. **The high priest as the one between the people and the light,** the sanctuary as one icon, not a set, so the IDEA stays clear.
12. **Hinges:** "But the story doesn't end there." "So this is the surprising message…" "And here's how:"; the landing a short sentence in the key word, then "all one has to do is receive it" or "we are invited…".
13. **Wonder aloud once per act;** a light cold open; the second voice may role-play.
14. **Their transitions:** a ribbon wipe across the lens; a word morphing into its original language; sunset → silhouette → dawn relight.
15. **Text on screen: one element, one job;** Scripture as an object in the world; the landing line may be lettered once.
16. **Name sources as people and books in speech,** never chapter and verse.
