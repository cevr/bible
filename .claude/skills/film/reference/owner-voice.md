# The owner's voice

How the owner preaches, measured for the scripts he narrates: every film is his voice (one narrator, and he records the final take), so a script should already sound like him on the page. Measured on nine sermons he preached live between March and June 2026, from the streams he pointed to (2026-10-14). The aims are set elsewhere and are not repeated here: the series' Voice and audience ([series/README.md](../../../../apps/animations/series/README.md)) sets the audience and the persuasion, and [CRAFT.md](../CRAFT.md) rules 2, 6, 9 and 11 set quoting, the viewer's question, the pace and the tone's doses. This file is how he does it, and how each church habit translates into a film. Every example cites his words as `<id4> m:ss` (or h:mm:ss) on the stream's clock; a line the two transcripts below disagree on is paraphrased, never quoted.

## How measured, how sure

- **His part only.** His turns were found by voice (speaker embeddings scored against his own named sample, evjw from 25:00) and by what he says, then transcribed with ElevenLabs Scribe, speakers separated. Everyone else's words are left out: 42,861 of his words in 4,614 sentences, from 8.5 hours of services.
- **Every quote is checked twice.** Each line quoted below is Scribe's, matched against the stream's own automatic captions at the same time (an independent recogniser): 78–100% of the words agree, and the misses are numerals and homophones ("11" for eleven, "arc" for ark).
- **The room shapes his cadence.** Every service is interpreted into a second language, phrase by phrase, so he stops after each phrase, and repeats references and key lines for the room. Inside a phrase he speaks at 197–229 words a minute. His sentence lengths are a floor the room set, not a target (section 5).
- **The counts include the Scripture he reads aloud,** and the sentences are Scribe's punctuation. One transcript (sI8Z) carried a second copy of some passages, 9% of its words, removed before counting.
- **This is live preaching to one congregation, not narration.** The moves, their order and the words are measured; how he would read a script is not. Section 7 gives the film's form of each church habit.

## 1. Openings

**He opens on one question and says the sermon will answer it.** It comes within a minute of the opening prayer, in plain words, about the listener's own faith.

- "So the question is, why are we still here? Why hasn't Jesus come?" (evjw 25:56)
- "Today, we're gonna be answering the question: What is salvation?" (sI8Z 25:00)
- "And I'll start with a question." … "And the question is, why are we not united?" (t1B1 46:51–47:18)
- "What is God's purpose? Tell me. Tell me, guys." (ig_W 20:03): he builds on the room's answer, restoration.

**The stakes come in the next breath.** "I want for all of us to truly understand it." (sI8Z 25:10), and then: if we don't understand what salvation is, we won't be saved (sI8Z 25:17, paraphrased).

**Or a thesis, with a promise of proof:** "We must show our faith. And I will show you why I believe this." (G1Zh 20:05)

**Before a hard doctrine, he asks its objection first,** in the doubter's words: "Why would God need to review people's lives again? Doesn't He know everything?" (XDZZ 23:37)

**A series resumes with a numbered recap,** a sentence a part: "Part one. We talked about why we're created. And the answer was that we were needed here." (xfay 25:35)

**In a film,** the opening question (CRAFT rules 1 and 6) is his kind: one the viewer half-asks already, under ten words, that the whole film answers. The film starts where his sermon starts after the prayer, with no greeting and no "Let's go to…". Where he opens on a doctrine's name and asks whether the room has heard of it (XDZZ 21:01–21:25), a film opens on the question the doctrine answers, since its viewer has never heard the name.

## 2. Building the case

**Text first, read as it reads.** Within two minutes of the question he is in a passage, reads it through, and draws out its plain sense. The one word that carries the argument gets one gloss: "The Hebrew word for blameless. And this word tam, it means he was complete." (XDZZ 25:00)

- His test for any claim: "But can you use the Bible to prove it?" (t1B1 58:22)
- The literal reading first: "Was it a spiritual ark? … It was a real literal ark." (G1Zh 1:53:44). He ends one sermon on the two ways to read, as the Bible reads or mystically, and asks which the listener will take (t1B1 1:59:47–2:00:07).

**He asks, then answers short.** A quarter of his sentences are questions (1,207 of 4,614; 41% in ig_W). He answers 28% of them himself at once, in five words or fewer, and about one in ten checks on the room ("Right?", "Does that make sense?").

- "Babylon is who? Babylon is Rome." (G1Zh 1:42:05)
- "So how long was the journey supposed to last to Canaan? Eleven days." (evjw 27:44) … "So a journey of eleven days took forty years." (evjw 28:11)
- "What do you think it devours? What do you think it burns?" (sI8Z 32:46)

**The objection is asked fairly, then turned.**

- After "Doesn't He know everything?" (XDZZ 23:46) his answer is Job: the judgment is not for God's information; God must show that His people's hearts are complete (XDZZ 31:03–32:55, paraphrased).
- Jesus tells the woman to sin no more: is that even possible? "Is Jesus mocking this woman?" (sI8Z 1:16:11). No: with the word He gives the power to obey it (sI8Z 1:15:26–1:16:27, paraphrased).

**A plain likeness carries the doctrine, from the lesser to the greater.**

- "The same way this man cannot walk even though he wishes to walk is the same way we sin even though we wish we could stop." (sI8Z 1:09:36)
- "It is like he puts clothing on us. It is not something that we made. It is not something that we deserve." (sI8Z 1:16:39)
- For the incarnation: "How many of you would be willing to become an ant to save the ant?" (D_j4 39:37)

**He puts the listener inside the scene, in "you", present tense:** "So imagine this." (D_j4 31:30) … "You confess your sins upon it. And then you must kill it yourself." (D_j4 31:47)

**One refrain holds the argument,** in parallel parts, returned to at each turn: "Justification is three things. It is faith. It is forgiveness. It is power." (sI8Z 1:07:00, again at 1:11:28, 1:14:14 and 1:16:59). That sermon tells the two stories the `righteousness-by-faith` film tells, the paralysed man and the woman brought to Jesus in the temple, and counts the same three gifts in each.

**The stakes are God's as well as ours.** Satan accused God's character, and His people are the answer: "God had made us to answer these accusations." (xfay 27:00); "So the honor of God is on the line when it comes to His people." (XDZZ 34:12). What God wants for us is "to have his character" (d1Hz 23:20).

**The order: reason first, feeling after, trust throughout.** The question, the text, its plain sense, the likeness, what it means for us, the honest heart, the choice. His authority is never his own: his opening prayers ask "let the words that I speak come from your throne" (D_j4 28:21; the same in evjw, G1Zh and xfay), and he tells the room where he has struggled (section 3).

## 3. The landing and the appeal

**He lands on a choice between two, said plainly.** The urgency lives here, in the last eighth.

- "What is before us is life or death." (xfay 1:12:58)
- "The judgments are coming. If you believe God, you will escape." (G1Zh 1:51:29): the warning and the way out in one breath.
- "The question is not, do you believe in God? Everyone here believes in God." … "Do you believe God can make you complete?" (XDZZ 56:53–57:04)

**What the choice costs is listed in parallel:** "To give up our careers. To give up our friends. To give up our time." (D_j4 1:03:07); "Though he slay me. Though I die. Though I lose my career." (XDZZ 58:51)

**Then the honest heart.** "Understand that many of us feel this way. I have felt this way many times in my life." (D_j4 1:01:49); "I don't even want it. Help me to want to want it." (D_j4 1:03:45). Honesty is the door, not the failure: "And if you say honestly, Lord, I don't love you." … "Hallelujah, because this is what Jesus waits for." (d1Hz 1:11:08–1:11:25)

**The pronouns climb.** Per 1,000 of his words, "I" goes from 14 in the body to 25 in the landing, "we" from 38 to 56, "you" from 25 to 41.

**He ends on a call, then a prayer that claims promises.** "If you wish to follow God, I say please stand." (xfay 1:15:37). The closing prayer names what God will do: give a new heart, write His law in it, cause us to walk in His statutes (ig_W 1:35:30–1:36:10, paraphrased).

**In a film,** the landing (CRAFT rule 7) puts his choice as the viewer's question, answers it with a promise in a verse, and ends on the thesis. A film asks no one to stand and prays for no one: the prayer's promise becomes the last line, in Scripture's words. The warning is one dose, with its way out in the same sentence (CRAFT rule 11).

**Say the wonder his way.** CRAFT rule 11 asks for wonder out loud once an act. He never says amazing, awesome or wonderful (none in 42,861 words) and says "Wow" twice: his wonder is the plain fact held a moment, what God did, in a dozen words or fewer.

**Name sin in "we", and include himself.** "We do not want to go to heaven. We do not want Jesus to return." (evjw 31:23): the hard line is said of us all, then reasoned with. He voices our excuses in our own words: "Oh, I gotta go to church today." (D_j4 1:01:29)

**Short statements, one idea each; join the room's phrases into the film's sentences.** His median sentence runs 4 to 10 words, partly for the interpreter. Narration at CRAFT rule 9's 150–165 words a minute reads well at 8 to 16 words, with one line of six words or fewer at each turn: "Eleven days." (evjw 27:44), "We've forgotten it." (G1Zh 22:14), "It is power." (sI8Z 1:07:00).

**Repeat by design, never for the room.** In church he gives a reference up to four times while the room finds it ("John, chapter one." ×3, then "John, chapter one, verse four.", sI8Z 44:18) and doubles lines for the interpreter: cut those. Keep the parallel run of three or four on one opening word:

- "Outside of their culture. Outside of their background. Outside of how they grew up." (t1B1 1:39:51)
- "we may not have money. We may not have any friend on this earth. We may not have any degrees." (xfay 1:13:55)
- a run of eight "We want…" lines lists what we put before God, from our careers to where we live (t1B1 1:53:43).

**The first word carries the logic.** And opens 8.9% of his sentences, So 6.0%, What 4.0%, We 3.9%, But 2.3%. "So" draws the conclusion from a text; "Because" gives the reason after a question; "But" makes the turn: "But God is waiting for a final answer to this accusation." (xfay 41:10). Church fillers lead too ("All right" 61 times, "Let's go" 55): cut them.

**"We" most, "you" for the scene and the choice, "I" for the confession:** 38, 25 and 14 per 1,000 words in the body (section 3 for the landing).

**Write the pauses in.** Inside a phrase he is quicker than BibleProject's narrators (197–229 words a minute against their 179–193, [bibleproject-study.md](bibleproject-study.md)); the stops for the interpreter bring him down. Short sentences with CRAFT rule 9's gaps give his recording that room; long ones run together lose it.

## 8. Do and don't

Do:

- **Open on the question,** in the viewer's words, and let the film answer it.
- **Go to the text early,** read it as it reads, and gloss the one word that carries it.
- **Ask, and answer short.** One sentence in four is a question in his sermons; a film may ask fewer, but every turn has one (CRAFT 6).
- **Ask the objection fairly** before answering it.
- **Find one likeness the viewer can see,** lesser to greater, and say "the same way…".
- **Give the argument one refrain** in three parallel parts, and come back to it at each turn.
- **Say sin in "we",** and the scene and the choice in "you".
- **Land on two ways,** with the way out in the same breath, then the promise.

Don't:

- **Don't exclaim.** No amazing, awesome or wonderful; no "beautiful truth", no "journey".
- **Don't assert what no text proves,** and don't spiritualize a literal text.
- **Don't joke in the landing,** or at the text's expense.
- **Don't put a life in his mouth.** He records the voice, so an "I" line must be true of him. Write the confession in "we"; mark any "I" line in the script for him to confirm at the recording, and never invent a story of his family, his work or his health.
- **Don't carry the church's scaffolding** (section 7), or its insider words undefined.
- **Don't scold.** A "must" follows from a text and brings the way to obey; the warning is one dose in the landing.

## 9. Sounds like him

Two film lines in his manner, each beside one he would never say.

**A teaching beat.**

- Sounds like him: "He wanted to walk. But Jesus said first, “Son, thy sins be forgiven thee.” Why forgiveness first? Because the same way this man could not walk, however much he wished to, we cannot stop sinning, however much we wish we could."
- Doesn't: "In this amazing story, we discover the beautiful truth that God's grace is a wonderful gift that transforms our brokenness."
- Why: his asks and answers, reasons by a likeness, says "we" and quotes one verse in KJV; the other exclaims, names no mechanism, and uses five words he never or hardly says.

**A landing.**

- Sounds like him: "So the question is not, do you believe in God? Do you believe God can make you complete? Job lost everything, and he said, “Though he slay me, yet will I trust in him.”"
- Doesn't: "As one inspired writer put it, God has a wonderful plan for your journey, and Job 13:15 reminds us never to lose hope."
- Why: his puts the choice as a question and lets a witness answer in Scripture; the other leans on a writer instead of a text, speaks chapter and verse, and uses three words he never or hardly says.
