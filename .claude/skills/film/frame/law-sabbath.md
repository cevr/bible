# Law and Sabbath (1889 Principles XI, XII, XIII)

## Pioneer position

Principles XI–XIII (FP1889 149.2, 149.3, 150.1) hold that God's law "is immutable and perpetual, being a transcript of the tables deposited in the ark in the true sanctuary on high": Ellen White saw the tables with "the ten commandments written on them with the finger of God" (Broadside3 April 7, 1847, par. 3), and Smith finds the law in the ark of Revelation 11:19 "unaltered in one jot or tittle" (DAR 507.4). The seventh-day Sabbath "was instituted in Paradise" and stands in "Paradise restored" (TSPL 20.2); it is "a sign (or seal)" given "twenty-five hundred years before there was a descendant of Jacob" (SLG 23.1) and "not a shadow; but a body of itself" (SDSNA 16.6); it "was not nailed to the cross", though "the Pope had changed it from the seventh to the first day of the week; for he was to change times and laws" (Broadside3 April 7, 1847, par. 3); and "Jewish Sabbath" and "Christian Sabbath" are "names of human invention, unscriptural in fact, and false in meaning" (FP1889 149.3; SDSNA 17.5).

## Film direction

MUST SHOW:

- **The heavenly ark, opened.** Show "the tables of stone which folded together like a book", "the ten commandments written on them with the finger of God", and the fourth commandment shining "above them all": "The holy Sabbath looked glorious—a halo of glory was all around it." (Broadside3 April 7, 1847, par. 3). The tables "are now in the ark, in the Most Holy Place of the Temple in heaven" (Broadside3 April 7, 1847, par. 4).
- **Revelation 11:19 as a reveal.** The temple in heaven opens and "the ark of his testament" is seen, holding the same law "unaltered in one jot or tittle" (DAR 507.4; FP1889 149.2). Anchor it to the end of the 2300 days (DAR 507.4).
- **One Sabbath line from Eden to the new earth.** It runs "instituted in Paradise" → Sinai → "Paradise restored" (TSPL 20.2; FP1889 149.3: "set apart before Paradise was lost … observed in Paradise restored").
- **The sign at creation.** It is given "on the seventh day of creation … for the whole Israel of God, from Adam", 2,500 years before Jacob (SLG 23.1). Draw Adam, not Moses, receiving it.
- **Two laws in two places.** "The ten commandments lay in unapproachable majesty inside the golden ark"; "the law of types and ceremonies lay outside the ark, written with ink, by human hands, on the perishable parchment" (WWNC 2.1).
- **The little horn reaching for the fourth commandment.** He "was to change times and laws" (Broadside3 April 7, 1847, par. 3; FP1889 150.1). The papacy "torn from its place the Sabbath of Jehovah … and erected in its place a rival institution" (DAR 140.3).

MUST NOT SAY OR SHOW:

- **The Sabbath as a shadow.** Do not show it as a type that ends at the cross, or that is fulfilled into a rest in Jesus that replaces the day. The sources say "it is not a shadow; but a body of itself" (SDSNA 16.6) and "The Sabbath is not a shadow, for it is to be observed as long as the New Heavens and the New Earth remain." (SDSNA 16.1).
- **The "Jewish Sabbath" label.** Do not use it, and do not frame the seventh day as Israel's ethnic covenant badge first given at Sinai (SDSNA 17.5; SLG 23.1; FP1889 149.3).
- **Sunday as "the Christian Sabbath" or "the Lord's day".** Do not show it as moved by Jesus, the apostles or the resurrection. In these sources the change is the papacy's (Broadside3 April 7, 1847, par. 3; FP1889 150.1; DAR 140.3). Smith: "no other day can be called the Lord’s day but the Sabbath of the great Creator" (DAR 340.2).
- **Colossians 2:14 over the stone tables.** Do not draw "nailing it to his cross" over the Ten Commandments. Ellen White: "the Sabbath was not nailed to the cross" (Broadside3 April 7, 1847, par. 3). What was nailed is the parchment law outside the ark (WWNC 2.1).
- **The law as one undifferentiated Torah.** Do not present it as a single code that expired as a block (WWNC 2.1; DAR 507.4).

## Distinctives

- **Decalogue continues, Sabbath does not.** Mainstream: the Decalogue's morals continue, but the Sabbath command was ceremonial or fulfilled in Christ. Pioneers: the fourth commandment is inside the one unaltered law in the heavenly ark (DAR 507.4; Broadside3 April 7, 1847, par. 3; FP1889 149.2). The weekly Sabbath is "a body of itself … as well as the other nine commandments" (SDSNA 16.6).
- **A sign for Israel only.** Mainstream: the Sabbath was Israel's covenant sign from Sinai. Pioneers: the sign or seal dates from creation and belongs to "the whole Israel of God, from Adam" (SLG 23.1). It stands in Paradise restored (TSPL 20.2).
- **Colossians 2:14-17.** Mainstream: these verses set the Sabbath aside. Pioneers: they concern the ceremonial "book of the law" kept beside the ark (WWNC 2.1), and a never-ending weekly Sabbath cannot be a shadow (SDSNA 16.6).
- **Sunday.** Mainstream: Sunday is the Lord's day or the Christian Sabbath. Pioneers:
  - the change was the papacy's attempt to "change times and laws" (Broadside3 April 7, 1847, par. 3; FP1889 150.1; DAR 140.3);
  - the names are "names of human invention, unscriptural in fact, and false in meaning" (FP1889 149.3; SDSNA 17.5);
  - "the Lord's day" of Revelation 1:10 is the seventh day (DAR 340.2).
- **The heavenly ark.** Mainstream explainers rarely use it. For the pioneers, Revelation 11:19 is the proof-text of the law's perpetuity (DAR 507.4; FP1889 149.2).

## Unverified / not found

- **Ezekiel 20:12, 20.** The clearest exposition found is the section "OBJECT OF THE SABBATH" in "The Day of Rest", _The Present Truth_ (UK) 9, no. 23 (PTUK September 7, 1893, page 354 for the heading; pages 358.3–358.6 for the section). It presents the Sabbath as the sign of God's sanctifying power that reveals Christ. It is an unsigned editorial reply. The volume carries an EJW (E. J. Waggoner) tag 544 times, including on the neighbouring articles in the same issue, so it cannot be told apart from an editor tag (compare the JWe tag throughout the Review). The writer is therefore not established, and it is not used as a quote.
- **Butler's Dan 7:25 paragraph.** _The Change of the Sabbath_ (ChSa 170.3) states the papal-change argument clearly, but the corpus gives no year. DAR 140.3 (1897) is used for the supplementary reference instead.
- **Corpus defect in FP1889 149.3.** The corpus text of Principle XII has a footnote from another section inserted mid-sentence. Quote it only with "…" around the intrusion, or from the scan-checked yearbook text.
- **TSPL 20.2 citation typo.** The citation list reads "Luke 22:55, 56" (corpus as printed). It was left out of `cites`.
