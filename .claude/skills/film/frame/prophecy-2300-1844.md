# prophecy-2300-1844 — The 2300 days, the 70 weeks, and the Disappointment of 1844

## Pioneer position

Under 1889 Principle IX, "the mistake of Adventists in 1844 pertained to the nature of the event then to transpire, not to the time": the 2300 days of Dan 8:14 "terminated in 1844, and brought us to an event called the cleansing of the sanctuary," and "no prophetic period is given to reach to the second advent"; its note records that the Adventists of 1844 "believed that the earth was the sanctuary then to be cleansed, and that its cleansing was to be accomplished with fire" (FP1889 148.7), and that "Seventh-day Adventists set no time" (FP1889 149.3). The quotes trace the whole arc: Miller's premise was that the earth is cleansed "by fire" "when our God shall come" (LJHCS 9.1); the 70 weeks, "cut off" from the 2300 days and so beginning with them "in the autumn of B. C. 457," bring the 2300 years "to the autumn of A. D. 1844" (TRMC August 22, 1844, page 2.2), with Christ crucified "in the midst of this week" in "the spring of A.D. 31" and the 490 ending "in the fall of A.D. 34" (SOTB 5.3); "the days, consequently did there end," but "the prophecy only said “Then shall the sanctuary be cleansed”" (LUJ 202.2), and that sanctuary "is not on earth, but in heaven" (LOM, page 38.7), so "the mistake had not been in the reckoning of the prophetic periods, but in the event to take place at the end of the 2300 days" (GC 424.1).

## Quotes

> The next question which arises is, How will the earth be cleansed? I answer, by fire. … When will the earth and the wicked be burned by fire? I answer, when our God shall come.

— **LJHCS 9.1** · William Miller, _Letter to Joshua V. Himes, on the Cleansing of the Sanctuary_ (1842)

> The period commenced with the 70 weeks of Daniel 9:24, which are determined or cut off and constitute apart of the 2300 days. They must therefore commence together. … From Ezra 7:8, 9, we learn that Ezra began to go up on the first day of the first month, and arrived at Jerusalem on the first day of the fifth month, in the 7th year of Artaxerxes, B. C. 457. … They therefore commenced when they begin to build, in the autumn of B. C. 457; from that point 2300 years reach to the autumn of A. D. 1844.

— **TRMC August 22, 1844, page 2.2** · S. S. Snow, _The True Midnight Cry_, vol. 1, no. 1, August 22 (1844)

> The sixty-nine weeks did, therefore end with the beginning of our Lord’s ministry in the fall of A.D. 27. … In the midst of this week, the sacrifice and oblation were to cease. … And so it was that our Lord preached during three years and a half, until the spring of A.D. 31, when he was crucified for the sins of men. … As the 490 ended in the fall of A.D. 34, the remaining 1810 days ended in the fall of 1844.

— **SOTB 5.3** · J. N. Andrews, _The Sanctuary of the Bible_ (n.d.)

> Why did not the Lord come in 1844, as the Adventists expected? … The days, consequently did there end; and if the prophecy had said that then the Lord would come, he would have come. But the prophecy only said “Then shall the sanctuary be cleansed.” … They said, The earth is the sanctuary, and this earth is finally to be purified by fire; and so the burning of this earth is what the prophet means when he says that “then shall the sanctuary be cleansed.”

— **LUJ 202.2** · Uriah Smith, _Looking Unto Jesus_, section “The Mistake Explained” (1898)

> The Sanctuary to be cleansed at the end of 2300 days is also the Sanctuary of the new covenant, for the vision of the treading down and cleansing, is after the crucifixion. We see that the Sanctuary of the new covenant is not on earth, but in heaven.

— **LOM, page 38.7** · O. R. L. Crosier, _The Law of Moses_, Day-Star Extra, Cincinnati, February 7 (1846)

> Both the prophecy of Daniel 8:14, “Unto two thousand and three hundred days; then shall the sanctuary be cleansed,” and the first angel’s message, “Fear God, and give glory to Him; for the hour of His judgment is come,” pointed to Christ’s ministration in the most holy place, to the investigative judgment, and not to the coming of Christ for the redemption of His people and the destruction of the wicked. The mistake had not been in the reckoning of the prophetic periods, but in the event to take place at the end of the 2300 days. Through this error the believers had suffered disappointment, yet all that was foretold by the prophecy, and all that they had any Scripture warrant to expect, had been accomplished.

— **GC 424.1** · Ellen G. White, _The Great Controversy_ (1911)

> The tenth day of the seventh month, the great day of atonement, the time of the cleansing of the sanctuary, which in the year 1844 fell upon the 22d of October, was regarded as the time of the Lord’s coming. This was in harmony with the proofs already presented that the 2300 days would terminate in the autumn, and the conclusion seemed irresistible.

— **GC88 399.3** · Ellen G. White, _The Great Controversy_ (1888 ed.) (1888). Added by the compiler: this is the pre-1915 statement of the calendar date.

## Film direction

**Must show**

- **The Millerite premise as they held it.** The earth is the sanctuary, to be cleansed by fire when the Lord comes (LJHCS 9.1). LJHCS 8.1 names "the EARTH and the CHURCH." See also the note to 1889 IX (FP1889 148.7) and GC 409.1.
- **One timeline bar.** Run 2300 years from the autumn of 457 B.C. to the autumn of A.D. 1844. The start is the decree carried out in the 7th year of Artaxerxes, from Ezra 7. Mark the 490 years (the 70 weeks) as cut off at the start of the bar (TRMC August 22, 1844, page 2.2; SOTB 5.3).
- **Inside the 70 weeks** (SOTB 5.3; see also GC 410.1):
  - 69 weeks run to Christ's ministry, in the fall of A.D. 27.
  - The crucifixion falls "in the midst" of the 70th week, in the spring of A.D. 31.
  - The 490 years end in A.D. 34.
  - 1810 years remain, reaching the fall of 1844.
- **The Disappointment.** The days end, the Lord does not come, and the believers "had suffered disappointment" (LUJ 202.2; GC 424.1).
- **The re-reading.** The camera lifts from the earth to the sanctuary in heaven (LOM, page 38.7). Christ comes to the Ancient of Days (Dan 7:13), not to the earth (GC 424.2, read but not quoted). GC 422.1 has the same point.
- **The date stands; the event is redefined** (GC 424.1; 1889 IX).

**Must not say or show**

- **"They got the math wrong."** Do not say the date was miscalculated. 1889 IX says the mistake was "not to the time," and LUJ 202.2 says "The days, consequently did there end." See also GC 424.1.
- **The 2300 "days" as literal days.** TRMC August 22, 1844, page 2.2 says the 2300 days "are so many years." SOTB 5.1 reckons Daniel's days as years and dates their start to B.C. 457.
- **A detached 70th week.** Do not detach the 70th week from the first 69 or move it to the end of history. TRMC 2.2 says "the 70th, or last week, covers the time of his crucifixion." SOTB 5.3 closes it in A.D. 34.
- **The sanctuary as the earth, the church or Canaan.** Do not show the "sanctuary" of Dan 8:14 as any of these (LOM, page 38.7; LUJ 207.4). 1889 X (FP1889 149.1) says "this, and not the earth, is the sanctuary to be cleansed."
- **Adventists as date-setters.** Do not present Seventh-day Adventists as date-setters, or any prophetic period as reaching to the second advent. See 1889 IX (FP1889 148.7) and its note, which says "Seventh-day Adventists set no time" (FP1889 149.3).

## Distinctives

- **1844 as a failed prediction.** A mainstream explainer that mentions 1844 at all treats it as a failed prediction. The pioneers keep the reckoning and correct the event (GC 424.1; 1889 IX; LUJ 202.2).
- **The earth as the sanctuary.** The pioneers themselves record that this was the common Christian view: "In common with the rest of the Christian world, Adventists then held that the earth, or some portion of it, was the sanctuary" (GC 409.1). The re-reading departs from it (LOM, page 38.7).
- **Daniel 8 and 9 linked.** The 70 weeks are "cut off" from the 2300 days and "must therefore commence together" (TRMC 2.2). That link is what dates the 2300. An explainer that treats Daniel 8 and 9 as separate visions with separate periods contradicts it.
- **Internal tension: Miller and heaven.** Miller rejected heaven as the sanctuary to be cleansed, "for that is not unclean" (LJHCS 7.2). The later pioneers cleanse the heavenly sanctuary from sin transferred to it (AERS 216.1; GC 421.3).
- **Internal tension: Snow and the tenth day.** Snow's True Midnight Cry read the tenth-day type as the high priest coming out, meaning the advent "on the tenth day of the seventh month" of 1844 (TRMC August 22, 1844, page 4.3). The later reading keeps the date but has the high priest going in (GC 424.2; LUJ 213.1). The film should present Snow's reading only as the pre-Disappointment expectation.

## Unverified / not found

- **"October 22, 1844."** The research pass reported that this date does not occur in pre-1915 primary texts. That is wrong (compiler's correction, checked by corpus search). The pre-1915 texts state it, usually as "the 22d" or "the 22nd of October":
  - Ellen White (1888), GC88 399.3, quoted above: "which in the year 1844 fell upon the 22d of October". The 1911 edition reads "the twenty-second of October" (GC 399.4).
  - Uriah Smith (1897), DAR 593.2: "It fell on the 22d of October." Smith again at STTHD 284.2: "it was found to fall on the 22nd of October."
  - Joseph Bates, BP2 105.1: "our history on the 22nd of October, 1844".
  - Sylvester Bliss, MWM 269.4: the tenth day of the seventh month, "following the reckoning of the Caraite Jews, fell on the 22nd day of October."
  - J. N. Loughborough (1905), GSAM 181.2: "On the 22nd of October, 1844, the day the twenty-three hundred days terminated". The exact form "October 22, 1844" is in the same book (GSAM 526.3).
  - I. D. Van Horn, sermon of February 28, 1897, GCDB March 8, 1897, page 307.4: "the judgment hour began on the twenty-second day of October, 1844." The corpus lists this Bulletin under A. T. Jones as editor; the sermon heading at page 305 names Van Horn.
- **A first-hand 1844 account of the Disappointment with an established writer.** The Advent Herald for August–December 1844 is installed as HST vol. 8. Its post-Disappointment address to "Dear Brethren and Sisters" (HST October 30, 1844, page 96.4–96.8) is unsigned, so the writer is not established and it was not used.
- **SOTB 13.1** has an OCR error ("only 190 of the 2300 days") and was avoided.
- **The note to 1889 IX is split across two corpus records.** Its first part is inside FP1889 148.7. Its continuation is inside FP1889 149.3, a record that opens with Principle XII.
- **Hiram Edson's own account.** A quick search surfaced only A. L. White's retelling (EGWMR 36.11, excluded as 20th-century). It was not pursued further.
