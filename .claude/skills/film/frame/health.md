# Health Reform (no 1889 principle; context: Principles XIV–XVI)

## Pioneer position

The 1889 statement has no health principle: Principles XIV–XV (FP1889 150.2, 150.3) call for "not following the maxims, nor conforming to the ways, of the world" and for "plainness and modesty of attire", and its only word on appetite is Principle XVI's refusal to raise church funds through occasions for "appetite-indulging propensities" (FP1889 150.4). Outside the statement, Ellen White was shown that "the Health Reform is a part of the third angel’s message, and is just as closely connected with this message, as the arm and hand with the human body" (T11 41.2), yet "it is not the message" (T12 85.1); its grounds are a body "purchased with a dear price" and to be kept "in the very best condition of health" (T18 1.2), a God who "tests man just where he tested the innocent pair in Eden" and so makes eating and drinking "a matter of grave importance" (BHY 190.3), "grains and fruits" as "the proper food for man" with "Heaven’s great remedies" (HHTL 3.2), and a practice "to be practiced by faith in Christ" (HOMI November 1893, page 229.5).

## Quotes

> I was shown that the Health Reform is a part of the third angel’s message, and is just as closely connected with this message, as the arm and hand with the human body. I saw that we as a people must make an advance move in this great work. Ministers and people must act in concert. God’s people are not prepared for the loud cry of the third angel.

— **T11 41.2** · Ellen G. White, _Testimony for the Church — No. 11_ (1867; the year comes from an editorial note, see below)

> The Health Reform is closely connected with the work of the third message, yet it is not the message. Our preachers should teach the Health Reform, yet they should not make this the leading theme in the place of the message. Its place is among those subjects which set forth the preparatory work to meet the events brought to view by the message, among which it is prominent.

— **T12 85.1** · Ellen G. White, _Testimony for the Church — No. 12_ (year not printed; no earlier than June 1867)

> We are not our own. … But we have been purchased with a dear price. We have cost an immense sum, even the sufferings and death of the Son of God. If we can understand this, and fully realize it, then shall we feel great responsibility resting upon us to keep ourselves in the very best condition of health, that we may render to God perfect service.

— **T18 1.2** · Ellen G. White, “Christian Temperance”, an address given in Battle Creek on March 6, 1869, _Testimony for the Church — No. 18_ (1869)

> The teachings of our Lord Jesus Christ and his holy apostles are in perfect accordance with the proposition that God, in all dispensations of probationary time, tests man just where he tested the innocent pair in Eden. … And the words of Paul, addressed to the Christian church, make proper eating and drinking a matter of grave importance: “Whether, therefore, ye eat, or drink, or whatsoever ye do, do all to the glory of God.”

— **BHY 190.3** · James White, _Bible Hygiene_ (year not given)

> We are deeply impressed with the great fact that grains and fruits are the proper food for man. These are best, and generally far the cheapest, which is a worthy consideration for the poor. Cheerful toil, or exercise, proper rest in sleep, air, water and light, are Heaven’s great remedies.

— **HHTL 3.2** · James White, _Health, or, How to Live_, No. 1, Introduction (1865)

> Health reform, as such, is to be practiced by faith in Christ. And when our people get to that place where they will live health reform by faith in Christ, then they will live righteousness by faith in Christ. Any one who does not live righteousness by faith in Christ, cannot live health reform as God has given it. One is just as really a matter of faith as the other.

— **HOMI November 1893, page 229.5** · A. T. Jones, “Health Reform by Faith”, a reported camp-meeting talk, _The Home Missionary_ 5, no. 10 (1893)

## Film direction

MUST SHOW:

- **Arm and hand joined to the body.** Draw the third angel's message as a body, with health reform as its arm and hand. It is "as closely connected with this message, as the arm and hand with the human body" (T11 41.2; the standard reprint is 1T 486.2). A vision of "December 10, 1871" repeats the image: "as closely connected with the third angel’s message as the hand is united to the body" (T22 49.1).
- **Readiness for the Lord's coming.** Health reform is "one branch of the great work to fit a people for the coming of the Lord" (T22 49.1). "In order for the people of God to be fitted for translation, they must know themselves." (T11 42.1). The stakes are named: "God’s people are not prepared for the loud cry of the third angel." (T11 41.2).
- **A body bought at a price.** Show the cross behind the body: "We are not our own. … But we have been purchased with a dear price." (T18 1.2), with 1 Corinthians 6:19-20 quoted at T18 1.1.
- **Eden's test at every table.** God "tests man just where he tested the innocent pair in Eden", and 1 Corinthians 10:31 makes eating and drinking "a matter of grave importance" (BHY 190.3).
- **The table and the remedies.** "grains and fruits are the proper food for man". "Cheerful toil, or exercise, proper rest in sleep, air, water and light, are Heaven’s great remedies." (HHTL 3.2).
- **A forerunner.** James White calls health reform "a sort of John the Baptist to prepare the way for the greater light of the last message" (LIFSK 384.2). _Bible Hygiene_ opens the same theme with John himself, "who sets an example of self-denial and temperance" (BHY 190.3).
- **Guests are coming.** "When we expect a visit from friends whom we love and honor, how natural to put things in good order, and dress suitably for the occasion!" The writer applies this to Adventists "adopting the rules of clean, pure, practical hygiene" (BHY 251.1).
- **By faith, not by rulebook.** Health reform "is to be practiced by faith in Christ" (HOMI November 1893, page 229.5).

MUST NOT SAY OR SHOW:

- **Health reform as the message itself.** "it is not the message", and preachers "should not make this the leading theme in the place of the message" (T12 85.1). James White: "The health reform has not taken the place in any respect whatever, of the third angel’s message. It is a work designed to follow in its wake." (SPCR 1.10).
- **Detached wellness.** Do not present it as generic self-care or lifestyle optimization. The sources tie it to the loud cry and to translation at the Lord's coming (T11 41.2; T11 42.1; T22 49.1).
- **A diet imposed on others, or pushed harshly.** James White warns against treating it "in a manner to disgust the people" (LIFSK 384.2). Jones: "You need not require everybody else to live exactly as you do, and eat just what you eat, in order to be health reformers" (HOMI November 1893, page 230.2).
- **Eating as morally neutral.**
  - "Gluttony has been the prevailing sin of this age." (T11 41.2).
  - "But when we take any course which decreases our strength, expends our vitality, beclouds the intellect, and destroys the powers of the mind, we sin against God." (T18 1.3).
  - Jones applies "Whatsoever is not of faith is sin" to eating: "And this is spoken of eating and drinking, too." (HOMI November 1893, page 229.5).
- **A health article in the 1889 statement.** Do not cite one; there is none (FP1889 150.2–150.4).

## Distinctives

- **Place in the gospel.** Mainstream: diet and health are private matters of Christian liberty, apart from end-time readiness. Pioneers: health reform is "a part of the third angel’s message" (T11 41.2), work "to fit a people for the coming of the Lord" (T22 49.1), yet preparatory and never the leading theme (T12 85.1).
- **1 Corinthians 6:19-20 and 10:31.** Mainstream explainers usually keep these verses in their Corinthian settings: sexual immorality in chapter 6, food offered to idols in chapter 10. Pioneers apply them directly to health, strength and eating (T18 1.1–1.3; BHY 190.3).
- **Disease and the law.** Mainstream: sickness is a general effect of the fall. Pioneers: "Had men ever been obedient to the law of ten commandments, carrying out in their lives the principles of these ten precepts, the curse of disease now flooding the world would not be." (T22 49.1).
- **Faith and health.** A works-or-grace framing would treat diet as law-keeping beside the gospel. Jones: "Any one who does not live righteousness by faith in Christ, cannot live health reform as God has given it." (HOMI November 1893, page 229.5).

## Unverified / not found

- **Wording of the arm-and-hand statement.** The familiar wording is the reprint in _Testimonies for the Church_, vol. 1: "The health reform, I was shown, is a part of the third angel’s message and is just as closely connected with it as are the arm and hand with the human body." (1T 486.2). The original pamphlet wording is T11 41.2, which is the quoted record.
- **T11's year.** The year 1867 comes from an EGW Estate appendix note in 1T, "In 1867 Testimony No. 11 appeared" (1T 717.6), used here only for the date. The vision behind the text was "Dec. 25, 1865" (T11 40.1). The corpus has no Review issues from 1867 or 1868 that could confirm the date independently.
- **T12's year.** It is not printed. The pamphlet prints letters from "the first of May, 1867, and the second, in June following" (T12 91.1), so it is no earlier than June 1867. The year is left null.
- **T18's year.** 1869 is the date of the address ("Delivered in Battle Creek, March 6, 1869, and reported by U. Smith.", T18 1). It is not a printed pamphlet date.
- **BHY.** The editor introduces this part of the book as "the following collation of the more important writings of Elder White on the subject of hygiene from a Bible standpoint" (BHY 163.1). The dates of the originals are not given, so the year is null. BHY 251.1 opens by describing another preacher ("he lost none of his ardor in the cause of moral reform"); the visitor sentence is the narrator's own comment on him.
- **SPCR 1.10.** The tract is headed "by James White" (695.3), and the paragraph credits The Review and Herald, March 17, 1868. The corpus has no 1868 Review, so the original printing was not checked.
- **Two reports of Jones's talk.** A second report titled "Health Reform by Faith" is in _The Home Missionary_ Extra (HOMI November 1893, page 10 ff.). Its text differs, and its own pages name no speaker (only an "ATJ" tag), so it is not used. The quoted report is the one the editor introduces as "a report of a talk on health reform given by Elder A. T. Jones, at the Lansing camp-meeting" (HOMI November 1893, page 229.1).
- **General Conference resolutions.** The resolutions of 1891 and 1893 endorse health and temperance work (for example GCDB March 16, 1891, page 122.18). They are actions of the Conference as a body, not a pioneer writer's exposition, so they are not used.
- **Loughborough's _Hand Book of Health_.** HBH (1868) is "A Brief Treatise on Physiology and Hygiene" (1631.3). No passage in it ties health to the third angel's message, so it is not used.

## Missing works

_The Health Reformer_ (HR) appears in the corpus only as Ellen White's articles. Its author field is Ellen Gould White, and no paragraph in it is signed by James White or J. H. Waggoner, so their health articles in that paper cannot be quoted.

One missing article is J. H. Waggoner's "A Moral Duty of Preserving Health" (_The Health Reformer_, February 1872). The corpus knows it only through a secondary citation in FSDA 230.1, from Damsteegt, who is an excluded author. That citation reports an argument from the sixth commandment. The report is secondary and is not used here.
