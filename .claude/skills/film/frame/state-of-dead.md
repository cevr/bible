# State of the dead

Slug `state-of-dead` · 1889 Fundamental Principles V, XXII, XXIII, XXIV (with XXV–XXVI) · 6 verified quotes from 5 writers: Uriah Smith, A. T. Jones, J. N. Andrews, Ellen G. White, E. J. Waggoner

## Pioneer position

Man is the dust God formed, “a lifeless man” before the breath of life and “a living one” after it, not a body holding a “superadded soul” (HHMLD 42.1); “being mortal,” he obtains immortality only “at the resurrection of the dead,” when the last trump sounds at the coming of the Lord Jesus (BQACM 4.3; 1889 V: “if dead, we are raised incorruptible”). Until then the dead sleep and “know not anything,” going to neither reward nor punishment at death, for “the being with Christ is by the resurrection” (GC 549.3, GC 556.1, DBC 7.4; 1889 XXII–XXIV: the grave has “no work, device, wisdom, nor knowledge,” death is “silence, inactivity, and entire unconsciousness,” and mankind come out of “this prison-house of the grave” by a “bodily resurrection”), and once the unconditional immortality of the soul is admitted, “Spiritualism follows as a natural sequence” (SITI September 8, 1881, page 403.7).

## Film direction

**MUST SHOW**

- Show man formed of dust, lying lifeless until God breathes into him, and then alive. The formed man himself becomes living. Do not show a glowing soul slipped inside him (HHMLD 42.1; Gen 2:7).
- Show death as sleep in the grave: stillness, silence, no dreams and no sense of time passing. The next thing the sleeper knows is the trump of God (GC 549.3: “Time, be it long or short, is but a moment to them.”; BQACM 7.3: “a silent, dreamless sleep”).
- Use Jesus's own words about Lazarus, where “sleepeth” means dead (BQACM 6.4: “when a man is dead he is asleep”; John 11:11–14).
- Show the grave as “this prison-house of the grave,” opened by a bodily resurrection at Christ's coming (1889 XXIV).
- Show immortality given on the resurrection morning: the last trump sounds, the dead are raised incorruptible and the living are changed (BQACM 4.3; 1889 V, XXV).
- Show Paul's “depart” and “be with Christ” as two separate moments with the grave between them (DBC 7.4).

**MUST NOT SAY OR SHOW**

- A soul or spirit leaving the body at death, rising to heaven or falling to hell (GC 549.3).
- “She went to be with the Lord” or “he is in heaven now” as the moment of death (DBC 7.4; GC 549.3).
- The dead looking down on the living, following their lives or sharing their grief. They “know nothing of the joys or sorrows of those who were dearest to them on earth” (GC 556.1).
- A dead loved one's spirit hovering near the mourners, ghosts, or any contact with the dead presented as real (SITI September 8, 1881, page 403.7; GC 556.1).
- Humans having immortality now, or “everyone has an immortal soul” (BQACM 4.3: “Man, being mortal, has it not; God has it.”).

## Distinctives

- **Death as a doorway.** A mainstream evangelical explainer usually has the believer conscious with Christ at death. The pioneers place the being with Christ at the resurrection and keep the dead asleep until then (DBC 7.4; GC 549.3). Andrews charges that natural immortality “makes man’s last foe, death, the gate to endless joy” (TAR 54.2).
- **What a human is.** The evangelical default is a body plus an immortal soul. The pioneers read Gen 2:7 as the formed man himself made alive (HHMLD 42.1). Immortality is God's gift, put on at the last trump (BQACM 4.3).
- **Spiritualism.** Evangelicals condemn spiritualism but keep the immortal soul. The pioneers make the immortal soul spiritualism's foundation (SITI September 8, 1881, page 403.7; GC 556.1). Andrews says of natural immortality, “It is the foundation of modern spiritualism.” (TAR 54.2). Smith says it “furnishes a well-laid track for the car of modern Spiritualism” (DAR 609.2).
- **Heaven is re-timed, not denied.** At the last trump the living and the risen righteous are caught up, “so forever to be with the Lord,” and are “taken to heaven, to the New Jerusalem” (1889 XXV–XXVI). The dispute is about when this happens: at death or at the advent.
