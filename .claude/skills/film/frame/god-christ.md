# God and Christ — 1889 Principles I–II

## Pioneer position

Principle I (1889): "That there is one God, a personal, spiritual being, the creator of all things, … unchangeable, and everywhere present by his representative, the Holy Spirit"; Principle II: "That there is one Lord Jesus Christ, the Son of the Eternal Father, the one by whom he created all things, and by whom they do consist" (1872: "by whom God created all things"). The quoted writers call the Father and the Son "two distinct beings, yet one in the design and accomplishment of redemption" (James White, 1868) and Christ "the only begotten of God … one with the eternal Father—one in nature, in character, in purpose" (Ellen White, 1890), but word the Son's origin differently: "begotten, not created … There was a time when Christ proceeded forth and came from God" (E. J. Waggoner, in print by 1891); "God alone is without beginning … by some divine impulse or process, not creation, … the Son of God appeared" (Uriah Smith, 1898); "In Christ is life, original, unborrowed, underived" (Ellen White, 1898).

## The 1889 text and the 1872 wording

- **Principle I** — FP1889 147.2 and FP1872 4.2 are the same words (1872 "eternal, infinite"; 1889 "eternal; infinite"). The corpus prints the proof text as "Psalm 139:7"; the Year Book prints "Ps. 139:7".
- **Principle II, christological clauses** — FP1889 147.3 as quoted below. FP1872 4.3 has the same clauses except "the one by whom God created all things" where 1889 has "the one by whom he created all things". The two versions then word the atonement clauses differently, and 1889 adds a footnote on the atonement. That belongs to the sanctuary topic and is not treated here.
- **No word before "Son"** — the Year Book scan (YB1889.pdf, p. 147), the scan-checked text in [1889.md](1889.md) and the corpus (FP1889 147.3) all read "the Son of the Eternal Father". The asitreads.com web transcription prints "the [Literal] Son of the Eternal Father", and "[Literal]" is an editor's insertion.
- **Terms absent** — a corpus search of FP1872 and FP1889 finds no "Trinity", "triune", "Godhead", "three persons", "God the Son", "eternal Son" or "co-eternal".

## Film direction

**MUST SHOW**

- The Father and the Son together on one throne, with the Father's person hidden by "a cloud of glorious light". Render the Father as veiled radiance and never show a face or body (Broadside1 April 6, 1846, par. 7). James White gives the same picture in 1861: God "wrapped in a cloud of glory. This cloud could be seen, but not that face" (ARSH June 18, 1861, page 28.14, read, not excerpted). In 1854 Ellen White said she saw only "the body of light and glory that enshrouded his person" (ExV54 33.2, read, not excerpted).
- God as a person with a form, enthroned, whose form is hidden rather than absent: "a personal, spiritual being" (FP1889 147.2). The Father "had a form like himself" (Broadside1 April 6, 1846, par. 7).
- Creation as the Father working through the Son: "the one by whom he created all things" (FP1889 147.3). The 1872 wording is "by whom God created" (FP1872 4.3).
- The Father and the Son as associates in counsel ("a co-worker who could appreciate His purposes", PP 34.1) and as "two distinct beings, yet one in the design and accomplishment of redemption" (LIFIN 343.2).
- In the Lazarus story, where Jesus answers Martha, Christ as the source of life: "In Christ is life, original, unborrowed, underived" (DA 530.3).

**MUST NOT SAY OR SHOW**

- Do not title or narrate Principles I–II as "the Trinity", "one God in three persons", "God the Son", "co-eternal" or "eternally begotten". The 1872 and 1889 texts use none of these words (FP1889 147.2; FP1889 147.3; FP1872 4.3).
- Do not use a triangle, triquetra, three-faced or three-in-one emblem for God. James White denies that the Father and the Son are "parts of the “three-one God.”" (LIFIN 343.2).
- Do not put "[Literal]" or any other added word before "Son" in on-screen text or narration of Principle II (FP1889 147.3).
- Do not show or say that the Son was created, and do not animate his beginning as an event: "begotten, not created … it is not for us to inquire" (CHR 21.2); "not creation, known only to Omniscience" (LUJ 10.1).
- Do not narrate one settled account of the Son's origin as "what the pioneers believed". E. J. Waggoner (in print by 1891) writes "There was a time when Christ proceeded forth" (CHR 21.2). Smith (1898) writes "God alone is without beginning" (LUJ 10.1). Ellen White (1898) writes "original, unborrowed, underived" (DA 530.3). If the film touches this, give each wording with its writer and date.
- Do not reduce the Father to formless light or an impersonal energy. The light "covered him" and he has a form (Broadside1 April 6, 1846, par. 7), and Principle I says "personal" (FP1889 147.2).

## Distinctives

- Mainstream evangelical explainers define God as one being in three co-equal persons. The 1889 statement instead says "one God, a personal, spiritual being" whose Holy Spirit is "his representative", and "one Lord Jesus Christ, the Son of the Eternal Father" (FP1889 147.2; FP1889 147.3). In 1868 James White wrote that the Father and Son are not "parts of the “three-one God.”" (LIFIN 343.2). In 1884 J. H. Waggoner wrote "we firmly believe in the divinity of Christ; but we cannot accept the idea of a trinity, as it is held by Trinitarians" (AERS 164.3, read, not excerpted) and "The Scriptures abundantly teach the pre-existence of Christ and his divinity; but they are entirely silent in regard to a trinity" (AERS 173.1, read, not excerpted).
- Evangelical explainers usually treat the Father as invisible, formless spirit. These sources give the Father a form veiled by glory (Broadside1 April 6, 1846, par. 7). James White argues "So is God a Spirit being", like the angels who ate with Abraham (ARSH June 18, 1861, page 28.20, read, not excerpted). Ellen White wrote in 1904: "God is a spirit; yet He is a personal being, for man was made in His image" (8T 263.1, read, not excerpted).
- These sources do not describe the Son's origin as "eternal generation", and they disagree with each other (CHR 21.2; LUJ 10.1; DA 530.3). PP 34.1 reads Micah 5:2 as "from everlasting", while CHR 21.2 uses the margin, "from the days of eternity".
- Uriah Smith on Revelation 3:14: DAR 371.2 (1897) reports that "Some understand by this language that Christ was the first created being" and answers that "the language does not necessarily imply that he was created". The 1909 printing, listed under Smith (DAR1909 400.2), reads "Some attempt by this language to uphold the error that Christ was a created being". The corpus does not say who changed the wording.

## Unverified / not found

- A James White statement tying the Trinity to reformers who stopped reforming was searched for in Life Incidents (LIFIN 342–344) and in the wider corpus. It was not found.
- James White's Review statements on the Trinity from the 1870s could not be checked because those Review volumes are not installed (see Missing works).
- Uriah Smith's 1865 reading of Revelation 3:14 (Christ as first created being) is not in the corpus. Only his 1897 and 1909 DAR wording is (see Distinctives).
- PERGO 6.2–8.1 ("IMMATERIALITY"; PERGO 7.4: "What is God? He is material, organized intelligence, possessing both body and parts") follows James White's signed article (which ends "J. W.", PERGO 6.1) but has no byline of its own. Its writer is not established, so it is not used.
- CHR (E. J. Waggoner): the corpus copy has no imprint date. It is dated only by the notice in GCDB March 19, 1891, page 165.1 ("just issued"). The same paragraph is reprinted at PTUK January 9, 1896, page 18.7.
- Read but not excerpted:
  - ExV 64.1 (Ellen White, 1851): "I am in the express image of my Father’s Person".
  - 1SP 17.2 (Ellen White, 1870): the Father made known "that it was ordained by himself that Christ, his Son, should be equal with himself".
  - PP 34.2 (1890): "The Father wrought by His Son in the creation of all heavenly beings".
  - LUJ 13.1 (Smith, 1898): "the Father the antecedent cause, the Son the acting agent".
  - ARSH June 25, 1861, page 36.2 (James White): "There on the Father’s throne, is our personal Jesus, beside our personal God".
