# Hell and the final destruction of the wicked

Slug `hell-destruction` · 1889 Fundamental Principle XXVII (with XXVI) · 6 verified quotes from 4 writers: George Storrs (Millerite-era forerunner), J. N. Andrews, Uriah Smith, Ellen G. White

## Pioneer position

The death threatened as the wages of sin is “not immortality in misery, but an actual and total deprivation of life” (SSII 46.2): however long each is punished “according to his deserts,” the wicked are “punished with everlasting destruction” and, “consumed, root and branch,” “shall be as though they had not been,” for God “did not shut them up in hell to endure endless misery” (TAR 130.2, 1SG 118.1), which is 1889 XXVII's “everlasting destruction from the presence of the Lord,” “which is everlasting death.” The fire is everlasting in its results, not “everlastingly burning”; like the fire of Sodom it does its work and is not burning now, and “The fire that consumes the wicked purifies the earth,” so that no eternally burning hell remains (HHMLD 277.1, WDNP 3.2, GC 674.1).

## Quotes

> I shall attempt to show you, that the death God has threatened, as the wages of sin, is not immortality in misery, but an actual and total deprivation of life. I say, then, in opposition to the old serpent, if men do not come to Christ, that they may have life, they SHALL surely die - past hope, past recovery.

— **SSII 46.2** · George Storrs, _Six Sermons on the Inquiry Is There Immortality in Sin and Suffering? (1855 ed.; sermons first circulated 1842-3)_ (1855)  
Proves: Millerite-era forerunner, not an SDA writer: the death threatened as the wages of sin is an actual and total deprivation of life, not immortality in misery.

> Thus, however dreadful and long-continued the punishment of the wicked will be, (for each is to be punished according to his deserts,) that punishment will finally result in the utter destruction of all transgressors. … They shall be punished with everlasting destruction, being burned up in unquenchable fire. 2 Thessalonians 1:9; Matthew 3:12. And thus having been consumed, root and branch, they shall be as though they had not been. Malachi 4:1; Obadiah 16.

— **TAR 130.2** · J. N. Andrews, _The Three Angels of Revelation 14:6-12_ (1855)  
Proves: Punishment is graded by desert but ends in utter destruction: everlasting destruction, consumed root and branch, as though they had not been (the 1889 XXVII wording). Same text in ARSH April 17, 1855, page 211.1 and James White, LIFIN 264.1. Print-page number 131 dropped.

> Here the Sodomites are set forth for an example in the very act of suffering the vengeance of eternal fire. Are they to this day in that fire? … Peter thus shows that the fire did its proper office upon the men of Sodom, and that they were not in his day alive in its flames. Their case is an example of what God will do to all the wicked after the resurrection to damnation, when fire shall descend out of heaven upon them and the whole earth become a lake of fire.

— **WDNP 3.2** · J. N. Andrews, _The Wicked Dead: Are They Now Being Punished?_ (1865)  
Proves: The "eternal fire" of Sodom (Jude 7) turned the cities to ashes and is not burning now; the same fire falls on all the wicked after the resurrection, when the whole earth becomes a lake of fire.

> Everlasting fire may not be fire which is everlastingly burning, but fire which produces results which are everlasting in their nature. The victims cast therein will be consumed; and if from that destruction they are never to be released, if that fiery work is never to be undone, it is to them an “everlasting fire.”

— **HHMLD 277.1** · Uriah Smith, _Here and Hereafter_ (1897)  
Proves: "Everlasting fire" (Matt 25:41) names results that are everlasting, not a fire that burns everlastingly; its victims are consumed and never restored.

> Beings whom God created have chosen to rebel against his government; but I saw that God did not shut them up in hell to endure endless misery. … He will destroy them utterly, and cause them to be as though they had not been, and then his justice will be satisfied. He formed man out of the dust of the earth, and the disobedient and unholy will be consumed by fire, and return to dust again. I saw that the benevolence and compassion of God in this, should lead all to admire his character, and to adore him; and after the wicked shall be destroyed from off the earth, all the heavenly host will say, Amen!

— **1SG 118.1** · Ellen G. White, _Spiritual Gifts, vol. 1_ (1858)  
Proves: The character of God: he does not keep the lost in endless misery; he destroys them utterly and returns them to dust, and his benevolence and compassion in this call all to adore him (Obad 16 and Gen 3:19 alluded to, not cited).

> “I saw a new heaven and a new earth: for the first heaven and the first earth were passed away.” Revelation 21:1. The fire that consumes the wicked purifies the earth. Every trace of the curse is swept away. No eternally burning hell will keep before the ransomed the fearful consequences of sin.

— **GC 674.1** · Ellen G. White, _The Great Controversy, ch. 42 “The Controversy Ended”_ (1911)  
Proves: The fire that consumes the wicked purifies the earth; no eternally burning hell remains before the redeemed.

## Film direction

**MUST SHOW**

- Show the end of the thousand years: the raised wicked gathered around the city on “the yet unrenewed earth,” and fire coming down from God out of heaven (1889 XXVII). After the resurrection “the whole earth become a lake of fire” (WDNP 3.2).
- Show a fire that goes out. The stubble is burned up and nothing is left, “root and branch” (TAR 130.2; Mal 4:1–3). Sodom turned to ashes is the pattern (WDNP 3.2; 2 Pet 2:6).
- Then show the same fire purifying the earth and giving way to the new earth (GC 674.1; see `new-earth`).
- Show God's character: justice and compassion together, with no endless misery (1SG 118.1).
- Show Satan destroyed in the same end, not ruling a kingdom of fire. The desolate earth is his prison-house, “and then the place of his final execution” (1889 XXVI).

**MUST NOT SAY OR SHOW**

- A hell that is burning now, underground or anywhere, with the wicked dead already in it. The men of Sodom “were not in his day alive in its flames” (WDNP 3.2). The gehenna of Matt 10:28 is “the place of punishment for the resurrected wicked” (WDNP 1.3).
- Eternal conscious torment, “endless misery,” or people kept alive in flames forever (1SG 118.1; SSII 46.2).
- “Everlasting fire” shown as a fire that never stops burning (HHMLD 277.1).
- A hell that goes on burning beside the redeemed forever (GC 674.1).
- Devils running hell or tormenting people. Satan himself is “finally destroyed” (1889 XXVI).
- One identical, instant end for everyone. Andrews keeps the punishment graded, “for each is to be punished according to his deserts,” before it ends in destruction (TAR 130.2).

## Distinctives

- **Torment or destruction.** A traditional evangelical explainer has the lost suffering consciously forever. The pioneers teach that the wages of sin is death, “an actual and total deprivation of life” (SSII 46.2), which means everlasting destruction (TAR 130.2; 1889 XXVII).
- **When and where.** The popular picture sends the wicked to hell at death, into a hell that exists now. For the pioneers the wicked dead are not being punished now. The fire falls after the resurrection and on this earth: “The punishment of the wicked will be inflicted upon them on this earth” (TAR 128.3; WDNP 3.2).
- **“For ever.”** The popular reading takes everlasting fire to mean burning without end. The pioneers read it as everlasting in its results (HHMLD 277.1). They point out that Sodom's “eternal fire” went out (WDNP 3.2). They read the smoke that goes up “for ever and ever” in Rev 14:11 alongside Isaiah 34, as a fire that “is not to last throughout unlimited duration” (TAR 128.2–130.1).
- **The character of God.** The pioneers present destroying the wicked, rather than tormenting them forever, as the benevolence and compassion of God (1SG 118.1).
- **Not the annihilation of matter.** Smith denies believing in the “annihilation of anything as matter.” Instead, “they will be annihilated as living beings, the matter of which they are composed passing into other forms” (HHMLD 319.2). Ellen White says they “return to dust again” (1SG 118.1).

## Unverified / not found

- **J. H. Waggoner's VDRU.** It was read. VDRU 69.3 (“The work of death ends with the utter destruction of the wicked in the lake of fire.”) supports the case but is not quoted, because of the six-quote cap.
- **James White.** No independent statement of his is quoted. His _Life Incidents_ (1868) reprints Andrews's TAR 130.1–130.2 word for word at LIFIN 263.3–264.1. Andrews's text also ran in ARSH April 17, 1855, page 211.1.
- **An EW parallel for 1SG 118.1.** None was found. A search for its key phrase returns only 1SG.
- **Matt 10:28.** It is handled in WDNP 1.3, which is not among the six quotes.
- **Storrs.** He is included because the brief names him as the Millerite forerunner. The contract admits Millerite writers only “where the topic is Millerite,” so his record is flagged in its note, and his line should not be presented on its own as the voice of the Adventist pioneers.
