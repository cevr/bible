# Nature of Christ (1889 Principle II)

Scope: what Jones, Waggoner and Ellen White wrote about the humanity Christ took, set side by side
and NOT reconciled. This file also carries Principle II's note on the atonement (same corpus paragraph,
FP1889 147.3), because the film's `exchange` beat raises both issues at once.

## Pioneer position

1889 Principle II says Christ "took on him the nature of the seed of Abraham for the redemption of our fallen race" and, having "died our sacrifice", became "our only mediator in the sanctuary in heaven", the atonement "not being completed till this work of blotting out sin is done" (FP1889 147.3). Beyond that wording the writers pull in different directions: Waggoner and Jones put "sinful tendencies" / "just the same tendencies to sin that are in you and me" in Christ's flesh, "not in himself" (CHR 26.2; GCB February 21, 1895, page 266.9), while Ellen White writes that "he took upon him our sinful nature" under "the great law of heredity" yet warns never to "leave the slightest impression upon human minds that a taint of, or inclination to corruption rested upon Christ" (RH December 15, 1896, par. 7; DA 48.6; 10LtMs, Lt 8, 1895, par. 16).

## Quotes

Side by side, in three groups. They are not harmonised here.

### The 1889 anchor

> that he took on him the nature of the seed of Abraham for the redemption of our fallen race; that he dwelt among men, full of grace and truth, lived our example, died our sacrifice, was raised for our justification, ascended on high to be our only mediator in the sanctuary in heaven … we take the position that what Christ did upon the cross was to provide a divine sacrifice for the world, sufficient to save all, and offered it to every one who will accept of it; that he then, through the merits of his offering, acts as mediator with the Father till time shall end, securing the forgiveness of sins for all who seek him for it; and that, as the last service of his priesthood, he will blot out the sins of all who have repented and been converted (Acts 3:19), the atonement not being completed till this work of blotting out sin is done.

— **FP1889 147.3** · unsigned (SDA Year Book), _Fundamental Principles_ II and its NOTE (1889)

### Waggoner and Jones: the flesh had sinful tendencies, "not in himself"

> A little thought will be sufficient to show anybody that if Christ took upon Himself the likeness of man in order that He might redeem man, it must have been sinful man that He was made like, for it is sinful man that He came to redeem. … Moreover, the fact that Christ took upon Himself the flesh, not of a sinless being, but of a sinful man, that is, that the flesh which He assumed had all the weaknesses and sinful tendencies to which fallen human nature is subject, is shown by the statement that He “was made of the seed of David according to the flesh.”

— **CHR 26.2** · E. J. Waggoner, _Christ and His Righteousness_ (no date in the corpus; announced as "just issued" in GCDB March 19, 1891, page 165.1). The embedded print-page number 27 has been dropped.

> Thus in the flesh of Jesus Christ, — not in himself, but in his flesh, — our flesh which he took in the human nature, — there were just the same tendencies to sin that are in you and me. And when he was tempted, it was the “drawing away of these desires that were in the flesh.” … But by the love of God and by his trust in God, he received the power, and the strength, and the grace to say, “No,” to all of it, and put it all under foot. And thus being in the likeness of sinful flesh, he condemned sin in the flesh.

— **GCB February 21, 1895, page 266.9** · A. T. Jones, “The Third Angel’s Message — No. 14,” _General Conference Bulletin_ (1895). The heading is at page 265, and the embedded print-page number 267 has been dropped.

### Ellen White: our sinful nature and heredity, and never a taint or inclination to corruption

> But Jesus accepted humanity when the race had been weakened by four thousand years of sin. Like every child of Adam He accepted the results of the working of the great law of heredity. What these results were is shown in the history of His earthly ancestors. He came with such a heredity to share our sorrows and temptations, and to give us the example of a sinless life.

— **DA 48.6** · Ellen G. White, _The Desire of Ages_ (1898)

> Clad in the vestments of humanity, the Son of God came down to the level of those he wished to save. In him was no guile or sinfulness; he was ever pure and undefiled; yet he took upon him our sinful nature. Clothing his divinity with humanity, that he might associate with fallen humanity, he sought to regain for man that which, by disobedience, Adam had lost for himself and for the world.

— **RH December 15, 1896, par. 7** · Ellen G. White, “The Importance of Obedience,” _Review and Herald_ (1896)

> These words do not refer to any human being, except to the Son of the Infinite God. Never, in any way, leave the slightest impression upon human minds that a taint of, or inclination to corruption rested upon Christ, or that He in any way yielded to corruption.

— **10LtMs, Lt 8, 1895, par. 16** · Ellen G. White, Letter 8, 1895, to “Bro. and Sr. Baker” (par. 1) (1895). “These words” are Luke 1:31-35, quoted in par. 15. The same text is printed as 13MR 19.1, 5BC 1128.6 and 7ABC 448.2; 5BC adds a comma: “inclination to, corruption”.

**Where they pull apart.** Jones and Waggoner put "tendencies to sin" in the flesh Christ took. Ellen White forbids leaving even "the slightest impression" of an "inclination to corruption" in Him. Yet she herself writes "took upon him our sinful nature" and "the great law of heredity".

Each side qualifies itself:

- Jones: "not in himself, but in his flesh".
- Ellen White: "In him was no guile or sinfulness" and "the example of a sinless life".

The film should not pick a side's gloss and present it as the pioneer view.

## Film direction

MUST SHOW

- **Rom 8:3 in its own words.** Keep "in the likeness of sinful flesh" as Scripture says it. Jones ends his statement with that very clause (GCB February 21, 1895, page 266.9), and Waggoner argues from it (CHR 26.2).
- **A real descent into our weakened race, and a sinless life.** Show Christ entering humanity after "four thousand years of sin", and pair it with "the example of a sinless life" — **DA 48.6**. "In him was no guile or sinfulness" sits in the same sentence as "our sinful nature" — **RH December 15, 1896, par. 7**.
- **Incarnation leads to priesthood.** Principle II runs straight from "the nature of the seed of Abraham" to "our only mediator in the sanctuary in heaven", so the incarnation should point forward to the High Priest — **FP1889 147.3**.

MUST NOT SAY-OR-SHOW

- **No stain on Christ.** Don't picture Him stained, and don't put the rags on Him as the incarnation itself. Leave not "the slightest impression" of "a taint of, or inclination to corruption" — **10LtMs, Lt 8, 1895, par. 16**. Our sins are laid on Him at the cross, the passage the 1889 note cites as 1 Pet 2:24 — **FP1889 147.3**.
- **Don't settle the debate in narration.** Don't say "sinful tendencies" without Jones's "not in himself", and don't say "a sinless nature" when EGW wrote "our sinful nature". The sources pull in different directions — **CHR 26.2**, **GCB February 21, 1895, page 266.9**, **RH December 15, 1896, par. 7**, **10LtMs, Lt 8, 1895, par. 16**.
- **The cross is not the end of the atonement.** Don't let the cross shadow read as the finished atonement. The note says "the atonement not being completed till this work of blotting out sin is done": the cross provided "a divine sacrifice", and then He "acts as mediator" — **FP1889 147.3**.

## Distinctives

- **Which humanity?** A mainstream explainer usually stops at "fully God, fully man". These writers press the question of which humanity:
  - "sinful man that He was made like" (CHR 26.2);
  - "our flesh" with "tendencies to sin" (GCB February 21, 1895, page 266.9);
  - "our sinful nature" (RH December 15, 1896, par. 7);
  - humanity "weakened by four thousand years of sin" (DA 48.6).
- **Nature is tied to priesthood and atonement.** Principle II binds the nature Christ took to His later mediation and to an atonement not completed at the cross (FP1889 147.3).
- **Ellen White's caution is part of the frame.** In the same period she guarded the subject: never a taint or inclination to corruption (10LtMs, Lt 8, 1895, par. 16).

## Unverified / not found

- **"took upon Him our sinful nature" in DA.** Not found in _The Desire of Ages_. The corpus has this wording in RH December 15, 1896, par. 7, lower-case "him". DA's nearest wording is at DA 112.3 (taking upon Himself our fallen nature, at His baptism) and DA 117.1 (the infirmities of degenerate humanity).
- **The comma in the Baker caution.** The wording "taint of, or inclination to, corruption", with a comma after "to", is 5BC 1128.6. The letter itself (10LtMs, Lt 8, 1895, par. 16) has no comma there, and the record follows the letter.
- **Baker's full name, location and the letter's date.** None of these is printed in the corpus copy. The heading is only "Lt 8, 1895", and the text names only "Bro. and Sr. Baker" / "Bro. Baker" (par. 1, 15). Nothing further is claimed.
