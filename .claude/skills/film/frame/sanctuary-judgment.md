# sanctuary-judgment — The heavenly sanctuary, the atonement and the investigative judgment

## Pioneer position

Under 1889 Principles II, X and XXI, Christ is "our only mediator in the sanctuary in heaven" (II), a sanctuary that "is the antitype of the Mosaic tabernacle," and its cleansing, "beginning in 1844," is "simply the entrance of the high priest into the most holy place" for "actually blotting out the sins of believers (Acts 3:19)" (X) — "the closing portion of his work as priest, before he takes his throne as king" (II) — in "a time of investigative judgment" on points "which must be determined before the Lord appears" (XXI). The quotes agree: the earthly service was "a shadow" of Christ's (SOTB 8.4); the daily atonement in the Holy was "for the forgiveness of sins" and the yearly one in the Holy of Holies "for blotting them out" (LOM, page 40.1); the atonement "was not made when the offering for this dispensation was provided" but "only by the service of the priest in the most holy place," a work that "did not commence … till 1844" (LUJ 238.2); that work is one with "the judgment of the saints" and "the blotting out of sin" (AERS 220.1), requires "an examination of the books of record" "prior to the coming of Christ" (GC 421.3), and ends with the sins laid on the scapegoat, which "typified Satan" (GC 422.2).

## Quotes

> The ministration in the earthly sanctuary could not actually take away sins; for it had only the blood of bulls and goats to offer. … It is a shadow or representation of the service of Christ in the sanctuary of God in Heaven. Hebrews 8:5; 10:1; Colossians 2:17. … But the ministration of Christ which casts this shadow fills out each part of the work once for all, and is not repeated.

— **SOTB 8.4** · J. N. Andrews, _The Sanctuary of the Bible_ (n.d.)

> The atonement which the priest made for the people in connection with their daily ministration was different from that made on the tenth day of the seventh month. In making the former, they went no further than in the Holy; but to make the latter they entered the Holy of Holies — the former was made for individual cases, the latter for the whole nation of Israel collectively — the former was made for the forgiveness of sins, the latter for blotting them out — the former could be made at any time, the latter only on the tenth day of the seventh month.

— **LOM, page 40.1** · O. R. L. Crosier, _The Law of Moses_, Day-Star Extra, Cincinnati, February 7 (1846)

> The antitypical atonement, which is the real removal of sin, was not made when the offering for this dispensation was provided, not by the service of the priest in the first apartment of the sanctuary; but is accomplished only by the service of the priest in the most holy place, which is the closing work of our Lord’s ministration, the cleansing of the sanctuary, a branch of the work which did not commence, as we have seen, till 1844.

— **LUJ 238.2** · Uriah Smith, _Looking Unto Jesus_ (1898)

> It does not seem to admit of a doubt that the judgment of the saints, the blotting out of sin, the making of the atonement, and the cleansing of the sanctuary, are identical. And we have seen that in the type the atonement was made—the sanctuary cleansed, when the high priest went into the most holy place before the ark; and the most holy was opened only on the day of atonement. … Under the sounding of the seventh trumpet it is said, “The temple of God was opened in Heaven, and there was seen in his temple the ark of his testament.” Revelation 11:19.

— **AERS 220.1** · J. H. Waggoner, _The Atonement_ (1884)

> And as the typical cleansing of the earthly was accomplished by the removal of the sins by which it had been polluted, so the actual cleansing of the heavenly is to be accomplished by the removal, or blotting out, of the sins which are there recorded. But before this can be accomplished, there must be an examination of the books of record to determine who, through repentance of sin and faith in Christ, are entitled to the benefits of His atonement. The cleansing of the sanctuary therefore involves a work of investigation—a work of judgment. This work must be performed prior to the coming of Christ to redeem His people; for when He comes, His reward is with Him to give to every man according to his works. Revelation 22:12.

— **GC 421.3** · Ellen G. White, _The Great Controversy_ (1911)

> It was seen, also, that while the sin offering pointed to Christ as a sacrifice, and the high priest represented Christ as a mediator, the scapegoat typified Satan, the author of sin, upon whom the sins of the truly penitent will finally be placed. When the high priest, by virtue of the blood of the sin offering, removed the sins from the sanctuary, he placed them upon the scapegoat. When Christ, by virtue of His own blood, removes the sins of His people from the heavenly sanctuary at the close of His ministration, He will place them upon Satan, who, in the execution of the judgment, must bear the final penalty.

— **GC 422.2** · Ellen G. White, _The Great Controversy_ (1911)

## Film direction

**Must show**

- **Shadow and original.** Show the earthly tabernacle as a shadow cast by the heavenly sanctuary. Christ's ministry "fills out each part of the work once for all" (SOTB 8.4). The heavenly sanctuary is the original and Moses' was the copy (GC 414.1, read but not quoted).
- **Two rooms, two phases.**
  - The daily ministry in the Holy brings forgiveness.
  - The once-a-year entry into the Holy of Holies, on the tenth day of the seventh month, blots sins out.
  - Sources: LOM, page 40.1; 1889 X (FP1889 149.1).
- **The cross as the offering.** Show the cross as the offering. The priestly work follows the ascension, and the atonement proper happens in the most holy place from 1844 (LUJ 238.2). See also LUJ 238.3 and GC 421.2.
- **The high priest's entry and the ark.**
  - Show the high priest going in "before the ark," and the heavenly temple opened with "the ark of his testament" seen (AERS 220.1).
  - Show the ark as the container of the tables of the law. GC 433.1–433.2 tie the opened temple and the ark to Christ's 1844 entry (read but not quoted).
- **The books of record.** Show the books examined before Christ comes, followed by the blotting out of sins (GC 421.3).
  - GC 421.2 has sins remaining "upon the books of record."
  - JEO 15.1 and LUJ 243.1 give the Daniel 7:9-10 throne scene with the books opened.
- **The scapegoat.** Bring the scapegoat in only after the sanctuary is cleansed, receiving the sins and sent away, as a figure of Satan (GC 422.2). See also SOTB 12.2 and LOM, page 43.4.

**Must not say or show**

- **"It is finished" as the completed atonement.** Do not present "It is finished" as the atonement completed at the cross, and do not label the cross "the atonement."
  - LUJ 238.2 separates the offering from the atonement.
  - LUJ 237.2 is the blunt denial.
  - The note to 1889 II (FP1889 147.3) makes the same point, and so does AERS 190.3.
- **Hebrews as abolishing the sanctuary.** Do not present Hebrews as retiring the sanctuary or its imagery. The pioneers cite Hebrews as their proof of the heavenly original (SOTB 8.4). 1889 X calls it the sanctuary "of which Paul speaks in Hebrews 8 and onward."
- **Entry into the most holy place at the ascension.** Do not show Christ entering the most holy place at the ascension, and do not show a one-room heavenly sanctuary.
  - LOM, page 41.2 reads the veil of Heb 6:19-20 as the first veil.
  - GC 421.2 has the first apartment for "eighteen centuries."
  - LUJ 213.1 has the change from the holy to the most holy place in 1844.
- **Judgment only at the end.** Do not put the judgment only at the end of the world or only at the second coming.
  - GC 421.3 places the judgment "prior to the coming of Christ."
  - SOTB 14.1 splits the judgment into an investigative part in heaven and its execution at the advent.
  - GC 480.1 applies 1 Pet 4:17 to the judgment beginning with God's people.
- **The scapegoat as Christ.** Do not draw the scapegoat (Azazel) as Christ or as a picture of the cross. See GC 422.2 and LOM, page 43.7. LOM, page 43.11 names the Christ reading as an error.
- **Forgiveness as final erasure.** Do not draw forgiveness as the final erasure of the record. Forgiven sins stay on the books until the blotting out (LOM, page 40.1; GC 421.2).

## Distinctives

- **The atonement.** The pioneers name the mainstream view themselves: the atonement "made upon the cross, as is generally held" (FP1889 147.3, note to II). They deny it. The cross provided "a divine sacrifice for the world, sufficient to save all," and the atonement is "not being completed till this work of blotting out sin is done" (same note). LUJ 238.2 and LOM, page 41.7 say the same. A standard evangelical explainer that ends the atonement at Calvary contradicts these.
- **Hebrews.** An explainer that treats Hebrews as the end of sanctuary categories contradicts SOTB 8.4 and 1889 X. Both read Hebrews as describing the heavenly original the tabernacle copied.
- **The judgment.** The pioneers' judgment has two parts: an investigative judgment in heaven before the advent, and its execution at the advent (SOTB 14.1; GC 421.3). An explainer with a single final judgment day contradicts this.
- **The scapegoat.** The pioneers knew the reading of the scapegoat as a type of Christ and rejected it as coming from ignorance of the law (LOM, page 43.11). See also GC 422.2 and SOTB 12.2.
- **Forgiveness and blotting out.** These are two distinct acts in two apartments (LOM, page 40.1), not one.
- **An internal tension to avoid.** Crosier's 1846 article has the antitypical yearly atonement occupying "a period or dispensation of at least 1000 years" (LOM, page 42.5). That is not the 1889 position, which calls the work "a brief but indefinite space of time" (X). Do not cite Crosier on duration.

- A strong unsigned editorial on the atonement and the scapegoat was not used because its writer is not established: "The Sanctuary (Continued.)", ARSH September 1, 1863, pages 108–109.
- GC 489.1 and PP 357.5, the pre-verified leads from the brief, were not used. The six quotes above make the same points more directly.
