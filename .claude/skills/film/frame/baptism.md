# Baptism (1889 Principle IV)

## Pioneer position

Principle IV (FP1889 148.2) makes baptism an ordinance "to follow faith and repentance", "by which we commemorate the resurrection of Christ, as by this act we show our faith in his burial and resurrection, and through that, in the resurrection of all the saints at the last day", and holds that no mode "more fitly represents these facts" than immersion. The pioneers read it as a burial and a rising: "The resurrection of Christ is commemorated by our being buried with him by baptism, and raised up out of the watery grave" (1SG 112.2), which is "the divinely authorized memorial of the resurrection of the Lord Jesus", the resurrection itself and not its day (HSFD 154.1); Paul's burial language "is not well chosen if it is intended to represent sprinkling or pouring" (TOB 38.1); and "when we, by baptism, confess our faith in the one, as a consequence we confess our faith in the other", the saints who "will be raised from the dead at his coming" (BIC 94.3).

## Quotes

> Both of these important events have their memorials. By partaking of the Lord’s supper, the broken bread and the fruit of the vine, we show forth the Lord’s death until he comes. … The resurrection of Christ is commemorated by our being buried with him by baptism, and raised up out of the watery grave in likeness of his resurrection, to live in newness of life.

— **1SG 112.2** · Ellen G. White, _Spiritual Gifts_, vol. 1 (1858)

> To be buried in the watery grave as our Lord was buried in the tomb, and to be raised from the water to walk in newness of life, as our Lord was raised from the dead by the glory of the Father, is the divinely authorized memorial of the resurrection of the Lord Jesus. And let it be observed, it is not the day of the resurrection, but the resurrection itself, that was thought worthy of commemoration. … It was the resurrection of the Saviour, and not the day of the resurrection, that was worthy of commemoration; and hence burial in baptism was ordained as its memorial.

— **HSFD 154.1** · J. N. Andrews, _History of the Sabbath and First Day of the Week_ (1873)

> The apostle Paul twice speaks of baptism as a burial. This expression is just according to the meaning of the word immersion. But the term is not well chosen if it is intended to represent sprinkling or pouring. It is likened to the burial and resurrection of Christ, to which the ordinance has undoubted reference.

— **TOB 38.1** · J. H. Waggoner, _Thoughts on Baptism_ (1878)

> And as the resurrection of Christ is the pledge of the resurrection of the saints, [1 Corinthians 15:12-18,] so when we, by baptism, confess our faith in the one, as a consequence we confess our faith in the other. For if we believe that Jesus died and rose again, even so them also which sleep in Jesus will God bring with him. 1 Thessalonians 4:14. They will be raised from the dead at his coming.

— **BIC 94.3** · R. F. Cottrell, _The Bible Class_ (1855)

## Film direction

MUST SHOW:

- **The water as a grave.** Lower the believer fully under the water and raise them out of it: "buried with him by baptism, and raised up out of the watery grave in likeness of his resurrection, to live in newness of life" (1SG 112.2).
- **Tomb and water, side by side.** Match-cut Christ laid in the tomb with the believer going under, and Christ rising with the believer coming up: "To be buried in the watery grave as our Lord was buried in the tomb, and to be raised from the water to walk in newness of life, as our Lord was raised from the dead" (HSFD 154.1).
- **Two memorials on one table.** Put the bread and cup beside the water: "Both of these important events have their memorials" (1SG 112.2). His death is remembered at the supper and his resurrection at baptism (HSFD 154.1).
- **Faith before the water.** Show a believer who has already turned to Christ, since baptism is "to follow faith and repentance" (FP1889 148.2).
- **The saints' resurrection ahead.** Let the rising from the water foreshadow the dead rising at the advent. The ordinance shows faith "in the resurrection of all the saints at the last day" (FP1889 148.2), who "will be raised from the dead at his coming" (BIC 94.3).
- **A historical scene the sources supply.** Ellen White's own baptism in the sea: "It was a windy day when we, twelve in number, were baptized, walking down into the sea." She came up feeling she "had risen from the watery grave into a newness of life" (LS80 145.3).

MUST NOT SAY OR SHOW:

- **Sprinkling or pouring.** Do not show them as equal forms. The burial language "is not well chosen if it is intended to represent sprinkling or pouring" (TOB 38.1). Ellen White: "Young as I was, I could see but one mode of baptism authorized by the Scriptures, and that was immersion." (LS80 145.2).
- **Sunday as the memorial of the resurrection.** Do not say it. Andrews: "it is not the day of the resurrection, but the resurrection itself, that was thought worthy of commemoration" (HSFD 154.1). "You need not keep the first day of the week. The Lord ordained a very different and far more appropriate memorial." (COTSN 40.2; FDNS 6.1 opens with the same sentences).
- **Infants, or baptism before belief.** Principle IV places baptism after "faith and repentance" (FP1889 148.2).
- **Washing or joining a community as the main picture.** In these sources the ordinance "is likened to the burial and resurrection of Christ, to which the ordinance has undoubted reference" (TOB 38.1; FP1889 148.2).

## Distinctives

- **Mode.** Mainstream: many traditions accept sprinkling or pouring, and ecumenical explainers leave the mode open. Pioneers:
  - only immersion "more fitly represents these facts" (FP1889 148.2);
  - Paul's word "burial" "is just according to the meaning of the word immersion" (TOB 38.1);
  - "The Catholic and Protestant churches have changed this ordinance to sprinkling, so that this divine memorial of the Lord’s resurrection is destroyed." (COTSN 40.2).
- **The memorial of the resurrection.** Mainstream: Sunday worship celebrates the resurrection. Pioneers: God appointed baptism, not a day, as its memorial (HSFD 154.1; COTSN 40.2; FDNS 6.1). Andrews ties the two: it is "the change of this memorial to sprinkling" that "has furnished s plausible a plea for first-day observance in memory of the resurrection" (HSFD 154.1; "s" is the corpus reading, evidently "so").
- **Believers only.** Mainstream practice includes infant baptism in many churches. Pioneers: baptism follows "faith and repentance" (FP1889 148.2).
- **Forward hope.** Mainstream explainers usually stop at identifying with Christ's death and new life. Pioneers add the saints' bodily resurrection "at his coming" (BIC 94.3; FP1889 148.2).

## Unverified / not found

- **TBI 292.4.** It says the burial of Christ "represents the true mode of baptism". _The Biblical Institute_ is "a synopsis of the lectures delivered at the Biblical Institute, held by Elders James White and U. Smith" (TBI i.1), so the speaker of any given paragraph is not established. It is not used as a quote.
- **ARSH December 30, 1862, page 38.32.** It is a clear statement ("Baptism beautifully represents the burial and resurrection of Christ, and at the same time points forward to the resurrection of the saints."). It is signed "D. HILDRETH." (ARSH December 30, 1862, page 38.34). Hildreth is not on the contract's list of pioneer writers, so it is not used.
- **COTSN 40.2 and FDNS 6.1.** These two paragraphs open with the same sentences and the same quotations of Romans 6:3-5 and Colossians 2:12, with small differences in punctuation. One is in James White's _Christ in the Old Testament, and the Sabbath in the New_ and the other in J. N. Andrews's _The First Day of the Week Not the Sabbath of the Lord_. Only COTSN 40.2 goes on to the sentence about sprinkling. Neither tract is dated in the corpus, so which text came first is not established. The shared sentences are used only as supporting phrases, with both refcodes.
- **BASI.** E. J. Waggoner's _Baptism: Its Significance_ has no date in the corpus, and BASI 13.1 prints one sentence twice (a corpus defect). It is not used.
- **1SG's year.** The corpus copy of _Spiritual Gifts_, vol. 1, has no dated title page. The year 1858 comes from the Review notice "Spiritual Gifts, or the Great Controversy, has now been sent to all who have ordered." (ARSH September 9, 1858, page 136.10).
