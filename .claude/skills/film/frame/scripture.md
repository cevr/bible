# Scripture — 1889 Principle III

## Pioneer position

Principle III (1889): "That the Holy Scriptures of the Old and New Testaments were given by inspiration of God, contain a full revelation of his will to man, and are the only infallible rule of faith and practice," and the preamble says Seventh-day Adventists "have no creed but the Bible" (1872: "no articles of faith, creed, or discipline, aside from the Bible"). The quoted pioneers add: "True visions are given to lead us to God, and his written word" (1847); "The Bible is our creed. … We take the Bible and the gifts of the Spirit" (James White, 1861); the Bible is "the standard and test of all moral teaching, all spiritual exercises, and all revelation purporting to be either human or divine" (Uriah Smith, 1887).

## Quotes

> As elsewhere stated, Seventh-day Adventists have no creed but the Bible; but they hold to certain well-defined points of faith, for which they feel prepared to give a reason “to every man that asketh” them. The following propositions may be taken as a summary of the principal features of their religious faith, upon which there is, so far as we know, entire unanimity throughout the body.

— **FP1889 147.1** · Unsigned statement (corpus lists General Conference of SDA), _Fundamental Principles of Seventh-day Adventists, 1889 Year Book, p. 147, preamble_ (1889)

> III. That the Holy Scriptures of the Old and New Testaments were given by inspiration of God, contain a full revelation of his will to man, and are the only infallible rule of faith and practice.

— **FP1889 148.1** · Unsigned statement (corpus lists General Conference of SDA), _Fundamental Principles of Seventh-day Adventists, 1889 Year Book, p. 148, Principle III_ (1889)

> In presenting to the public this synopsis of our faith, we wish to have it distinctly understood that we have no articles of faith, creed, or discipline, aside from the Bible. We do not put forth this as having any authority with our people, nor is it designed to secure uniformity among them, as a system of faith, but is a brief statement of what is, and has been, with great unanimity, held by them.

— **FP1872 3.1** · Unsigned statement (corpus lists General Conference of SDA), _A Declaration of the Fundamental Principles Taught and Practiced by the Seventh-day Adventists (Battle Creek: SDA Publishing Association, 1872), preface_ (1872)

> The bible is a perfect, and complete revelation. It is our only rule of faith and practice. But this is no reason, why God may not show the past, present, and future fulfilment of his word, in these last days, by dreams and visions; according to Peter’s testimony. True visions are given to lead us to God, and his written word; but those that are given for a new rule of faith and practice, separate from the bible, cannot be from God, and should be rejected.

— **AWLF 13.4** · James White (unsigned editorial section introducing Ellen White’s vision; the corpus lists the pamphlet under James White), _A Word to the “Little Flock” (1847)_ (1847)

> The first step of apostasy is to get up a creed, telling us what we shall believe. The second is, to make that creed a test of fellowship. The third is to try members by that creed. The fourth to denounce as heretics those who do not believe that creed. And, fifth, to commence persecution against such.

— **ARSH October 8, 1861, page 149.7** · J. N. Loughborough (speech as reported in the conference minutes; U. Smith, secretary), _“Doings of the Battle Creek Conference, Oct. 5 & 6, 1861,” Advent Review and Sabbath Herald, October 8, 1861_ (1861)

> Now I take the ground that creeds stand in direct opposition to the gifts. … The Bible is our creed. We reject everything in the form of a human creed. We take the Bible and the gifts of the Spirit; embracing the faith that thus the Lord will teach us from time to time.

— **ARSH October 8, 1861, page 149.9** · James White (speech as reported in the conference minutes), _“Doings of the Battle Creek Conference, Oct. 5 & 6, 1861,” Advent Review and Sabbath Herald, October 8, 1861_ (1861)

> But God will have a people upon the earth to maintain the Bible, and the Bible only, as the standard of all doctrines and the basis of all reforms. The opinions of learned men, the deductions of science, the creeds or decisions of ecclesiastical councils, as numerous and discordant as are the churches which they represent, the voice of the majority,—not one or all of these should be regarded as evidence for or against any point of religious faith. Before accepting any doctrine or precept, we should demand a plain “Thus saith the Lord” in its support.

— **4SP 413.2** · Ellen G. White, _The Spirit of Prophecy, vol. 4 (1884); the same passage stands in The Great Controversy (GC 595.1)_ (1884)

> We stand on the great Protestant platform that “the Bible and the Bible alone” is our rule of faith and practice. … We believe that God by his grace and his providence has given existence to the book we call “the Bible” as a revelation of his will to man; that holy men wrote it, as God spoke to them face to face, or moved upon them by the Holy Spirit, or revealed truths to them in visions or dreams, … that this volume is complete in itself, and is to have nothing taken from it nor added to it; and that this is set forth as the standard and test of all moral teaching, all spiritual exercises, and all revelation purporting to be either human or divine.

— **RCASDA 75.4** · Uriah Smith, _Replies to Elder Canright’s Attacks on Seventh-day Adventists (1887)_ (1887)

## Film direction

**MUST SHOW**

- One Bible of both Testaments as the single measuring rule. Creeds, councils, scholars' opinions, science, majority votes and visions are all measured by it and never set beside it (FP1889 148.1; 4SP 413.2; RCASDA 75.4).
- "A plain “Thus saith the Lord”" as the test before any doctrine is accepted (4SP 413.2).
- The Bible's origin as these writers state it: "holy men wrote it, as God spoke to them face to face, or moved upon them by the Holy Spirit, or revealed truths to them in visions or dreams" (RCASDA 75.4), and it was "given by inspiration of God" (FP1889 148.1).
- The statement of beliefs as a summary offered to answer "every man that asketh". It is not put forth "as having any authority with our people" (FP1889 147.1; FP1872 3.1).

**MUST NOT SAY OR SHOW**

- Do not present the 1872/1889 statement as a creed, confession or test of fellowship (FP1889 147.1; FP1872 3.1). Loughborough, 1861: "The first step of apostasy is to get up a creed" (ARSH October 8, 1861, page 149.7). James White, 1861: "The Bible is our creed" (ARSH October 8, 1861, page 149.9).
- Do not show Ellen White's writings or any vision as a second Bible, an addition to it, or "a new rule of faith and practice, separate from the bible" (AWLF 13.4; RCASDA 75.4; see gift-of-prophecy).
- Do not narrate the Bible's formation as the work of later editors, schools or communities. These sources say "holy men wrote it" as God spoke, moved or revealed (RCASDA 75.4). BibleProject-style explainers often make this move, and nothing here supports it.
- Do not place tradition, church councils, scholarly consensus or majority opinion beside Scripture as evidence for doctrine (4SP 413.2).

## Distinctives

- Many evangelical explainers pair sola scriptura with the historic creeds as boundary markers. These pioneers reject creeds as such (FP1889 147.1; FP1872 3.1; ARSH October 8, 1861, page 149.7; ARSH October 8, 1861, page 149.9).
- Cessationist evangelicals tie Scripture's sufficiency to the end of visions. These pioneers hold the Bible as the only rule and still expect visions under it, visions "given to lead us to God, and his written word" (AWLF 13.4; ARSH October 8, 1861, page 149.9).
