# The Three Angels' Messages (1889 Principles XX, XXI)

## Pioneer position

Principles XX–XXI (FP1889 150.8, 151.1) hold that the proclamation of the second advent "is symbolized by the three messages of Revelation 14, the last one bringing to view the work of reform on the law of God", each angel symbolizing "a body of religious teachers" (DAR 586.2): the first announces "The hour of his Judgment is come", a "work of judgment which began in 1844" and "will extend to the close of human probation" (GC88 435.2); the second, Babylon's "moral fall" (TAR 51.2); the third, a warning whose period "synchronizes with the period of the cleansing of the sanctuary" (DAR 622.2). The mark it warns against is "not a literal mark, but a prominent profession", namely "the observance of the first day of the week as a holy day of rest instead of the seventh" (PTJW April 1850, page 67.1), received when "Sunday observance shall be enforced by law" and "the issue is thus plainly set before the people" (GC88 449.1); the messages' fruit is "they that keep the commandments of God, and the faith of Jesus" (GC88 435.2).

— **GC88 435.2** · Ellen G. White, _The Great Controversy_ (1888 ed.) (1888)

> These angels are evidently symbolic; for the work assigned them is that of preaching the everlasting gospel to the people. But the preaching of the gospel has not been intrusted to literal angels; it has been committed unto men, who are responsible for this sacred trust placed in their hands. Each of these three angels, therefore, symbolizes a body of religious teachers, who are commissioned to make known to their fellow men the special truths which constitute the burden of these messages respectively.

— **DAR 586.2** · Uriah Smith, _Daniel and the Revelation_ (1897)

> What constitutes the fall of Babylon? Those who contend that the Babylon of Revelation is the city of Rome, answer that the fall of Babylon is the burning of Rome; while those who make Babylon a symbol of the church of Rome only, answer that this fall is the loss of her civil power-the fall of the woman from the beast. We dissent from both these positions, believing that the fall of Babylon is a moral fall, and that it denotes her rejection as a body, by God.

— **TAR 51.2** · J. N. Andrews, _The Three Angels of Revelation 14:6-12_ (1855)

> Thus the Sabbath reform was brought to view; and it was seen that whatever had been done in opposition to this law, especially in the introduction of a day of rest and worship which destroyed the Sabbath of Jehovah, must be the work of the papal beast, that power which was to oppose God, and try to exalt himself above him. But this is the very work in reference to which the third angel utters his warning; hence it began to be seen that the period of the third message synchronizes with the period of the cleansing of the sanctuary, which began with the ending of the 2300 days in 1844, and that the proclamation is based on the great truths developed by this subject.

— **DAR 622.2** · Uriah Smith, _Daniel and the Revelation_ (1897)

> This mark is very conspicuous, in the forehead or hand, and signifies not a literal mark, but a prominent profession, that all may see and know. It is the mark of the beast; therefore it is a prominent point of religious faith introduced by the Papal power, which is the observance of the first day of the week as a holy day of rest instead of the seventh.

> …there are now true Christians in every church, not excepting the Roman Catholic communion, who honestly believe that Sunday is the Sabbath of divine appointment. But when Sunday observance shall be enforced by law, and the world shall be enlightened concerning the obligation of the true Sabbath, then whoever shall transgress the command of God, to obey a precept which has no higher authority than that of Rome, will thereby honor popery above God. … And it is not until the issue is thus plainly set before the people, and they are brought to choose between the commandments of God and the commandments of men, that those who continue in transgression will receive “the mark of the beast.”

## Film direction

MUST SHOW:

- **Angels as symbols of people.** Show the angel figures resolving into groups of human preachers: "Each of these three angels, therefore, symbolizes a body of religious teachers" (DAR 586.2).
- **First angel as a clock.** Start the clock for "The hour of his Judgment is come" at 1844 and run it to "the close of human probation" (GC88 435.2). It is a judgment in progress before the advent, "a time of investigative judgment" (FP1889 151.1).
- **Second angel as a moral collapse.** Babylon's fall is her "rejection as a body, by God", not a burning city (TAR 51.2). "Babylon includes Protestant as well as Catholic churches" (TAR 57.1).
- **Third angel as a chain.** Link the images in this order (DAR 622.2):
  1. The opened heavenly ark: "Having their attention thus called to the ark, they were naturally led to an examination of the law contained in the ark."
  2. The fourth commandment: "its fourth precept, now as in the beginning, demands the observance of the seventh day of the week as the Sabbath".
  3. The "Sabbath reform".
  4. The third angel's warning.
- **The final choice.** When "Sunday observance shall be enforced by law", people are "brought to choose between the commandments of God and the commandments of men" (GC88 449.1). The two-horned beast "establishes by law the observance of the first day of the week" (DAR 624.6).
- **The people at the end.** The messages produce "they that keep the commandments of God, and the faith of Jesus" (GC88 435.2). Smith explains this as "the ten commandments, and the faith of Jesus — all the teachings of Christ and his apostles as contained in the New Testament" (DAR 633.1). Draw the tables together with Christ, not the tables alone.

MUST NOT SAY OR SHOW:

- **A physical mark.** No chip, tattoo, barcode or brand: it is "not a literal mark, but a prominent profession" (PTJW April 1850, page 67.1; TTAM 8.1).
- **A mark with no content.** Do not leave it as generic allegiance to empire or a symbol of economic compromise. The sources name its content: "a false and counterfeit Sabbath, the first day of the week, is set up in its stead" (DAR 624.3; PTJW April 1850, page 67.1).
- **Sunday-keepers as marked now.** Do not say or show it. In 1888, "there are now true Christians in every church … God accepts their sincerity" (GC88 449.1). The mark comes only with legal enforcement and a plainly set issue (GC88 449.1).
- **Babylon as Rome alone.** Do not make it only ancient Rome burning, or only the Roman church (TAR 51.2; TAR 57.1).
- **The judgment hour only at the Second Coming.** The judgment "began in 1844" (GC88 435.2; FP1889 151.1).
- **Literal angels preaching.** The preaching "has been committed unto men" (DAR 586.2).

## Distinctives

- **Judgment hour.** Mainstream: the last judgment at Christ's return. Pioneers: a pre-advent "investigative judgment" (FP1889 151.1). It began in 1844 and runs to the close of probation (GC88 435.2; DAR 622.2).
- **Babylon.** Mainstream readings: first-century Rome, a future world system, or every rebellious empire. Pioneers: a religious body whose fall is "a moral fall" (TAR 51.2), including "Protestant as well as Catholic churches" (TAR 57.1).
- **The mark.** Mainstream readings: a future literal mark, or allegiance to the emperor. Pioneers:
  - it is not literal (PTJW April 1850, page 67.1);
  - it is "an institution of Papacy, enforced by Protestantism" (TAR 110.1);
  - its content is first-day rest in place of the Sabbath (DAR 624.3), enforced "by law" (DAR 624.6).
- **The commandments of God in Revelation 14:12.** Mainstream: Christ's commands in general. Pioneers: "the ten commandments", alongside "the faith of Jesus" (DAR 633.1).
- **When the mark is received (a tension inside the pioneer sources; the script must pick one).** James White in 1850 identifies the mark with first-day observance as such (PTJW April 1850, page 67.1). Ellen White in 1888 says no one receives it until Sunday observance is "enforced by law" and "the issue is thus plainly set before the people", and she counts present sincere Sunday-keepers as accepted (GC88 449.1). Smith also has first-day observance established "by law" (DAR 624.6). Use the 1888 enforcement framing.

## Unverified / not found

- **PTJW author field.** The corpus's author field for PTJW lists S.N. Haskell, Wm. Ings and J.H. Durland, which is wrong for this 1850 Oswego paper. The masthead reads "VOL. I.-OSWEGO, N. Y., APRIL, 1850.-NO. 9." The writer is taken from the identical text of the tract _The Third Angel's Message_ (TTAM 8.1). That tract closes with the signature line JAMES WHITE., a paragraph the corpus stores with no refcode, so the verifier cannot check it; it was read directly in the database.
- **Why GC88.** _The Great Controversy_ (1911: GC 435.2 and GC 449.1) carries the same two paragraphs with small changes in wording, capitals and punctuation. GC88 is the 1888 edition, whose author's preface is dated May 1888. It is quoted as the earlier text.
- **TAR's year.** The corpus copy of TAR has no dated title page. The year 1855 comes from _The Advent Review, and Sabbath Herald_: on March 20, 1855 the paper announced the pamphlet as "ready for delivery in about one week" (ARSH March 20, 1855, page 200.4), and on April 17, 1855 it listed it at 148 pages (ARSH April 17, 1855, page 216.43). The same notice says "A portion of this Work has been published in the REVIEW."
- **Why TAR rather than TMR.** Andrews's later _The Three Messages of Revelation 14:6-12_ (TMR) is the 1877 revision; its preface is signed "J. N. A. Battle Creek, Mich., Jan., 1877" (TMR 5.2; compiler's correction of the research pass, which reported TMR undated). The earlier 1855 TAR is quoted here; `prophecy-chains` quotes TMR 108.3.
