# The Holy Spirit — 1889 Principles I, XVII, XIX

## Pioneer position

Principle I (1889) says God is "everywhere present by his representative, the Holy Spirit"; Principle XVII makes the heart's transformation "the special work of the Holy Spirit," and Principle XIX gives the Spirit the work "to lead to an understanding of that word which it had inspired, to convince of sin, and to work a transformation in the heart and life." On the Spirit's nature the quoted writers differ by date: "that awful and mysterious power which proceeds from the throne of the universe" (J. H. Waggoner, 1877); "the divine afflatus and medium of their power, representative of them both" (Uriah Smith, 1898); "Christ’s representative, but divested of the personality of humanity" and "the Third Person of the Godhead" (Ellen White, 1898); "The nature of the Holy Spirit is a mystery" (Ellen White, 1911).

## Quotes

> I. That there is one God, a personal, spiritual being, … unchangeable, and everywhere present by his representative, the Holy Spirit. Psalm 139:7.

— **FP1889 147.2** · Unsigned statement (corpus lists General Conference of SDA), _Fundamental Principles of Seventh-day Adventists, 1889 Year Book, p. 147, Principle I_ (1889)

> XVII. That as the natural or carnal heart is at enmity with God and his law, this enmity can be subdued only by a radical transformation of the affections, the exchange of unholy for holy principles; that this transformation follows repentance and faith, is the special work of the Holy Spirit, and constitutes regeneration, or conversion.

— **FP1889 150.5** · Unsigned statement (corpus lists General Conference of SDA), _Fundamental Principles of Seventh-day Adventists, 1889 Year Book, p. 150, Principle XVII_ (1889)

> … that, in specifying the various channels of its operation, that Spirit has simply made provision for its own existence and presence with the people of God to the end of time, to lead to an understanding of that word which it had inspired, to convince of sin, and to work a transformation in the heart and life; and that those who deny to the Spirit its place and operation, do plainly deny that part of the Bible which assigns to it this work and position.

— **FP1889 150.7** · Unsigned statement (corpus lists General Conference of SDA), _Fundamental Principles of Seventh-day Adventists, 1889 Year Book, p. 150, Principle XIX_ (1889)

> God is everywhere by virtue of his Spirit, which is his representative, and is manifested wherever he pleases, as will be seen by the very words the objector claims, referred to above. Verses 7-10. “Whither shall I go from thy Spirit? or whither shall I flee from thy presence? …

— **ARSH June 18, 1861, page 28.23** · James White, _“Personality of God,” unsigned editorial, Advent Review and Sabbath Herald, June 18, 1861 (the same text is signed “J. W.” in the tract Personality of God, PERGO 6.1)_ (1861)

> We are not only willing but anxious to leave it just where the word of God leaves it. From it we learn that the Spirit of God is that awful and mysterious power which proceeds from the throne of the universe, and which is the efficient actor of the work of creation and of redemption.

— **SGOM 9.1** · J. H. Waggoner, _The Spirit of God (Battle Creek, 1877)_ (1877)

> And then the Holy Spirit (by an infirmity of translation called .. the Holy Ghost”), the Spirit of God, the Spirit of Christ, the divine afflatus and medium of their power, representative of them both (Psalm 139:7), was in existence also.

— **LUJ 10.1** · Uriah Smith, _Looking Unto Jesus (Battle Creek, 1898)_ (1898)

> The Holy Spirit is Christ’s representative, but divested of the personality of humanity, and independent thereof. Cumbered with humanity, Christ could not be in every place personally. Therefore it was for their interest that He should go to the Father, and send the Spirit to be His successor on earth. … By the Spirit the Saviour would be accessible to all.

— **DA 669.2** · Ellen G. White, _The Desire of Ages (1898)_ (1898)

> The Spirit was to be given as a regenerating agent, and without this the sacrifice of Christ would have been of no avail. … Sin could be resisted and overcome only through the mighty agency of the Third Person of the Godhead, who would come with no modified energy, but in the fullness of divine power. It is the Spirit that makes effectual what has been wrought out by the world’s Redeemer. It is by the Spirit that the heart is made pure.

— **DA 671.2** · Ellen G. White, _The Desire of Ages (1898)_ (1898)

> The nature of the Holy Spirit is a mystery. Men cannot explain it, because the Lord has not revealed it to them. Men having fanciful views may bring together passages of Scripture and put a human construction on them, but the acceptance of these views will not strengthen the church. Regarding such mysteries, which are too deep for human understanding, silence is golden.

— **AA 52.1** · Ellen G. White, _The Acts of the Apostles (1911)_ (1911)

## Film direction

**MUST SHOW**

- God's presence reaching every place through his Spirit. Stage the Psalm 139 flight (heaven, "the wings of the morning", "the uttermost parts of the sea") with God already present everywhere (FP1889 147.2; ARSH June 18, 1861, page 28.23).
- The ascended Christ sending the Spirit "to be His successor on earth", so that the Saviour is "accessible to all". Show Christ gone to the Father and the Spirit making him reachable in every place (DA 669.2).
- The Spirit's work as the sources list it: convincing of sin, leading to an understanding of the word it inspired, transforming heart and life, regeneration, and the heart "made pure" (FP1889 150.5; FP1889 150.7; DA 671.2).
- Power "which proceeds from the throne of the universe" and acts in creation and redemption (SGOM 9.1).

**MUST NOT SAY OR SHOW**

- Do not give the Spirit a human body or face, and do not seat a third figure on the throne. The Spirit is Christ's representative "divested of the personality of humanity" (DA 669.2), and Ellen White's throne vision shows only the Father and the Son (Broadside1 April 6, 1846, par. 7, in god-christ).
- Do not settle the Spirit's nature in narration. Present neither "an impersonal force" nor "the third person of the Trinity" as "the pioneer view". The sources differ by date (SGOM 9.1, 1877; LUJ 10.1, 1898; DA 671.2, 1898), and Ellen White (1911) says "silence is golden" (AA 52.1). If "the Third Person of the Godhead" is quoted, credit it to Ellen White, 1898 (DA 671.2), not to the 1889 statement.
- When quoting Principle XIX, keep its "it"/"its" for the Spirit. Do not silently change them to "he"/"his" (FP1889 150.7).
- Do not show the Spirit guiding apart from Scripture. In XIX the Spirit leads "to an understanding of that word which it had inspired" (FP1889 150.7).

## Distinctives

- A mainstream evangelical explainer introduces the Spirit as the third person of the Trinity, co-equal with Father and Son. The sources here speak in other terms:
  - The 1889 statement calls the Spirit God's "representative" (FP1889 147.2) and uses "it"/"its" (FP1889 150.7).
  - James White, 1861: "his Spirit, which is his representative" (ARSH June 18, 1861, page 28.23).
  - J. H. Waggoner, 1877: the "personality of the Spirit of God" is a question "upon which we have never presumed to enter" (SGOM 8.2, read, not excerpted). He calls the Spirit "that awful and mysterious power" (SGOM 9.1).
  - Smith, 1898: "the divine afflatus and medium of their power" (LUJ 10.1).
  - Ellen White writes "the Third Person of the Godhead" in 1898 (DA 671.2), "The Holy Spirit is a person" in a 1906 manuscript (21LtMs, Ms 20, 1906, par. 31, read, not excerpted), and "The nature of the Holy Spirit is a mystery" in 1911 (AA 52.1).
  - Show these as dated wordings. Do not merge them into one position.
- Explainers that center the Spirit on gifts or experiences differ from the 1889 emphasis on regeneration and understanding the inspired word (FP1889 150.5; FP1889 150.7).

## Unverified / not found

- MEDM March 27, 1907, page 98.7 ("The Holy Spirit is a Person, eternally a divine Person"): the corpus lists this Medical Missionary volume under A. T. Jones as editor. The article's writer was not established, so it is not used.
- ARSH January 10, 1899, page 24.2 ("God is one. Jesus Christ is one. The Holy Spirit is one. And these three are one"): the corpus lists this Review volume under A. T. Jones. The writer was not established, so it is not used.
- AMS November 23, 1899, page 732.2 ("the Holy Spirit is Christ’s representative"): the corpus lists E. J. Waggoner. The writer was not established, so it is not used.
- The Year Book scan's OCR prints Principle I's proof text as "Ps. 189; 7". The scan-checked text and the corpus read Psalm 139:7.
